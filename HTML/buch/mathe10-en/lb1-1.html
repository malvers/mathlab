UNIT 1SYSTEMATISING · 4 LESSONS

1Sorting triangles and quadrilaterals

When you sort them, the square ends up right at the bottom: it is a rectangle, a rhombus, a parallelogram, a trapezium and a kite all at once. Right at the top is the general quadrilateral, which need not have any special property at all. This order is called the family tree of quadrilaterals.

You sort triangles in two ways: by their sides (scalene, isosceles, equilateral) and by their largest angle (acute-angled, right-angled, obtuse-angled). For every figure: if you split an $n$-gon into triangles by diagonals from one vertex, you get $n - 2$ triangles. The angle sum follows from this.

KEY POINT · ANGLE SUMS AND QUADRILATERALS

Angle sum in a triangle $180^\circ$, in a quadrilateral $360^\circ$, in an $n$-gon $(n - 2) \cdot 180^\circ$.

Trapezium: one pair of parallel sides. Parallelogram: two pairs of parallel sides, the diagonals bisect each other. Rhombus: four sides of equal length, the diagonals are perpendicular. Rectangle: four right angles, diagonals of equal length. Square: a rectangle and a rhombus at the same time. Kite: two pairs of equal adjacent sides, the diagonals perpendicular.

Areas: parallelogram $A = g \cdot h$, trapezium $A = \dfrac{a + c}{2} \cdot h$, rhombus and kite $A = \dfrac{e \cdot f}{2}$, triangle $A = \dfrac{g \cdot h}{2}$.

Neighbouring angles: in a parallelogram and on each leg of a trapezium, neighbouring angles add up to $180^\circ$.

A polygon is called regular if all its sides and all its angles are equal.

EXAMPLE 1 · ANGLES IN A TRAPEZIUM

In an isosceles trapezium $ABCD$ with $AB \parallel CD$, $\alpha = 65^\circ$. Find the other angles.

Isosceles: $\beta = \alpha = 65^\circ$. On each leg the angles add up to $180^\circ$: $\gamma = \delta = 115^\circ$.

Check: $65 + 65 + 115 + 115 = 360$.

EXAMPLE 2 · REGULAR DECAGON

How large is an interior angle of a regular decagon?

Angle sum $(10 - 2) \cdot 180^\circ = 1440^\circ$, shared among ten equal angles: $144^\circ$.

EXERCISES

True or false? a) Every square is a rhombus. b) Every parallelogram is a trapezium. c) Every rectangle is a kite.

a) true   b) true, it even has two pairs of parallel sides   c) false, only the square

In an isosceles triangle the angle at the apex is $36^\circ$. How large are the base angles?

$(180^\circ - 36^\circ) : 2 = 72^\circ$

A rhombus has the diagonals 12 cm and 9 cm. Calculate its area and its side length.

The diagonals bisect each other and are perpendicular: four right-angled triangles with 6 cm and 4.5 cm.

$A = \tfrac{12 \cdot 9}{2} = 54\,\text{cm}^2$, $a = \sqrt{6^2 + 4.5^2} = \sqrt{56.25} = 7.5$ cm.

A pentagon has the angles $100^\circ$, $110^\circ$, $95^\circ$ and $120^\circ$. How large is the fifth angle?

Angle sum $540^\circ$: $540^\circ - 425^\circ = 115^\circ$.

A regular polygon has interior angles of $150^\circ$. How many vertices does it have? Give a reason.

Each exterior angle is $30^\circ$, and all the exterior angles together make one full turn.

$360^\circ : 30^\circ = 12$: a dodecagon. Check: $(12 - 2) \cdot 180^\circ : 12 = 150^\circ$.

2Trigonometry in figures

In the gable triangle you drop a perpendicular from the ridge to the loft floor. It divides the triangle into two right-angled halves: half the width, 4.5 m, is adjacent to the angle of slope $40^\circ$, and the height of the gable lies opposite it. So $h = 4.5 \cdot \tan 40^\circ \approx 3.8$ m.

In a right-angled triangle the side opposite the right angle is called the hypotenuse. Seen from an acute angle, one of the other two sides lies next to it (the adjacent side), the other lies opposite it (the opposite side). If you know an acute angle and one side, you get every other side. If you know two sides, you get the angle with the keys $\sin^{-1}$, $\cos^{-1}$ or $\tan^{-1}$.

KEY POINT · SINE, COSINE, TANGENT

$\sin \alpha = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$, $\quad \cos \alpha = \dfrac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$, $\quad \tan \alpha = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$

In figures: draw in a height or a diagonal so that a right-angled triangle with known parts appears.

Gradient $p\,\%$ of a road or ramp: $\tan \alpha = \dfrac{p}{100}$.

Calculator: it must be set to DEG (degrees). If it shows $\sin 30 = -0.988$, it is set to radians.

Special values: $\sin 30^\circ = \cos 60^\circ = 0.5$, $\tan 45^\circ = 1$.

INTERACTIVE · SINE, COSINE, TANGENT

Change the angle and watch how the three ratios of sides change. At which angle are the opposite and the adjacent side equally long?

EXAMPLE 3 · ISOSCELES TRIANGLE

An isosceles triangle has the base 10 cm and legs of 13 cm. Calculate the height and the base angles.

The height bisects the base: part triangle with 5 cm and 13 cm. $h = \sqrt{169 - 25} = 12$ cm.

$\cos \beta = \tfrac{5}{13}$, so $\beta \approx 67.4^\circ$.

EXAMPLE 4 · ISOSCELES TRAPEZIUM

A trapezium has the parallel sides 14 cm and 8 cm and legs of 5 cm. How high is it, and how large are the angles at the bottom?

Overhang on each side $(14 - 8) : 2 = 3$ cm. $h = \sqrt{25 - 9} = 4$ cm, $\cos \alpha = \tfrac35$, $\alpha \approx 53.1^\circ$.

EXERCISES

In a right-angled triangle the hypotenuse is 20 cm long and $\alpha = 25^\circ$. Calculate the two other sides.

$a = 20 \cdot \sin 25^\circ \approx 8.5$ cm, $b = 20 \cdot \cos 25^\circ \approx 18.1$ cm.

A ladder 5 m long makes an angle of $70^\circ$ with the ground. At what height does it lean against the wall?

$h = 5 \cdot \sin 70^\circ \approx 4.7$ m

A ramp has a gradient of 6 %. How large is the angle of slope? How long must it be horizontally to rise 0.5 m?

$\tan \alpha = 0.06$, $\alpha \approx 3.4^\circ$. Length $0.5 : 0.06 \approx 8.3$ m.

A rectangle is 8 cm long and 6 cm wide. At what angle do its diagonals intersect?

The diagonal makes the angle $\alpha$ with $\tan \alpha = \tfrac68$ with the long side. The diagonals form an isosceles triangle with the long side.

$\alpha \approx 36.9^\circ$. In the isosceles triangle on the long side the angle at the apex is $180^\circ - 2 \cdot 36.9^\circ \approx 106.3^\circ$, the other angle of intersection $73.7^\circ$.

From 50 m away you see the top of a tower at $32^\circ$. Your eyes are 1.6 m above the ground. How high is the tower? What error arises if you forget the eye height?

$50 \cdot \tan 32^\circ \approx 31.2$ m above eye height, tower $\approx 32.8$ m. Without the eye height the result would be 1.6 m too small.

REVIEW · DEEPEN · CONNECT

A kite has the diagonals 60 cm and 40 cm. How large is its area?

$60 \cdot 40 : 2 = 1200\,\text{cm}^2$

A pitched roof is 9 m wide, and its slope is $40^\circ$. Calculate the height of the gable and the length of the rafters from the ridge to the eaves.

$h = 4.5 \cdot \tan 40^\circ \approx 3.8$ m, rafters $s = \tfrac{4.5}{\cos 40^\circ} \approx 5.9$ m.

A path is 120 m long, measured along the slope, and rises 9 m. What is its angle of slope, and roughly what percentage gradient does it have?

$\sin \alpha = \tfrac{9}{120}$, $\alpha \approx 4.3^\circ$. Horizontally $\sqrt{120^2 - 81} \approx 119.7$ m, gradient $\approx 7.5\,\%$.

Connecting: a rhombus has the side 6 cm and an interior angle of $60^\circ$. Calculate both diagonals and the area.

Short diagonal 6 cm (two equilateral triangles), long one $2 \cdot 6 \cdot \cos 30^\circ \approx 10.4$ cm, $A = \tfrac{6 \cdot 10.39}{2} \approx 31.2\,\text{cm}^2$.

EXCURSIONTHE HISTORY OF A WORD

How the sine got its name

Indian astronomers were already calculating with half-chords in the circle around the year 500. The mathematician Aryabhata called the half-chord “jya-ardha”, or “jya” for short. Arab scholars took over the word as the sound “jiba” and wrote it, as is usual in Arabic, without vowels: “jb”.

When the texts were translated into Latin in Spain in the 12th century, “jb” was read as “jaib”, the Arabic word for a bay or a fold in a garment. It was translated with the Latin word for bay: sinus. And so a misreading found its way into every maths book in the world.

Source: V. J. Katz: A History of Mathematics. 3rd edition, Addison-Wesley 2009, chapters 6 and 8.
