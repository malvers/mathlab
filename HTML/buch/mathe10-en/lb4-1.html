UNIT 14 LESSONS

1Wages, deductions and household budget

At the top of the payslip is the gross wage. Income tax and the social security contributions are deducted from it: pension, health, long-term care and unemployment insurance. What is left is transferred to you: the net wage. The company pays the social security contributions a second time on top, in roughly the same amount: the employer’s contribution.

KEY POINT · GROSS AND NET

Net = gross − income tax − social security contributions. Percentages refer to the gross amount.

Cost to the company = gross + employer’s contribution.

Purchasing power: if wages rise by $p\%$ and prices by $q\%$, purchasing power grows by the factor $\dfrac{1 + p/100}{1 + q/100}$.

Assumed: the rates in this chapter are simplified. The real statutory values change almost every year.

Rule of thumb for rent: at most about a third of your net income should go on rent including heating.

EXAMPLE 1 · FROM GROSS TO NET

From a gross wage of €2400, €250 income tax and €480 social security contributions are deducted. Calculate the net wage and the share of the deductions.

Net $2400 - 730 =$ €1670, deductions $\tfrac{730}{2400} \approx$ 30.4%.

EXAMPLE 2 · THE HOUSEHOLD BUDGET

Lea has €1700 net. Rent €550, food €300, electricity and phone €120, transport €90, leisure €150. What is left, how high is the share of the rent?

Expenses €1210, left over €490. Rent $\tfrac{550}{1700} \approx$ 32.4% – just under a third.

EXERCISES

A temporary worker earns €14 per hour and works 160 hours a month. Deductions: 20%. How much is left net?

Gross €2240, net $2240 \cdot 0.8 = 1792$ euros.

Assume the pension contribution is 18.6% and is split half and half. How much does the employee pay on €2500 gross, how much goes to the pension fund in total?

$2500 \cdot 0.093 = 232.50$ euros, €465 in total.

There is a 25% supplement for overtime. The hourly wage is €16. What do 12 hours of overtime bring in gross?

$16 \cdot 1.25 = 20$ euros per hour, so €240.

Wages rise by 3%, prices by 2%. By what percentage can you afford more in real terms?

$\tfrac{1.03}{1.02} \approx 1.0098$: just under 1%.

A full-time job (40 hours) pays €3200 gross. Someone works 30 hours at the same hourly wage and saves 10% of €1650 net. Calculate the gross wage and the annual savings.

Gross $3200 \cdot 0.75 = 2400$ euros. Savings $165 \cdot 12 = 1980$ euros a year.

UNIT 24 LESSONS

2Saving, credit and the debt trap

With the phone contract you pay $24 \cdot 45 = 1080$ euros. Without the phone it would be $24 \cdot 15 = 360$ euros. So the “free” phone costs €720 – €120 more than in the shop. Instalment purchases and contracts often hide exactly such costs.

KEY POINT · INTEREST AND CREDIT

Compound interest: $K_n = K_0 \cdot \left(1 + \tfrac{p}{100}\right)^n$. $\;$ Interest for parts of a year: $Z = K \cdot \tfrac{p}{100} \cdot \tfrac{t}{365}$ ($t$ in days).

Cost of credit = everything you pay back minus the amount borrowed.

The annual percentage rate (APR) includes all the costs of a loan. It is what you use to compare offers.

Debt trap: if the instalment only covers the interest, the debt never goes down. New loans to pay old instalments are an alarm signal.

Help: debt advice services run by consumer advice centres and charities give advice free of charge.

Overdraft: the overdraft on a current account is convenient, but usually one of the most expensive kinds of credit.

EXAMPLE 3 · BUYING IN INSTALMENTS

A television costs €900 in cash or 12 instalments of €82. How much more does buying in instalments cost, as a percentage of the cash price?

$12 \cdot 82 = 984$ euros, so €84 more, $\tfrac{84}{900} \approx$ 9.3%.

EXAMPLE 4 · THE TRAP

€1000 of debt at 12% per year, €10 is paid back every month. What happens?

Monthly interest $1000 \cdot 0.12 \div 12 = 10$ euros. The instalment only covers the interest: the debt never goes down.

EXERCISES

€5000 is invested for 5 years at 3% with compound interest. How large is the balance? How much would it be without compound interest?

$5000 \cdot 1.03^5 \approx 5796.37$ euros, without compound interest €5750.

An account is €500 overdrawn for 3 months, interest rate 12% per year. How much interest is charged?

$500 \cdot 0.12 \cdot \tfrac{3}{12} = 15$ euros

A loan of €2400 is paid back in 24 monthly instalments of €110. What does it cost, what percentage is that?

$2640 - 2400 = 240$ euros, that is 10% of the loan (spread over two years).

For €2000 over one year: bank A 5% interest, bank B 4.5% interest plus a €100 fee. Which offer is cheaper?

A: €100, B: $90 + 100 = 190$ euros. A is cheaper.

€1000 lies in a cupboard without interest, prices rise by 2% a year. What is the money worth after 5 years in today’s purchasing power?

$1000 \div 1.02^5 \approx 905.73$ euros.

REVIEW · DEEPEN · CONNECT

In one year, €2000 becomes €2060. What is the interest rate?

$60 \div 2000 = 3\%$

After one year at 2.5% there is €4100 in the account. What was the initial capital? Why is $4100 - 2.5\%$ wrong?

$4100 \div 1.025 = 4000$ euros. The interest refers to the initial capital, not to the final amount.

€3650 earns interest for 73 days at 2% per year. How much interest is there?

$3650 \cdot 0.02 \cdot \tfrac{73}{365} = 14.60$ euros

Connecting with learning area 2: after about how many years does a balance double at 4%? Check the rule of thumb $72 \div p$ with the calculator.

$72 \div 4 = 18$ years, and $1.04^{18} \approx 2.03$. The rule of thumb fits.

EXCURSIONHISTORY

Saving for everyone

For a long time only rich people could invest their money at interest. In 1778 the “Patriotic Society” in Hamburg founded an “Ersparungsclasse” (savings fund) so that servants, craftsmen and sailors could also save small amounts safely and receive interest. It is regarded as the world’s first savings bank.

The idea spread quickly. Today compound interest matters to almost everyone: with a savings account, with a building society savings plan – and with an overdraft, where it works against you.

Source: Deutscher Sparkassen- und Giroverband: Geschichte der Sparkassen, “1778 – Ersparungsclasse der Hamburger Patriotischen Gesellschaft”.
