UNIT 14 LESSONS

1Probabilities in word problems

If the traffic lights are independent, you get through without stopping in $0.4 \cdot 0.5 = 0.2$, that is in 20% of cases. So you have to stop at least once in 80%. With a “green wave” the lights would be coordinated – then the path rule no longer holds with these numbers. A model is always based on assumptions.

KEY POINT · MODELLING WITH PROBABILITIES

1. Recognise the stages of the experiment, draw a tree diagram, write the probabilities on the branches.

2. Apply the path rule and the sum rule, use the complementary event for “at least once”.

3. Answer the question in a sentence and check: does the result lie between 0 and 1? Is it plausible?

With or without replacement? With tickets, balls and cards, the probability changes after the first draw if they are not put back.

PICTURE · THE TREE DIAGRAM OF THE TRAFFIC LIGHTS
EXAMPLE 1 · QUALITY CONTROL

5% of the parts in a delivery are defective. Two parts are checked. How likely is it that exactly one is defective?

Two paths: $2 \cdot 0.05 \cdot 0.95 =$ 0.095, that is 9.5%.

EXAMPLE 2 · THE WARRANTY

A repair costs €120 and is needed with 15%. An extended warranty costs €25. Is it worth it on average?

Expected damage €18 < €25: on average, no.

EXERCISES

A bus is on time with 90% on each school day. How likely is it to be on time for a whole week (5 days)?

$0.9^5 \approx 0.59$

A test has 3 questions with 4 answers each. Someone guesses. How likely is it that all are right? How many right answers do you expect?

$\left(\tfrac14\right)^3 = \tfrac{1}{64}$, expected $3 \cdot \tfrac14 = 0.75$ right answers.

A raffle drum contains 5 tickets, 2 of them prizes. Two are drawn without replacement. How likely is at least one prize?

$1 - \tfrac35 \cdot \tfrac24 = 0.7$

In a board game you may roll three times to get a 6. How likely are you to succeed?

$1 - \left(\tfrac56\right)^3 \approx 0.421$

How many four-digit PINs are there? A card is blocked after three wrong attempts. How likely is it that a thief guesses the PIN?

$10^4 = 10{,}000$ PINs. Three different attempts: $\tfrac{3}{10{,}000} = 0.03\%$.

UNIT 24 LESSONS

2Functional relationships in everyday life

Plan A costs a basic fee of €5 and €0.10 per minute, plan B only €0.15 per minute. If you draw both as lines, you see at once: for few minutes B is cheaper, from the point of intersection on A. You find the point of intersection by setting the terms equal.

KEY POINT · CHECKING MODELS

Linear $y = mx + n$: the same increase per step, $n$ is the basic amount. Exponential $y = c \cdot a^x$: the same factor per step. Quadratic: trajectories, areas, braking distances.

Every model only holds within a range: a candle cannot have a negative length, followers cannot keep doubling for ever.

Vertex: for $y = ax^2 + bx + c$ it lies at $x = -\tfrac{b}{2a}$.

PICTURE · TWO PRICE PLANS

Up to 100 minutes plan B is cheaper, above that plan A.

EXAMPLE 3 · THE POINT OF INTERSECTION

From how many minutes on is plan A cheaper?

$5 + 0.10 t = 0.15 t$, $\;5 = 0.05 t$, $\;t = 100$. From 100 minutes on A is cheaper.

EXAMPLE 4 · THE TRAJECTORY

A ball flies along the path $h(x) = -0.05x^2 + x + 1.5$. What is the greatest height it reaches?

Vertex at $x = -\tfrac{1}{2 \cdot (-0.05)} = 10$: $h(10) = -5 + 10 + 1.5 =$ 6.5 m.

EXERCISES

A taxi costs a basic fare of €4 plus €2.20 per km. What do 12 km cost? How far do you get for €25?

€30.40. $(25 - 4) \div 2.2 \approx 9.5$ km.

An electricity plan: €12 per month plus €0.35 per kWh. What do 200 kWh cost? What do the two numbers in the term mean?

€82. €12 is the basic charge, €0.35 the price per kWh (gradient).

A device costing €800 loses a) €200 every year b) 20% of its value every year. What is it worth after 3 years in each case?

a) €200 b) $800 \cdot 0.8^3 = 409.60$ euros

A candle is 20 cm long and burns down 2 cm per hour. Give the term and the range in which the model makes sense.

$h(t) = 20 - 2t$ for $0 \le t \le 10$.

How high is the ball from example 4 after a horizontal distance of 4 m? Where does it land (to one decimal place)?

Landing means $h(x) = 0$. Use the quadratic formula.

$h(4) = 4.7$ m. $-0.05x^2 + x + 1.5 = 0$ gives $x \approx 21.4$ m.

UNIT 34 LESSONS

3Problem solving and basic knowledge without aids

With the Fermi question only a plan helps: how many people are there in the school? How often does each go to the toilet, how many litres does one flush need? If you split the question into sub-questions, you arrive at a well-founded estimate. The same strategy helps with every complex problem.

KEY POINT · STEPS OF PROBLEM SOLVING

1. Understand: what is given, what is wanted? Make a sketch. 2. Plan: which formula, which model fits? Split into sub-problems.

3. Calculate with units. 4. Check: estimate, substitute back, is the result realistic? 5. Answer in a sentence.

Without a calculator: percentages via 10% and 1%, fractions as decimals ($\tfrac14 = 0.25$), square numbers up to 20², converting units.

EXAMPLE 5 · THE LAWN

A circular lawn has a radius of 5 m. You need 30 g of seed per m². How much seed is needed (with $\pi \approx 3.14$)?

Plan: area, then mass. $A \approx 78.5\,\text{m}^2$, $78.5 \cdot 30 = 2355$ g. About 2.4 kg.

EXAMPLE 6 · THE CAR JOURNEY

A family drives 360 km, the car uses 6 l per 100 km, a litre costs €1.80. What does the fuel cost?

$3.6 \cdot 6 = 21.6$ l, $21.6 \cdot 1.80 =$ €38.88. Estimate: $20 \cdot 2 = 40$ euros.

REVIEW · DEEPEN · CONNECT

Solve $x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0$ and $x^2 + 2x - 8 = 0$.

The share of cyclists rises from 20% to 25%. By how many percentage points, by what percentage has it risen?

A ramp is to overcome 0.5 m and have a gradient of at most 6%. How long must it be horizontally, how large is the angle?

$0.5 \div 0.06 \approx 8.33$ m, $\alpha \approx 3.4^\circ$.

Connecting: a family is thinking of buying a car for €18,000 for 6 years, which loses 15% of its value every year. What is it worth afterwards? How much has it cost per year on average?

$18{,}000 \cdot 0.85^6 \approx 6789$ euros. Loss of value $\approx$ €11,211, so about €1869 per year.

EXCURSIONESTIMATING LIKE A PHYSICIST

How many piano tuners are there in Chicago?

The physicist Enrico Fermi liked to ask his students questions that sound impossible to answer. The most famous: how many piano tuners work in Chicago? You estimate the number of inhabitants, the share of households with a piano, how often a piano is tuned and how many pianos a tuner manages per year.

Each individual estimate is inaccurate, but the errors often cancel out. What you get is a number of the right order of magnitude – and that is exactly the point of a Fermi problem: to arrive at a well-founded result with common sense and estimation.

Source: H. C. von Baeyer: The Fermi Solution. Random House 1993.
