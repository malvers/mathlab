Quadratic functions and equations

A falling stone, a thrown ball, the braking distance of a car, the arch of a bridge: there is an $x^2$ everywhere. In uniformly accelerated motion the distance grows with the square of the time. In this chapter you learn to read parabolas, to solve quadratic equations without a calculator and to answer real questions with them.

UNIT 15 LESSONS

1Parabolas and uniformly accelerated motion

In time $t$ a freely falling body covers the distance $s(t) = \tfrac{g}{2}\,t^2$, with $g \approx 9.81\,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^2}$. After 2 seconds that is $4.905 \cdot 4 \approx 19.6$ m, but after 4 seconds $4.905 \cdot 16 \approx 78.5$ m, four times as much.

The speed grows linearly: $v(t) = g \cdot t$. After 10 s it is $98.1\,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}$. Because the body keeps getting faster, the distance grows quadratically. The graph of $s$ is one branch of a parabola.

KEY POINT · QUADRATIC FUNCTION

Vertex form: $f(x) = a(x - d)^2 + e$ with vertex $S(d, e)$.

Standard form: $f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$ with $y$-intercept $c$.

$a > 0$: opens upwards, the vertex is the lowest point · $a < 0$: opens downwards, the vertex is the highest point · $|a| > 1$: narrower than the standard parabola, $|a| < 1$: wider.

Uniformly accelerated: $s(t) = \tfrac{a}{2}\,t^2$, $v(t) = a\,t$ (starting from rest).

From vertex form to standard form: expand. Back: complete the square.

Symmetry: every parabola is symmetric about the vertical line through its vertex.

INTERACTIVE · PARABOLA WORKSHOP

Drag the vertex or use the sliders. The dashed curve is the standard parabola $y = x^2$.

EXAMPLE 1 · EXPANDING

Convert $f(x) = 2(x - 1)^2 - 8$ into standard form and find the zeros.

$2(x^2 - 2x + 1) - 8 = 2x^2 - 4x - 6$.

Zeros from the vertex form: $2(x - 1)^2 = 8 \Rightarrow (x - 1)^2 = 4 \Rightarrow x - 1 = \pm 2$: $x = 3$, $x = -1$.

EXAMPLE 2 · COMPLETING THE SQUARE

Find the vertex of $f(x) = x^2 - 6x + 5$.

Half the number in front of $x$ is $3$, its square $9$: $x^2 - 6x + 9 - 9 + 5 = (x - 3)^2 - 4$.

$S(3, -4)$. Zeros: $(x - 3)^2 = 4 \Rightarrow x = 1$ or $x = 5$.

a) $S(-2, -1)$, upwards   b) $S(4, 5)$, downwards, narrow   c) $S(0, 2)$, upwards, wide

A stone falls freely for 3 seconds. How far does it fall, how fast is it at the end?

$s = 4.905 \cdot 9 \approx 44.1$ m, $v = 9.81 \cdot 3 \approx 29.4\,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}$ (about 106 km/h).

Find the vertex by completing the square: a) $x^2 + 4x + 1$   b) $x^2 - 3x$   c) $2x^2 - 8x + 3$

In c) first factor the 2 out of the two $x$-terms: $2(x^2 - 4x) + 3$.

a) $(x + 2)^2 - 3$, $S(-2, -3)$   b) $(x - 1.5)^2 - 2.25$, $S(1.5, -2.25)$   c) $2(x - 2)^2 - 5$, $S(2, -5)$

Solve the well exploration: how deep is the well for a falling time of 2 s? How long does a stone take to fall into a well 45 m deep? (Ignore the time the sound takes.)

$s(2) \approx 19.6$ m. $4.905\,t^2 = 45 \Rightarrow t = \sqrt{45 : 4.905} \approx 3.03$ s.

A parabola has the vertex $S(2, -3)$ and passes through $P(4, 5)$. Find its equation.

$f(x) = a(x - 2)^2 - 3$, substitute $P$: $5 = 4a - 3 \Rightarrow a = 2$. $f(x) = 2(x - 2)^2 - 3$

2Solving quadratic equations

Where does the parabola $f(x) = x^2 - 4x - 5$ cross the $x$-axis? We are looking for the solutions of $x^2 - 4x - 5 = 0$.

Completing the square turns this into $(x - 2)^2 - 9 = 0$, so $x - 2 = \pm 3$ and $x = 5$ or $x = -1$. If you go this way once in general, you get a formula that always works.

KEY POINT · SOLUTION FORMULA AND VIETA'S FORMULAS

The equation $x^2 + px + q = 0$ has the solutions

$$x_{1,2} = -\frac{p}{2} \pm \sqrt{\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)^2 - q}$$

The discriminant $D = \left(\tfrac{p}{2}\right)^2 - q$ decides: $D > 0$ two solutions, $D = 0$ one solution, $D < 0$ no solution.

Vieta's formulas: $x_1 + x_2 = -p$ and $x_1 \cdot x_2 = q$.

If there is a number $a \neq 1$ in front of $x^2$, first divide the whole equation by $a$.

Quicker without the formula: $x^2 - 3x = 0$: factor out, $x(x - 3) = 0$. $x^2 - 16 = 0$: $x = \pm 4$.

Checking with Vieta: for $x^2 - 4x - 5 = 0$, $5 + (-1) = 4 = -p$ and $5 \cdot (-1) = -5 = q$.

EXAMPLE 3 · WITH THE FORMULA

Solve $2x^2 - 8x + 6 = 0$.

Divide by 2: $x^2 - 4x + 3 = 0$, so $p = -4$, $q = 3$.

$x_{1,2} = 2 \pm \sqrt{4 - 3} = 2 \pm 1$: $x_1 = 3$, $x_2 = 1$. Vieta: $3 + 1 = 4$, $3 \cdot 1 = 3$.

EXAMPLE 4 · BACKWARDS WITH VIETA

Give a quadratic equation with the solutions $2$ and $-7$.

$-p = 2 + (-7) = -5$, so $p = 5$. $q = 2 \cdot (-7) = -14$. $x^2 + 5x - 14 = 0$, which is the same as $(x - 2)(x + 7) = 0$.

EXERCISES

Solve: a) $x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0$   b) $x^2 + 2x - 15 = 0$   c) $x^2 - 6x + 9 = 0$   d) $x^2 + 4x + 5 = 0$

a) $2$ and $3$   b) $3$ and $-5$   c) only $3$ ($D = 0$)   d) no solution ($D = 4 - 5 < 0$)

Solve without the formula: a) $x^2 - 3x = 0$   b) $4x^2 = 36$   c) $(x + 1)(x - 6) = 0$

a) $x(x - 3) = 0$: $0$ and $3$   b) $x^2 = 9$: $\pm 3$   c) $-1$ and $6$

Find the zeros of $f(x) = -x^2 + 2x + 8$.

Multiply by $-1$: $x^2 - 2x - 8 = 0$.

$x = 1 \pm \sqrt{1 + 8} = 1 \pm 3$: $4$ and $-2$

For which number $q$ does $x^2 + 6x + q = 0$ have exactly one solution? For which $q$ none?

$D = 9 - q$. Exactly one solution for $q = 9$, none for $q > 9$.

The product of two consecutive natural numbers is 156. Which numbers are they?

$n(n + 1) = 156 \Rightarrow n^2 + n - 156 = 0 \Rightarrow n = -\tfrac12 \pm \sqrt{\tfrac14 + 156} = -0.5 \pm 12.5$. Natural: $12$ and $13$.

TEST YOURSELF!

What is the larger solution of $x^2 - 7x + 10 = 0$?

$x = 3.5 \pm \sqrt{12.25 - 10} = 3.5 \pm 1.5$: $5$ and $2$.

How many solutions does $x^2 + 10x + 25 = 0$ have?

$D = 25 - 25 = 0$: one solution, $x = -5$.

The solutions of $x^2 + px + q = 0$ are $3$ and $-4$. What is $q$?

Vieta: $q = 3 \cdot (-4) = -12$.

3Modelling with parabolas

A ball is thrown. Its path is approximately $h(x) = -0.05x^2 + 0.8x + 1.8$ ($x$ and $h$ in m). How high does it fly, and where does it land? The picture in the opener shows the path.

The highest point is the vertex. The landing point is given by the positive zero. The starting point is $h(0) = 1.8$ m, the release height.

KEY POINT · MODELLING WITH PARABOLAS

Largest or smallest value (extreme value) → vertex. When or where is the value zero? → zeros. When is a certain value reached? → solve the equation $f(x) = c$.

At the end always check whether the result makes sense in context: negative times or lengths are dropped.

Braking distance: German driving-school rule of thumb $s = \left(\tfrac{v}{10}\right)^2$ (v in km/h, s in m). The stopping distance including reaction time is $\tfrac{3v}{10} + \left(\tfrac{v}{10}\right)^2$.

INTERACTIVE · VERTICAL THROW

A ball is thrown vertically upwards: $h(t) = h_0 + v_0\,t - \tfrac{g}{2}\,t^2$. Change the release height and the speed.

EXAMPLE 5 · THE TRAJECTORY

Find the highest point and the landing point of $h(x) = -0.05x^2 + 0.8x + 1.8$.

Zeros: $\cdot(-20)$ gives $x^2 - 16x - 36 = 0$, so $x = 8 \pm \sqrt{64 + 36} = 8 \pm 10$. Landing point at $x = 18$ m.

The vertex lies halfway between the zeros $-2$ and $18$, i.e. at $x = 8$: $h(8) = -3.2 + 6.4 + 1.8 = $ 5 m.

EXAMPLE 6 · THE ENCLOSURE

Solve the exploration: 40 m of fence, one side is the wall. Which dimensions give the largest area?

Width $x$ (twice), length $40 - 2x$: $A(x) = x(40 - 2x) = -2x^2 + 40x = -2(x - 10)^2 + 200$.

Vertex $(10, 200)$: 10 m wide, 20 m long, 200 m².

EXAMPLE 7 · THE BRIDGE

A bridge arch spans 40 m and is 10 m high in the middle. How wide is the arch at a height of 6.4 m?

Coordinate system with the vertex on the $y$-axis: $f(x) = ax^2 + 10$, $f(20) = 0 \Rightarrow 400a = -10 \Rightarrow a = -0.025$.

$-0.025x^2 + 10 = 6.4 \Rightarrow x^2 = 144 \Rightarrow x = \pm 12$. At a height of 6.4 m the arch is 24 m wide.

EXERCISES

A rectangle has a perimeter of 30 cm. Which side lengths give the largest area?

$A(x) = x(15 - x) = -(x - 7.5)^2 + 56.25$: a square with side 7.5 cm, $A = 56.25\,\text{cm}^2$.

From what speed on is the stopping distance $\tfrac{3v}{10} + \left(\tfrac{v}{10}\right)^2$ longer than 100 m?

Put $u = \tfrac{v}{10}$ and solve $u^2 + 3u - 100 = 0$.

$u = -1.5 + \sqrt{2.25 + 100} \approx 8.61$, so from about 86 km/h.

A ball is thrown vertically upwards at $12\,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}$ from a height of 1.5 m. How high does it rise? When does it land? Check with the widget.

Vertex at $t = \tfrac{12}{9.81} \approx 1.22$ s, height $\approx 8.84$ m. Landing: $1.5 + 12t - 4.905t^2 = 0 \Rightarrow t \approx 2.57$ s.

A cinema sells 300 tickets at €10. Every price cut of €1 brings 50 more visitors. At which price is the revenue largest?

With a cut of $x$ euros: $E(x) = (10 - x)(300 + 50x) = -50x^2 + 200x + 3000 = -50(x - 2)^2 + 3200$. Best: €8 per ticket, 400 visitors, €3,200.

A rectangle is fenced all round with 60 m of fence. What is the largest area it can have?

REVIEW · DEEPEN · CONNECT

Match: $f(x) = (x - 2)^2$, $g(x) = -x^2 + 2$, $h(x) = 2x^2$. Which graph opens downwards, which is shifted to the right, which is narrow?

$g$ opens downwards, $f$ is shifted 2 to the right, $h$ is narrow.

Do $f(x) = x^2 - 2x$ and the line $g(x) = x + 4$ intersect? Calculate the points of intersection.

$x^2 - 3x - 4 = 0 \Rightarrow x = 4$ or $x = -1$. $S_1(4, 8)$, $S_2(-1, 3)$.

Connecting with chapter 3.1: describe the monotonicity, range and symmetry of $f(x) = -2(x + 1)^2 + 8$.

Increasing for $x \leq -1$, decreasing for $x \geq -1$. $W$: $y \leq 8$. Symmetric about the line $x = -1$, not about the $y$-axis.

Show by completing the square how the solution formula arises from $x^2 + px + q = 0$.

$x^2 + px + \left(\tfrac{p}{2}\right)^2 = \left(\tfrac{p}{2}\right)^2 - q \Rightarrow \left(x + \tfrac{p}{2}\right)^2 = \left(\tfrac{p}{2}\right)^2 - q \Rightarrow x + \tfrac{p}{2} = \pm\sqrt{\left(\tfrac{p}{2}\right)^2 - q}$.

EXCURSIONHISTORY OF PHYSICS

Galileo's inclined plane

Free fall is too fast to be measured accurately with the clocks of the 17th century. So Galileo Galilei let balls roll down a sloping channel and measured the time with water running out of a vessel. He found: in equal intervals of time the balls cover distances in the ratio $1 : 3 : 5 : 7 : \ldots$

If you add up the odd numbers, you get square numbers: $1$, $1 + 3 = 4$, $1 + 3 + 5 = 9$, $1 + 3 + 5 + 7 = 16$. So the total distance grows with the square of the time, exactly like $s(t) = \tfrac{a}{2}\,t^2$.

Source: Galileo Galilei, “Discorsi e dimostrazioni matematiche intorno a due nuove scienze” (1638), Third Day.
