Graphs and parameters

You should be able to draw six basic functions in your sleep. Almost everything else comes from them: by stretching, compressing, reflecting and shifting. Once you master these four moves, you read every function term like a set of building instructions. To round off the learning area you model with all types of function.

UNIT 1WITHOUT A CALCULATOR · 5 LESSONS

1The basic functions in your head

For each basic function a few key points and the typical shape are enough. Learn the profiles by heart.

$f(x) = x$

Straight line through $(0, 0)$ and $(1, 1)$ · increasing · symmetric about the origin

$f(x) = x^2$

$(0, 0)$, $(\pm1, 1)$, $(\pm2, 4)$ · $W$: $y \geq 0$ · symmetric about the $y$-axis

$f(x) = \sqrt{x}$

$(0, 0)$, $(1, 1)$, $(4, 2)$ · only $x \geq 0$ · increasing, flatter and flatter

$f(x) = \tfrac1x$

$(1, 1)$, $(-1, -1)$ · asymptotes $x = 0$ and $y = 0$ · symmetric about the origin

$f(x) = a^x$

$(0, 1)$, $(1, a)$ · $W$: $y > 0$ · asymptote $y = 0$ · $a > 1$ increasing, $a < 1$ decreasing

$f(x) = \sin x$

Zeros $0, \pi, 2\pi$ · max $\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2}, 1\right)$ · period $2\pi$ · $W = [-1, 1]$

EXERCISES

Sketch all six basic functions from memory, each in its own coordinate system. Then compare with the profiles.

Check above all the key points and the asymptotes. Typical mistakes: continuing $\sqrt x$ to the left, letting $\tfrac1x$ cross the axes, drawing $2^x$ below the $x$-axis.

Which basic function is meant? a) passes through $(0, 1)$, increases, is never negative   b) is only defined for $x \geq 0$   c) has infinitely many zeros   d) has two asymptotes

a) $2^x$ (in general $a^x$ with $a > 1$)   b) $\sqrt{x}$   c) $\sin x$   d) $\tfrac1x$

At which points do $f(x) = x^2$ and $g(x) = \sqrt{x}$ intersect? Give reasons without a calculator.

At $(0, 0)$ and $(1, 1)$. For $0 < x < 1$, $\sqrt{x} > x^2$, for $x > 1$, $x^2$ is larger.

How many points of intersection do $f(x) = 2^x$ and $g(x) = x^2$ have? Try values and sketch.

Three: at $x = 2$ and $x = 4$ (both values 4 and 16) and another at $x \approx -0.77$ (where both are about 0.59).

TEST YOURSELF!

Which graph has a horizontal asymptote?

For $x \to -\infty$, $2^x$ approaches the $x$-axis.

What is the value of $\sqrt{x}$ at $x = 4$?

$2$

How many zeros does $\tfrac1x$ have?

None: $\tfrac1x$ is never zero.

UNIT 25 LESSONS

2Stretching, compressing, reflecting, shifting

$g(x) = (x + 3)^2$ has the value $0$ at $x = -3$, i.e. where $f(x) = x^2$ has the value $0$ at $x = 0$. Everything $f$ does at $x$, $g$ does 3 units earlier. The graph lies 3 units to the left.

KEY POINT · FOUR PARAMETERS
TermEffect on the graph of $f$
$c \cdot f(x)$stretch in the $y$-direction by factor $c$ (for $|c| < 1$ a compression, for $c < 0$ also a reflection in the $x$-axis)
$f(x) + c$shift by $c$ upwards (for $c < 0$ downwards)
$f(c \cdot x)$compression in the $x$-direction by factor $\tfrac1c$ (for $c < 0$ also a reflection in the $y$-axis)
$f(x + c)$shift by $c$ to the left (for $c < 0$ to the right)

Rule of thumb: what stands outside acts in the $y$-direction and the way you expect. What stands inside with the $x$ acts in the $x$-direction and “the other way round”.

Period: $\sin(c \cdot x)$ has the period $\tfrac{2\pi}{c}$, the compression makes it shorter.

Asymptotes move too: $\tfrac{1}{x - 2} + 1$ has the asymptotes $x = 2$ and $y = 1$.

Choose a basic function and change the four parameters. Dashed: the basic function. The lab describes every change in words.

EXAMPLE 1 · READING THE BUILDING INSTRUCTIONS

Describe how the graph of $g(x) = 2\sqrt{x - 3} + 1$ arises from $\sqrt{x}$. State $D$, $W$ and the starting point.

3 to the right, stretched in the $y$-direction by factor 2, 1 upwards.

$D$: $x \geq 3$ · $W$: $y \geq 1$ · starting point $(3, 1)$ · another point: $g(4) = 3$.

EXAMPLE 2 · TERM FROM A DESCRIPTION

The standard parabola is reflected in the $x$-axis, shifted 2 to the left and 4 upwards. Give the term and the zeros.

$g(x) = -(x + 2)^2 + 4$. Zeros: $(x + 2)^2 = 4 \Rightarrow x + 2 = \pm 2$, so $x = 0$ and $x = -4$.

EXERCISES

Describe how the graph arises from the basic function: a) $x^2 - 5$   b) $3 \cdot 2^x$   c) $\sin(x - \pi)$   d) $\tfrac{1}{x + 1}$   e) $-\sqrt{x}$

a) 5 down   b) factor 3 in the $y$-direction   c) $\pi$ to the right   d) 1 to the left   e) reflected in the $x$-axis

State the period: a) $\sin(3x)$   b) $\sin\left(\tfrac{x}{2}\right)$   c) $5\sin x + 2$

a) $\tfrac{2\pi}{3}$   b) $4\pi$   c) $2\pi$ (a factor outside and a shift do not change the period)

Find the asymptotes, domain and zero of $h(x) = \tfrac{1}{x + 2} - 1$.

Asymptotes $x = -2$ and $y = -1$, $D = \mathbb{R} \setminus \{-2\}$. Zero: $\tfrac{1}{x + 2} = 1 \Rightarrow x = -1$.

The graph of $f(x) = 2^x$ is shifted 3 down. Where is the asymptote, where the zero?

$g(x) = 2^x - 3$, asymptote $y = -3$, zero $2^x = 3 \Rightarrow x = \log_2 3 \approx 1.585$.

Show: $2^{x + 3} = 8 \cdot 2^x$. What does that mean for the graph? Is something similar true for $x^2$?

Power law $2^{x + 3} = 2^x \cdot 2^3$. For $2^x$, shifting 3 to the left is the same as stretching by factor 8. Not for $x^2$: $(x + 3)^2 = x^2 + 6x + 9$ is not a multiple of $x^2$.

TEST YOURSELF!

Compared with $f$, the graph of $f(x + 4)$ lies …

Plus inside means to the left.

Which horizontal asymptote does $2^x - 3$ have? Give the $y$-value.

$y = -3$

What is the period of $\sin(4x)$ (decimal)?

$\tfrac{2\pi}{4} = \tfrac{\pi}{2} \approx 1.5708$

UNIT 3ROUNDING OFF LEARNING AREA 3 · 5 LESSONS

3Modelling with all types of function

KEY POINT · THE MODELLING CYCLE
  1. Understand: what is given, what is wanted? Which quantities depend on which?
  2. Mathematise: choose a suitable type of function (equal increase, equal factor, vertex, period) and find the parameters.
  3. Calculate: with or without digital tools.
  4. Interpret: answer in words and with a unit.
  5. Validate: is this plausible? Where does the model hold, where not?

Identifying features: “the same every hour” → linear · “by 5% per year” → exponential · “highest point”, “throw” → quadratic · “repeats itself” → sine

Phone tariffs. Tariff A: €8 monthly fee plus €0.09 per minute, tariff B: €15 flat rate. From how many minutes on is B worth it?

$8 + 0.09x = 15 \Rightarrow x \approx 77.8$. From 78 minutes per month B is cheaper.

Temperature. On a spring day $T(t) = 18 + 6 \cdot \sin\left(\tfrac{\pi}{12}(t - 9)\right)$ ($t$ in h after midnight, $T$ in °C). When is it warmest, when coldest? For how long is it warmer than 21 °C?

Period 24 h. Maximum 24 °C when $\tfrac{\pi}{12}(t - 9) = \tfrac{\pi}{2}$, i.e. at 15:00. Minimum 12 °C at 03:00.

$\sin(\ldots) > 0.5$ for $\tfrac{\pi}{6} < \tfrac{\pi}{12}(t - 9) < \tfrac{5\pi}{6}$, i.e. from 11:00 to 19:00: 8 hours.

Water jet. A fountain sprays from a height of 0.5 m. The jet reaches its greatest height of 2.5 m at a distance of 2 m from the pipe. Set up a function. Where does it hit the ground?

Vertex $(2, 2.5)$: $h(x) = a(x - 2)^2 + 2.5$ with $h(0) = 0.5 \Rightarrow 4a = -2 \Rightarrow a = -0.5$.

$-0.5(x - 2)^2 + 2.5 = 0 \Rightarrow (x - 2)^2 = 5 \Rightarrow x = 2 + \sqrt5 \approx 4.24$ m.

Which model? Match and give reasons: a) a cup of tea cooling down   b) costs with a standing charge and a price per kWh   c) height of a swing against time   d) area of a square against its side length

a) exponential (difference from room temperature)   b) linear   c) periodic, sine   d) quadratic

Medicine. A patient takes 100 mg of a drug every 8 hours, which is broken down at 10% per hour. How much is in the body just before and just after the second dose? What happens in the long run?

Before the 2nd dose: $100 \cdot 0.9^8 \approx 43.0$ mg, after it $143.0$ mg. After each dose a little more, but the increases get smaller. In the long run the amount just after a dose levels off at $\tfrac{100}{1 - 0.9^8} \approx 176$ mg.

TEST YOURSELF!

At what time (full hour, 24-hour clock) is it warmest according to $T(t) = 18 + 6\sin\left(\tfrac{\pi}{12}(t - 9)\right)$?

At 15:00: $\tfrac{\pi}{12} \cdot 6 = \tfrac{\pi}{2}$.

How far does the jet $h(x) = -0.5(x - 2)^2 + 2.5$ reach?

$2 + \sqrt5 \approx 4.24$ m

A roller coaster made of functions

Design the profile of a roller coaster 100 m long from pieces of functions:

  • a straight climb (linear) to a height of 30 m,
  • a hill as a parabola,
  • a wavy section as a sine curve,
  • a run-out that gets closer and closer to a height of 1 m (exponential).

The pieces must fit together without a jump. Give the term and the $x$-range for each piece, draw the whole thing in GeoGebra (piece by piece: If(0 ≤ x ≤ 20, 1.5x, …)) and present your profile. Evaluate: where would the ride be fastest? Where does it get uncomfortable?

OUTLOOK · YEAR 12

Whether the pieces fit together not only without a jump but also without a kink is checked with the derivative. It measures the gradient at every point and is the big topic of differential calculus in Year 12.
