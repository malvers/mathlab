Numerical methods and simulations

Many equations cannot be solved exactly, many areas cannot be calculated exactly. So computers calculate numerically: they approach the solution in small steps, as accurately as needed. Here you meet three methods that are inside every calculator, every simulation and every engineering office, and you write small programs for them yourself.

UNIT 14 LESSONS

1Zeros by halving

For $f(x) = x^3 - x - 2$ there is no simple solution formula. But: $f(1) = -2 < 0$ and $f(2) = 4 > 0$. The graph is an unbroken line, so it must cross the $x$-axis between 1 and 2.

You check the midpoint: $f(1.5) = 3.375 - 1.5 - 2 = -0.125 < 0$. So the change of sign lies between 1.5 and 2. Halve again, check again, and so on. With every step the interval becomes half as wide.

KEY POINT · BISECTION METHOD

If $f$ is continuous (without a jump) and $f(a)$ and $f(b)$ have different signs, there is at least one zero in the interval $[a, b]$.

  1. Calculate the midpoint $m = \tfrac{a + b}{2}$.
  2. If $f(m)$ has the same sign as $f(a)$, set $a := m$, otherwise $b := m$.
  3. Repeat until $b - a$ is small enough.

After $n$ steps the interval is only $\tfrac{b - a}{2^n}$ wide. So ten steps make it over a thousand times smaller.

Guess the number: by halving you find any number from 1 to 1,000 in at most 10 guesses, because $2^{10} = 1024$.

Outlook: Newton's method is much faster, but needs the derivative, a topic of Year 12.

INTERACTIVE · BISECTION

Choose a function and halve the interval step by step. The blue lines bound the interval, the white point is the midpoint.

METHOD · THE PROGRAM

In JavaScript (runs in the console of any browser, F12):

const f = x => x ** 3 - x - 2;
let a = 1, b = 2;
while (b - a > 1e-6) {
  const m = (a + b) / 2;
  if (Math.sign(f(m)) === Math.sign(f(a))) a = m; else b = m;
}
console.log((a + b) / 2);   // 1.5213797…

In a spreadsheet: columns for $a$, $b$, $m$ and $f(m)$. In the next row use =IF(…) to decide which bound is replaced, then drag down.

Carry out three steps of bisection by hand for $f(x) = x^2 - 2$ on $[1, 2]$. Which number is being approximated?

$m = 1.5$: $f > 0$ → $[1, 1.5]$ · $m = 1.25$: $f < 0$ → $[1.25, 1.5]$ · $m = 1.375$: $f < 0$ → $[1.375, 1.5]$. It approximates $\sqrt2 \approx 1.414$.

How many steps are needed to pin down a zero in $[0, 1]$ to within $0.001$?

$\tfrac{1}{2^n} \leq 0.001 \Rightarrow 2^n \geq 1000 \Rightarrow n = 10$.

The function $2^x - 3x$ has two zeros. Find intervals for both and determine the second one numerically.

Why does bisection fail for $f(x) = \tfrac1x$ on $[-1, 1]$, although $f(-1) < 0 < f(1)$? And why for $f(x) = x^2$ on $[-1, 1]$?

$\tfrac1x$ jumps at $0$ and has no zero at all, the condition “continuous” is missing. $x^2$ does have a zero, but no change of sign: $f(-1) = f(1) = 1 > 0$.

UNIT 23 LESSONS

2Approximating areas with strips

How big is the area under $y = x^2$ between $x = 0$ and $x = 2$? There is no area formula for a curve like this. So you cut the area into narrow strips and replace every strip by a rectangle or a trapezium.

KEY POINT · STRIP METHODS

Divide $[a, b]$ into $n$ strips of equal width $h = \tfrac{b - a}{n}$.

Rectangles: height = function value at the left edge (or in the middle) of each strip. Add up all the rectangle areas.

Trapeziums: every strip becomes a trapezium with the function values at both edges: $A \approx \tfrac{h}{2}\big(f(x_0) + 2f(x_1) + \ldots + 2f(x_{n-1}) + f(x_n)\big)$.

The more strips, the more accurate. Midpoint rectangles and trapeziums are much more accurate than rectangles at the edge.

Outlook: the exact value is called the integral, $\int_0^2 x^2\,dx = \tfrac83$. Integral calculus is a big topic of Year 13.

EXAMPLE 1 · FOUR STRIPS

Calculate the area under $y = x^2$ on $[0, 2]$ with $n = 4$ strips: with left rectangles, midpoint rectangles and trapeziums.

$h = 0.5$. Left: $0.5 \cdot (0 + 0.25 + 1 + 2.25) = 1.75$. Midpoint: $0.5 \cdot (0.0625 + 0.5625 + 1.5625 + 3.0625) = 2.625$. Trapeziums: $0.25 \cdot (0 + 2 \cdot 0.25 + 2 \cdot 1 + 2 \cdot 2.25 + 4) = 2.75$.

Exact: $\tfrac83 \approx 2.667$. Errors: $-0.917$ · $-0.042$ · $+0.083$.

UNIT 33 LESSONS

3Areas by chance: the Monte Carlo method

Throw random points into a rectangle that contains the area you want. The proportion of hits inside the area roughly equals the proportion of the rectangle that the area takes up. That sounds like gambling and works surprisingly well.

KEY POINT · MONTE CARLO METHOD

$$A \approx \frac{\text{number of hits}}{\text{number of all points}} \cdot A_{\text{rectangle}}$$

The result is an estimate, as with every simulation (see chapter 1). The accuracy only grows slowly: for one more digit you need about a hundred times as many points.

The method is strong for complicated shapes and in many dimensions, where strip methods hardly work any more.

Name: the method was developed in the 1940s at Los Alamos and was named after the casino of Monte Carlo.

INTERACTIVE · AREA UNDER THE PARABOLA

Random points in the rectangle $2 \times 4$: yellow below $y = x^2$, grey above.

METHOD · ESTIMATING $\pi$ IN EIGHT LINES
const n = 1000000;
let hits = 0;
for (let i = 0; i < n; i++) {
  const x = Math.random(), y = Math.random();
  if (x * x + y * y < 1) hits++;          // inside the quarter circle
}
console.log(4 * hits / n);                 // about 3.14…

The quarter circle has the area $\tfrac{\pi}{4}$, the unit square the area 1. So the proportion of hits estimates $\tfrac{\pi}{4}$.

EXERCISES

Of 1,000 random points in the rectangle $2 \times 4$, 338 lie below $y = x^2$. Which area is estimated? How big is the error?

$\tfrac{338}{1000} \cdot 8 = 2.704$. Exact $\tfrac83 \approx 2.667$, error about $0.04$.

Run the $\pi$ program with $n = 100$, $10{,}000$ and $1{,}000{,}000$. Roughly how many digits are right each time?

With 100 points often only the 3, with 10,000 usually $3.1$, with a million usually $3.14$. A hundred times as many points give about one digit.

Compare Monte Carlo and the trapezium rule for the area under $x^2$: which is more accurate with 100 steps of calculation? When would you still choose Monte Carlo?

Trapeziums with $n = 100$: error about $0.0001$. Monte Carlo with 100 points: error typically $0.2$ to $0.4$. Monte Carlo is worth it for shapes that are hard to cut into strips, or for volumes in many dimensions.

EXCURSIONTHE INFINITELY LARGE

Hilbert's hotel

A hotel has infinitely many rooms, numbered 1, 2, 3, … All of them are occupied. A new guest arrives. The porter asks every guest to move from room $n$ to room $n + 1$. Room 1 becomes free, although everything was full before.

Then a coach with infinitely many guests arrives. Now every guest moves from room $n$ to room $2n$. All the odd-numbered rooms become free, infinitely many.

So you cannot count infinite sets like finite ones: there are just as many even numbers as natural numbers, because $n \mapsto 2n$ pairs them off one to one. But Georg Cantor also showed: there are more real numbers than natural numbers. Infinity is not the same as infinity.

Source: David Hilbert presented the thought experiment in a lecture in 1924. It became famous through George Gamow's book “One Two Three … Infinity” (1947).

THINK ALONG

The full hotel gets 3 new guests. Into which room does the guest from room 4 move?

Everyone moves up by 3: from $n$ to $n + 3$, so room 7.
