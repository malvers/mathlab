UNIT 18 LESSONS

1Function profile problems: functions from conditions

We want a parabola through the points $(0, 1)$, $(1, 0)$ and $(3, 4)$. Approach: $f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$ with three unknown numbers.

Every point gives an equation: $f(0) = 1 \Rightarrow c = 1$; $f(1) = 0 \Rightarrow a + b + c = 0$; $f(3) = 4 \Rightarrow 9a + 3b + c = 4$. The system of equations (Gaussian elimination from Year 11) gives $a = 1$, $b = -2$, $c = 1$, so $f(x) = x^2 - 2x + 1 = (x - 1)^2$.

KEY POINT · A PROFILE IN EQUATIONS

A polynomial function of degree $n$ has $n + 1$ coefficients. For these you need $n + 1$ conditions.

PropertyEquation
point $P(x_0, y_0)$ lies on the graph$f(x_0) = y_0$
turning point (horizontal tangent) at $x_0$$f'(x_0) = 0$
point of inflection at $x_0$$f''(x_0) = 0$
gradient $m$ (tangent) at $x_0$$f'(x_0) = m$
symmetric about the $y$-axis / about the originonly even / odd exponents in the approach

How to do it: approach with $f$, $f'$, $f''$ · translate the conditions · solve the system of equations · check that all conditions are satisfied.

A maximum point is two conditions: maximum $(2, 4)$ means $f(2) = 4$ and $f'(2) = 0$.

Digital tools: from four unknowns on, the calculator is worth it (system of equations or matrix function).

INTERACTIVE · FUNCTION FROM CONDITIONS

Choose a profile and drag the points. The system of equations and the solution adapt at once.

EXAMPLE 1 · MAXIMUM AND MINIMUM

A cubic function passes through the origin, has the maximum point $(1, 2)$ and a turning point at $x = 3$. Find $f$.

$f(0) = 0 \Rightarrow d = 0$ · $f(1) = 2$: $a + b + c = 2$ · $f'(1) = 0$: $3a + 2b + c = 0$ · $f'(3) = 0$: $27a + 6b + c = 0$.

Solution: $a = 0.5$, $b = -3$, $c = 4.5$. $f(x) = 0.5x^3 - 3x^2 + 4.5x$. Check: $f'(x) = 1.5(x - 1)(x - 3)$, $f''(1) = -3 < 0$. ✓

EXAMPLE 2 · WITH SYMMETRY

A quartic function that is symmetric about the $y$-axis cuts the $y$-axis at 2 and has a minimum point at $(1, 0)$.

Approach $f(x) = ax^4 + bx^2 + c$. $c = 2$, $f(1) = a + b + 2 = 0$, $f'(1) = 4a + 2b = 0$. So $b = -2a$, $a = 2$, $b = -4$. $f(x) = 2x^4 - 4x^2 + 2 = 2(x^2 - 1)^2$

EXERCISES

Translate into equations: the graph of a cubic function has the point of inflection $(1, 3)$ and cuts the $y$-axis at $-1$.

$f(1) = 3$, $f''(1) = 0$, $f(0) = -1$. Those are three conditions, for degree 3 one is still missing.

Find the parabola through $(0, 3)$, $(1, 2)$ and $(2, 3)$.

$c = 3$, $a + b = -1$, $4a + 2b = 0$: $a = 1$, $b = -2$. $f(x) = x^2 - 2x + 3$

Solve the exploration “Closing the gap”: find a cubic function with $f(0) = 0$, $f'(0) = 0$, $f(4) = 2$, $f'(4) = 0$.

$d = 0$, $c = 0$; $64a + 16b = 2$, $48a + 8b = 0$ ⇒ $b = -6a$, $64a - 96a = 2$, $a = -\tfrac{1}{16}$, $b = \tfrac38$. $f(x) = -\tfrac{1}{16}x^3 + \tfrac38 x^2$, the point of inflection lies exactly in the middle at $(2, 1)$.

A cubic function symmetric about the origin has a minimum point at $(1, -2)$. Find it.

Approach $f(x) = ax^3 + cx$. $a + c = -2$, $3a + c = 0$: $a = 1$, $c = -3$. $f(x) = x^3 - 3x$.

A bridge is to cross a 40 m wide river as a parabolic arch, 8 m high in the middle. Find an equation if the origin is in the middle at the water. How steeply does the arch rise at the bank?

Symmetric: $f(x) = ax^2 + 8$, $f(20) = 0 \Rightarrow a = -0.02$. $f'(x) = -0.04x$, $f'(-20) = 0.8$: gradient 80%, angle $\arctan 0.8 \approx 38.7°$.

2Optimisation problems

From a sheet of card measuring 30 cm × 20 cm, squares with side $x$ are cut out at the corners. For which $x$ does the box hold the most?

The box is $30 - 2x$ long, $20 - 2x$ wide and $x$ high. Its volume is the objective function $V(x) = x(30 - 2x)(20 - 2x) = 4x^3 - 100x^2 + 600x$ with the domain $0 < x < 10$. $V'(x) = 12x^2 - 200x + 600 = 0$ gives $x \approx 3.92$ cm (the second solution 12.75 lies outside). The volume is then about 1,056 cm³.

KEY POINT · SOLVING OPTIMISATION PROBLEMS

1. Objective function: which quantity is to be maximal or minimal? Set up a formula for it, often with two variables.

2. Constraint: what is fixed (perimeter, volume, material)? Use it to replace one variable so that the objective function depends on one only.

3. Domain: fix it from the context.

4. Extremum: find it with $f' = 0$ and the sufficient condition. Compare the values at the end points.

5. Answer in context with a unit, and check the model critically.

Extremum at an end point: if the value with $f' = 0$ lies outside the domain, the optimum can be at an end point.

Without a derivative: if an objective function is too complicated, computers look for the optimum by clever trial and error, for example with particle swarms.

INTERACTIVE · OPTIMISING

Choose a problem and look for the optimum with the slider. Then compare with the solution using the derivative.

EXAMPLE 3 · RECTANGLE UNDER A PARABOLA

Under the parabola $f(x) = 9 - x^2$ there is a rectangle with one side on the $x$-axis and two corners on the parabola. When is its area largest?

Corners at $\pm u$: width $2u$, height $9 - u^2$. $A(u) = 2u(9 - u^2) = 18u - 2u^3$, $0 < u < 3$.

$A'(u) = 18 - 6u^2 = 0 \Rightarrow u = \sqrt3 \approx 1.73$, $A''(u) = -12u < 0$. $A_{\max} = 12\sqrt3 \approx 20.8$ square units.

EXAMPLE 4 · THE CAN

A can is to hold 1 litre. Which dimensions need the least tin?

Constraint $\pi r^2 h = 1000 \Rightarrow h = \tfrac{1000}{\pi r^2}$. Objective function $S(r) = 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi r h = 2\pi r^2 + \tfrac{2000}{r}$.

$S'(r) = 4\pi r - \tfrac{2000}{r^2} = 0 \Rightarrow r^3 = \tfrac{500}{\pi}$, $r \approx 5.42$ cm, $h \approx 10.84$ cm. The most economical can is as tall as it is wide.

EXERCISES

Two numbers add up to 20. When is their product largest?

$P(x) = x(20 - x)$, $P'(x) = 20 - 2x = 0$: $x = 10$, both numbers 10, product 100.

With 60 m of fencing a rectangle is marked out against a wall (the wall as one side). Find the largest area.

$A(x) = x(60 - 2x)$, $A'(x) = 60 - 4x = 0$: $x = 15$ m, side along the wall 30 m, $A = 450$ m².

As in exercise 7, but the wall is only 20 m long. What changes?

Now $60 - 2x \le 20$, so $x \ge 20$. On $[20, 30)$, $A$ decreases, the optimum lies at the end point $x = 20$: $A = 20 \cdot 20 = 400$ m².

The profit of a business is $G(x) = -x^3 + 6x^2 + 15x - 20$ (in €1,000, $x$ in hundreds of units, $0 \le x \le 8$). At which quantity is it largest?

$G'(x) = -3x^2 + 12x + 15 = 0 \Rightarrow x^2 - 4x - 5 = 0 \Rightarrow x = 5$. $G''(5) = -18 < 0$. $G(5) = 80$, so €80,000 at 500 units. End points: $G(0) = -20$, $G(8) = -76$.

A window consists of a rectangle with a semicircle on top. The perimeter is 6 m. For which radius does it let through the most light?

Width $2r$, height of the rectangle $h$. Perimeter $2r + 2h + \pi r = 6$. Area $A = 2rh + \tfrac{\pi}{2} r^2$.

$h = 3 - r - \tfrac{\pi}{2} r$, $A(r) = 6r - 2r^2 - \tfrac{\pi}{2} r^2$, $A'(r) = 6 - (4 + \pi) r = 0$: $r = \tfrac{6}{4 + \pi} \approx 0.84$ m. Then $h \approx 0.84$ m too: the height of the rectangle equals the radius.

REVIEW · DEEPEN · CONNECT

Find the quadratic function with the vertex $(2, -1)$ that passes through $(0, 3)$.

$f(x) = a(x - 2)^2 - 1$, $f(0) = 4a - 1 = 3 \Rightarrow a = 1$. $f(x) = (x - 2)^2 - 1 = x^2 - 4x + 3$.

Solve the exploration “The box” for an A4 sheet (29.7 cm × 21 cm). Which $x$ is optimal?

$V(x) = x(29.7 - 2x)(21 - 2x)$, $V'(x) = 12x^2 - 202.8x + 623.7 = 0$. Solution in $0 < x < 10.5$: $x \approx 4.04$ cm, $V \approx 1128$ cm³.

Connecting with chapter 2.4: explain why the box really has a maximum, without the second derivative.

At the end points $x = 0$ and $x = 10$, $V = 0$, in between $V > 0$. The only point inside with $V' = 0$ must be the maximum.

Criticising the model of the can: real tin cans are often taller than the calculation $h = 2r$ suggests. Give reasons why the model only partly matches reality.

For example: the lid and the base are thicker than the side or have a seam that costs material. Cans have to fit on shelves, in your hand and on machines. The price of the tin is only part of the costs. The label and stacking also play a role.

EXCURSIONTHE OLDEST OPTIMISATION PROBLEM

Dido's problem

According to legend, the Phoenician princess Dido fled to the coast of North Africa. There she was allowed to buy as much land as could be encircled by an ox hide. She cut the hide into a long, thin strip and laid it out so that it enclosed as much land as possible. That is how Carthage came into being.

Which shape encloses the largest area for a given length? For a rectangle, the calculation in this chapter shows: the square. Over all shapes it is the circle, and along a straight coast a semicircle. This “isoperimetric problem” was only fully proved in the 19th century.

Source: Virgil: Aeneid, book I, lines 365–368.
