The definite integral

How much water is in the tank if you only know how fast it flows in and out? How far has a car driven if you only know the speedometer? The answer is an area under a graph, the definite integral. And the fundamental theorem tells you how to calculate this area with an antiderivative in a single line. It joins differentiating and integrating into one single theory.

UNIT 14 LESSONS

1The integral as a reconstructed total

In the speedometer log the car drives $20 \cdot 10 + 30 \cdot 5 = 350$ m. That is exactly the area under the speed graph: two rectangles.

If the rate of change is not piecewise constant, you split the time into many small sections in which it is almost constant and add up rate times time. In the limit of ever finer subdivisions you get the area under the graph, the definite integral.

KEY POINT · RECONSTRUCTED TOTAL

If $r(t)$ is the rate of change of a total $B(t)$ and $B(a)$ is the initial amount, then $$B(b) = B(a) + \int_a^b r(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.$$

The integral measures the total change in the period from $a$ to $b$. Areas above the $t$-axis count positive (increase), below the axis negative (decrease).

Units: rate in l/min times time in min gives litres. The unit of the integral is always “unit of the rate times unit of $t$”.

Examples: speed → distance, rate of inflow → volume, power → energy, marginal cost → cost.

INTERACTIVE · FROM THE RATE TO THE TOTAL

Move the time $t$. At the top the area under the rate grows (gold positive, red negative), at the bottom the total.

EXAMPLE 1 · THE WATER TANK

How much water is in the tank after 6 and after 9 minutes? (An antiderivative of $r$ is $R(t) = -\tfrac16 t^3 + \tfrac32 t^2$.)

$B(6) = 20 + R(6) - R(0) = 20 + (-36 + 54) = 38$ l. $B(9) = 20 + (-121.5 + 121.5) = 20$ l. After 9 minutes as much has flowed out as had flowed in before.

EXAMPLE 2 · THE CAR JOURNEY

A car drives with $v(t) = 1.2t^2 - 0.1t^3$ (m/s) from $t = 0$ to $t = 12$. How far does it get?

Antiderivative $s(t) = 0.4t^3 - 0.025t^4$. Distance $s(12) - s(0) = 691.2 - 518.4 = 172.8$ m.

EXERCISES

A pool is filled at a constant 40 l/min for 30 minutes, then emptied at 20 l/min for 10 minutes. At the start it holds 500 l. How much is it at the end?

$500 + 40 \cdot 30 - 20 \cdot 10 = 1500$ l

A company produces at the rate $p(t) = 200 + 20t$ units per day. How many units are made in the first 10 days?

Antiderivative $200t + 10t^2$, so $2000 + 1000 = 3000$ units. (Geometrically: a trapezium with the heights 200 and 400.)

For the water tank: when is there most water in it, and how much? Explain using the rate.

The total grows as long as $r(t) > 0$, that is up to $t = 6$. There is the maximum with 38 l (example 1).

Show for the water tank: for which $t > 0$ is there exactly as much water in the tank again as at the start?

$R(t) = -\tfrac16 t^3 + \tfrac32 t^2 = 0 \Rightarrow t^2(9 - t) = 0 \Rightarrow t = 9$ min.

UNIT 24 LESSONS

2Properties of the integral and the fundamental theorem

The area under $f(x) = 2x$ from 0 to $b$ is a triangle with the area $\tfrac12 \cdot b \cdot 2b = b^2$. The function $b \mapsto b^2$ is an antiderivative of $2x$!

That is no coincidence. The “area function” grows at every point exactly as fast as the graph is high, so its derivative is $f$ itself. It follows: every antiderivative gives the area via the difference of its values at the limits.

KEY POINT · FUNDAMENTAL THEOREM OF CALCULUS

If $F$ is an antiderivative of the continuous function $f$, then $$\int_a^b f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x = \Big[F(x)\Big]_a^b = F(b) - F(a).$$

Properties: $\int_a^b f\,\mathrm{d}x + \int_b^c f\,\mathrm{d}x = \int_a^c f\,\mathrm{d}x$ (additivity over intervals) · $\int_a^b (c \cdot f + g)\,\mathrm{d}x = c\int_a^b f\,\mathrm{d}x + \int_a^b g\,\mathrm{d}x$ (linearity) · $\int_a^a f\,\mathrm{d}x = 0$ · $\int_b^a f\,\mathrm{d}x = -\int_a^b f\,\mathrm{d}x$

The constant $C$ drops out in $F(b) - F(a)$. So you can always take the simplest antiderivative.

Definition: strictly speaking the integral is the limit of sums of rectangles, see chapter 3.4. The fundamental theorem shows that you usually do not have to work it out.

INTERACTIVE · THE DEFINITE INTEGRAL

Drag the limits $a$ and $b$ along the $x$-axis. The integral is the sum of the golden areas minus the red ones.

EXAMPLE 3 · WITH THE FUNDAMENTAL THEOREM

Calculate $\int_0^2 x^2\,\mathrm{d}x$ and $\int_1^3 (2x + 1)\,\mathrm{d}x$.

$\Big[\tfrac13 x^3\Big]_0^2 = \tfrac83 \approx 2.667$ · $\Big[x^2 + x\Big]_1^3 = 12 - 2 = 10$

EXAMPLE 4 · SINE AND EXPONENTIAL FUNCTION

Calculate $\int_0^{\pi} \sin x\,\mathrm{d}x$ and $\int_0^1 \mathrm{e}^x\,\mathrm{d}x$.

$\Big[-\cos x\Big]_0^{\pi} = 1 - (-1) = 2$ · $\Big[\mathrm{e}^x\Big]_0^1 = \mathrm{e} - 1 \approx 1.718$

Calculate $\int_1^4 \sqrt{x}\,\mathrm{d}x$ and $\int_1^4 \dfrac{1}{x^2}\,\mathrm{d}x$.

$\Big[\tfrac23 x^{\frac32}\Big]_1^4 = \tfrac{16}{3} - \tfrac23 = \tfrac{14}{3} \approx 4.667$ · $\Big[-\tfrac1x\Big]_1^4 = -\tfrac14 + 1 = \tfrac34$

We have $\int_0^2 f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x = 5$ and $\int_2^6 f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x = -3$. Find $\int_0^6 f\,\mathrm{d}x$, $\int_6^2 f\,\mathrm{d}x$ and $\int_0^2 3f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x$.

For which $b > 0$ is $\int_0^b x^2\,\mathrm{d}x = 9$?

$\tfrac13 b^3 = 9 \Rightarrow b^3 = 27 \Rightarrow b = 3$

Work out $\int_{-2}^{2} (x^3 - 4x)\,\mathrm{d}x$ without calculating and explain with the symmetry. Is this also the area?

The graph is symmetric about the origin, the areas on the left and right cancel out: integral 0. The area, however, is $2 \cdot 4 = 8$ (chapter 3.3).

Calculate $\int_{-1}^{1} (x^2 + 1)\,\mathrm{d}x$ and $\int_0^1 (\mathrm{e}^x - x)\,\mathrm{d}x$.

$\Big[\tfrac13 x^3 + x\Big]_{-1}^{1} = \tfrac43 + \tfrac43 = \tfrac83$ · $\Big[\mathrm{e}^x - \tfrac12 x^2\Big]_0^1 = \mathrm{e} - 0.5 - 1 \approx 1.218$

A spring with the spring constant $D = 200$ N/m is stretched by 10 cm. The force is $F(s) = D \cdot s$. Calculate the work $W = \int_0^{0.1} F(s)\,\mathrm{d}s$.

Connecting with Year 12: the marginal cost of a business is $K'(x) = 0.06x^2 - 2.4x + 30$ (€ per unit), the fixed costs are €200. Find the cost function and the costs for 40 units.

$K(x) = 0.02x^3 - 1.2x^2 + 30x + 200$, $K(40) = 1280 - 1920 + 1200 + 200 = 760$ euros

Explain intuitively why the area function $A(b) = \int_0^b f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x$ has the derivative $A'(b) = f(b)$.

If $b$ grows by a small $h$, a narrow strip is added, almost a rectangle with width $h$ and height $f(b)$. So $\tfrac{A(b + h) - A(b)}{h} \approx f(b)$, and in the limit $h \to 0$ exactly $f(b)$.

EXCURSIONWHO SAW THE FUNDAMENTAL THEOREM FIRST

Isaac Barrow, Newton's teacher

Areas were already being calculated in antiquity, tangents since the 17th century. One of the first to realise that the two are inverses of each other was Isaac Barrow, professor of mathematics in Cambridge. In his “Lectiones geometricae” of 1670 he describes the connection, but entirely in the language of geometry, with curves and line segments.

His pupil Isaac Newton and, independently, Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz turned it into a method of calculation. In 1669 Barrow gave up his chair in Cambridge, and his successor was Isaac Newton.

Source: Isaac Barrow: Lectiones geometricae. London 1670.
