Areas

An integral can be 0 although the graph encloses a large area. Because below the $x$-axis the area counts negative. If you are looking for a real area, you have to split at the zeros. And if you are looking for the area between two curves, you need their points of intersection. With these two ideas you calculate areas that you could never get with formulas from geometry.

UNIT 14 LESSONS

1Area between a graph and the $x$-axis

$\int_{-1}^{2} (x^2 - 1)\,\mathrm{d}x = 0$. But between $-1$ and $1$ the graph lies below the axis, between $1$ and $2$ above it. The two parts cancel each other out exactly.

Calculated separately: $\int_{-1}^{1} (x^2 - 1)\,\mathrm{d}x = -\tfrac43$ and $\int_{1}^{2} (x^2 - 1)\,\mathrm{d}x = \tfrac43$. The area is $\tfrac43 + \tfrac43 = \tfrac83$.

KEY POINT · AREA WITH THE x-AXIS

1. Find the zeros of $f$ in the interval $[a, b]$.

2. Integrate separately between neighbouring zeros (and the limits).

3. Add up the absolute values of the partial integrals: $A = \left|\int_a^{x_1} f\,\mathrm{d}x\right| + \left|\int_{x_1}^{b} f\,\mathrm{d}x\right| + \ldots$

Symmetry helps: for graphs symmetric about the origin the parts on the left and right are equal in size, for graphs symmetric about the $y$-axis as well.

Without limits: “the area that the graph encloses with the $x$-axis” means the area between the zeros.

INTERACTIVE · INTEGRAL AND AREA

EXAMPLE 1 · TWO EQUAL PARTS

What area does $f(x) = x^3 - 4x$ enclose with the $x$-axis?

Zeros $-2$, $0$, $2$. $\int_0^2 (x^3 - 4x)\,\mathrm{d}x = -4$, by symmetry $\int_{-2}^0 = 4$. $A = 4 + 4 = 8$

EXAMPLE 2 · THE TUNNEL

How large is the cross-section of the tunnel $f(x) = -0.25x^2 + 4$?

Zeros $\pm 4$: 8 m wide, 4 m high. $A = \int_{-4}^{4} (-0.25x^2 + 4)\,\mathrm{d}x = \Big[-\tfrac{1}{12}x^3 + 4x\Big]_{-4}^{4} = \tfrac{32}{3} + \tfrac{32}{3} \approx 21.3$ m².

EXERCISES

Calculate the area between the graph and the $x$-axis: a) $f(x) = -x^2 + 4$   b) $f(x) = x^2 - 4$

a) $\int_{-2}^{2} (-x^2 + 4)\,\mathrm{d}x = \tfrac{32}{3} \approx 10.67$   b) mirror image, the same area $\tfrac{32}{3}$ (the integral is $-\tfrac{32}{3}$).

Calculate the area that $f(x) = \sin x$ encloses with the $x$-axis on the interval $[0, 2\pi]$.

Zero at $\pi$: $\int_0^{\pi} \sin x\,\mathrm{d}x = 2$, $\int_{\pi}^{2\pi} \sin x\,\mathrm{d}x = -2$. $A = 4$ (the integral is 0).

Calculate the area that $f(x) = x^3 - x$ encloses with the $x$-axis.

Zeros $-1$, $0$, $1$. $\int_0^1 (x^3 - x)\,\mathrm{d}x = -\tfrac14$, symmetric: $A = 2 \cdot \tfrac14 = \tfrac12$.

For which $a > 0$ does $f(x) = -x^2 + a^2$ enclose the area 36 with the $x$-axis?

$A = \int_{-a}^{a} (a^2 - x^2)\,\mathrm{d}x = 2a^3 - \tfrac23 a^3 = \tfrac43 a^3 = 36 \Rightarrow a^3 = 27 \Rightarrow a = 3$

UNIT 24 LESSONS

2Area between two graphs

The parabola $f(x) = -x^2 + 4$ and the line $g(x) = x + 2$ intersect at $x = -2$ and $x = 1$. In between, the parabola lies on top.

The area in between is “area under $f$ minus area under $g$”, that is the integral of the difference function $d(x) = f(x) - g(x) = -x^2 - x + 2$. It does not matter whether the graphs lie above or below the $x$-axis.

KEY POINT · AREA BETWEEN TWO GRAPHS

1. Find the points of intersection from $f(x) = g(x)$.

2. Calculate $\int (f(x) - g(x))\,\mathrm{d}x$ between neighbouring points of intersection.

3. Add up the absolute values: $A = \sum \left|\int_{x_i}^{x_{i+1}} (f(x) - g(x))\,\mathrm{d}x\right|$.

Which one is on top? With the absolute values it does not matter. Without them you have to calculate top minus bottom.

CAS: points of intersection with the equation solver, the integrals with the integral function.

INTERACTIVE · AREA BETWEEN TWO GRAPHS
EXAMPLE 3 · PARABOLA AND LINE

Calculate the area between $f(x) = -x^2 + 4$ and $g(x) = x + 2$.

$\int_{-2}^{1} (-x^2 - x + 2)\,\mathrm{d}x = \Big[-\tfrac13 x^3 - \tfrac12 x^2 + 2x\Big]_{-2}^{1} = \tfrac76 - \left(-\tfrac{10}{3}\right) = $ $\tfrac92 = 4.5$

EXAMPLE 4 · THREE POINTS OF INTERSECTION

Calculate the area between $f(x) = x^3$ and $g(x) = x^2 + 2x$.

$d(x) = x^3 - x^2 - 2x = x(x + 1)(x - 2)$, points of intersection $-1$, $0$, $2$. $\int_{-1}^{0} d\,\mathrm{d}x = \tfrac{5}{12}$, $\int_0^2 d\,\mathrm{d}x = -\tfrac83$. $A = \tfrac{5}{12} + \tfrac83 = \tfrac{37}{12} \approx 3.08$

EXERCISES

Calculate the area between $f(x) = x$ and $g(x) = x^2$.

Points of intersection 0 and 1: $\int_0^1 (x - x^2)\,\mathrm{d}x = \tfrac12 - \tfrac13 = \tfrac16$

Calculate the area between $f(x) = 2x$ and $g(x) = x^2$.

Points of intersection 0 and 2: $\int_0^2 (2x - x^2)\,\mathrm{d}x = 4 - \tfrac83 = \tfrac43$

Solve the exploration “The leaf”: calculate the area between $y = x^2$ and $y = \sqrt{x}$.

$\int_0^1 (\sqrt{x} - x^2)\,\mathrm{d}x = \tfrac23 - \tfrac13 = \tfrac13$

Calculate the area between $\sin x$ and $\cos x$ from $x = \tfrac{\pi}{4}$ to $x = \tfrac{5\pi}{4}$ (in between, $\sin x$ lies on top).

$\Big[-\cos x - \sin x\Big]_{\pi/4}^{5\pi/4} = \sqrt2 - (-\sqrt2) = 2\sqrt2 \approx 2.83$

A logo consists of the area between $f(x) = -\tfrac12 x^2 + 2$ and $g(x) = \tfrac12 x^2 - 2$ (in cm). How many cm² of paint do you need?

Points of intersection $\pm 2$. $\int_{-2}^{2} (4 - x^2)\,\mathrm{d}x = \tfrac{32}{3} \approx 10.7$ cm²

REVIEW · DEEPEN · CONNECT

Calculate the area between $f(x) = x^2 - 2x$ and the $x$-axis.

Zeros 0 and 2, $\int_0^2 (x^2 - 2x)\,\mathrm{d}x = \tfrac83 - 4 = -\tfrac43$, $A = \tfrac43$.

The tangent to $f(x) = x^2$ at the point $(1, 1)$, the graph of $f$ and the $y$-axis bound an area. Calculate it.

Tangent $t(x) = 2x - 1$. $\int_0^1 (x^2 - 2x + 1)\,\mathrm{d}x = \tfrac13 - 1 + 1 = \tfrac13$

Connecting with Year 12: find the line through the turning points of $f(x) = x^3 - 3x$ and the area it encloses with the graph between the turning points.

Maximum $(-1, 2)$, minimum $(1, -2)$, line $g(x) = -2x$. $d(x) = x^3 - x$, points of intersection $-1$, $0$, $1$, $A = 2 \cdot \tfrac14 = \tfrac12$.

For which $k > 0$ does the line $y = k$ halve the area between $f(x) = -x^2 + 4$ and the $x$-axis? (With a CAS.)

The area above $y = k$: $\tfrac43 (4 - k)^{3/2}$ (parabolic segment), it is to be $\tfrac{16}{3}$: $(4 - k)^{3/2} = 4 \Rightarrow k = 4 - 4^{2/3} \approx 1.48$.

EXCURSIONINTEGRATING 2,200 YEARS AGO

Archimedes and the parabolic segment

In the third century BC Archimedes of Syracuse calculated the area of a parabolic segment, entirely without antiderivatives. He filled the area with smaller and smaller triangles and proved that the segment is exactly $\tfrac43$ of the largest inscribed triangle.

Check it: the area between $y = x^2$ and $y = 1$ is $\int_{-1}^{1} (1 - x^2)\,\mathrm{d}x = \tfrac43$. The largest triangle in it has the corners $(-1, 1)$, $(1, 1)$ and $(0, 0)$, its area is 1. The ratio is exactly $\tfrac43$, as Archimedes proved more than 2,000 years ago.

Source: Archimedes: The Quadrature of the Parabola (Tetragonismos), 3rd century BC.
