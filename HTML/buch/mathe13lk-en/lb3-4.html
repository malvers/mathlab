Numerical integration and solids of revolution

Not every function has an antiderivative that you can write down. And often there is no function at all, only measurements: a speedometer log, a temperature curve, meter readings. Then you calculate the integral approximately, with rectangles or trapeziums. After that you rotate graphs about the $x$-axis: areas become solids of revolution, from the cone and the sphere to the wine barrel, and their volume is again an integral. To round off integral calculus you apply everything: in physics, economics and technology.

UNIT 14 LESSONS

1Rectangle and trapezium methods

In the speedometer log you join the measured points with straight lines. Under each section there is a trapezium: $10 \cdot \left(\tfrac{0 + 8}{2} + \tfrac{8 + 14}{2} + \tfrac{14 + 17}{2} + \tfrac{17 + 18}{2}\right) = 10 \cdot 48 = 480$ m.

In this way you can calculate any integral approximately: you divide $[a, b]$ into $n$ strips of equal width $h = \tfrac{b - a}{n}$ and replace the area in each strip by a rectangle or a trapezium. The more strips, the more accurate.

KEY POINT · NUMERICAL INTEGRATION

Nodes $x_k = a + k \cdot h$ with $h = \tfrac{b - a}{n}$.

Rectangle method (midpoints): $\int_a^b f\,\mathrm{d}x \approx h \cdot \left(f(m_1) + f(m_2) + \ldots + f(m_n)\right)$ with the midpoints $m_k$ of the strips.

Trapezium method: $\int_a^b f\,\mathrm{d}x \approx h \cdot \left(\tfrac12 f(x_0) + f(x_1) + \ldots + f(x_{n-1}) + \tfrac12 f(x_n)\right)$

With twice as many strips the error of both methods becomes about four times smaller.

Left bend: if the graph is convex, the trapeziums lie above it and the trapezium method gives too much. For right bends too little.

Why numerical at all? For $\mathrm{e}^{-x^2}$ there is no antiderivative made of the known functions, and for measurements there is no term at all. The CAS calculates like this, too.

INTERACTIVE · STRIPS, RECTANGLES, TRAPEZIUMS

EXAMPLE 1 · THREE METHODS COMPARED

Approximate $\int_0^2 x^2\,\mathrm{d}x$ with $n = 4$ strips ($h = 0.5$).

Left rectangles: $0.5 \cdot (0 + 0.25 + 1 + 2.25) = 1.75$ · Midpoints: 2.625 · Trapeziums: 2.75. Exactly $\tfrac83 \approx 2.667$: midpoints and trapeziums are much closer.

EXAMPLE 2 · THE BELL CURVE

Approximate $\int_0^1 \mathrm{e}^{-x^2}\,\mathrm{d}x$ with the trapezium method for $n = 4$ and $n = 10$.

$n = 4$: $\approx 0.7430$ · $n = 10$: $\approx 0.7462$ · A CAS gives 0.7468. The error falls from 0.004 to 0.0006.

The power of a solar module was measured every two hours (in kW): 6 a.m. 0, 8 a.m. 0.8, 10 a.m. 1.9, 12 noon 2.4, 2 p.m. 2.0, 4 p.m. 1.1, 6 p.m. 0.2. Estimate the energy produced in kWh with the trapezium method.

Use the graph to explain why the trapezium method always gives too much for $\int_0^2 x^2\,\mathrm{d}x$, however large $n$ is.

$x^2$ is convex: every chord between two points lies above the graph. So the areas of the trapeziums are always slightly larger than the area under the graph.

2Volume of solids of revolution

If the segment $y = \tfrac12 x$ over $[0, 4]$ rotates about the $x$-axis, a cone with height 4 and radius 2 is formed. If you cut it perpendicular to the axis into thin slices of thickness $\Delta x$, every slice is almost a cylinder: radius $f(x)$, volume $\pi\,f(x)^2\,\Delta x$. The sum of all slices is a Riemann sum, and for $\Delta x \to 0$ it turns into an integral.

As with the area, the integral is the limit of sums, only now you add up slices instead of strips. The square makes sure that parts below the $x$-axis count just like parts above it: a volume cannot become negative. If the area between two graphs rotates, a hollow solid is formed. Then you subtract the volume of the inner solid from that of the outer one.

KEY POINT · ROTATION ABOUT THE X-AXIS

If the graph of $f$ over $[a, b]$ rotates about the $x$-axis, the solid formed has the volume $$V = \pi \int_a^b f(x)^2\,\mathrm{d}x$$

If the area between $f$ and $g$ with $f(x) \ge g(x) \ge 0$ rotates: $V = \pi \displaystyle\int_a^b \big(f(x)^2 - g(x)^2\big)\,\mathrm{d}x$, not $\pi \int (f - g)^2\,\mathrm{d}x$.

Units: if $x$ and $f(x)$ are in cm, $V$ is in cm³. One litre is one dm³.

On the CAS: $\pi \cdot \int (f(x)^2, x, a, b)$. First find the limits, often as zeros or points of intersection.

INTERACTIVE · SOLIDS OF REVOLUTION IN 3D

Choose a solid, turn it with the mouse or your finger and switch on circular slices. The more slices, the closer their sum comes to the integral.

EXAMPLE 3 · THE CONE FORMULA DERIVED

A cone has the height $h$ and the base radius $r$. Derive $V = \tfrac13 \pi r^2 h$.

The slant line is $f(x) = \tfrac rh x$ on $[0, h]$. $V = \pi \int_0^h \tfrac{r^2}{h^2} x^2\,\mathrm{d}x = \pi \tfrac{r^2}{h^2} \left[\tfrac13 x^3\right]_0^h = \pi \tfrac{r^2}{h^2} \cdot \tfrac{h^3}{3}$.

$V = \tfrac13 \pi r^2 h$, the formula from lower secondary school is proved.

EXAMPLE 4 · THE SPHERE FORMULA DERIVED

Derive the volume of a sphere $V = \tfrac43 \pi r^3$.

The semicircle $f(x) = \sqrt{r^2 - x^2}$ on $[-r, r]$ generates the sphere. $f(x)^2 = r^2 - x^2$, the root disappears:

$V = \pi \left[r^2 x - \tfrac13 x^3\right]_{-r}^{r} = \pi\left(\tfrac23 r^3 + \tfrac23 r^3\right) = $ $\tfrac43 \pi r^3$.

EXERCISES

The graph of $f(x) = x^2$ over $[0, 2]$ rotates about the $x$-axis. Calculate the volume.

$V = \pi \int_0^2 x^4\,\mathrm{d}x = \pi \cdot \tfrac{32}{5} \approx 20.11$

$f(x) = 1 + \tfrac12 x$ over $[0, 2]$ generates a frustum of a cone. Calculate its volume and compare with the formula $V = \tfrac{\pi h}{3}(R^2 + Rr + r^2)$.

$\pi \int_0^2 \left(1 + x + \tfrac14 x^2\right)\mathrm{d}x = \pi\left(2 + 2 + \tfrac23\right) = \tfrac{14}{3}\pi \approx 14.66$. Formula with $h = 2$, $R = 2$, $r = 1$: $\tfrac{2\pi}{3} \cdot 7 = \tfrac{14}{3}\pi$ ✔

A barrel has the profile $f(x) = 2 - 0.08x^2$ for $-2.5 \le x \le 2.5$ (in dm). How many litres does it hold?

$f(x)^2 = 4 - 0.32x^2 + 0.0064x^4$. $\int_{-2.5}^{2.5} f(x)^2\,\mathrm{d}x = 20 - \tfrac{10}{3} + 0.25 = \tfrac{203}{12}$. $V = \tfrac{203}{12}\pi \approx 53.1$ dm³, so a good 53 litres.

The area between $y = x$ and $y = x^2$ rotates about the $x$-axis. Calculate the volume of the hollow solid and show that $\pi \int_0^1 (x - x^2)^2\,\mathrm{d}x$ gives a wrong result.

Correct: $\pi \int_0^1 (x^2 - x^4)\,\mathrm{d}x = \pi\left(\tfrac13 - \tfrac15\right) = \tfrac{2\pi}{15} \approx 0.419$. Wrong: $\pi \int_0^1 (x^2 - 2x^3 + x^4)\,\mathrm{d}x = \tfrac{\pi}{30} \approx 0.105$. That would be a solid made of slices with radius $x - x^2$, that is without a hole and much thinner.

UNIT 32 LESSONS

3Applications of the definite integral

The temperature varies during a day. How warm was it on average? We want the height of a rectangle that has the same area as the area under the temperature curve.

Integrals turn up wherever a quantity is made up of many small contributions: distance from speed, energy from power, work from force, costs from marginal costs. And the mean value of a function is the integral divided by the width of the interval.

KEY POINT · APPLICATIONS

Mean value of a function on $[a, b]$: $\bar{f} = \dfrac{1}{b - a} \displaystyle\int_a^b f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x$

Physics: distance $= \int v\,\mathrm{d}t$, energy $= \int P\,\mathrm{d}t$, work $= \int F\,\mathrm{d}s$ · Economics: costs $= $ fixed costs $+ \int K'\,\mathrm{d}x$

How to do it: recognise the quantity and the rate, the limits from the text, check the unit, interpret the result in context.

The unit as a check: kW times h gives kWh, N times m gives J (joules).

EXAMPLE 5 · MEAN VALUE

What is the mean value of $f(x) = x^2$ on $[0, 3]$ and of $\sin x$ on $[0, \pi]$?

$\tfrac13 \int_0^3 x^2\,\mathrm{d}x = \tfrac13 \cdot 9 = 3$ · $\tfrac{1}{\pi} \int_0^{\pi} \sin x\,\mathrm{d}x = \tfrac{2}{\pi} \approx 0.637$

EXAMPLE 6 · ENERGY

A kettle draws $P(t) = 2 - 0.1t$ kW for 4 minutes ($t$ in min). How much energy does it use?

$\int_0^4 (2 - 0.1t)\,\mathrm{d}t = 8 - 0.8 = 7.2$ kW·min $= 0.12$ kWh.

EXERCISES

Calculate the mean value of $f(x) = 2x + 1$ on $[0, 4]$. Why is it simply the value in the middle here?

$\tfrac14 \int_0^4 (2x + 1)\,\mathrm{d}x = \tfrac14 \cdot 20 = 5 = f(2)$. For straight lines the mean is the function value in the middle of the interval.

The temperature during a day is $T(t) = 12 + 6\sin\left(\tfrac{\pi}{12}(t - 8)\right)$ (°C, $t$ in hours). Use a CAS to find the mean temperature between 8 a.m. and 8 p.m.

With a CAS: $\tfrac{1}{12} \int_8^{20} T(t)\,\mathrm{d}t = 12 + \tfrac{12}{\pi} \approx 15.8$ °C.

A company has the marginal costs $K'(x) = 0.06x^2 - 2.4x + 30$ (€ per unit). By how much do the costs rise if it increases production from 20 to 40 units?

$\int_{20}^{40} K'(x)\,\mathrm{d}x = \Big[0.02x^3 - 1.2x^2 + 30x\Big]_{20}^{40} = 560 - 280 = 280$ euros (the fixed costs drop out).

Water flows into a pool at $r(t) = 6t - t^2$ (m³/h) for $0 \le t \le 6$. When has half of the total amount flowed in?

Total $\int_0^6 r\,\mathrm{d}t = 36$ m³. $3t^2 - \tfrac13 t^3 = 18$ has the solution $t = 3$ for reasons of symmetry: the graph of $r$ is symmetric about $t = 3$.

A cyclist starts from rest with $v(t) = 0.5t$ (m/s). How far has he got after 20 s? What is his average speed?

$\int_0^{20} 0.5t\,\mathrm{d}t = 100$ m, average speed $\tfrac{100}{20} = 5$ m/s.

Connecting with chapter 3.3: approximate the area between $f(x) = \sqrt{x}$ and the $x$-axis on $[0, 4]$ with four trapeziums and compare with the exact value.

$1 \cdot (0 + 1 + 1.414 + 1.732 + 1) = 5.146$. Exactly $\tfrac{16}{3} \approx 5.333$: too little, because $\sqrt{x}$ is concave.

For the trapezium method with $n$ strips, the error for $\int_0^2 x^2\,\mathrm{d}x$ is $\tfrac{4}{3n^2}$. How many strips do you need for an error below 0.001?

$\tfrac{4}{3n^2} < 0.001 \Rightarrow n^2 > 1333.3 \Rightarrow n \ge 37$. Check the formula with $n = 4$: $\tfrac{4}{48} \approx 0.083 = 2.75 - 2.667$ ✓

Numerical methods and rotation: a barrel is measured at intervals of 1 dm. The radii are 2.0 · 2.3 · 2.4 · 2.3 · 2.0 dm. Estimate its volume with the trapezium method for $\pi \int r(x)^2\,\mathrm{d}x$.

First square the radii, then apply the trapezium method with $h = 1$ to the squares.

$r^2$: 4 · 5.29 · 5.76 · 5.29 · 4. Trapeziums: $1 \cdot (2 + 5.29 + 5.76 + 5.29 + 2) = 20.34$. $V \approx 20.34\,\pi \approx 63.9$ dm³, so just under 64 litres.

The graph of $f(x) = \mathrm{e}^{-x}$ over $[0, b]$ rotates about the $x$-axis. Find $V(b)$ and show that the volume stays finite for $b \to \infty$, although the solid is infinitely long. From which $b$ on is less than 1% missing from the limit?

$f(x)^2 = \mathrm{e}^{-2x}$, an antiderivative is $-\tfrac12 \mathrm{e}^{-2x}$ (check by differentiating).

$V(b) = \pi \left[-\tfrac12 \mathrm{e}^{-2x}\right]_0^b = \tfrac{\pi}{2}\left(1 - \mathrm{e}^{-2b}\right) \to \tfrac{\pi}{2}$ for $b \to \infty$.

The missing share is $\mathrm{e}^{-2b} < 0.01$, so $b > \tfrac{\ln 100}{2} \approx 2.30$.

EXCURSIONNUMERICAL METHODS FROM THE WINE CELLAR

Kepler's barrel rule

In 1613 the astronomer Johannes Kepler bought wine in Linz for his wedding. The merchant measured the contents of the barrels with a rod that he pushed diagonally through the bung hole. Kepler doubted that this could be right for barrels of different shapes and wrote a whole book about calculating the volume of barrels.

It contains an approximation that is called Kepler's barrel rule today: $\int_a^b f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x \approx \tfrac{b - a}{6} \cdot \left(f(a) + 4f\left(\tfrac{a + b}{2}\right) + f(b)\right)$. For $\int_0^1 \mathrm{e}^{-x^2}\,\mathrm{d}x$ it gives 0.7472, with only three function values even more accurate than the trapezium method with ten strips. For polynomials up to degree three it is even exact: for $\int_0^2 x^2\,\mathrm{d}x$ it gives exactly $\tfrac83$.

Source: Johannes Kepler: Nova stereometria doliorum vinariorum. Linz 1615.
