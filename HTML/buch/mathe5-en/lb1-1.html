UNIT 12 LESSONS

1Natural numbers on the number line

How many goals has a team scored that has not scored a goal yet? None, zero. The numbers 0, 1, 2, 3, … are called natural numbers. Zero is the smallest natural number. There is no biggest one: you can always add 1 to any number.

On the number line the numbers sit next to each other at equal distances. It starts at 0, and the arrow shows: it goes on and on. For large numbers you only show a section and choose a suitable scale, for example always 100 or always 1,000 from mark to mark.

The number directly before a number is called its predecessor, the number directly after it its successor. The successor of 999 is 1,000, the predecessor of 1,000 is 999.

KEY POINT · NATURAL NUMBERS

The numbers 0, 1, 2, 3, … are called natural numbers. 0 is the smallest natural number; there is no biggest one.

Every natural number has a successor (1 more). Every one except 0 has a predecessor (1 less).

On the number line the smaller number lies on the left, the bigger one on the right: $3 < 7$ means “3 is less than 7”, $7 > 3$ means “7 is greater than 3”.

Memory aid: The point of $<$ and $>$ always points to the smaller number.

Check the scale: first work out how big one step from mark to mark is, then count.

INTERACTIVE · NUMBERS ON THE NUMBER LINE

Choose a scale and drag the point. Under “Read off” you get exercises: which number is marked?

EXAMPLE 1 · READING OFF

A number line starts at 2,000 and ends after 10 equal steps at 3,000. Which number is at the 4th mark?

From 2,000 to 3,000 is 1,000. One step is $1{,}000 \div 10 = 100$.

At the 4th mark is $2{,}000 + 4 \cdot 100 = 2{,}400$.

EXAMPLE 2 · PREDECESSOR AND SUCCESSOR

Give the predecessor and the successor of 40,000.

Predecessor: $40{,}000 - 1 = 39{,}999$. Successor: $40{,}000 + 1 = 40{,}001$.

$39{,}999 < 40{,}000 < 40{,}001$

2Large numbers in the place value table

On 31 December 2025, 4,026,599 people lived in Saxony. How do you read a number like that? You split it into groups of three, starting from the right: 4 | 026 | 599. Then you read: four million twenty-six thousand five hundred and ninety-nine.

Our numbers are built from ten digits: 0, 1, 2, …, 9. What a digit is worth depends on its place. In 444 the first 4 means four hundred, the second forty and the third four. Every place is worth ten times as much as the place to its right. That is why our number system is called the decimal system.

A zero keeps a place free: in 4,026,599 there are no hundred thousands. Without the zero you would read 426,599, a completely different number.

1 million = 1,000,000 (6 zeros)  ·  1 billion = 1,000,000,000 (9 zeros)  ·  1 trillion = 1,000,000,000,000 (12 zeros)

A thousand thousands are a million, a thousand millions a billion, a thousand billions a trillion. Large numbers are written in groups of three from the right: 12,500,000.

Abbreviations: O ones, T tens, H hundreds, Th thousands, TTh ten thousands, HTh hundred thousands, M millions.

Careful, German: a billion is called “eine Milliarde” in German. The German word “Billion” means a trillion.

3Rounding, estimating and comparing

The newspaper says: “Around 4 million people live in Saxony.” But at the end of 2025 there were exactly 4,026,599. The newspaper has rounded. For most readers that is enough, and the number is easier to remember.

When you round, you replace a number with a round number close to it. On the number line it is the nearest round number. For “approximately” you write $\approx$: $4{,}026{,}599 \approx 4{,}000{,}000$.

When you compare, you first count the digits: more digits means bigger. With the same number of digits you compare from the left, digit by digit.

When you estimate, you count only a small part exactly and then scale up: how many times does the part fit into the whole?

KEY POINT · ROUNDING

When you round, the digit to the right of the rounding place decides:

0, 1, 2, 3, 4 → round down  ·  5, 6, 7, 8, 9 → round up

$4{,}026{,}599 \approx 4{,}030{,}000$ (to the nearest ten thousand)  ·  $4{,}026{,}599 \approx 4{,}000{,}000$ (to the nearest million)

Always from the number itself: $3{,}449 \approx 3{,}400$ to the nearest hundred. If you first round to tens (3,450) and then round again, you wrongly end up at 3,500.

Never round: phone numbers, house numbers, postcodes and PINs.

EXAMPLE 6 · ESTIMATING

An aerial photo of a square is divided into 20 fields of the same size. In a typical field you count 45 people. About how many people are in the square?

All 20 fields are about equally full: $20 \cdot 45 = 900$.

REVIEW · DEEPEN · CONNECT

Place values: Write the number with 4 millions, 3 ten thousands, 8 hundreds and 5 ones.

4,030,805

A million sheets: A pack of copier paper has 500 sheets and is 5 cm high. How many packs are a million sheets? How high is the pile in metres?

$1{,}000{,}000 \div 500 = 2{,}000$ packs. $2{,}000 \cdot 5\text{ cm} = 10{,}000\text{ cm} = 100\text{ m}$. The pile would be as high as a tower block with about 30 floors.

Making up questions: A news report says: “In 2025, 98,234 people moved to Saxony and 84,729 moved away.” Make up two questions about it. For one of them a rounded answer should be enough; for the other you need the exact calculation.

For example: “About how many people moved to Saxony?” – around 100,000. “How many more people moved in than moved away?” – $98{,}234 - 84{,}729 = 13{,}505$.

Counting to a million: Suppose you say one number every second, without a break. A day has 86,400 seconds. Are 10 days enough? Are 12 days enough?

Work out how many seconds 10 days and 12 days have, and compare with 1,000,000.

10 days: $864{,}000$ seconds, that is less than a million. 12 days: $1{,}036{,}800$ seconds, that is enough. So you need almost 12 days without sleeping. And because large numbers take longer to say, in reality it takes much longer still.

EXCURSIONHISTORY

Where our digits come from

The digits 0 to 9 are called Hindu-Arabic numerals. In India people were already calculating with ten digits and a zero almost 1,400 years ago. In the year 628 the Indian mathematician Brahmagupta even wrote down rules for calculating with zero. Through Arab scholars the digits came to North Africa and Spain.

In Europe at that time people still calculated with Roman numerals. A merchant’s young son, Leonardo of Pisa, later called Fibonacci, got to know the new digits in North Africa. In 1202 he wrote the arithmetic book “Liber abaci”. It begins roughly like this: “The nine figures of the Indians are 9 8 7 6 5 4 3 2 1. With these nine figures and the sign 0, which the Arabs call zephirum, any number at all can be written.” From this word later came the Italian “zero”.

Try it yourself: Why is zero so important? Try to write down the numbers 3020 and 302 without a zero in such a way that nobody mixes them up.

Without zero both would look like “32”. Zero keeps a place free and shows: there is nothing here. Only with it does the place value table work. The Romans had no zero and needed many different symbols instead (more about that in the elective area).

Sources: Leonardo of Pisa (Fibonacci), “Liber abaci”, 1202, second version 1228, chapter 1; English edition: L. E. Sigler, “Fibonacci’s Liber Abaci”, Springer, New York 2002. Brahmagupta, “Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta”, 628.
