Coordinates, lines and angles

A square in a game of Battleships, a crossroads, a door half open: there are points, lines and angles everywhere. In this chapter you get to know the coordinate system, recognise lines that are parallel or perpendicular to each other, and measure, draw and estimate angles with the protractor.

UNIT 14 LESSONS

1Coordinate system, parallel and perpendicular

In Battleships you call out squares such as B4. In mathematics you describe a point with two numbers: $P(3, 5)$ means: from the origin 3 to the right and 5 up. The two numbers are called coordinates.

A coordinate system has two axes that are perpendicular to each other: the x-axis to the right and the y-axis upwards. They meet at the origin $O(0, 0)$. The first coordinate always belongs to the x-axis, the second to the y-axis.

A line segment AB has two end points. A ray has a starting point and goes on for ever in one direction. A line goes on for ever in both directions. Two lines can be parallel to each other; then they never meet. Otherwise they intersect, and if they make a right angle where they cross, they are perpendicular to each other.

KEY POINT · COORDINATES AND POSITIONS OF LINES

$P(x, y)$: first $x$ (to the right), then $y$ (up). The origin is $O(0, 0)$.

$g \parallel h$: parallel, the lines are the same distance apart everywhere and never meet.

$g \perp h$: perpendicular, the lines meet at a right angle (90°).

Memory aid: along the corridor (x), then up the stairs (y).

Set square: on a Geodreieck, the set square most German pupils use, the middle line is perpendicular to the long edge. The thin lines parallel to the long edge help you draw parallel lines.

INTERACTIVE · POINTS AND LINES

Drag the four points. Can you make the two lines parallel? And perpendicular? With “Find the point” you get exercises.

EXAMPLE 1 · A RECTANGLE

Plot $A(2, 1)$, $B(6, 1)$, $C(6, 4)$ and $D(2, 4)$ and join them in order. Which sides are parallel, which are perpendicular?

You get a rectangle, 4 squares wide and 3 high. AB and DC are horizontal, BC and AD vertical.

$AB \parallel DC$, $AD \parallel BC$, $AB \perp BC$

EXAMPLE 2 · LINE SEGMENT, RAY, LINE

How long is the line segment from $A(1, 3)$ to $B(1, 8)$? And how long is the line through A and B?

Both points lie 1 to the right of the origin; only the y-coordinate changes: $8 - 3 = 5$ squares.

The line segment is 5 squares long. A line has no length, it never ends.

EXERCISES

Plot $A(1, 1)$, $B(5, 1)$, $C(5, 5)$ and $D(1, 5)$ in a coordinate system and join them in order. Which shape do you get?

A square with a side length of 4 squares.

Give the coordinates of: a) the origin   b) a point on the x-axis, 7 to the right of the origin   c) a point on the y-axis, 3 above the origin

a) $(0, 0)$   b) $(7, 0)$   c) $(0, 3)$

Draw the line through $A(1, 2)$ and $B(7, 5)$. Draw a parallel line to it through $C(1, 5)$. Does $D(5, 7)$ lie on the parallel line?

From A to B you go 6 to the right and 3 up, so 2 to the right and 1 up. Go the same way from C.

From C: 2 right, 1 up gives $(3, 6)$, then $(5, 7)$. Yes, D lies on the parallel line.

A rectangle has the corners $A(2, 1)$, $B(8, 1)$ and $C(8, 5)$. Where is D? Which sides are parallel, which are perpendicular to each other?

$D(2, 5)$. Parallel: $AB \parallel DC$ and $AD \parallel BC$. Any two neighbouring sides are perpendicular, for example $AB \perp BC$.

Give four points that lie on the line through $P(3, 4)$ parallel to the x-axis. What do their coordinates have in common?

For example $(0, 4)$, $(1, 4)$, $(5, 4)$, $(9, 4)$. They all have the y-coordinate 4; only the x-coordinate is different.

UNIT 23 LESSONS

2Measuring and drawing angles

When you open a door, it turns on its hinges. How far it is open is described by an angle. Two rays with the same starting point make an angle.

The common starting point is called the vertex, the two rays are called the arms. Angles are named with small Greek letters: $\alpha$ (alpha), $\beta$ (beta), $\gamma$ (gamma), $\delta$ (delta).

Angles are measured in degrees. A full turn has 360°. To measure, you use a protractor (in Germany most pupils use a Geodreieck, a set square with a protractor scale): its centre point goes on the vertex, its base line along one arm. Then you read off at the other arm, on the scale that starts with 0° at the first arm.

KEY POINT · MEASURING AND DRAWING ANGLES

Measuring: 1. Centre point of the protractor on the vertex. 2. Base line along one arm. 3. Read off at the other arm on the scale that starts at 0°.

Drawing: 1. Draw one arm. 2. Put the protractor in place and mark the number of degrees. 3. Draw the second arm from the vertex through the mark.

A full turn has 360°.

Two scales: the protractor has two scales that run in opposite directions. Estimate first whether the angle is acute or obtuse; then you will not mix up 50° and 130°.

Bigger than 180°: measure what is missing to make a full turn: $360° - 120° = 240°$.

INTERACTIVE · ANGLES WITH A SCALE

Drag the second arm. The scale shows how to read off, beyond 180° too.

EXAMPLE 3 · THE RIGHT SCALE

Paul measures an angle $\alpha$. At the second arm he reads 65° on one scale and 115° on the other. The angle looks smaller than a right angle. Which is it?

An angle that is smaller than a right angle has less than 90°.

$\alpha = 65°$. The 115° belong to the wrong scale.

EXAMPLE 4 · DRAWING AN ANGLE

Draw an angle $\beta = 135°$ with the vertex V.

From V draw one arm to the right. Put the centre point on V and the base line along the arm, and mark 135° on the scale that starts with 0° at this arm. Join V to the mark.

The angle is open a bit wider than a right angle, but less than a straight angle.

EXERCISES

Draw the angles $\alpha = 40°$, $\beta = 90°$, $\gamma = 125°$ and $\delta = 170°$.

Check: $\alpha$ is acute, $\beta$ is a right angle, $\gamma$ and $\delta$ are obtuse, $\delta$ almost a straight angle. Let your neighbour measure them again.

How big is the angle between the two hands of a clock at 3 o’clock, at 2 o’clock and at 5 o’clock?

From one hour mark to the next it is $360° \div 12 = 30°$.

3 o’clock: 90°   2 o’clock: 60°   5 o’clock: 150°

Draw an angle of 250°. Describe how you went about it.

$360° - 250° = 110°$. Draw an angle of 110° and mark the arc on the outside: that is the angle of 250°.

Through how many degrees does the minute hand turn in 10 minutes, in 25 minutes and in 45 minutes?

In one minute 6°, because $360° \div 60 = 6°$. So 60°, 150° and 270°.

How big is the smaller angle between the two hands of a clock at half past one (1:30)?

At half past one the hour hand is exactly halfway between 1 and 2.

The minute hand points to the 6, that is 180° from the 12. The hour hand is at $1.5 \times 30° = 45°$. The angle between them: $180° - 45° = 135°$.

TEST YOURSELF!

How many degrees does a full turn have?

360°

Through how many degrees does the minute hand turn in 15 minutes?

A quarter turn: $360° \div 4 = 90°$.

An angle and 100° together make a full turn. How big is the angle?

$360° - 100° = 260°$

UNIT 32 LESSONS

3Types of angle and estimating

You can open a window a little, halfway or all the way. Depending on how far it is open, you get an acute, a right or an obtuse angle. There is a name for every size of angle.

Two angles to compare with help you to estimate: the right angle of 90°, as at the corner of an exercise book, and the straight angle of 180°, as in a straight line. Half a right angle is 45°, a third of it 30°.

KEY POINT · TYPES OF ANGLE
acute anglebetween 0° and 90°
right angleexactly 90°
obtuse anglebetween 90° and 180°
straight angleexactly 180°
reflex anglebetween 180° and 360°
full turnexactly 360°

Right angle: it is marked with a small square in the corner. (In German books you often see a quarter circle with a dot in it.)

Estimating aid: if you hold the corner of an exercise book against the angle, you can see straight away whether it is acute or obtuse.

INTERACTIVE · ESTIMATING ANGLES

Choose “Estimate”: an angle without a scale appears. Type in your estimate and check it.

EXAMPLE 5 · SORTING

Which type of angle are 35°, 90°, 179° and 181°?

35° lies between 0° and 90°, 179° between 90° and 180°, 181° between 180° and 360°.

35° acute; 90° right; 179° obtuse; 181° reflex

EXAMPLE 6 · MAKING IT UP TO 180°

Two angles lie next to each other and together make a straight angle. One of them is 35°. How big is the other one, and what type is it?

Together 180°: $180° - 35° = 145°$.

The other angle is 145°, an obtuse angle.

EXERCISES

Give the type of angle: 12°, 90°, 145°, 180°, 200°, 359°, 360°

acute, right, obtuse, straight, reflex, reflex, full turn

Draw an acute, a right, an obtuse and a reflex angle. Estimate their sizes, write each estimate next to it and then measure.

Compare your estimate and your measurement. If you are less than 10° out, you have estimated well.

At which full hours do the hands of a clock make a right angle? And when do they make a straight angle?

A right angle at 3 o’clock and at 9 o’clock. A straight angle at 6 o’clock.

A right angle is split into two parts. One part is 28°. How big is the other?

$90° - 28° = 62°$, also an acute angle.

Can two acute angles together make an obtuse angle? Can two obtuse angles together make a reflex angle? Give examples and reasons.

Yes: $60° + 50° = 110°$ is obtuse. Two obtuse angles are each bigger than 90°, so together they are always bigger than 180° and smaller than 360°: always reflex, for example $100° + 100° = 200°$.

TEST YOURSELF!

What type is an angle of 95°?

95° lies between 90° and 180°: obtuse.

How big is a straight angle?

180°, half of a full turn.

How many degrees are missing from an angle of 70° to make a right angle?

$90° - 70° = 20°$

REVIEW · DEEPEN · CONNECT

A triangle: Plot $A(1, 1)$, $B(7, 1)$ and $C(1, 5)$ and join them. At which corner is there a right angle?

At A: AB lies on a horizontal line, AC on a vertical one.

A kite: Plot $A(4, 0)$, $B(7, 4)$, $C(4, 9)$ and $D(1, 4)$ and join them in order. Draw the diagonals AC and BD. How do they lie in relation to each other?

AC is vertical (all its points have x = 4), BD is horizontal (y = 4). The diagonals are perpendicular to each other.

Angles in a triangle: Measure the three angles of the triangle from exercise 16 and add them. What do you notice?

About 90°, 34° and 56°. Together about 180°. This is true for every triangle; you will learn more about it in Year 6.

The hour hand: Through how many degrees does the hour hand turn in one hour? And in one minute?

In 12 hours 360°, so in one hour 30°. An hour has 60 minutes, so $30° \div 60 = 0.5°$ per minute.

EXCURSIONTECHNOLOGY

The Geodreieck

Almost every pupil in Germany has one: the Geodreieck. It is a triangle with a right angle and two sides of the same length, so its angles are 90°, 45° and 45°. It is a ruler, a protractor and a set square in one.

The transparent Geodreieck was developed in 1964 by the company Aristo, whose head office was in Hamburg, at its factory in Wörgl in Tyrol. It is still made there today. “Geodreieck” is even a protected brand name.

Try it yourself: Lay a Geodreieck or a set square with its long side at the bottom. Which angles do you find at its corners? What are the thin lines parallel to the long side good for?

At the top a right angle, at the bottom left and right 45° each. With the lines parallel to the long side you draw parallel lines: put one of these lines on the given line and draw along the edge.

Source: ARISTO, catalogue 2026 and company history (aristo.at): the Geodreieck was developed in 1964 at the Wörgl factory and is an internationally registered trade mark.
