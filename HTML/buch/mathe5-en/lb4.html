UNIT 13 LESSONS

1Symmetry in nature, art and technology

A butterfly, a heart, the letter A: if you fold them along a line, the two halves lie exactly on top of each other. Shapes like these have reflective symmetry, and the line is called the line of symmetry.

You find symmetry in nature in leaves, flowers and animals, in art in patterns and buildings, and in technology in wheels, signs and logos. Some shapes have several lines of symmetry: a rectangle two, a square four, a circle infinitely many.

Whether a line is a line of symmetry you check with the fold test or the mirror test. Two points that land on each other when you fold are the same distance from the line, and the line joining them is perpendicular to the line of symmetry.

KEY POINT · REFLECTIVE SYMMETRY

A shape has reflective symmetry if you can fold it along a straight line so that the two halves lie exactly on top of each other. The line is called the line of symmetry.

Points that belong together are the same distance from the line. The line segment joining them is perpendicular to the line of symmetry.

Mirror test: stand a small mirror upright on the line you think is the line of symmetry. If the reflection completes the half shape exactly, it is a line of symmetry.

Letters: A, M, T, U have a vertical line of symmetry, B, C, D, E a horizontal one, H, I, O, X both.

INTERACTIVE · PATTERN WORKSHOP

Choose the symmetry and colour in squares. Their reflections are coloured automatically. With “Example” you get a finished pattern.

EXAMPLE 1 · COUNTING LINES

How many lines of symmetry do a rectangle, a square and a circle have?

The rectangle: the two lines through the middle. The square: the two lines through the middle and the two diagonals. The circle: every line through the centre.

Rectangle 2, square 4, circle infinitely many

EXAMPLE 2 · SYMMETRICAL WORDS

Which words are symmetrical about a vertical line? OTTO, ANNA, WOW, MAMA

Every letter must have a vertical line of symmetry itself, and the word must be the same backwards. In ANNA the N has no line of symmetry, and MAMA backwards is AMAM.

OTTO and WOW

On squared paper, draw a shape with exactly one line of symmetry and one with exactly two lines of symmetry. Draw the lines in colour.

For example a house with a roof (one line) and a rectangle or a plus sign with arms of different lengths (two lines).

Explain why an ink blot picture always has reflective symmetry.

When you fold it shut, every spot of paint is pressed onto the other half, exactly opposite across the fold and the same distance from it. The fold is the line of symmetry.

Find a word that is symmetrical about a vertical line and one that is symmetrical about a horizontal line.

For a horizontal line you need letters such as B, C, D, E, H, I, K, O, X.

Vertical: OTTO, WOW, TOOT. Horizontal: BOB, BOX, DECK. With a horizontal line, each letter only has to be symmetrical on its own; the order stays the same.

Two lines through the middle and two diagonals: 4.

How many lines of symmetry does a rectangle that is not a square have?

The two lines through the middle. The diagonals are not lines of symmetry.

Which word is symmetrical about a vertical line?

OTTO: all its letters have a vertical line of symmetry, and backwards it is OTTO again.

UNIT 24 LESSONS

2Reflection in a line

When you raise your right hand in front of a mirror, your reflection raises its left. In a reflection in a line the original becomes an image that is just as big, but flipped over.

The line is called the mirror line $m$. Every point $P$ has an image point $P'$, read “P dash”. To find it, you draw the perpendicular to the mirror line through $P$ and mark off the same distance on the other side. On squared paper you simply count the squares to the mirror line.

Points that lie on the mirror line stay where they are. The image is congruent to the original, but its orientation is reversed: if you go A → B → C anticlockwise in the original, you go clockwise in the image.

KEY POINT · REFLECTION IN A LINE

Every point $P$ has an image point $P'$: the line segment $PP'$ is perpendicular to the mirror line $m$ and is cut in half by it.

Points on the mirror line stay fixed. The original and the image are congruent, but flipped over.

Set square: put the middle line of a Geodreieck on the mirror line. Then its long edge is perpendicular to the mirror line, and you can read off the distance on both sides.

In the coordinate system: with a vertical mirror line only the x-coordinate changes, with a horizontal one only the y-coordinate.

INTERACTIVE · REFLECTING IN THE COORDINATE SYSTEM

Drag the mirror line and choose its direction. On the right are the coordinates of the original and the image.

EXAMPLE 3 · REFLECTING A TRIANGLE

Reflect the triangle $A(1, 1)$, $B(4, 1)$, $C(1, 3)$ in the vertical line $x = 5$.

A is 4 squares to the left of the mirror line, so A′ is 4 squares to the right of it. B is 1 square away. The y-coordinates stay the same.

$A'(9, 1)$, $B'(6, 1)$, $C'(9, 3)$

EXAMPLE 4 · FINDING THE MIRROR LINE

The point $P(2, 4)$ has the image $P'(8, 4)$. Where is the mirror line?

The mirror line cuts the line segment $PP'$ in half: halfway between 2 and 8 is 5.

The mirror line is the vertical line $x = 5$.

EXERCISES

Reflect in the vertical line $x = 4$: $P(1, 2)$, $Q(3, 5)$, $R(4, 1)$.

$P'(7, 2)$, $Q'(5, 5)$, $R'(4, 1)$, because R lies on the mirror line.

On squared paper, draw a rectangle with one diagonal. Draw a vertical mirror line 2 squares to the right of it and reflect the whole thing.

The image is a rectangle of the same size, 2 squares to the right of the mirror line. The diagonal is flipped over: if it rises to the right in the original, it falls to the right in the image.

Reflect the triangle $A(2, 1)$, $B(5, 1)$, $C(5, 3)$ in the horizontal line $y = 4$.

Now the x-coordinates stay the same: $A'(2, 7)$, $B'(5, 7)$, $C'(5, 5)$.

The point $P(3, 2)$ has the image $P'(3, 8)$. Give the mirror line.

The x-coordinate stays the same, so the mirror line is horizontal. Halfway between 2 and 8 is 5: the horizontal line $y = 5$.

Reflect $P(2, 1)$ in the line $x = 4$ and then reflect the image again in the line $x = 7$. Where does the point end up? What do you notice?

First $P'(6, 1)$, then $P''(8, 1)$. Altogether the point has moved 6 squares to the right, which is twice the distance between the mirror lines. Two reflections in parallel mirror lines give a translation.

TEST YOURSELF!

$P(2, 5)$ is reflected in the vertical line $x = 6$. What is the x-coordinate of $P'$?

P is 4 squares to the left of the mirror line, P′ 4 squares to the right: $6 + 4 = 10$.

And what is the y-coordinate of $P'$?

With a vertical mirror line the y-coordinate stays the same: 5.

A point lies on the mirror line. How far is its image point from it?

It stays where it is: distance 0.

UNIT 33 LESSONS

3Translation

A drawer is pulled open, a cupboard is pushed across the floor, a cable car travels straight along its cable: every point moves the same distance in the same direction. That is a translation.

You describe a translation with a translation arrow. It shows the direction, and its length is the distance moved. In the coordinate system you say, for example: “3 to the right and 2 up”. Then the point $P(1, 1)$ becomes the point $P'(4, 3)$.

Unlike in a reflection, the shape is not flipped over. All the line segments from an original point to its image point are parallel and of the same length.

KEY POINT · TRANSLATION

In a translation every point moves in the same direction by the same distance. A translation arrow shows this.

All the line segments $PP'$ are parallel and of the same length. The original and the image are congruent and not flipped over.

In the coordinate system: you add the same number to every x-coordinate, and likewise to every y-coordinate.

Reflection or translation? If the shape is flipped over, it was a reflection. If everything points in the same direction as before, a translation.

INTERACTIVE · TRANSLATING WITH THE ARROW

Drag the arrowhead. Under “Puzzle” you can test whether you can tell reflections and translations apart.

EXAMPLE 5 · TRANSLATING A TRIANGLE

Translate the triangle $A(1, 1)$, $B(3, 1)$, $C(2, 3)$ by 4 to the right and 2 up.

Add 4 to every x-coordinate and 2 to every y-coordinate.

$A'(5, 3)$, $B'(7, 3)$, $C'(6, 5)$

EXAMPLE 6 · FINDING THE ARROW

A translation takes $P(2, 5)$ to $P'(7, 3)$. Describe the arrow.

x: from 2 to 7, so 5 to the right. y: from 5 to 3, so 2 down.

5 to the right and 2 down

$A'(3, 1)$, $B'(5, 5)$, $C'(8, 3)$

a) translation   b) reflection   c) translation   d) reflection

The square $A(1, 1)$, $B(3, 1)$, $C(3, 3)$, $D(1, 3)$ is translated so that A ends up at $A'(6, 2)$. Where are $B'$, $C'$ and $D'$?

The arrow is 5 to the right and 1 up: $B'(8, 2)$, $C'(8, 4)$, $D'(6, 4)$.

After a translation by 6 to the right and 3 up, a point is at $P'(9, 4)$. Where was it before?

Backwards: 6 to the left and 3 down: $P(3, 1)$.

Two vertical mirror lines are 3 squares apart. A shape is reflected first in the left one, then in the right one. Show with a drawing that the result is a translation. How far?

The second image is no longer flipped over, because it was flipped over twice. Every point has moved 6 squares to the right, twice the distance between the mirror lines.

TEST YOURSELF!

$P(2, 3)$ is translated by 5 to the right and 4 up. What is the x-coordinate of $P'$?

$2 + 5 = 7$

And what is the y-coordinate of $P'$?

$3 + 4 = 7$

In which transformation is a shape flipped over?

In a reflection the orientation is reversed, in a translation it is not.

UNIT 42 LESSONS

4Congruent figures and patterns

Two stamps from the same sheet can be placed exactly on top of each other. Figures like these are called congruent. A stamp and its enlargement on a poster have the same shape, but they are not congruent.

Congruent figures have the same shape and the same size. Corresponding sides are the same length, corresponding angles are the same size. Reflecting and translating change no lengths and no angles: the original and the image are always congruent.

A pattern is a design in which a motif is repeated again and again, on tiles, fabrics, borders or the fronts of houses. In a frieze pattern each motif comes from the one before by a translation or a reflection. With compasses and a set square you can draw motifs made of circles and rectangles.

KEY POINT · CONGRUENT

Two figures are congruent if you can place them exactly on top of each other. Corresponding sides are the same length, corresponding angles are the same size.

Reflecting and translating always give congruent images. Enlarging and reducing do not.

Turning over is allowed: even if you have to turn a figure over first to place it on the other one, the two are congruent.

Drawing circles: set the compasses to the radius, put the point on the centre and turn them once round without lifting them.

INTERACTIVE · FRIEZE PATTERNS

Choose a motif made of circles and rectangles and a transformation. That is how the frieze is made.

EXAMPLE 7 · CONGRUENT OR NOT?

Are a square with a side length of 3 cm and a square with a side length of 4 cm congruent?

Both have the same shape, but their sides are different lengths.

No, they are not congruent.

EXAMPLE 8 · DRAWING A FRIEZE

Draw a frieze pattern: a square with a side length of 2 cm and a circle with a radius of 1 cm inside it. The motif is translated by 3 cm to the right four times.

Draw the first square and the circle round the centre of the square. Then draw the same thing four more times, 3 cm further to the right each time. There is 1 cm of space between the squares.

Five congruent motifs in a row; the frieze is $4 \times 3 + 2 = 14$ cm long.

EXERCISES

Which pairs are congruent? a) two circles with a radius of 2 cm   b) a rectangle 2 cm by 6 cm and one 3 cm by 4 cm   c) two triangles with the sides 3 cm, 4 cm and 5 cm   d) a square with a side length of 4 cm and a rectangle 4 cm by 4 cm

a) yes   b) no, the same area but different sides   c) yes   d) yes, the rectangle is itself a square

Draw a frieze pattern of your own made of circles and rectangles. The motif should appear five times. Describe how you did it.

For example: a rectangle with a circle at its top right corner, reflected in a vertical line each time. What matters is that all the motifs are the same size.

A triangle has the sides 3 cm, 4 cm and 5 cm. It is reflected in a line. How long are the sides of the image? What is its perimeter?

Also 3 cm, 4 cm and 5 cm, because reflecting changes no lengths. Perimeter: 12 cm.

In a frieze every motif is 2 cm wide; the motifs touch each other and are reflected each time. Where are the mirror lines?

Exactly where two motifs touch each other, so every 2 cm.

Can you make a square with a side length of 4 cm from a square with a side length of 2 cm by reflecting and translating? Give a reason.

No. Reflecting and translating change no lengths. The image always has sides 2 cm long again. For 4 cm you would have to enlarge it, and then the image is no longer congruent.

TEST YOURSELF!

A rectangle 5 cm long and 2 cm wide is translated. What is the area of the image?

The same as the original: $5 \times 2 = 10$ cm².

Which does not give a congruent figure?

When you enlarge, the sides get longer.

An angle in the original is 70°. How big is the corresponding angle in the reflection?

Also 70°: reflecting changes no angles.

REVIEW · DEEPEN · CONNECT

Honeycomb: How many lines of symmetry does a regular hexagon with six sides of the same length and angles of the same size have?

6: three lines through opposite vertices and three through the midpoints of opposite sides.

Reflect, then translate: Reflect the quadrilateral $A(1, 1)$, $B(4, 1)$, $C(4, 2)$, $D(1, 4)$ in the line $x = 5$. Then translate the image 3 up.

Reflecting: $A'(9, 1)$, $B'(6, 1)$, $C'(6, 2)$, $D'(9, 4)$. Translating: $A''(9, 4)$, $B''(6, 4)$, $C''(6, 5)$, $D''(9, 7)$.

Tiles: There is a circle on a square tile. How many lines of symmetry does the tile have if the circle is exactly in the middle? And if its centre lies on a diagonal near a corner?

Circle in the middle: 4 lines, as for the square. Circle on the diagonal near a corner: only 1 line left, the diagonal through that corner.

Footprints in the sand: left, right, left, right … Which transformation takes a left footprint onto the next left one? And which onto the next right one?

Left onto the next left: a translation by the length of a double step. Left onto the next right: a reflection in the middle line of the track together with a translation by one step.

EXCURSIONART AND EVERYDAY LIFE

Mirror writing

The Italian artist and inventor Leonardo da Vinci (1452 to 1519) filled thousands of pages with drawings and notes, and he wrote almost all of it in mirror writing: from right to left, with every letter reflected. It is easiest to read with a mirror. One of his notebooks, the Codex Arundel, is kept today in the British Library in London.

Leonardo was left-handed. If you write with your left hand from right to left, you do not smudge the fresh ink. That he wanted to hide his ideas from others with it is thought to be a legend.

You can see mirror writing today as well: on the front of many ambulances the word is written in mirror writing. Whoever is driving in front of the ambulance sees it in the rear-view mirror and can read it the right way round there.

Try it yourself: Write your first name in capital letters in mirror writing and check it with a mirror. Which capital letters look the same in mirror writing as normally?

All the letters with a vertical line of symmetry: A, H, I, M, O, T, U, V, W, X, Y. The mirror line is the vertical line at the edge of the mirror.

Source: British Library, London, “Leonardo da Vinci’s notebook” (Codex Arundel, Arundel MS 263): written in Italian and in Leonardo’s left-handed mirror writing from right to left.
