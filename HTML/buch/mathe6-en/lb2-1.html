Representing assignments

Every item in a shop is assigned a price, every time of day a temperature, every person a seat. Such assignments are everywhere. You learn to tell them apart by their type and to represent them in five ways: in words, as a table, with arrows, as a graph in a coordinate system and as an equation.

UNIT 15 LESSONS

1Types of assignments

Every person has exactly one birthday. But two people can have their birthday on the same day. And a teacher often teaches several subjects. So not all assignments are the same.

An assignment connects the elements of a starting set (on the left) with the elements of a target set (on the right). In the arrow diagram you can see straight away which type it is:

KEY POINT · TYPES OF ASSIGNMENTS

Single-valued: exactly one arrow starts at each element on the left.   item → price

One-to-one: single-valued, and exactly one arrow arrives at each element on the right.   car → number plate

Many-valued: several arrows start at at least one element on the left.   number → its factors

Turning it round: “person → first name” is single-valued, “first name → person” is often many-valued when two people have the same name.

Thinking backwards: with a one-to-one assignment you can safely work back from the element on the right to the one on the left.

INTERACTIVE · ARROW DIAGRAM

Choose an assignment, decide on a type and check your answer.

EXAMPLE 1 · WHICH TYPE?

Which type is “month → number of its days”?

Every month has exactly one number of days: single-valued. But April, June, September and November all have 30 days.

single-valued, but not one-to-one

EXAMPLE 2 · A NUMBER RULE

Each number is assigned three times itself plus 1. Which number belongs to 4, and which number has the partner 22?

$3 \cdot 4 + 1 = 13$. Backwards: $22 - 1 = 21$, $21 : 3 = 7$.

$4 \to 13$ and $7 \to 22$

EXERCISES

Single-valued, one-to-one or many-valued? a) person → shoe size   b) number → its double   c) person → their hobbies   d) natural number → its successor

a) single-valued   b) one-to-one   c) many-valued   d) one-to-one

Draw the arrow diagram for “number → remainder when divided by 4” with the numbers 5, 6, 8, 9, 10. Which type is it?

$5 \to 1$, $6 \to 2$, $8 \to 0$, $9 \to 1$, $10 \to 2$. Single-valued, but not one-to-one (two arrows each arrive at 1 and at 2).

A bus leaves at 7:12, 7:32 and 7:52, always at the same interval. When does the bus after next leave after 7:52?

Interval 20 minutes: 8:12 and then 8:32.

Find your own example from everyday life for a single-valued, a one-to-one and a many-valued assignment. Swap with someone and check each other’s.

For example: bike → colour (single-valued), locker → key (one-to-one), class → the people in the class (many-valued).

UNIT 25 LESSONS

2Table, graph and equation

At the start the candle is 20 cm tall, after one hour 17.5 cm, after two hours 15 cm. In the table of values these pairs stand next to each other. If you plot them as points, they lie on a falling line – the graph of the assignment.

In the coordinate system you plot the starting quantity to the right ($x$-axis) and the assigned quantity upwards ($y$-axis). A point $P(3, 12.5)$ means: 3 to the right, 12.5 up.

If you can describe an assignment with a rule, you write it as an equation: $h = 20 - 2.5 \cdot t$. You only join the points if the values in between make sense too – for time yes, for the number of cinema tickets no.

KEY POINT · REPRESENTATIONS

In words: Hiring a bike costs €3 per hour.

Table: 1 h → €3, 2 h → €6, 3 h → €9   ·   Equation: $p = 3 \cdot t$

Graph: points $(t, p)$ in the coordinate system, the starting quantity horizontally, the assigned quantity vertically.

Scaling the axes: check what one square stands for. For prices up to €90 on 9 cm, this works: 1 cm stands for €10.

Reading off: from the $x$-axis straight up to the graph, then across to the $y$-axis.

INTERACTIVE · ONE ASSIGNMENT, FOUR REPRESENTATIONS

Choose an assignment and a row of the table. The matching point on the graph lights up.

EXAMPLE 3 · TAXI

A taxi costs a €3 basic charge and €2 per kilometre. Set up an equation and calculate the price for 7 km.

$y = 2 \cdot x + 3$ ($x$ in km, $y$ in €). For $x = 7$: $y = 14 + 3$.

7 km cost €17.

EXAMPLE 4 · READING A GRAPH

A bath fills evenly: 30 litres after 2 minutes, 60 litres after 4 minutes. How much is in it after 5 minutes?

Every minute 15 litres flow in. $5 \cdot 15 = 75$.

After 5 minutes there are 75 litres in the bath.

EXERCISES

For $y = 2 \cdot x + 1$, make a table of values for $x$ = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 and draw the points in a coordinate system.

$y$-values: 1, 3, 5, 7, 9. The points $(0, 1)$, $(1, 3)$, $(2, 5)$, $(3, 7)$, $(4, 9)$ lie on a rising straight line.

A bread roll costs €0.40. Make a table for 1 to 6 rolls. Why do you not join the points on the graph?

€0.40; €0.80; €1.20; €1.60; €2.00; €2.40. There are only whole rolls; values in between such as 2.5 rolls make no sense.

Jana rides her bike. After 1 hour she is 12 km from home, takes a half-hour break and then rides back in 1.5 hours. Sketch the graph “time → distance”. What does the horizontal part show?

The graph rises to $(1, 12)$, stays horizontal until $(1.5, 12)$ (the break) and falls to $(3, 0)$.

Prices up to €90 are to fit on a vertical axis 9 cm long, times up to 6 hours on a horizontal axis 12 cm long. Which scale do you choose?

Vertical axis: 1 cm stands for €10. Horizontal axis: 2 cm stand for 1 hour.

The points $(1, 5)$, $(2, 7)$, $(3, 9)$ belong to an assignment with a simple rule. Find the equation and the $y$-value for $x = 10$.

By how much does $y$ grow when $x$ grows by 1?

$y$ grows by 2 each time: $y = 2 \cdot x + 3$. For $x = 10$: $y = 23$.

Taxi: $y = 2 \cdot x + 3$. How many euros do 7 km cost?

$2 \cdot 7 + 3 = 17$

15 litres per minute: how many litres are in the bath after 5 minutes?

$5 \cdot 15 = 75$

Calculate $y = 0.5 \cdot x + 2$ for $x = 6$.

$3 + 2 = 5$

REVIEW · DEEPEN · CONNECT

On one day these were measured: 8:00 12 °C, 10:00 15 °C, 12:00 19 °C, 14:00 21 °C, 16:00 17 °C. When was it warmest? When did the temperature rise most?

Warmest at 14:00. It rose most between 10:00 and 12:00 (by 4 degrees).

Connecting with sets: Which type is “person in your class → month of birth” if there are 28 people in your class? Can it be one-to-one?

Single-valued. It cannot be one-to-one: there are only 12 months, so at least two people have their birthday in the same month.

A mobile phone plan costs €10 a month, no matter how much you phone. Draw the graph “minutes → monthly price”. What does it look like, and which equation fits?

A horizontal line at height 10. Equation: $y = 10$.

EXCURSIONHISTORY

René Descartes and the coordinate system

That numbers and pictures can be connected is something we owe, among others, to the Frenchman René Descartes (1596–1650). In his book “La Géométrie” of 1637 he described how points can be fixed by numbers and curves by equations.

After his Latin name Cartesius, the right-angled pair of axes is still called the Cartesian coordinate system today. A well-known story says that Descartes had the idea while watching a fly on the ceiling of his room – but there is no evidence for it.

Try it yourself: Join the points $(1, 1)$, $(5, 1)$, $(5, 4)$, $(3, 6)$, $(1, 4)$ and back to $(1, 1)$. What do you get?

A house with a roof.

Source: René Descartes, “La Géométrie”, appendix to the “Discours de la méthode”, Leiden 1637. The story of the fly is a legend.
