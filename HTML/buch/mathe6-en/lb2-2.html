UNIT 15 LESSONS

1Directly and inversely proportional

1 kg of apples costs €2.50, 2 kg cost €5, 3 kg cost €7.50. If you divide the price by the mass, you always get 2.50. 24 pieces of cake for 2, 3 or 4 people: 12, 8 or 6 pieces per person. Here the product is always 24.

In a directly proportional assignment, double, triple, … belongs to double, triple, … All pairs have equal quotients. Its graph is a straight line through the origin.

In an inversely proportional assignment, double belongs to half, triple to a third. All pairs have equal products. Its graph is a falling curve that comes closer and closer to the axes.

KEY POINT · PROPORTIONAL

Directly proportional: $y : x$ is always the same.   mass → price: $5 : 2 = 7.5 : 3 = 2.5$

Inversely proportional: $x \cdot y$ is always the same.   people → pieces: $2 \cdot 12 = 3 \cdot 8 = 24$

Not proportional: neither the quotient nor the product is the same, for example because of a basic charge.

“The more, the more” is not enough: the older, the taller – but someone who is twice as old is not twice as tall.

Checking in the table: work out the quotient and the product for each column. Is one of them always the same?

INTERACTIVE · PROPORTIONAL OR NOT?

Choose an assignment and move the point. The table shows the quotient and the product, the graph the shape.

EXAMPLE 1 · CHECKING A TABLE

$x$: 2, 3, 6   $y$: 12, 8, 4. Which type is it?

Quotients: 6; about 2.7; about 0.7 – different. Products: 24, 24, 24 – the same.

inversely proportional

UNIT 25 LESSONS

2The rule of three

3 exercise books cost €2.40. What do 7 cost? Work it out via a single exercise book: it costs €0.80, so 7 exercise books cost $7 \cdot 0.80 = 5.60$ €. Those are three steps – hence the name rule of three.

With the rule of three, you first work out the value for one unit (1 exercise book, 1 person, 1 hour) and then for the amount you are looking for. With direct proportionality you divide to get to the unit and then multiply. With inverse proportionality it is the other way round.

KEY POINT · RULE OF THREE

Direct: 3 exercise books → €2.40 · 1 exercise book → $2.40 : 3 = 0.80$ € · 7 exercise books → $7 \cdot 0.80 = 5.60$ €

Inverse: 6 people → 8 days · 1 person → $6 \cdot 8 = 48$ days · 4 people → $48 : 4 = 12$ days

Always check first: is the assignment directly or inversely proportional – or not at all?

Table with arrows: write one quantity on the left and the other on the right, one below the other, and note “: 3” and “· 7” on the arrows.

Sometimes it is quicker without the unit: 6 scoops of ice cream cost €7.20, 9 scoops are 1.5 times as many: €10.80.

INTERACTIVE · RULE OF THREE STEP BY STEP

Choose a problem and go step by step. Watch out for when you divide and when you multiply.

EXAMPLE 3 · DIRECT

A car uses 7 litres of petrol per 100 km. How much does it use for 350 km?

100 km → 7 ℓ · 50 km → 3.5 ℓ · 350 km is 3 times 100 km and 50 km: $21 + 3.5$.

24.5 litres

EXAMPLE 4 · INVERSE

3 excavators need 12 days for a building pit. How long do 4 identical excavators need?

1 excavator: $3 \cdot 12 = 36$ days. 4 excavators: $36 : 4$.

9 days

UNIT 35 LESSONS

3Word problems with proportionality

250 g of butter cost €2.29, the 1 kg pack costs €7.99. With the rule of three you would get €9.16 for 1 kg – but the big pack is cheaper. Not everything in everyday life is proportional. That is why the rule is: check first, then calculate.

Good solutions to word problems follow a plan: note what is given and what you are looking for, check the type of assignment, calculate, check the result with an estimate and write an answer sentence with sensible accuracy.

Money is rounded to the cent, everyday lengths to the centimetre. If 7 people share €100, each gets €14.28 – with €14.29, 3 cents would be missing.

KEY POINT · WORD PROBLEMS

1. What is given, what are you looking for?   2. Which type of assignment?   3. Calculate (rule of three).   4. Estimate and check.   5. Answer sentence with sensible accuracy.

Ratios such as 2 : 3 mean parts: 2 parts juice and 3 parts yoghurt are 5 parts altogether.

Scale: 1 : 25,000 means: 1 cm on the map is 25,000 cm = 250 m in reality.

Comparing prices: convert both offers to the same amount, for example to 1 kg.

INTERACTIVE · THREE ASSIGNMENTS COMPARED

Select “neither”: for the taxi the straight line is there, but it does not go through the origin.

EXAMPLE 5 · COMPARING PRICES

Pack A: 500 g of pasta for €2.40. Pack B: 750 g for €3.30. Which is better value?

A: 1 kg → €4.80. B: 250 g → €1.10, 1 kg → €4.40.

Pack B is better value.

EXAMPLE 6 · RATIO

Juice and yoghurt in the ratio 2 : 3. How much juice is in 600 ml of smoothie?

5 parts are 600 ml, one part 120 ml, 2 parts 240 ml.

240 ml of juice

EXERCISES

A recipe for 6 people needs 450 ml of milk. How much do you need for 4 people?

1 person 75 ml, 4 people 300 ml.

Paula counts 18 heartbeats in 15 seconds. How many is that in a minute?

$4 \cdot 18 = 72$ beats per minute.

On a hiking map with a scale of 1 : 25,000 you measure 4 cm. How far is that in reality?

$4 \cdot 25{,}000 = 100{,}000$ cm = 1000 m = 1 km.

A tradesperson charges €40 for the call-out and €30 per hour. What does a 3-hour job cost? Why can you not simply use the rule of three to go from 1 hour to 3 hours here?

$40 + 3 \cdot 30 = 130$ €. The call-out always costs the same – the assignment is not proportional (1 hour costs €70, but 3 hours do not cost €210).

REVIEW · DEEPEN · CONNECT

A dripping tap loses 0.5 litres per hour. How much water is that in a day and in a week?

12 litres a day, 84 litres a week.

A car uses 6 litres per 100 km, and a litre costs €1.80. What does the petrol for 250 km cost?

15 litres, $15 \cdot 1.80 = 27$ €.

3 identical machines produce 450 parts in 5 hours. How many parts do 2 machines produce in 8 hours?

First work it out for 1 machine and 1 hour.

1 machine in 5 hours: 150 parts, in 1 hour: 30 parts. 2 machines in 8 hours: $2 \cdot 8 \cdot 30 = 480$ parts.

EXCURSIONHISTORY

Adam Ries and the “regula de tri”

Adam Ries (1492–1559) was a master of arithmetic in Annaberg in the Ore Mountains. He wrote his arithmetic books in German and not in Latin, so that merchants and craftsmen could read them. They were so well known that Germans still say today: “Das macht nach Adam Riese …” (“According to Adam Ries, that makes …”).

In them, the rule of three is called the “Regel de tri”, the rule of the three numbers. Because it was so useful, old arithmetic books also called it the “golden rule”. From three known numbers you find the fourth – exactly as you have done in this chapter.

Try it yourself: A problem in the style of the old arithmetic books: 3 ells of cloth cost 9 groschen. What do 7 ells cost?

1 ell costs 3 groschen, 7 ells cost 21 groschen.

Source: Adam Ries, “Rechenung auff der linihen und federn”, Erfurt 1522. The Adam Ries Museum is in Annaberg-Buchholz.
