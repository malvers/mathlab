Area and perimeter

Cut a triangle off a parallelogram, attach it on the other side – and you have a rectangle. With tricks like this you find the areas of the triangle, parallelogram, kite and trapezium and also calculate awkward plots of land, walls and flower beds.

UNIT 15 LESSONS

1Parallelogram and triangle

By cutting off and attaching, the parallelogram becomes a rectangle. Its length is the base $g$, its width the height $h$ of the parallelogram. Two identical triangles make a parallelogram – so a triangle is half as big.

The height is the perpendicular distance from the base to the opposite side or vertex. It is not the slanting side. In an obtuse-angled triangle the height can lie outside; then you extend the base.

KEY POINT · PARALLELOGRAM AND TRIANGLE

Parallelogram: $A = g \cdot h$

Triangle: $A = \dfrac{g \cdot h}{2}$

Base and height belong together: the height is perpendicular to the base.

Right-angled triangle: the two sides at the right angle are base and height: $A = 6 \cdot 8 : 2 = 24$ cm².

Make the units match: 5 cm and 30 mm: first 30 mm = 3 cm, then calculate.

INTERACTIVE · SPLITTING AND MOVING

Choose the parallelogram or the triangle and drag the slider: the shape is rearranged into a rectangle.

EXAMPLE 1 · PARALLELOGRAM

A parallelogram has the base 7.5 cm and the height 4 cm.

$A = 7.5 \cdot 4$

$A = 30$ cm²

EXAMPLE 2 · FINDING THE HEIGHT

A triangle has an area of 24 cm² and the base 8 cm. How long is the height?

$24 = 8 \cdot h : 2$, so $48 = 8 \cdot h$.

$h = 6$ cm

EXERCISES

Calculate the area: a) parallelogram $g = 6$ cm, $h = 4$ cm   b) triangle $g = 12$ m, $h = 7$ m   c) triangle $g = 5$ cm, $h = 30$ mm

a) 24 cm²   b) 42 m²   c) $5 \cdot 3 : 2 = 7.5$ cm²

A triangular sail is 3 m wide and 4.5 m high. How much fabric do you need?

$3 \cdot 4.5 : 2 = 6.75$ m²

A parallelogram has an area of 36 cm² and the base 9 cm. How high is it? A triangle with the same base is to be just as big. How high must it be?

Parallelogram: $h = 4$ cm. Triangle: $36 = 9 \cdot h : 2$, $h = 8$ cm.

For a parallelogram with the sides 6 cm and 5 cm, Tom calculates: $A = 30$ cm². What is wrong?

He took the slanting side instead of the height. The height is shorter than 5 cm, so $A$ is less than 30 cm².

A triangle has the vertices $A(1, 1)$, $B(7, 1)$ and $C(4, 5)$ (1 unit = 1 cm). How big is it? Move $C$ to $(6, 5)$. What changes?

$g = 6$, $h = 4$: $A = 12$ cm². With $C(6, 5)$, $g$ and $h$ stay the same – the area stays 12 cm².

UNIT 25 LESSONS

2Kite, trapezium and perimeter

A kite fits exactly into a rectangle made from its two diagonals – and covers half of it. Two identical trapeziums, one of them turned round, make a parallelogram with the base $a + c$.

In a kite the diagonals $e$ and $f$ are perpendicular to each other. In a trapezium two sides $a$ and $c$ are parallel, and their distance apart is the height $h$.

The perimeter is the length of all the sides together – like a fence around the shape. It is measured in cm or m, not in cm².

KEY POINT · KITE, TRAPEZIUM, PERIMETER

Kite (also for the rhombus): $A = \dfrac{e \cdot f}{2}$   ·   Trapezium: $A = \dfrac{(a + c) \cdot h}{2}$

Perimeter: add all the sides. For the parallelogram: $u = 2a + 2b$, for the square $u = 4a$.

Area or perimeter? Carpet and paint: area. Fence, skirting board, frame: perimeter.

Backwards: a square with a perimeter of 36 cm has the side $36 : 4 = 9$ cm.

INTERACTIVE · REARRANGING THE TRAPEZIUM AND THE KITE

Choose the trapezium or the kite. Change the measurements and drag the slider.

EXAMPLE 3 · TRAPEZIUM

A trapezium has $a = 10$ cm, $c = 6$ cm and $h = 4$ cm.

$A = (10 + 6) \cdot 4 : 2 = 16 \cdot 4 : 2$

$A = 32$ cm²

EXAMPLE 4 · KITE

How much paper does a kite with the sticks 80 cm and 50 cm need at least?

$A = 80 \cdot 50 : 2 = 2000$ cm²

2000 cm² = 0.2 m²

a) 22.5 cm²   b) 24 cm²   c) 50 cm²

a) 22 cm   b) 22 cm   c) 20 cm

A dyke has a cross-section in the shape of a trapezium: 20 m wide at the bottom, 6 m at the top, 5 m high. How big is the cross-sectional area?

$(20 + 6) \cdot 5 : 2 = 65$ m²

A trapezium has an area of 30 cm², $a = 7$ cm and $c = 5$ cm. How high is it?

$30 = 12 \cdot h : 2 = 6 \cdot h$, so $h = 5$ cm.

Split a trapezium with $a = 10$ cm, $c = 4$ cm, $h = 4$ cm (both legs of equal length) into a rectangle and two triangles. Check that you get the same as with the formula.

Rectangle $4 \cdot 4 = 16$, two triangles with $g = 3$ and $h = 4$: 6 each. Together 28 cm² = $(10 + 4) \cdot 4 : 2$.

UNIT 35 LESSONS

3Composite shapes and problem solving

An L-shaped terrace: you can split it into two rectangles and add them – or complete it to a large rectangle and subtract the missing piece. Both ways lead to the same result.

For geometry word problems a fixed plan helps: a sketch with all the measurements, find the sub-problems, introduce variables for what is unknown, calculate, check.

KEY POINT · SPLITTING AND COMPLETING

Splitting: cut the shape into parts with known formulas and add the areas.

Completing: complete the shape to a simple shape and subtract the added part again.

Plan: sketch · sub-problems · calculate · estimate · answer sentence

Are and hectare: 1 a = 100 m² (10 m × 10 m), 1 ha = 100 a = 10,000 m².

Missing measurements: often you first have to work out a length, for example $8 - 3 = 5$ m.

INTERACTIVE · SPLITTING OR COMPLETING

Change the shape and compare both ways. The result is always the same.

EXAMPLE 5 · A WALL WITH A WINDOW

A wall is 4 m wide and 2.5 m high, and it has a window of 1.2 m × 1 m. How much of the wall is painted?

Wall $4 \cdot 2.5 = 10$ m², window 1.2 m². $10 - 1.2$

8.8 m²

EXAMPLE 6 · A PATH AROUND THE LAWN

A lawn measures 12 m × 8 m. All around the outside there is a path 1 m wide. How big is the path?

With the path: $14 \cdot 10 = 140$ m². Lawn: 96 m². $140 - 96$

44 m²

EXERCISES

An L-shaped terrace is 8 m long and 3 m deep at the bottom; on the left it goes back 6 m in total and is 3 m wide there. Calculate the area and the perimeter.

Sketch! Split it into a rectangle of 8 m × 3 m and one of 3 m × 3 m.

$24 + 9 = 33$ m². Perimeter: $8 + 3 + 5 + 3 + 3 + 6 = 28$ m.

The wall of a house consists of a rectangle (8 m × 3 m) and a gable triangle (8 m wide, 2.5 m high). How big is it?

$24 + 10 = 34$ m²

A rectangle is twice as long as it is wide, and its perimeter is 36 cm. How long and how wide is it?

Width $b$, length $2b$: $6b = 36$, $b = 6$ cm, length 12 cm.

One litre of paint is enough for 5 m². How many litres do you buy for 8.8 m² if there are only whole litres?

$8.8 : 5 = 1.76$, so 2 litres.

A rectangle is to be laid from 12 squares of 1 cm² each. Find all the possibilities. Which one has the smallest perimeter?

1 × 12 (26 cm), 2 × 6 (16 cm), 3 × 4 (14 cm). The almost square 3 × 4 has the smallest perimeter.

REVIEW · DEEPEN · CONNECT

A plot of land is a trapezium: street side 30 m, back 20 m, depth 25 m. How big is it in m² and in ares?

$(30 + 20) \cdot 25 : 2 = 625$ m² = 6.25 a

A corner is cut off a metal sheet (10 cm × 6 cm): a right-angled triangle with 4 cm and 3 cm. How big is the sheet now?

$60 - 4 \cdot 3 : 2 = 60 - 6 = 54$ cm²

A square plaza with a side length of 15 m gets a fence; 1 m costs €12. For the lawn inside, 1 m² of turf costs €6. What do both cost together?

Fence: $60 \cdot 12 = 720$ €. Lawn: $225 \cdot 6 = 1350$ €. Together €2070.

EXCURSIONHISTORY

Fields on the Nile

In ancient Egypt the Nile burst its banks every year. Afterwards the fields had to be measured again, because the taxes depended on their size. The Greek historian Herodotus (5th century BC) reports that geometry grew out of this land surveying – the word means “earth measuring”.

The Rhind Papyrus (around 1550 BC) contains problems on the areas of rectangles and triangles. The Egyptians already calculated the triangle correctly as half a rectangle.

Try it yourself: A triangular field has a base of 10 rods and a height of 4 rods. How many square rods is it?

$10 \cdot 4 : 2 = 20$ square rods.

Sources: Herodotus, Histories, Book II, chapter 109; Rhind Papyrus, problem 51 (area of a triangle), British Museum.
