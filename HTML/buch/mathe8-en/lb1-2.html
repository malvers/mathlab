Linear equations and formulas

A tradesman charges €45 for the call-out and €38 per working hour. His bill comes to €197. How long did he work? Behind this question is the equation $45 + 38x = 197$. In this chapter you solve such equations confidently step by step, rearrange formulas for any quantity and crack word problems, number puzzles and age problems with an equation.

UNIT 14 LESSONS

1Solving linear equations

The balance from the explorations shows the equation $3x + 2 = x + 6$. If you take 2 kg away on both sides, it stays level: $3x = x + 4$. If you take one parcel away on both sides: $2x = 4$. If you halve both sides: $x = 2$. One parcel weighs 2 kg.

KEY POINT · EQUIVALENCE TRANSFORMATIONS

The solution of an equation does not change if you add or subtract the same number or the same multiple of $x$ on both sides, or multiply or divide both sides by the same number (not 0).

  1. Remove brackets and collect like terms on each side.
  2. Bring all the terms with $x$ to one side, all the numbers to the other.
  3. Divide by the number in front of $x$.
  4. Check: substitute the solution into the original equation.

If you get a false statement such as $6 = 1$, the equation has no solution. If you get a true statement such as $0 = 0$, every number is a solution.

Note the transformation: to the right of the equation, after a vertical line, write what you do on both sides, for example $\mid -2x$.

Never divide by 0. And multiplying by 0 turns every equation into $0 = 0$. That is not an allowed transformation.

INTERACTIVE · TRANSFORMING LIKE ON A BALANCE

Type in a transformation, for example −3, :4 or −2x, until $x$ stands on its own. “New equation” gives you the next one.

EXAMPLE 1 · x ON BOTH SIDES

Solve $3x + 45 = 12x - 9$.

$3x + 45 = 12x - 9 \quad \mid -3x$

$45 = 9x - 9 \quad \mid +9$

$54 = 9x \quad \mid :9$

$x = 6$

Check: $3 \cdot 6 + 45 = 63$ and $12 \cdot 6 - 9 = 63$.

EXAMPLE 2 · WITH BRACKETS

Solve $4 \cdot (x - 3) - 2 = 2 \cdot (x + 5)$.

Remove the brackets: $4x - 12 - 2 = 2x + 10$, collect: $4x - 14 = 2x + 10$.

$\mid -2x$: $2x - 14 = 10$, $\;\mid +14$: $2x = 24$, $\;\mid :2$:

$x = 12$

Check: $4 \cdot 9 - 2 = 34$ and $2 \cdot 17 = 34$.

EXAMPLE 3 · NO SOLUTION

Solve $2 \cdot (x + 3) = 2x + 1$.

$2x + 6 = 2x + 1 \quad \mid -2x$

$6 = 1$: a false statement, whichever number you substitute.

The equation has no solution: $L = \{\,\}$.

EXAMPLE 4 · INFINITELY MANY SOLUTIONS

Solve $3 \cdot (x - 2) + x = 4x - 6$.

$3x - 6 + x = 4x - 6$, so $4x - 6 = 4x - 6 \quad \mid -4x$

$-6 = -6$: a true statement for every $x$.

Every number is a solution. Both sides are equivalent terms.

Solve the equations with brackets. a) $3 \cdot (x + 4) = 27$   b) $5 \cdot (x - 2) = 2 \cdot (x + 7)$   c) $12 - (x - 3) = 2x$

a) $3x + 12 = 27$, $x = 5$   b) $5x - 10 = 2x + 14$, $3x = 24$, $x = 8$   c) $15 - x = 2x$, $15 = 3x$, $x = 5$

Does the equation have exactly one, no or infinitely many solutions? a) $4x + 2 = 2 \cdot (2x + 1)$   b) $3x - 5 = 3 \cdot (x - 1)$   c) $6x - 4 = 2x + 8$

a) $4x + 2 = 4x + 2$: infinitely many.   b) $3x - 5 = 3x - 3$ leads to $-5 = -3$: none.   c) $4x = 12$: exactly one, $x = 3$.

Mia solves $5x - 3 = 2x + 9$ like this: $\mid -2x$: $3x - 3 = 9$; $\;\mid +3$: $3x = 6$; $\;\mid :3$: $x = 2$. Find the mistake and check your result.

UNIT 22 LESSONS

2Rearranging formulas

A rectangular garden gets a fence 26 m long. One side is 8 m long. $u = 2 \cdot (a + b)$ becomes $26 = 2 \cdot (8 + b)$, so $13 = 8 + b$ and $b = 5$. If you have to work this out often, you rearrange the formula for $b$ once and then only substitute.

KEY POINT · REARRANGING FORMULAS

You rearrange a formula like an equation. The quantity you are looking for plays the part of $x$, all the other quantities are treated like numbers.

The steps of the calculation are undone in reverse order: what was calculated last is reversed first.

$u = 2 \cdot (a + b) \quad \mid :2 \qquad \tfrac{u}{2} = a + b \quad \mid -a \qquad b = \tfrac{u}{2} - a$

Finally, a check with numbers.

Check the units: if $u$ and $a$ are given in metres, $b$ must come out in metres too.

Two ways: for a single calculation you can also substitute the numbers first and then solve the equation.

INTERACTIVE · REARRANGING STEP BY STEP

Choose a formula and the quantity you are looking for. Every step shows what happens on both sides.

EXAMPLE 5 · THE JOURNEY TIME

Rearrange $s = v \cdot t$ for $t$. How long does a train take for 420 km at an average of $140\,\tfrac{\text{km}}{\text{h}}$?

$s = v \cdot t \quad \mid :v \qquad t = \dfrac{s}{v}$

$t = \dfrac{420\,\text{km}}{140\,\tfrac{\text{km}}{\text{h}}} = 3\,\text{h}$

The train takes 3 hours.

EXAMPLE 6 · THE HEIGHT OF A TRAPEZIUM

Rearrange $A = \dfrac{(a + c) \cdot h}{2}$ for $h$. Work out $h$ for $A = 30\,\text{cm}^2$, $a = 7\,\text{cm}$ and $c = 5\,\text{cm}$.

$\mid \cdot 2$: $2A = (a + c) \cdot h$, $\;\mid :(a + c)$: $h = \dfrac{2A}{a + c}$

$h = \dfrac{60\,\text{cm}^2}{12\,\text{cm}} = 5\,\text{cm}$

The trapezium is 5 cm high.

EXERCISES

Rearrange for the given quantity. a) $A = a \cdot b$ for $a$   b) $u = 4a$ for $a$   c) $V = a \cdot b \cdot c$ for $c$   d) $P = U \cdot I$ for $I$

a) $a = \tfrac{A}{b}$   b) $a = \tfrac{u}{4}$   c) $c = \tfrac{V}{a \cdot b}$   d) $I = \tfrac{P}{U}$

For the tradesman from the opener, $K = 45 + 38 \cdot t$ ($K$ in euros, $t$ in hours). Rearrange for $t$ and work out $t$ for $K = 254$.

$t = \dfrac{K - 45}{38}$. For $K = 254$: $t = \tfrac{209}{38} = 5.5$, so 5.5 hours.

Rearrange $u = 2 \cdot (a + b)$ for $a$. A rectangular plot of land has a perimeter of 86 m and is 18 m wide. How long is it?

$a = \tfrac{u}{2} - b = 43\,\text{m} - 18\,\text{m} = 25\,\text{m}$

In the USA, temperatures are measured in degrees Fahrenheit: $F = 1.8 \cdot C + 32$, where $C$ is the temperature in degrees Celsius. Rearrange for $C$. How many degrees Celsius are 104 °F and 50 °F?

$C = \dfrac{F - 32}{1.8}$. 104 °F: $\tfrac{72}{1.8} = 40$, so 40 °C. 50 °F: $\tfrac{18}{1.8} = 10$, so 10 °C.

For density, $\varrho = \dfrac{m}{V}$. Rearrange for $m$ and for $V$. A piece of aluminium ($\varrho = 2.7\,\tfrac{\text{g}}{\text{cm}^3}$) weighs 135 g. What is its volume?

$m = \varrho \cdot V$ and $V = \dfrac{m}{\varrho}$. $V = \dfrac{135\,\text{g}}{2.7\,\tfrac{\text{g}}{\text{cm}^3}} = 50\,\text{cm}^3$

TEST YOURSELF!

$u = 2a + 2b$ with $u = 30\,\text{cm}$ and $a = 9\,\text{cm}$. How big is $b$?

$b = \tfrac{30 - 18}{2} = 6$, so 6 cm.

$s = v \cdot t$ with $s = 150\,\text{km}$ and $v = 60\,\tfrac{\text{km}}{\text{h}}$. How big is $t$?

$t = \tfrac{150}{60} = 2.5$, so 2.5 h.

Which formula do you get if you rearrange $A = \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot g \cdot h$ for $h$?

UNIT 32 LESSONS

3Word problems with equations

Back to the tradesman: $x$ stands for the number of working hours. The text becomes the equation $45 + 38x = 197$. It gives $38x = 152$ and $x = 4$. Check against the text: $45\,\text{€} + 4 \cdot 38\,\text{€} = 197\,\text{€}$. He worked for 4 hours.

KEY POINT · WORD PROBLEMS IN FOUR STEPS
  1. Understand: what is given, what are you looking for? Choose a variable for what you are looking for and write down what it stands for.
  2. Set up the equation: translate the text into terms sentence by sentence.
  3. Solve with equivalence transformations.
  4. Check and answer: check against the text, not only against the equation. Answer in a sentence, with the unit.

Translating: double $\to 2x$, 5 more $\to x + 5$, three consecutive numbers $\to x$, $x + 1$, $x + 2$.

Age problems: in 5 years every person is 5 years older, the other one too.

INTERACTIVE · THINK OF A NUMBER

A number passes through a chain of calculations. Guess the starting number or work backwards with the inverse operations.

EXAMPLE 7 · A NUMBER PUZZLE

The sum of three consecutive natural numbers is 87. What are the numbers?

Let the smallest number be $x$: $x + (x + 1) + (x + 2) = 87$, so $3x + 3 = 87$, $3x = 84$, $x = 28$.

The numbers are 28, 29 and 30. Check: $28 + 29 + 30 = 87$.

EXAMPLE 8 · AN AGE PROBLEM

A father is four times as old as his daughter today. In 12 years he will only be twice as old. How old are both of them today?

Daughter today $x$, father today $4x$. In 12 years: $4x + 12 = 2 \cdot (x + 12)$, so $4x + 12 = 2x + 24$, $2x = 12$, $x = 6$.

The daughter is 6 years old, the father 24. Check: in 12 years they will be 18 and 36, and 36 is double 18.

EXERCISES

Five times a number, decreased by 8, gives 37. Set up an equation and find the number.

$5x - 8 = 37$, $5x = 45$, $x = 9$.

A climbing park costs €8 admission and €3.50 per hour. Max pays €22. How long was he in the park?

$8 + 3.5t = 22$, $3.5t = 14$, $t = 4$. Max was in the park for 4 hours.

A rectangle is 7 cm longer than it is wide. Its perimeter is 50 cm. How long are the sides?

$2 \cdot (x + x + 7) = 50$, $4x + 14 = 50$, $x = 9$. The sides are 9 cm and 16 cm long.

Three friends share €120: Ana gets twice as much as Bea, Cleo €20 more than Bea. How much does each get?

Bea $x$: $x + 2x + (x + 20) = 120$, $4x = 100$, $x = 25$. Bea €25, Ana €50, Cleo €45.

A father is 32 years older than his son. In 4 years he will be three times as old as his son. How old are both of them today?

Son $x$: $x + 36 = 3 \cdot (x + 4)$, $x + 36 = 3x + 12$, $24 = 2x$, $x = 12$. The son is 12, the father 44. Check: in 4 years 16 and 48.

$3.5 + 2x = 27.5$, $2x = 24$, $x = 12$: 12 km.

Two consecutive natural numbers add up to 51. What is the smaller one?

$2x + 1 = 51$, $x = 25$

Solve $0.5x + 3 = 0.2x + 6$. Hint: you can multiply both sides by 10 first.

$0.3x = 3$, $x = 10$. Multiplied by 10: $5x + 30 = 2x + 60$, $3x = 30$.

Connect with Year 7: after a price increase of 15 %, a rucksack costs €69. What did it cost before?

$1.15 \cdot x = 69$, $x = 60$. Before, it cost €60.

Candle A is 24 cm long and burns down 3 cm every hour. Candle B is 18 cm long and burns down 1.5 cm every hour. Both are lit at the same time. When are they the same length, and how long are they then?

$24 - 3t = 18 - 1.5t$, $6 = 1.5t$, $t = 4$. After 4 hours both are 12 cm long. In chapter 2.3 you will also solve problems like this graphically.

EXCURSIONHISTORY

Restoring and balancing: where the word algebra comes from

Around 825, the scholar Muhammad ibn Musa al-Khwarizmi wrote a textbook in Baghdad on solving equations. Its title names two steps of calculation: al-jabr, restoring, and al-muqabala, balancing. In restoring, a term that has been subtracted is added on the other side, so $5x - 3 = 2x + 9$ becomes $5x = 2x + 12$. In balancing, you take away the same on both sides: $3x = 12$. That is exactly what you do with your equivalence transformations.

Al-Khwarizmi still wrote entirely without symbols, everything in words. When his book was translated into Latin in 1145, al-jabr became the word algebra. And his name became the word algorithm.

Sources: Muhammad ibn Musa al-Khwarizmi, The Compendious Book on Calculation by Restoring and Balancing, Baghdad around 825. Latin translation by Robert of Chester, Liber algebrae et almucabala, 1145.
