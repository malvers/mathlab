Functions

The weather station assigns exactly one maximum temperature to each day, the petrol pump exactly one price to each number of litres, your way to school exactly one distance from home to each minute. Such unique assignments are called functions. You represent them as a table, as a graph and as an equation and get to know the simplest ones: the proportional functions, whose graph is a straight line through the origin.

UNIT 12 LESSONS

1What is a function?

Each person has exactly one month of birth: exactly one arrow starts at each person. The other way round, several people or none at all can belong to one month. Only the first assignment is unique.

KEY POINT · FUNCTION

An assignment that assigns exactly one element of the target set to each element of the starting set is called unique. A unique assignment is called a function.

Functions can be represented as a rule in words, a table of values, an arrow diagram, a graph or an equation, for example $y = 2x + 1$.

You recognise a function from its graph like this: every vertical line meets it at most once.

Many-valued: several numbers are assigned to one number, for example its divisors 1, 2, 3 and 6 to the number 6.

Allowed: two different $x$ may have the same value. Two people may have their birthday in the same month.

INTERACTIVE · ARROW DIAGRAMS

Choose an assignment and decide: many-valued, single-valued (unique) or even one-to-one? Unique assignments are functions.

EXAMPLE 1 · FUNCTION OR NOT?

a) Each person is assigned their height.   b) Each natural number is assigned its divisors.   c) Each number is assigned its square.

a) Each person has exactly one height: function.   b) 1, 2, 3 and 6 are assigned to the number 6: not a function.   c) Each number has exactly one square: function. That 3 and $-3$ both lead to 9 is allowed.

a and c are functions, b is not.

EXAMPLE 2 · FOUR REPRESENTATIONS

Each number is assigned double the number, increased by 1. Represent the function as a table, an equation and a graph.

$x$$-1$$0$$1$$2$
$y$$-1$$1$$3$$5$

Equation: $y = 2x + 1$. The graph is a straight line through $(0, 1)$ that rises by 2 with each step to the right.

Rule in words, table, equation and graph describe the same function.

UNIT 22 LESSONS

2Argument, function value, domain

Mia’s way to school takes 20 minutes. Exactly one distance from home is assigned to each minute. For the time, only values from 0 to 20 minutes make sense, and the distance lies between 0 m and the length of the way to school.

KEY POINT · TECHNICAL TERMS

The number $x$ that you substitute is called the argument. The number assigned to it is called the function value. You write $f(x) = 2x + 1$ and read “$f$ of $x$”. For example, $f(3) = 7$.

The domain $D$ contains all the arguments that may be substituted or that make sense in the context.

The range $W$ contains all the function values that come out.

The point $(x, f(x))$ lies on the graph of $f$.

In context: a time is not negative, a number of people is a natural number, a candle burns down at some point.

Reading off the graph: from the argument go vertically up to the graph, then horizontally across to the y-axis.

INTERACTIVE · MIA’S WAY TO SCHOOL

Move the time. The point on the graph and Mia on the road move together.

EXAMPLE 3 · FUNCTION VALUES

You are given $f(x) = 3x - 2$. Work out $f(4)$ and $f(-1)$. For which argument is the function value 13?

$f(4) = 3 \cdot 4 - 2 = 10$ and $f(-1) = 3 \cdot (-1) - 2 = -5$.

$3x - 2 = 13$ leads to $3x = 15$, so $x = 5$.

$f(4) = 10$, $f(-1) = -5$, and $f(5) = 13$.

EXAMPLE 4 · A CANDLE BURNS DOWN

A candle is 20 cm high and burns down 2 cm every hour. Give the function and a sensible domain and range.

Height after $t$ hours: $h(t) = 20 - 2t$. After 10 hours the candle has burnt down: $h(10) = 0$.

$D$: $0 \leq t \leq 10$ (hours), $W$: $0 \leq h \leq 20$ (cm)

$f(4) = 17$, so €17. $D$: $0 \leq x \leq 8$ (hours), $W$: $5 \leq y \leq 29$ (euros).

You are given $f(x) = x^2 - 1$ with the domain $-2 \leq x \leq 3$. Find the range. Careful: the smallest value is not at the edge.

$f(-2) = 3$, $f(0) = -1$, $f(3) = 8$. The smallest value is $-1$ at $x = 0$, the largest 8 at $x = 3$. $W$: $-1 \leq y \leq 8$.

UNIT 34 LESSONS

3Proportional functions $y = m \cdot x$

A litre of petrol costs €1.80. Then $x$ litres cost $y = 1.8 \cdot x$ euros. Twice as many litres cost twice as much, and for 0 litres you pay nothing. The points of the table lie on a straight line through the origin.

KEY POINT · PROPORTIONAL FUNCTION

A function with the equation $y = m \cdot x$ ($m \neq 0$) is called a proportional function. Its graph is a straight line through the origin $(0, 0)$.

The number $m$ is called the gradient. On the gradient triangle: if you go 1 to the right, it goes $m$ up if $m > 0$, and down if $m < 0$.

For every point except the origin, $m = \dfrac{y}{x}$.

The larger $m$ is without its sign, the steeper the line.

Drawing without a table: start at the origin, 1 to the right and $m$ up. With $m = \tfrac{2}{3}$ it is easier to go 3 to the right and 2 up.

In everyday life $m$ is a price per litre, a speed or an hourly wage: always “so much per unit”.

INTERACTIVE · THE GRADIENT m

Change $m$. Watch the green gradient triangle and the table: if $x$ grows by 1, $y$ changes by $m$.

EXAMPLE 5 · THE GRADIENT FROM ONE POINT

A line through the origin passes through $P(4, 6)$. Give its equation. Does $Q(-2, -3)$ lie on it?

$m = \dfrac{6}{4} = 1.5$, so $y = 1.5x$. For $x = -2$: $1.5 \cdot (-2) = -3$.

$y = 1.5x$, and $Q$ lies on the line.

EXAMPLE 6 · ON THE BIKE

A cyclist rides at a steady $18\,\tfrac{\text{km}}{\text{h}}$. How far does he get in 2.5 hours? How long does he need for 27 km?

Distance after $t$ hours: $s = 18 \cdot t$. $s(2.5) = 45$. From $18t = 27$ it follows that $t = 1.5$.

45 km in 2.5 hours; for 27 km he needs 1.5 hours. The gradient 18 is the speed.

Proportional or not? Table A: $x$: 1, 2, 3, 4 and $y$: 3, 6, 9, 12. Table B: $x$: 1, 2, 3, 4 and $y$: 2, 4, 7, 8. In the proportional case, give the equation.

Connect with Year 6: 5 m of fabric cost €42. Set up the equation of the proportional function. What do 7.5 m cost? Solve with the rule of three as well.

$y = 8.4x$, because $42 : 5 = 8.4$. $8.4 \cdot 7.5 = 63$. Rule of three: 1 m costs €8.40, 7.5 m cost €63.

Tariff A costs €0.09 per minute without a basic fee. Tariff B costs €5 a month, no matter how long you phone. Which assignment minutes → costs is proportional? Draw both graphs for 0 to 100 minutes.

Only A: $y = 0.09x$, a line through the origin. B is a horizontal line at $y = 5$, so a function, but not proportional. At about 56 minutes both cost the same. More on this in chapter 2.3.

A line through the origin crosses the vertical line $x = 4$ at $y = -6$. Give its equation and work out $f(-10)$.

$m = \tfrac{-6}{4} = -1.5$, so $f(x) = -1.5x$ and $f(-10) = 15$.

EXCURSIONHISTORY

A word from Leipzig, a symbol from Saint Petersburg

It was Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz who brought the word function into mathematics. Leibniz was born in Leipzig in 1646 and studied there. From 1673 he called quantities that change from point to point along a curve functiones, from the Latin fungi, “to perform a task”.

The notation $f(x)$ goes back to Leonhard Euler. He worked at the Academy in Saint Petersburg and used the symbol in a paper from the year 1734, printed in 1740. Euler wrote so much that the collection of his works fills more than 70 thick volumes today.

Sources: J. J. O’Connor, E. F. Robertson: Earliest Uses of Function Symbols, MacTutor History of Mathematics (after F. Cajori, A History of Mathematical Notations). L. Euler, Additamentum ad dissertationem de infinitis curvis eiusdem generis, Commentarii Academiae Scientiarum Petropolitanae 7 (1734/35, printed 1740).
