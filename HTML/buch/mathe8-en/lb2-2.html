UNIT 14 LESSONS

1Gradient and y-intercept

At the bike hire, 0, 1, 2, 3 hours cost €5, €8, €11, €14. Every hour €3 is added, and everything starts at €5. The equation is $y = 3x + 5$: the 3 is the gradient, the 5 the value at $x = 0$.

KEY POINT · LINEAR FUNCTION

A function with the equation $y = m \cdot x + n$ is called a linear function. Its graph is a straight line.

$m$ is the gradient: 1 to the right, $m$ up (down if $m$ is negative). $n$ is the y-intercept: the line crosses the y-axis at the point $(0, n)$.

Drawing: plot the point $(0, n)$, find a second point with the gradient triangle, draw the line through both points.

Lines with the same gradient are parallel. For $m = 0$ the line is horizontal: $y = n$.

Special case: proportional functions are linear functions with $n = 0$.

In context $n$ is often a starting value or a basic fee and $m$ the amount added per unit.

INTERACTIVE · CHANGING m AND n

First change only $n$: the line is shifted, its direction stays the same. Then only $m$: the line turns around the white point.

EXAMPLE 1 · DRAWING A LINE

Draw the graph of $y = -0.5x + 3$.

Start at the point $(0, 3)$. The gradient $-0.5$ means: 2 to the right, 1 down. Second point: $(2, 2)$.

The line falls and passes through $(0, 3)$ and $(2, 2)$. Check: $(6, 0)$ lies on it too.

EXAMPLE 2 · READING OFF THE EQUATION

A line crosses the y-axis at $-2$ and passes through $(3, 4)$. What is its equation?

$n = -2$. From $(0, -2)$ to $(3, 4)$: 3 to the right, 6 up, so $m = \tfrac{6}{3} = 2$.

$y = 2x - 2$

2Zeros and the point test

After $t$ hours, the candle from the explorations has the height $h = -1.5t + 15$. It has burnt down when $h = 0$: $-1.5t + 15 = 0$, so $t = 10$. The graph meets the time axis at 10 hours.

KEY POINT · ZERO AND POINT TEST

A zero is an argument $x_0$ with $f(x_0) = 0$. There the graph crosses the x-axis at the point $(x_0, 0)$.

Graphically: read it off the graph. By calculation: solve the equation $0 = m \cdot x + n$.

Point test: the point $P(a, b)$ lies on the graph if $f(a) = b$.

A horizontal line $y = n$ with $n \neq 0$ has no zero.

Reading off is inexact. The calculation gives the exact value, the drawing shows whether it can be right.

Two points on the axes: with the y-axis $(0, n)$, with the x-axis $(x_0, 0)$. Together they are enough for drawing.

FIGURE · THREE LINES, THREE ZEROS

$f(x) = 2x - 3$, $g(x) = -0.5x + 2$ and $h(x) = x + 1$. The white points are the zeros. Check them by calculation.

EXAMPLE 3 · WORKING OUT A ZERO

Work out the zero of $f(x) = 2x - 3$.

$0 = 2x - 3 \quad \mid +3 \qquad 3 = 2x \quad \mid :2 \qquad x_0 = 1.5$

The line crosses the x-axis at $N(1.5, 0)$.

EXAMPLE 4 · POINT TEST

Do $P(6, -1)$ and $Q(-2, 2)$ lie on the graph of $g(x) = -0.5x + 2$?

$g(6) = -3 + 2 = -1$: that is the y-coordinate of $P$.   $g(-2) = 1 + 2 = 3$, not 2.

$P$ lies on the line, $Q$ does not.

UNIT 32 LESSONS

3Setting up equations of functions

A taxi ride over 5 km costs €14, one over 12 km costs €28. For 7 km more you pay €14 more, so €2 per kilometre. How high is the basic fare? The whole line can be found from two points.

KEY POINT · EQUATION FROM TWO POINTS

You are given $P_1(x_1, y_1)$ and $P_2(x_2, y_2)$.

  1. Gradient: $m = \dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$
  2. y-intercept: substitute one point into $y = m \cdot x + n$ and solve for $n$.
  3. Write down the equation and check it with the other point.

If a point and the gradient are given, you start straight away with step 2.

The order does not matter: $\tfrac{y_1 - y_2}{x_1 - x_2}$ gives the same gradient. Only the order at the top and at the bottom must be the same.

Vertically above each other ($x_1 = x_2$): no linear function passes through such points.

INTERACTIVE · A LINE THROUGH TWO POINTS

Drag the two points. The green triangle shows $\Delta x$ and $\Delta y$; below are the gradient, the y-intercept and the equation.

EXAMPLE 5 · THE TAXI

5 km cost €14, 12 km cost €28. Find the equation distance → price.

$m = \dfrac{28 - 14}{12 - 5} = \dfrac{14}{7} = 2$. Substituting $(5, 14)$: $14 = 2 \cdot 5 + n$, so $n = 4$.

$y = 2x + 4$: basic fare €4, plus €2 per kilometre. Check: $2 \cdot 12 + 4 = 28$.

EXAMPLE 6 · POINT AND GRADIENT

A line has the gradient $-3$ and passes through $P(2, 1)$. What is its equation?

$1 = -3 \cdot 2 + n$, so $1 = -6 + n$ and $n = 7$.

$y = -3x + 7$

On an electricity tariff you pay €82 for 200 kWh a month and €117 for 300 kWh. Find the equation. How high are the standing charge and the price per kWh?

$m = \tfrac{35}{100} = 0.35$, $82 = 0.35 \cdot 200 + n$, so $n = 12$. $y = 0.35x + 12$: standing charge €12, €0.35 per kWh. That is the tariff from the opener.

Do the points $A(-1, 1)$, $B(1, 5)$ and $C(4, 11)$ lie on a straight line? Give a reason with a calculation.

Line through $A$ and $B$: $m = 2$, $n = 3$, so $y = 2x + 3$. Point test with $C$: $2 \cdot 4 + 3 = 11$. Yes.

2 hours after being lit, a candle is 21 cm high, after 5 hours only 12 cm. How high was it at the start? When has it burnt down?

$m = \tfrac{12 - 21}{5 - 2} = -3$, $21 = -6 + n$, $n = 27$: $h = -3t + 27$. 27 cm at the start, burnt down after 9 hours.

EXCURSIONGRADIENT IN EVERYDAY LIFE

The steepest street in the world

On road signs the gradient is given as a percentage. 12 % means: over 100 m of horizontal distance it goes 12 m up, so $m = 0.12$.

Baldwin Street in Dunedin, New Zealand, is considered the steepest street in the world, with 34.8 % on its steepest stretch of 10 m. In 2019 it lost the title to Ffordd Pen Llech in Harlech, Wales, which had been measured at 37.45 %. A surveyor from Dunedin lodged an appeal: the Welsh street makes a bend, and it had been measured at the inner edge, where it is steepest. In the middle of the street it is only 28.6 %. Since 2020 the measurement has been taken in the middle of the street, and Baldwin Street holds the record again.

With $m = 0.348$ this means: over 10 m of horizontal distance it goes almost 3.5 m up.

Source: Guinness World Records, “Baldwin Street in New Zealand reinstated as the world’s steepest street”, 8 April 2020.
