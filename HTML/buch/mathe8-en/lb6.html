UNIT 14 LESSONS

1Living costs

An advert: “2 rooms, 62 m², basic rent €465, service charges €155.” You pay €620 a month, €7440 a year. On top of that come electricity and internet, which you settle with the providers yourself.

KEY POINT · RENT AND BILLS

Basic rent + service charges (heating, water, rubbish, cleaning of the building, insurance …) = total rent. Price per square metre = basic rent : living space.

Electricity, gas, water: costs = standing charge + unit price · consumption. You pay monthly instalments. The annual bill results in a back payment or a credit.

VAT (value added tax): usually 19 %, on food and books 7 %. Gross price = net price · 1.19.

Meter reading: consumption = new reading − old reading.

Checking a bill: are the meter readings, the period, the prices and the total right? Bills from companies sometimes contain mistakes too.

INTERACTIVE · THE ELECTRICITY BILL

Change the consumption, the prices and the instalment. Are the instalments enough, or is there a back payment?

EXAMPLE 1 · THE FLAT

A flat has 62 m². The basic rent is €7.50 per m², the service charges €2.50 per m². Work out the total rent and the yearly costs.

Basic rent: $62 \cdot 7.50\,\text{€} = 465\,\text{€}$, service charges: $62 \cdot 2.50\,\text{€} = 155\,\text{€}$.

Total rent €620 a month, €7440 a year.

EXAMPLE 2 · THE ANNUAL BILL

Old meter reading 23,418 kWh, new 25,968 kWh. Unit price €0.32 per kWh, standing charge €12.50 a month, instalment €70 a month.

Consumption 2550 kWh. Costs: $2550 \cdot 0.32\,\text{€} + 12 \cdot 12.50\,\text{€} = 816\,\text{€} + 150\,\text{€} = 966\,\text{€}$. Paid: $12 \cdot 70\,\text{€} = 840\,\text{€}$.

Back payment €126. A suitable new instalment would be $966\,\text{€} : 12 \approx 81\,\text{€}$.

Basic rent €520, heating costs €85, water €40, other service charges €60. How high is the total rent?

a) A device costs €250 net. How much does it cost with 19 % VAT?   b) An article costs €59.50 gross. How high is the net price?

a) $250 \cdot 1.19 = 297.50$, so €297.50   b) $59.50 : 1.19 = 50$, so €50

A family has used 95 m³ of water. Fresh water costs €2.20 per m³, waste water €2.80 per m³, plus a standing charge of €8 a month. They paid 12 instalments of €45. How does the bill turn out?

$95 \cdot 5.00\,\text{€} + 12 \cdot 8\,\text{€} = 475\,\text{€} + 96\,\text{€} = 571\,\text{€}$. Paid €540: back payment €31.

Flat A has 48 m² for €432 basic rent, flat B 65 m² for €552.50 basic rent. Which is cheaper per square metre?

A: $432 : 48 = 9.00$, so €9.00 per m². B: $552.50 : 65 = 8.50$, so €8.50 per m². B is cheaper.

Check this bill: three articles at €12.40, €8.90 and €25.00 (net), “total net €46.30, VAT 19 % €8.80, amount due €56.10”.

The net amount is right: €46.30. VAT: $46.30 \cdot 0.19 \approx 8.80$, right. But $46.30 + 8.80 = 55.10$, not 56.10. The amount due is €1 too high.

UNIT 24 LESSONS

2Building costs

A room is 4.20 m long and 3.50 m wide and gets new laminate. Because pieces are sawn off at the edges, the exact area is not enough: you add about 10 % for waste. And you can only buy whole packs.

KEY POINT · MATERIALS AND COSTS

Floor area = length · width. Other floor plans are split into rectangles. Because of waste you buy 5 to 10 % more.

Wall area = perimeter of the room · room height − areas of windows and doors.

Material needed = area · amount needed per square metre. Packs, tins and sacks only come whole: round up.

You work out enclosed space and capacity as the volume of a cuboid or a cylinder: $1\,\text{m}^3 = 1000\,\ell$.

Rounding up: 7.3 packs means 8 packs. Anyone who rounds down ends up with a gap in the floor.

Tiles are counted in pieces: a tile of 30 cm × 60 cm has $0.18\,\text{m}^2$.

INTERACTIVE · RENOVATING THE ROOM

EXAMPLE 3 · LAMINATE

How many packs of laminate of 2.22 m² each do you need for the room of 4.20 m × 3.50 m with 10 % waste? A pack costs €21.90.

$4.2 \cdot 3.5 = 14.7\,\text{m}^2$, with waste $14.7 \cdot 1.1 = 16.17\,\text{m}^2$. $16.17 : 2.22 \approx 7.3$, so 8 packs.

8 packs for $8 \cdot 21.90\,\text{€} = 175.20\,\text{€}$.

EXAMPLE 4 · THE GARDEN POOL

Inside, a rectangular pool is 3 m long, 2 m wide and 1.20 m deep. It is filled to a height of 80 cm. How much water is that? What does it cost at €5 per m³?

Capacity: $3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1.2 = 7.2\,\text{m}^3 = 7200\,\ell$. Filled: $3 \cdot 2 \cdot 0.8 = 4.8\,\text{m}^3$.

4800 litres of water for $4.8 \cdot 5\,\text{€} = 24\,\text{€}$.

EXERCISES

A room is 5 m × 4 m. Carpet costs €18 per m². What does the carpet cost without and with 10 % waste?

$20\,\text{m}^2 \cdot 18\,\text{€} = 360\,\text{€}$. With waste: $22\,\text{m}^2 \cdot 18\,\text{€} = 396\,\text{€}$.

An L-shaped room consists of a rectangle 6 m × 4 m and an attached rectangle 2 m × 3 m. How big is its area?

$24\,\text{m}^2 + 6\,\text{m}^2 = 30\,\text{m}^2$

A room is 4 m × 3 m and 2.50 m high, with one door (2 m²) and two windows (1.5 m² each). The walls get two coats, 1 litre of paint covers 6 m². A 10 ℓ tin costs €49, a 5 ℓ tin €29. What do you buy?

Wall area $14 \cdot 2.5 - 2 - 3 = 30\,\text{m}^2$, twice: $60\,\text{m}^2$, so 10 ℓ. One 10 ℓ tin for €49 is cheaper than two small ones for €58.

A bathroom wall of 2.40 m × 2 m is covered with tiles of 30 cm × 60 cm. How many tiles do you need if you add 10 % for breakage and cutting?

$4.8 : 0.18 \approx 26.7$. With 10 %: $26.7 \cdot 1.1 \approx 29.3$, so 30 tiles.

A room of 20 m² gets a screed layer 5 cm thick. A sack of screed (40 kg) makes 20 litres of finished screed and costs €5.49. How many sacks do you need, what do they cost?

Volume $20\,\text{m}^2 \cdot 0.05\,\text{m} = 1\,\text{m}^3 = 1000\,\ell$. $1000 : 20 = 50$ sacks for $50 \cdot 5.49\,\text{€} = 274.50\,\text{€}$.

UNIT 34 LESSONS

3Comparing offers

The mobile phone costs €799 straight away or 24 times €36.99. In instalments you pay $24 \cdot 36.99\,\text{€} = 887.76\,\text{€}$, so €88.76 more. This amount is the price for paying the money only later.

KEY POINT · DISCOUNT, CASH DISCOUNT, INSTALMENTS, INTEREST

A discount is a price reduction in per cent. With a 15 % discount you pay 85 %: new price = old price · 0.85.

A cash discount (German “Skonto”) is a reduction (usually 2 to 3 %) if you pay within a few days.

Buying in instalments: total price = down payment + number of instalments · instalment. The difference from the cash price is the price of the credit.

Interest for one year: $Z = K \cdot \tfrac{p}{100}$. With compound interest a capital grows in $n$ years to $K_n = K \cdot \left(1 + \tfrac{p}{100}\right)^n$.

Several reductions: 10 % discount and then 2 % cash discount give the factor $0.9 \cdot 0.98 = 0.882$, so a reduction of 11.8 %, not 12 %.

Interest for months: $Z = K \cdot \tfrac{p}{100} \cdot \tfrac{\text{months}}{12}$.

INTERACTIVE · INTEREST AND COMPOUND INTEREST

Change the starting capital, the interest rate and the term. Compare the bars: with compound interest the money grows faster and faster.

EXAMPLE 5 · WHAT DO THE INSTALMENTS COST?

What percentage of the cash price of €799 do the 24 instalments of €36.99 cost extra?

Extra costs $887.76\,\text{€} - 799\,\text{€} = 88.76\,\text{€}$. Share: $88.76 : 799 \approx 0.111$.

The instalments cost about 11 % more than the cash price.

EXAMPLE 6 · A SAVINGS PLAN

€2000 are invested for 5 years at 3 %, the interest stays in the account. How much money is there at the end? How much would it be if the interest were paid out every year?

With compound interest: $2000\,\text{€} \cdot 1.03^5 \approx 2318.55\,\text{€}$. Without: $5 \cdot 60\,\text{€} = 300\,\text{€}$ interest, €2300 together.

Compound interest brings €18.55 more.

EXERCISES

A jacket costs €120. There is a 25 % discount, and for immediate payment another 2 % cash discount. What do you pay?

$120 \cdot 0.75 = 90$, so €90, then $90 \cdot 0.98 = 88.20$, so €88.20.

€1500 are invested at 2.5 %. How much interest is there after one year, how much after 8 months?

One year: $1500 \cdot 0.025 = 37.50$, so €37.50. 8 months: $37.50 \cdot \tfrac{8}{12} = 25$, so €25.

A television costs €650. For cash payment there is a 3 % cash discount. When buying in instalments you pay €50 down and then 12 instalments of €54. How big is the difference?

Cash: $650 \cdot 0.97 = 630.50$, so €630.50. Instalments: $50 + 12 \cdot 54 = 698$, so €698. Difference €67.50.

For €5000 there are two offers over 3 years: A with 2.5 % interest a year and compound interest, B without interest but with a 7 % bonus at the end. Which is better?

A: $5000 \cdot 1.025^3 \approx 5384.45$, so €5384.45. B: $5000 \cdot 1.07 = 5350$, so €5350. A brings €34.45 more.

After how many years does a capital double at 6 % interest with compound interest? Try it out with the calculator. Compare with the rule of thumb “72 divided by the interest rate”.

$1.06^{11} \approx 1.90$ and $1.06^{12} \approx 2.01$: after 12 years. The rule of thumb: $72 : 6 = 12$.

REVIEW · DEEPEN · CONNECT

A trainee gets €950 a month. She pays €380 rent, €15 for the mobile phone, €49 for the travel pass and €220 for food. How much is left? What percentage of the money goes on rent?

$950 - 664 = 286$, so €286 is left. Rent: $380 : 950 = 0.4 = 40\,\%$.

Connect with chapter 2.3: electricity tariff A costs €9 a month and €0.36 per kWh, tariff B €15 a month and €0.31 per kWh. From which yearly consumption does B pay off?

Per year: A $108 + 0.36x$, B $180 + 0.31x$. Setting equal: $0.05x = 72$, $x = 1440$. At 1440 kWh a year both cost the same, above that B pays off.

Connect with chapter 3.2: a round garden pool has an inner diameter of 3.60 m and is filled to a height of 1 m. How much water do you need, what does it cost at €4.50 per m³?

$V = \pi \cdot 1.8^2 \cdot 1\,\text{m}^3 \approx 10.18\,\text{m}^3$. Costs: $10.18 \cdot 4.50\,\text{€} \approx 45.80\,\text{€}$.

A loan of €3000 is paid back in 12 monthly instalments of €262.50. What percentage of the loan amount does it cost? Why is the loan actually more expensive than this number suggests?

$12 \cdot 262.50 = 3150$, so €150 or 5 % of the loan amount. But you have not borrowed €3000 for the whole year: every month a part is paid back, on average you only owe about half. For so little borrowed money, €150 is a lot.

EXCURSIONA STORY FROM SAXONY

“According to Adam Riese”: arithmetic for everyday life in the Ore Mountains

Adam Ries (1492 to 1559) lived as a master of arithmetic in Annaberg in the Ore Mountains. His arithmetic books were written in German, not in Latin, and they calculated exactly what merchants, craftsmen and miners needed: prices, weights, profit and loss, interest and sharing a profit among several people.

His second arithmetic book of 1522, Rechenung auff der linihen und federn, was printed again and again until the 17th century. The German saying “nach Adam Riese” (“according to Adam Riese”), used when a calculation is certainly right, goes back to him. In Annaberg-Buchholz you can visit his house as a museum today.

Sources: A. Ries, Rechenung auff der linihen und federn, Erfurt 1522. Adam Ries Museum, Annaberg-Buchholz.
