Sine, cosine and tangent

After take-off an aeroplane climbs at an angle of 12°. How high is it after 3 km of flight? Pythagoras’ theorem does not help here, it knows no angles. Sine, cosine and tangent connect angles and side lengths in a right triangle. With them and the calculator you work out every side and every angle.

UNIT 14 LESSONS

1Ratios of sides in a right triangle

All right triangles with an angle of $30^\circ$ agree in two angles. By the main similarity theorem from Year 8 they are similar. That is why they have the same ratios of sides: the side opposite the $30^\circ$ angle is always exactly half as long as the longest side.

KEY POINT · SINE, COSINE, TANGENT

For an acute angle $\alpha$ in a right triangle the opposite side lies opposite $\alpha$, the adjacent side lies next to $\alpha$. The hypotenuse lies opposite the right angle.

$\sin \alpha = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}} \qquad \cos \alpha = \dfrac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}} \qquad \tan \alpha = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$

These ratios depend only on the angle, not on the size of the triangle. The calculator knows them when it is set to DEG.

In short: SOH CAH TOA. Sine is O over H, cosine is A over H, tangent is O over A.

Range of values: for acute angles sine and cosine lie between 0 and 1, because the hypotenuse is the longest side. The tangent can be any positive number.

INTERACTIVE · THREE RATIOS

Change the angle and the size of the triangle. The sides change, the ratios stay the same as long as the angle stays the same.

EXAMPLE 1 · THE 3-4-5 TRIANGLE

A triangle has the right angle at $C$ and the sides $a = 3\,\text{cm}$, $b = 4\,\text{cm}$, $c = 5\,\text{cm}$. State $\sin \alpha$, $\cos \alpha$ and $\tan \alpha$.

For $\alpha$, $a$ is the opposite side and $b$ the adjacent side: $\sin \alpha = \tfrac{3}{5} = 0.6$, $\cos \alpha = \tfrac{4}{5} = 0.8$, $\tan \alpha = \tfrac{3}{4} = 0.75$.

For $\beta$ it is exactly the other way round: $\sin \beta = 0.8$, $\cos \beta = 0.6$.

EXAMPLE 2 · VALUES FROM THE CALCULATOR

Find $\sin 30^\circ$, $\cos 60^\circ$, $\tan 45^\circ$ and $\sin 70^\circ$.

$\sin 30^\circ = 0.5$, $\cos 60^\circ = 0.5$, $\tan 45^\circ = 1$, $\sin 70^\circ \approx 0.940$.

With the $45^\circ$ angle both legs are equally long, which is why the tangent is 1.

EXERCISES

A triangle $ABC$ has the right angle at $C$. Name the opposite and the adjacent side for the angle $\alpha$ and for the angle $\beta$.

For $\alpha$: opposite $a$, adjacent $b$. For $\beta$: opposite $b$, adjacent $a$. The hypotenuse is $c$ in both cases.

Find with the calculator to three decimal places: $\sin 25^\circ$, $\cos 25^\circ$, $\tan 25^\circ$, $\tan 80^\circ$.

$0.423$; $0.906$; $0.466$; $5.671$

A right triangle has the legs $a = 8\,\text{cm}$ and $b = 15\,\text{cm}$. Calculate the hypotenuse and then $\sin \alpha$, $\cos \alpha$ and $\tan \alpha$.

$c = 17$ cm. $\sin \alpha = \tfrac{8}{17} \approx 0.471$, $\cos \alpha = \tfrac{15}{17} \approx 0.882$, $\tan \alpha = \tfrac{8}{15} \approx 0.533$.

Explain: in a right triangle $\sin \alpha = \cos \beta$. Check it with $\alpha = 20^\circ$.

The opposite side of $\alpha$ is the adjacent side of $\beta$, the hypotenuse is the same. $\sin 20^\circ \approx 0.342$ and $\cos 70^\circ \approx 0.342$.

Show that $\tan \alpha = \dfrac{\sin \alpha}{\cos \alpha}$. Hint: write both sides with $a$, $b$ and $c$.

$\dfrac{\sin \alpha}{\cos \alpha} = \dfrac{a/c}{b/c} = \dfrac{a}{c} \cdot \dfrac{c}{b} = \dfrac{a}{b} = \tan \alpha$

UNIT 24 LESSONS

2Calculating sides and angles

For the aeroplane the flight distance of 3 km is the hypotenuse, the height we are looking for is the opposite side of the climbing angle $12^\circ$. From $\sin 12^\circ = \tfrac{h}{3\,\text{km}}$ we get $h = 3\,\text{km} \cdot \sin 12^\circ \approx 0.624\,\text{km}$. The aeroplane is about 624 m high.

KEY POINT · CALCULATING SIDES AND ANGLES

Side wanted: choose the function that contains the given and the wanted side, and rearrange. For example $a = c \cdot \sin \alpha$ or $c = \dfrac{a}{\sin \alpha}$.

Angle wanted: form the ratio of two sides and find the angle with the inverse key: $\alpha = \sin^{-1}\left(\tfrac{a}{c}\right)$, in the same way with $\cos^{-1}$ and $\tan^{-1}$.

The third angle is $90^\circ - \alpha$, and Pythagoras’ theorem also gives the third side.

Inverse key: usually it is called SHIFT sin or 2nd sin and shows $\sin^{-1}$.

Check: the hypotenuse is the longest side. The larger leg lies opposite the larger angle.

INTERACTIVE · SOLVING THE TRIANGLE

Choose what is given and set the values. Every step of the calculation is shown.

EXAMPLE 3 · THE KITE

A kite string is 50 m long and makes an angle of $55^\circ$ with the ground. The hand holds the string at a height of 1 m. How high is the kite flying?

The string is the hypotenuse, the height above the hand is the opposite side: $50\,\text{m} \cdot \sin 55^\circ \approx 40.96\,\text{m}$. Add the hand at a height of 1 m: $40.96\,\text{m} + 1\,\text{m} \approx 42\,\text{m}$.

The kite is flying about 42 m high.

EXAMPLE 4 · THE ANGLE OF THE RAMP

A ramp rises 1 m over a horizontal length of 12 m. What is its angle of inclination?

Opposite side 1 m, adjacent side 12 m: $\tan \alpha = \tfrac{1}{12} \approx 0.0833$, so $\alpha = \tan^{-1}(0.0833)$.

$\alpha \approx 4.8^\circ$

EXERCISES

Right angle at $C$, $c = 10\,\text{cm}$, $\alpha = 30^\circ$. Calculate $a$ and $b$.

$a = 10 \cdot \sin 30^\circ = 5$ cm, $b = 10 \cdot \cos 30^\circ \approx 8.66$ cm

Right angle at $C$, $a = 4\,\text{cm}$, $b = 7\,\text{cm}$. Calculate $\alpha$ and $\beta$.

$\tan \alpha = \tfrac{4}{7}$, $\alpha \approx 29.7^\circ$, $\beta \approx 60.3^\circ$

A 6 m long ladder stands 1.80 m away from the wall. What angle does it make with the ground? An angle between $65^\circ$ and $75^\circ$ counts as safe. How high does it reach?

$\cos \alpha = \tfrac{1.8}{6} = 0.3$, $\alpha \approx 72.5^\circ$: safe. Height: $6 \cdot \sin 72.5^\circ \approx 5.72$ m.

At a horizontal distance of 15 km an aeroplane is to have reached a height of 1200 m. At what angle must it climb on average?

$\tan \alpha = \tfrac{1.2}{15} = 0.08$, $\alpha \approx 4.6^\circ$

Right angle at $C$, $b = 9\,\text{cm}$, $\beta = 52^\circ$. Calculate all the missing sides and angles and the area.

$\alpha = 38^\circ$. For $\beta$, $b$ is the opposite side: $a = \tfrac{b}{\tan \beta} \approx 7.03$ cm, $c = \tfrac{b}{\sin \beta} \approx 11.42$ cm. Area $\tfrac{a \cdot b}{2} \approx 31.6\,\text{cm}^2$.

We know $\sin 30^\circ = 0.5$. How long is the side opposite a $30^\circ$ angle if the hypotenuse is 14 cm long?

7 cm

Connecting with chapter 1.1: right angle at $C$, $a = 6\,\text{cm}$, $c = 10\,\text{cm}$. Calculate $b$ once with Pythagoras’ theorem and once via $\alpha$ and the cosine.

Pythagoras: $b = \sqrt{100 - 36} = 8$ cm. Via the angle: $\alpha = \sin^{-1}(0.6) \approx 36.87^\circ$, $b = 10 \cdot \cos 36.87^\circ \approx 8$ cm.

Connecting with Year 8: the sun is $35^\circ$ above the horizon. A tree casts a 20 m long shadow. How tall is it?

$h = 20\,\text{m} \cdot \tan 35^\circ \approx 14.0\,\text{m}$

An isosceles triangle has a 10 cm long base and base angles of $70^\circ$. Calculate the height and the length of the equal sides.

The height halves the base: $h = 5 \cdot \tan 70^\circ \approx 13.74$ cm, equal sides $\tfrac{5}{\cos 70^\circ} \approx 14.62$ cm.

EXCURSIONHISTORY

How the sine got its name

Indian astronomers were already calculating with half chords in a circle 1500 years ago. Around the year 500 the scholar Aryabhata called them jya, the bowstring. Arab scholars took over the word as jiba and, as is usual in Arabic, wrote it without vowels.

When Arabic books were translated into Latin in Spain in the 12th century, the word was read as jaib, “bay” or “bosom”, and translated with the Latin word for bay: sinus. That is how a word came to us that no longer has anything to do with its original meaning.

Sources: Aryabhata, Aryabhatiya (499). V. J. Katz, A History of Mathematics, 3rd edition, Addison-Wesley 2009, chapters 6 and 8.
