Right triangles in everyday life

A road sign warns: 12 % gradient. A ladder is to stand safely against the wall of a house. A roof needs new tiles. Behind all of this there is a right triangle, sometimes well hidden. In this chapter you learn to find such triangles in everyday life, to calculate them with Pythagoras, sine, cosine and tangent and to split difficult problems into smaller problems.

UNIT 14 LESSONS

1Gradient and angle of inclination

A 12 % gradient means: over 100 m of horizontal distance the road goes up 12 m. That is a right triangle with the legs 100 m and 12 m. The gradient is the ratio of the opposite to the adjacent side, so it is exactly the tangent of the angle of inclination: $\tan \alpha = 0.12$, $\alpha \approx 6.8^\circ$.

KEY POINT · GRADIENT IN PERCENT

Gradient $= \dfrac{\text{difference in height}}{\text{horizontal distance}}$, given in percent. A gradient of $p\,\%$ means $p$ m of height over 100 m horizontally.

Angle of inclination: $\tan \alpha = \dfrac{p}{100}$, so $\alpha = \tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{p}{100}\right)$. The other way round: $p = 100 \cdot \tan \alpha$.

The road itself is the hypotenuse. It is a little longer than the horizontal distance that a map shows.

100 % is $45^\circ$: the road goes up exactly as far as it goes forward. Only stairs, climbing walls and mountain railways are steeper than 100 %.

Not proportional: double the percentage does not give double the angle. With small gradients you hardly notice it, with large ones clearly.

INTERACTIVE · PERCENT AND ANGLE

Move the gradient. Compare 10 % with 20 % and 50 % with 100 %: how does the angle change?

EXAMPLE 1 · FROM THE SIGN TO THE ANGLE

A road has an 8 % gradient. How large is the angle of inclination?

$\tan \alpha = \tfrac{8}{100} = 0.08$, so $\alpha = \tan^{-1}(0.08)$.

$\alpha \approx 4.6^\circ$

EXAMPLE 2 · THE WHEELCHAIR RAMP

A ramp for wheelchairs may have a gradient of at most 6 %. It is to overcome a height of 0.9 m. How long must it be horizontally at least? How long is the ramp itself?

Horizontally: $0.9\,\text{m} : 0.06 = 15\,\text{m}$. Ramp: $\sqrt{15^2 + 0.9^2} = \sqrt{225.81} \approx 15.03\,\text{m}$.

At least 15 m horizontally, the ramp is about 15.03 m long.

EXERCISES

Calculate the gradient in percent: a) 30 m of height over 500 m horizontally   b) 4 m over 25 m   c) 120 m over 1.5 km

a) $\tfrac{30}{500} = 6\,\%$   b) $\tfrac{4}{25} = 16\,\%$   c) $\tfrac{120}{1500} = 8\,\%$

Calculate the angle of inclination to one decimal place: a) 10 %   b) 25 %   c) 100 %

a) $\tan^{-1}(0.1) \approx 5.7^\circ$   b) $\tan^{-1}(0.25) \approx 14.0^\circ$   c) $45^\circ$

A railway line climbs at an angle of $3^\circ$. What gradient in percent is that?

$p = 100 \cdot \tan 3^\circ \approx 100 \cdot 0.0524 \approx 5.2\,\%$

A road with a 9 % gradient is 2.4 km long on the map (measured horizontally). How large is the difference in height? How long is the road really?

Height: $0.09 \cdot 2400\,\text{m} = 216\,\text{m}$. Road: $\sqrt{2400^2 + 216^2} \approx 2410\,\text{m}$, so about 2.41 km.

Show with the calculator: from 10 % to 20 % the angle almost doubles, but from 50 % to 100 % it does not. Explain the difference.

$10\,\% \to 5.71^\circ$, $20\,\% \to 11.31^\circ$: almost double. $50\,\% \to 26.57^\circ$, $100\,\% \to 45^\circ$: clearly less than double. The tangent does not grow in proportion to the angle, for large angles it grows faster and faster.

2Ladder, roof and guy rope

A mast is secured with a rope. The rope is fixed at a height of 8 m and makes an angle of $60^\circ$ with the ground. Mast, ground and rope form a right triangle. The rope is the hypotenuse: $s = \tfrac{8\,\text{m}}{\sin 60^\circ} \approx 9.24\,\text{m}$. On the ground it is anchored $\tfrac{8\,\text{m}}{\tan 60^\circ} \approx 4.62\,\text{m}$ away from the mast.

KEY POINT · WORD PROBLEMS WITH RIGHT TRIANGLES
  1. Make a sketch and look for the right triangle. If there is none, draw in a helper line, for example a height.
  2. Mark the given and the wanted quantities: which side is the hypotenuse, which the opposite or the adjacent side?
  3. Choose the fitting relation: Pythagoras’ theorem, sine, cosine or tangent.
  4. Calculate, round sensibly, write an answer sentence.

The angle of elevation and the angle of depression are always measured from the horizontal: upwards to a higher point, downwards to a lower point.

Smaller problems: an isosceles triangle falls into two equal right triangles along its height. A trapezium falls into a rectangle and two triangles.

Eye level: whoever measures an angle of elevation is not standing on the ground. The eye level is added to the calculated height.

INTERACTIVE · THE GABLE ROOF

Change the width of the house, the pitch of the roof and the length of the house. The height of the ridge divides the gable into two right triangles.

EXAMPLE 3 · THE ROOF

A house is 9 m wide and 12 m long, the gable roof has a pitch of $40^\circ$. Calculate the height of the ridge, the length of the rafters and the roof area (without overhang).

Half the width, 4.5 m, as the adjacent side: $h = 4.5 \cdot \tan 40^\circ \approx 3.78$ m, $s = \tfrac{4.5}{\cos 40^\circ} \approx 5.87$ m.

Roof area: $2 \cdot 5.87 \cdot 12 \approx 141\,\text{m}^2$

EXAMPLE 4 · THE ANGLE OF ELEVATION

Lea stands 50 m from the foot of a tower and sees the top at an angle of elevation of $32^\circ$. Her eyes are 1.60 m above the ground. How tall is the tower?

Part above eye level: $50 \cdot \tan 32^\circ \approx 31.24$ m. Add the eye level: $31.24 + 1.60 = 32.84$ m.

The tower is about 32.8 m tall.

EXERCISES

A 5 m long ladder leans against a wall. Its foot stands 1.40 m away from the wall. What angle does it make with the ground? How high does it reach?

$\cos \alpha = \tfrac{1.4}{5} = 0.28$, $\alpha \approx 73.7^\circ$ (safe, between $65^\circ$ and $75^\circ$). Height: $\sqrt{25 - 1.96} = 4.8$ m.

A tent pole is 2.50 m high. A guy rope from its top to the ground makes an angle of $50^\circ$ with the ground. How long is the rope? How far from the pole is the tent peg?

Rope: $\tfrac{2.5}{\sin 50^\circ} \approx 3.26$ m. Distance: $\tfrac{2.5}{\tan 50^\circ} \approx 2.10$ m.

A gable is an isosceles triangle with an 8 m base and 5 m long equal sides. Calculate the height and the base angles.

The height halves the base: $h = \sqrt{5^2 - 4^2} = 3$ m. $\cos \alpha = \tfrac45$, $\alpha \approx 36.9^\circ$.

From the top of a 40 m high lighthouse you see a boat at an angle of depression of $8^\circ$. How far is the boat from the foot of the lighthouse?

The angle of depression at the top is as large as the angle of elevation at the boat. $d = \tfrac{40}{\tan 8^\circ} \approx 285$ m.

A dam is 4 m wide at the top and 3 m high. Both embankments slope at $35^\circ$. How wide is the dam at the bottom? How large is its cross-section?

Split the trapezium into a rectangle and two right triangles.

Each embankment is $\tfrac{3}{\tan 35^\circ} \approx 4.28$ m wide horizontally. At the bottom: $4 + 2 \cdot 4.28 \approx 12.57$ m. Area: $\tfrac{4 + 12.57}{2} \cdot 3 \approx 24.9\,\text{m}^2$.

REVIEW · DEEPEN · CONNECT

A ramp with a 6 % gradient is to overcome a height of 0.5 m. How long must it be horizontally?

$0.5 : 0.06 \approx 8.3$ m

Connecting with chapter 1.1: a rectangle is 6 m long and 2.5 m wide. How long is the diagonal? What angle does it make with the long side?

$d = \sqrt{36 + 6.25} = 6.5$ m. $\tan \alpha = \tfrac{2.5}{6}$, $\alpha \approx 22.6^\circ$.

A slide is 4.20 m long and slopes at $35^\circ$. How high is its top end? How much room does it need on the ground?

Height: $4.2 \cdot \sin 35^\circ \approx 2.41$ m. On the ground: $4.2 \cdot \cos 35^\circ \approx 3.44$ m.

A road with a 7 % gradient is 3 km long, measured on the road itself. How large is the difference in height? Compare with the simple calculation $0.07 \cdot 3000\,\text{m}$.

$\alpha = \tan^{-1}(0.07) \approx 4.00^\circ$, $h = 3000 \cdot \sin 4.00^\circ \approx 209$ m. The simple calculation gives 210 m. With small gradients the difference between the road and the horizontal distance is tiny.

EXCURSIONRECORDS

The steepest routes in the world

The Pilatus Railway in Switzerland has been running up Mount Pilatus near Lucerne since 1889. It is considered the steepest cogwheel railway in the world: at its steepest point it has a 48 % gradient. An ordinary train would slide there. The railway grips a rack between the rails with two horizontal cogwheels.

According to the Guinness Book of Records, the steepest residential street in the world is Baldwin Street in Dunedin, New Zealand, with a 34.8 % gradient. Once a year people race up it, and in the past thousands of chocolate balls rolled down the street in a race for a good cause.

Work it out: which angles of inclination belong to 48 % and to 34.8 %?

$\tan^{-1}(0.48) \approx 25.6^\circ$ and $\tan^{-1}(0.348) \approx 19.2^\circ$. So even the record routes are flatter than $30^\circ$.

Sources: Pilatus-Bahnen AG, facts about the cogwheel railway (opened 1889, maximum gradient 48 %). Guinness World Records, “Steepest street” (Baldwin Street, Dunedin, confirmed in 2020 with 34.8 %).
