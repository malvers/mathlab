Functions and linear functions

The price depends on the call time, the length of a candle on how long it has burned, the costs on the number of items. A function describes such dependencies unambiguously. The simplest ones are the linear functions: their graph is a straight line, and two numbers tell you everything about them.

UNIT 13 LESSONS

1The idea of a function

A candle is 20 cm long and burns down 2.5 cm every hour. After $t$ hours it is still $h(t) = 20 - 2.5\,t$ centimetres long. Every burning time has exactly one length. Conversely, you can read off the burning time from the length.

The same function can be represented in four ways: in words as above, as a table of values, as a term $h(t) = 20 - 2.5\,t$ and as a graph. The candle only burns as long as it is there: sensible values are times from 0 to 8 hours and lengths from 0 to 20 cm.

KEY POINT · FUNCTION, DOMAIN AND RANGE

A function $f$ assigns to every element $x$ of a set $D$ exactly one element $y = f(x)$.

$D$ is called the domain, the set of all function values is called the range $W$. Notation: $D = \{\, x \in \mathbb{R} \mid x \geq -4 \,\}$, read “all real $x$ for which $x \geq -4$”.

Numbers for which the term is not defined are excluded: denominator equal to zero, a negative number under the root. In a real-life context only sensible values count.

Vertical line test: a curve is the graph of a function if and only if every parallel to the $y$-axis cuts it at most once.

Notation: $f(x) = 2x + 1$, for short $y = 2x + 1$ or $f\colon x \mapsto 2x + 1$.

Table of values on the graphing calculator: enter the term, set the start value and step size, show the table.

INTERACTIVE · FUNCTION OR NOT?

Six curves, one vertical line. A function allows at most one point for every $x$.

EXAMPLE 1 · DOMAIN

State the largest possible domain: $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x - 3}$ and $g(x) = \sqrt{x + 2}$.

$f$: the denominator must not become zero, so $x \neq 3$: $D_f = \mathbb{R} \setminus \{3\}$.

$g$: under the root we need $x + 2 \geq 0$: $D_g = \{\, x \in \mathbb{R} \mid x \geq -2 \,\}$.

A water tank holds 600 litres. A pump removes 40 litres per minute. State a term, $D$ and $W$. When is the tank half empty?

$V(t) = 600 - 40t$, $D = \{\, t \mid 0 \leq t \leq 15 \,\}$, $W = \{\, V \mid 0 \leq V \leq 600 \,\}$. Half empty: $600 - 40t = 300 \Rightarrow t = 7.5$ min.

A car park charges €2 for every hour or part of an hour, but no more than €12 a day. Sketch the graph for $0 < t \leq 24$. Is this a function? State $D$ and $W$.

UNIT 23 LESSONS

2Linear functions

Tariff A from the exploration costs $K_A(x) = 0.09\,x + 10$ euros for $x$ minutes. Every minute costs the same amount more, so all the points lie on a straight line. The €10 monthly fee is the starting value, the €0.09 per minute determines how steeply it goes uphill.

If you go one unit to the right along the line, it rises by $m$ units. The gradient triangle shows it: $m = \tfrac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$. For costs, $n$ is called the fixed costs and $m$ the variable costs per item or per minute.

KEY POINT · LINEAR FUNCTION

$f(x) = m\,x + n$ with the gradient $m = \dfrac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$ and the $y$-intercept $n$: the graph is a straight line through $(0, n)$.

$m > 0$: increasing · $m < 0$: decreasing · $m = 0$: constant, parallel to the $x$-axis

Zero: $f(x_0) = 0 \Rightarrow x_0 = -\dfrac{n}{m}$ (for $m \neq 0$)

Direct proportionality: $n = 0$, i.e. $f(x) = m\,x$. The quotient $\tfrac{y}{x} = m$ is the same for all pairs of values.

Drawing without a table: start at $(0, n)$, then one unit to the right and $m$ units up (down for $m < 0$).

Fractions as gradients: $m = \tfrac23$ means 3 to the right, 2 up.

INTERACTIVE · A LINE THROUGH TWO POINTS

Drag the two points. The green gradient triangle shows $\Delta x$ and $\Delta y$, the white point the intercept.

EXAMPLE 3 · COMPARING TARIFFS

Tariff A: $K_A(x) = 0.09\,x + 10$. Tariff B: $K_B(x) = 0.19\,x$. For how many minutes do both cost the same?

$0.09\,x + 10 = 0.19\,x \Rightarrow 10 = 0.1\,x \Rightarrow x = 100$. At 100 minutes both cost €19.

Because B is steeper: for more than 100 minutes, tariff A is cheaper. B is not proportional to A, but B itself is proportional: no monthly fee.

EXAMPLE 4 · DRAWING AND ZERO

Draw $f(x) = -1.5\,x + 3$ and determine the zero.

Start at $(0, 3)$, then 2 to the right and $1.5 \cdot 2 = 3$ down: $(2, 0)$.

$-1.5\,x + 3 = 0 \Rightarrow x_0 = -\tfrac{3}{-1.5} = $ $2$

3Equations of lines and angle of inclination

A 12% gradient means: over 100 m of horizontal distance the road rises 12 m. So the gradient is $m = 0.12$. The tangent gives the angle: $\tan\alpha = 0.12$, so $\alpha \approx 6.8^\circ$. Steep roads are much flatter than 12% sounds.

If you know two points of a line, you also know its gradient: it is the ratio of the difference in height to the difference in width. You get the intercept by substituting one of the points into $y = mx + n$.

KEY POINT · LINE THROUGH TWO POINTS, ANGLE OF INCLINATION

Through $P_1(x_1, y_1)$ and $P_2(x_2, y_2)$ with $x_1 \neq x_2$: $\;m = \dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$, then $n$ by substituting a point.

Point-gradient form: $y = m\,(x - x_1) + y_1$

Angle of inclination $\alpha$ between the line and the positive $x$-direction: $\tan\alpha = m$. For decreasing lines $\alpha$ is obtuse, and the acute angle of intersection with the $x$-axis is then $180^\circ - \alpha$.

A vertical line $x = c$ has no gradient and is not the graph of a function.

Calculator: $\alpha = \tan^{-1}(m)$, in degree mode (DEG). For negative $m$ it gives a negative angle; then add $180^\circ$.

Parallel lines have the same gradient.

EXAMPLE 5 · LINE THROUGH TWO POINTS

Determine the line through $A(-1, 5)$ and $B(3, -3)$ and its angle of inclination.

$m = \dfrac{-3 - 5}{3 - (-1)} = \dfrac{-8}{4} = -2$. Substitute $A$: $5 = -2 \cdot (-1) + n \Rightarrow n = 3$: $y = -2x + 3$.

$\tan^{-1}(-2) \approx -63.4^\circ$, so $\alpha \approx 116.6^\circ$. The acute angle of intersection with the $x$-axis is $63.4^\circ$.

EXAMPLE 6 · POINT AND ANGLE

A line passes through $P(2, 1)$ and has the angle of inclination $30^\circ$. State its equation.

$m = \tan 30^\circ \approx 0.577$. Point-gradient form: $y = 0.577\,(x - 2) + 1$,

multiplied out $y \approx 0.577\,x - 0.155$.

EXCURSIONTHE HISTORY OF A CONCEPT

What is a function? From Euler to Dirichlet

For Leonhard Euler in 1748 a function was still a formula: an “analytic expression” made up of the variable quantity and fixed numbers. Functions like $x^2 + 1$ fit this picture; the car park tariff from exercise 5 does not quite.

In 1837 Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet broke away from the formula. For him all that mattered was that every $x$ has a definite value $y$, no matter whether there is a rule of calculation for it or not. That is essentially the definition in the key-point box. Since then a function can also be a table, a series of measurements or a tariff.

Sources: Leonhard Euler, “Introductio in analysin infinitorum”, volume 1 (1748), chapter 1, § 4. · Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet, “Über die Darstellung ganz willkürlicher Functionen durch Sinus- und Cosinusreihen”, Repertorium der Physik 1 (1837).
