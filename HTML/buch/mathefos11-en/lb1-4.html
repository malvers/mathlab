Lines and systems of linear equations

Two cyclists ride towards each other, two kinds of coffee are blended, two tariffs compete: there are always two conditions to be met at the same time. Graphically these are two straight lines, algebraically a system of linear equations. You learn how parameters change a line, how two lines lie in relation to each other and how to solve systems of equations with three methods.

UNIT 13 LESSONS

1Parameters and relative positions

A delivery service changes its prices $y = mx + n$: if it raises the basic fee $n$, the line moves up. If it raises the price $m$ per kilometre, the line gets steeper and turns about the point $(0, n)$. Two tariffs with the same $m$ run side by side and never meet.

Two lines in the plane can intersect in exactly one point, not intersect at all or lie completely on top of each other. A special kind of intersection is the right angle: if you turn a gradient triangle with $\Delta x = 1$ and $\Delta y = m$ through $90^\circ$, you get the gradient $-\tfrac1m$.

KEY POINT · PARAMETERS AND THE POSITION OF TWO LINES

In $y = mx + n$, $n$ shifts the line parallel in the $y$-direction, $m$ turns it about the point $(0, n)$.

For $g\colon y = m_1 x + n_1$ and $h\colon y = m_2 x + n_2$:

$m_1 = m_2$, $n_1 \neq n_2$: parallel · $m_1 = m_2$, $n_1 = n_2$: identical · $m_1 \neq m_2$: intersecting in exactly one point · $m_1 \cdot m_2 = -1$: orthogonal (perpendicular)

Point of intersection: set the two terms equal, calculate $x$, substitute into one of the equations.

Family of lines: $y = ax + 2$ describes a line for every $a$. All of them pass through $(0, 2)$.

Angle of intersection of two lines: the difference of their angles of inclination; if it is more than $90^\circ$, take what is left to $180^\circ$.

INTERACTIVE · TWO LINES

Choose an example or set $m$ and $n$ of both lines yourself. Keep an eye on the product $m_1 \cdot m_2$.

EXAMPLE 1 · POSITION AND POINT OF INTERSECTION

How do $g\colon y = 2x - 1$ and $h\colon y = -0.5x + 4$ lie in relation to each other?

$m_1 \cdot m_2 = 2 \cdot (-0.5) = -1$: perpendicular.

$2x - 1 = -0.5x + 4 \Rightarrow 2.5x = 5 \Rightarrow x = 2$, $y = 3$: $S(2, 3)$

EXAMPLE 2 · A FAMILY OF LINES

Given $g_a\colon y = ax + 2$. For which $a$ is $g_a$ parallel or perpendicular to $y = 3x - 1$? For which $a$ does $g_a$ pass through $P(4, 0)$?

Parallel: $a = 3$. Perpendicular: $3a = -1$, so $a = -\tfrac13$.

Substitute $P$: $0 = 4a + 2 \Rightarrow$ $a = -0.5$

EXERCISES

How do the lines lie in relation to each other? a) $y = 3x + 1$, $y = 3x - 2$   b) $y = 0.5x$, $y = -2x + 5$   c) $y = x + 1$, $y = 2x - 3$   d) $y = -x + 2$, $2y = -2x + 4$

a) parallel   b) perpendicular   c) intersecting at $S(4, 5)$   d) identical, because $2y = -2x + 4$ means $y = -x + 2$

Calculate the point of intersection of $y = 2x + 1$ and $y = -x + 7$.

$3x = 6$, $x = 2$, $y = 5$: $S(2, 5)$

State the equation of the line that passes through $P(8, 1)$ and is perpendicular to $y = 4x - 3$.

$m = -\tfrac14$, $1 = -\tfrac14 \cdot 8 + n \Rightarrow n = 3$: $y = -0.25x + 3$

Show that all the lines $y = ax - 2a + 1$ pass through the point $P(2, 1)$. For which $a$ does the line pass through $(0, 5)$?

For $x = 2$: $y = 2a - 2a + 1 = 1$, for every $a$. $(0, 5)$: $-2a + 1 = 5 \Rightarrow a = -2$.

The lines $y = x$, $y = -x + 6$ and the $x$-axis enclose a triangle. Determine its vertices and its area. Is the triangle right-angled?

Vertices $(0, 0)$, $(6, 0)$ and $(3, 3)$. $A = \tfrac12 \cdot 6 \cdot 3 = 9$. Right-angled at $(3, 3)$, because $1 \cdot (-1) = -1$.

UNIT 23 LESSONS

2Systems of linear equations with two unknowns

Cinema night: $\text{I: } 2e + 3k = 41$ and $\text{II: } e + 2k = 24$. From II it follows that $e = 24 - 2k$. Substituted into I: $2(24 - 2k) + 3k = 41$, so $48 - k = 41$ and $k = 7$. Then $e = 10$: adults pay €10, children €7.

Each of the two equations describes a straight line. A pair of numbers that satisfies both lies on both lines, so it is their point of intersection. Algebraically there are three ways, all aiming at the same goal: make one variable disappear.

KEY POINT · THREE METHODS OF SOLUTION

Equating: solve both equations for the same variable and set them equal.

Substitution: solve one equation for one variable and substitute into the other.

Elimination: multiply the equations by numbers so that one variable drops out when you add them.

Solution set: exactly one solution $L = \{(x, y)\}$ if the lines intersect · no solution $L = \{\,\}$ if they are parallel · infinitely many if they are identical.

Which method? If a variable already stands alone: substitution. If both are solved for $y$: equating. If the numbers in front of a variable match: elimination.

With the graphing calculator: solve both equations for $y$, draw them and let it calculate the point of intersection (often “intersect”).

INTERACTIVE · ONE, NONE OR INFINITELY MANY SOLUTIONS

Equation I stays fixed; you set equation II with the sliders. Each equation is a straight line.

EXAMPLE 3 · ELIMINATION

Solve $\text{I: } 3x + 2y = 16$, $\;\text{II: } 5x - 2y = 0$.

$\text{I} + \text{II}$: $8x = 16$, so $x = 2$. Into I: $6 + 2y = 16 \Rightarrow y = 5$.

Check in II: $10 - 10 = 0$. $L = \{(2, 5)\}$

EXAMPLE 4 · EQUATING

Solve $\text{I: } y = 2x - 3$, $\;\text{II: } y = -x + 6$.

$2x - 3 = -x + 6 \Rightarrow 3x = 9 \Rightarrow x = 3$, into I: $y = 3$.

$L = \{(3, 3)\}$, the lines intersect at $S(3, 3)$.

EXERCISES

Solve by equating: $y = 3x - 4$ and $y = x + 2$.

$2x = 6$, $x = 3$, $y = 5$: $L = \{(3, 5)\}$

Solve by substitution: $x = 2y + 1$ and $3x - y = 13$.

$3(2y + 1) - y = 13 \Rightarrow 5y = 10 \Rightarrow y = 2$, $x = 5$: $L = \{(5, 2)\}$

Solve by elimination: $2x + 3y = 12$ and $4x - 3y = 6$.

Add: $6x = 18$, $x = 3$, then $3y = 6$, $y = 2$: $L = \{(3, 2)\}$

State the solution set and interpret it graphically: a) $x + y = 4$, $2x + 2y = 8$   b) $x + y = 4$, $x + y = 6$

a) The second equation is twice the first: identical lines, $L = \{(x, 4 - x) \mid x \in \mathbb{R}\}$   b) $4 = 6$ is a contradiction: parallel lines, $L = \{\,\}$

In each case choose the most convenient method and give a reason: a) $5x + 4y = 3$, $2x - 4y = 18$   b) $y = 4x$, $3x + 2y = 22$

a) Elimination, because $+4y$ and $-4y$ drop out: $7x = 21$, $x = 3$, $y = -3$   b) Substitution, because $y$ already stands alone: $11x = 22$, $x = 2$, $y = 8$

UNIT 33 LESSONS

3Applications from business and technology

A roastery blends coffee at €12 per kg with coffee at €20 per kg. The aim is 10 kg of a blend at €15 per kg. With $a$ and $b$ kg of the two kinds: $a + b = 10$ and $12a + 20b = 150$. Result: 6.25 kg of the cheap and 3.75 kg of the expensive kind.

The hardest step is the translation. If you define the variables cleanly with units and write every piece of information in the text as exactly one equation, you have almost won.

KEY POINT · WORD PROBLEMS WITH SYSTEMS OF EQUATIONS

1. Define the variables, with meaning and unit: “$a$ = amount of kind A in kg”.

2. Write every condition in the text as an equation. 3. Solve the system of equations.

4. Give the answer in context and check it against the text, not just against the equations.

Mixtures: the amounts add up, the prices (or proportions) weighted by the amounts.

Motion: distance $=$ speed $\cdot$ time. Whoever starts later has been travelling for $t - t_0$ hours.

PICTURE · THE MEETING POINT IN THE DISTANCE-TIME DIAGRAM

Cyclist A: $s_A = 18\,t$. Cyclist B starts 54 km away: $s_B = 54 - 12\,t$. The point of intersection is the meeting point.

EXAMPLE 5 · ROOMS AND BEDS

A hotel has 50 rooms, single and double rooms, with 84 beds altogether. How many rooms of each kind are there?

$e$ single, $d$ double rooms: $\text{I: } e + d = 50$, $\text{II: } e + 2d = 84$. $\text{II} - \text{I}$: $d = 34$, then $e = 16$.

16 single and 34 double rooms. Check: $16 + 68 = 84$ beds.

EXAMPLE 6 · THE MEETING POINT

Solve the exploration “Meeting point”: 54 km, 18 km/h and 12 km/h, both starting at 8 o’clock.

$18\,t = 54 - 12\,t \Rightarrow 30\,t = 54 \Rightarrow t = 1.8$ h $= 1$ h 48 min.

They meet at 9:48, 32.4 km from A ($18 \cdot 1.8 = 32.4$).

EXERCISES

The sum of two numbers is 56, their difference 14. What are the numbers?

$x + y = 56$, $x - y = 14$: adding gives $2x = 70$, so $35$ and $21$.

At the open-air pool 3 adults and 2 children pay €21, 2 adults and 5 children €25. Determine the admission prices.

$3e + 2k = 21$ and $2e + 5k = 25$. Times 2 and times 3: $6e + 4k = 42$, $6e + 15k = 75$, difference $11k = 33$: $k = 3$ euros, $e = 5$ euros.

30 litres of 20 per cent acid are to be mixed from 10 per cent and 40 per cent acid. How many litres of each kind are needed?

$a + b = 30$, $0.1a + 0.4b = 0.2 \cdot 30 = 6$. Result: 20 litres of the 10 per cent and 10 litres of the 40 per cent acid.

An electrician charges a flat call-out fee and an hourly rate. 3 hours of work cost €159, 5 hours €239. Determine both amounts.

$3s + a = 159$, $5s + a = 239$: $2s = 80$, so €40 per hour and €39 call-out fee.

A lorry sets off at 8:00 at 60 km/h. At 8:30 a car follows it on the same route at 100 km/h. When and where does the car overtake the lorry?

$60\,t = 100\,(t - 0.5) \Rightarrow 40\,t = 50 \Rightarrow t = 1.25$ h: at 9:15, 75 km after the start.

REVIEW · DEEPEN · CONNECT

How do $y = -2x + 1$ and $4x + 2y = 2$ lie in relation to each other?

$2y = -4x + 2$, so $y = -2x + 1$: identical.

For which $a$ does the system $2x + a\,y = 4$, $x + 3y = 2$ have infinitely many solutions? What holds for the other values of $a$?

Twice II is $2x + 6y = 4$: for $a = 6$ both equations are equivalent, infinitely many solutions. For $a \neq 6$ the gradients are different: exactly one solution.

Connecting with chapter 1.2: the line $g$ passes through $A(1, 3)$ and $B(4, 9)$, the line $h$ through $C(0, 7)$ and $D(5, 2)$. Calculate the point of intersection.

$g\colon y = 2x + 1$, $h\colon y = -x + 7$. $3x = 6$: $S(2, 5)$

In a hutch there are chickens and rabbits, with 20 heads and 56 legs altogether. How many animals of each kind are there? Solve it once with a system of equations and once with the trick from the excursion.

$h + k = 20$, $2h + 4k = 56$: $k = 8$, $h = 12$. Trick: all of them lift two legs, then $56 - 40 = 16$ legs are still standing, two per rabbit: 8 rabbits.

EXCURSIONA PUZZLE FROM CHINA

Chickens and rabbits in a cage

An old Chinese arithmetic book contains this problem: in a cage there are pheasants and rabbits. At the top you count 35 heads, at the bottom 94 feet. How many animals of each kind are there?

Today we would write $f + k = 35$ and $2f + 4k = 94$. The book solves it without equations: halve the number of feet, which gives 47. Each pheasant then counts once, each rabbit twice. Subtract the 35 heads and you are left with 12 rabbits, so 23 pheasants. Check it with the elimination method.

Source: “Sunzi suanjing” (The Mathematical Classic of Sunzi), China, written between the 3rd and 5th centuries, book 3. In many retellings the pheasants become chickens.

LOOKING BACKTHE ESSENTIALS AT A GLANCE

PARAMETERS

$n$ shifts the line in the $y$-direction, $m$ turns it about $(0, n)$.

$y = ax + 2$: all lines through $(0, 2)$

RELATIVE POSITIONS

Same $m$: parallel or identical. Different $m$: exactly one point of intersection. $m_1 m_2 = -1$: perpendicular.

$y = 2x - 1$ and $y = -0.5x + 4$: perpendicular, $S(2, 3)$

METHODS OF SOLUTION

Equating, substitution, elimination: make one variable disappear.

$3x + 2y = 16$, $5x - 2y = 0$: $8x = 16$

SOLUTION SETS

Point of intersection: one solution · parallel: none · identical: infinitely many.

$x + y = 4$, $x + y = 6$: $L = \{\,\}$

WORD PROBLEMS

Define variables with units, conditions as equations, solve, check against the text.

Coffee: 6.25 kg at €12 and 3.75 kg at €20
