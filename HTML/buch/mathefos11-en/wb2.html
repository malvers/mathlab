UNIT 13 LESSONS

1Calculating interest and compound interest

Grandma’s present: if the interest is withdrawn every year, it brings in €150 a year, so after 20 years €3,000 interest, €8,000 altogether. If the interest stays in the account, it is multiplied by $1.03$ every year: $5000 \cdot 1.03^{20} \approx 9030.56$ euros. Over €1,000 more, just because interest earns interest again.

Compound interest is growth with a constant factor. The calculations with percentages and powers from chapter 1.1 are all you need for it. For terms of less than a year, banks often calculate with 30 days per month and 360 days per year.

KEY POINT · INTEREST AND COMPOUND INTEREST

Annual interest: $Z = K \cdot \tfrac{p}{100}$ · interest for days: $Z = K \cdot \tfrac{p}{100} \cdot \tfrac{t}{360}$ ($t$ days)

Compound interest: $K_n = K_0 \cdot q^n$ with the accumulation factor $q = 1 + \tfrac{p}{100}$

Present value (discounting): $K_0 = \dfrac{K_n}{q^n}$ · interest rate from $q = \sqrt[n]{\dfrac{K_n}{K_0}}$ · term with the graphing calculator (table) or $n = \dfrac{\log\left(\frac{K_n}{K_0}\right)}{\log q}$

Rule of 72: at $p$% interest, a capital doubles in about $72 : p$ years.

Link to economics and law: banks must state the effective annual interest rate. It includes all costs and makes offers comparable.

EXAMPLE 1 · FINAL CAPITAL

€5,000 is invested for 20 years at 3%. Calculate the final capital with and without compound interest.

With compound interest: $K_{20} = 5000 \cdot 1.03^{20} \approx$ €9,030.56.

Without: $5000 + 20 \cdot 150 = 8000$ euros.

EXAMPLE 2 · INTEREST RATE AND TERM

a) Which interest rate doubles a capital in 12 years? b) How long does doubling take at 4%?

a) $q^{12} = 2 \Rightarrow q = \sqrt[12]{2} \approx 1.0595$, so about 5.95%.

b) $1.04^n = 2$: the calculator table shows $1.04^{17} \approx 1.95$ and $1.04^{18} \approx 2.03$. After 18 years it has doubled ($n \approx 17.7$).

2Savings plans, instalment and annuity repayment

In the savings plan from the exploration the first payment earns interest for 9 years, the second for 8 years, and so on, the last not at all. Together: $1200 \cdot (1.03^9 + 1.03^8 + \ldots + 1.03 + 1)$. This sum has a short formula: $1200 \cdot \tfrac{1.03^{10} - 1}{0.03} \approx 13\,756.65$ euros.

With a loan it works the other way round: every instalment consists of interest on the outstanding debt and a repayment that reduces the outstanding debt. With instalment repayment the repayment is the same every year, with annuity repayment the whole instalment is.

KEY POINT · REGULAR PAYMENTS

Savings plan (payment $R$ at the end of each year): $K_n = R \cdot \dfrac{q^n - 1}{q - 1}$

Instalment repayment: equal repayment $T = \tfrac{S_0}{n}$; the interest on the outstanding debt falls, so the instalment falls as well.

Annuity repayment: equal instalment (annuity) $A = S_0 \cdot \dfrac{q^n \cdot (q - 1)}{q^n - 1}$. The interest share falls, the repayment share grows.

A repayment plan lists the outstanding debt, interest, repayment and instalment for each year.

Total cost of a loan: the sum of all instalments minus the amount of the loan, i.e. the sum of the interest.

Buying on instalments: “only €105 a month” sounds cheap. Always compare the sum of all instalments with the cash price.

EXAMPLE 3 · THE SAVINGS PLAN

€1,200 at the end of every year at 3%, for 10 years. What is the final capital, and how much of it is interest?

$K_{10} = 1200 \cdot \dfrac{1.03^{10} - 1}{0.03} \approx 13\,756.65$ euros.

€12,000 was paid in, so about €1,756.65 interest.

EXAMPLE 4 · TWO LOAN MODELS

€10,000 at 6% over 5 years: compare instalment repayment and annuity repayment.

Instalments: repayment €2,000, interest €600, 480, 360, 240, 120, together €1,800. First instalment €2,600.

Annuity: $A = 10\,000 \cdot \tfrac{1.06^5 \cdot 0.06}{1.06^5 - 1} \approx 2373.96$ euros, interest altogether $\approx 1869.82$ euros. More even, but slightly more expensive.

REVIEW · DEEPEN · CONNECT

To what amount does €1,000 grow in 2 years at 5% compound interest?

$1000 \cdot 1.05^2 = 1102.50$ euros

Connecting with chapter 1.1: check the rule of 72 for 6%. How accurate is it?

Rule of thumb: $72 : 6 = 12$ years. Exactly: $1.06^{12} \approx 2.012$, so just under 12 years ($n \approx 11.9$). Very accurate.

Connecting with chapter 1.2: show that with simple interest the capital is a linear function of time. What are the gradient and the intercept?

$K(n) = K_0 + K_0 \cdot \tfrac{p}{100} \cdot n$: gradient $K_0 \cdot \tfrac{p}{100}$ (interest per year), intercept $K_0$. With compound interest, $K_0 \cdot q^n$ is not linear.

A current account is overdrawn by €1,500 for 30 days. The overdraft interest rate is 12% per year. What does that cost?

$1500 \cdot 0.12 \cdot \tfrac{30}{360} = 15$ euros. Over a whole year it would be €180.

EXCURSIONA FAMOUS NUMBER

Interest at every instant

Jacob Bernoulli asked himself a strange question: a bank pays 100% interest a year. €1 becomes €2. What happens if instead it pays 50% every half year? Then it is $1.5^2 = 2.25$ euros. Monthly $\tfrac{1}{12}$: $\left(1 + \tfrac{1}{12}\right)^{12} \approx 2.613$ euros. Daily: $\left(1 + \tfrac{1}{365}\right)^{365} \approx 2.715$ euros.

The more often interest is paid, the more you get, but not an unlimited amount: the amounts approach a fixed number, $e = 2.71828\ldots$, Euler’s number. You will meet it in Year 12 and wherever something grows or decays continuously.

Source: Jacob Bernoulli, “Quaestiones nonnullae de usuris, cum solutione problematis de sorte alearum”, Acta Eruditorum, Leipzig 1690.
