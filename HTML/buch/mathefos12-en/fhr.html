EXAM PROBLEM ANALYSIS · THE RAMP

For $0 \le x \le 6$ the profile of a skate ramp is described by $f(x) = -\tfrac18 x^3 + \tfrac34 x^2$ ($x$ and $f(x)$ in m). The ramp is 3 m wide.

a) Show that the ramp rests on the ground at $x = 0$ and $x = 6$ and starts horizontally at $x = 0$.

$f(0) = 0$, $f(6) = -27 + 27 = 0$. $f'(x) = -\tfrac38 x^2 + \tfrac32 x$, $f'(0) = 0$: horizontal start.

b) Calculate the highest point of the ramp.

$f'(x) = x\left(\tfrac32 - \tfrac38 x\right) = 0$: $x = 4$, $f''(4) = -\tfrac34 \cdot 4 + \tfrac32 = -1.5 < 0$. Highest point $H(4, 4)$, 4 m high.

c) Where does the ramp rise most steeply? Give the angle of inclination.

$f''(x) = -\tfrac34 x + \tfrac32 = 0$: $x = 2$, $W(2, 2)$, $f'(2) = 1.5$. Angle of inclination $\tan^{-1} 1.5 \approx 56.3^\circ$.

d) To paint the side walls you need the cross-sectional area under the profile. Calculate it.

$\int_0^6 f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x = \left[-\tfrac{1}{32} x^4 + \tfrac14 x^3\right]_0^6 = -40.5 + 54 = 13.5$ m² per side wall.

e) At the point where the ramp rises most steeply, a support is to be extended in a straight line in the direction of the tangent down to the ground. Where does it meet the ground?

Tangent at $W(2, 2)$: $y = 1.5(x - 2) + 2 = 1.5x - 1$. Zero $x = \tfrac23$: about 67 cm from the start of the ramp.

EXAM PROBLEM VECTORS · THE TENT ROOF

A pavilion has a tent roof in the shape of a pyramid with the square base $A(0, 0, 0)$, $B(6, 0, 0)$, $C(6, 6, 0)$, $D(0, 6, 0)$ and the apex $S(3, 3, 6)$, all in m.

a) Find a coordinate equation of the plane $E$ through $B$, $C$ and $S$.

$\vec{BC} \times \vec{BS} = (0, 6, 0) \times (-3, 3, 6) = (36, 0, 18)$, simplified $(2, 0, 1)$. With $B$: $E\colon 2x + z = 12$. Check with $S$: $6 + 6 = 12$ ✔

b) At what angle is this roof surface inclined to the ground?

Normal vectors $(2, 0, 1)$ and $(0, 0, 1)$: $\cos \alpha = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt5}$, $\alpha \approx 63.4^\circ$.

c) A mast stands at the centre $M(3, 3, 0)$ of the floor. Calculate the distance from $M$ to the roof surface $E$ and the foot of the perpendicular.

$d = \tfrac{|6 - 12|}{\sqrt5} \approx 2.68$ m. Perpendicular line $(3, 3, 0) + t \cdot (2, 0, 1)$: $6 + 5t = 12$, $t = 1.2$, foot of the perpendicular $F(5.4, 3, 1.2)$.

d) How much tent fabric is needed for the whole roof? What is the volume under the roof?

One lateral face $\tfrac12\,|(36, 0, 18)| = \tfrac12\sqrt{1620} \approx 20.12$ m², four equal ones: about 80.5 m². Volume $\tfrac13 \cdot 36 \cdot 6 = 72$ m³.

e) Show that the edges $AS$ and $BS$ are not perpendicular to each other, and calculate the angle between them at the apex.

$\vec{SA} = (-3, -3, -6)$, $\vec{SB} = (3, -3, -6)$, $\vec{SA} \circ \vec{SB} = -9 + 9 + 36 = 36 \neq 0$. $|\vec{SA}| = |\vec{SB}| = \sqrt{54}$, $\cos \varphi = \tfrac{36}{54} = \tfrac23$, $\varphi \approx 48.2^\circ$.

EXAM PROBLEM PROBABILITY · QUALITY CONTROL

A machine makes sealing rings. Experience shows that 5 % of them are defective. The rings are checked independently of one another.

a) Three rings are checked. What is the probability that none is defective? That exactly one is defective?

$0.95^3 \approx 0.857$. Exactly one: $3 \cdot 0.05 \cdot 0.95^2 \approx 0.135$.

b) Ten rings are checked. Calculate the probability of at most one defective ring.

$0.95^{10} + 10 \cdot 0.05 \cdot 0.95^9 \approx 0.599 + 0.315 = 0.914$

c) What is the smallest number of rings you must check so that the probability of at least one defective ring among them is more than 90 %?

Complementary event: all $n$ rings are fine, $1 - 0.95^n > 0.9$.

$0.95^n < 0.1$. With the graphing calculator or logarithms: $n > \tfrac{\ln 0.1}{\ln 0.95} \approx 44.9$, so at least 45 rings.

TIPS FOR THE EXAMAND FOR THE ORAL

TIME

First skim all the problems and start with the one you are surest of. Do not get stuck on one part: leave a gap, carry on, come back later.

Follow-on errors are usually not penalised twice. It pays to carry on cleanly with a wrong intermediate result.

AIDS

Graphing calculator without CAS and a formula booklet. Check beforehand: batteries, degree mode for angles, window reset.

Still write down your working. A calculator result without an approach is usually worth only part of the marks.

CHECKING

Check derivatives, extreme points and integrals with the graphing calculator. With vectors, substitute to check: does the point really lie in the plane?

Does the result fit the sketch? An area is never negative, an angle between lines at most 90°.

ANSWER SENTENCES

Every applied problem ends with a sentence in the language of the problem, with a unit.

“At its highest point the ramp is 4 m high.”

ORAL EXAM

Be able to explain terms: what is a derivative, a normal vector, an asymptote? Ideally with a small sketch on the board.

Practise in pairs: one person asks, the other explains for two minutes without interruption.

EXCURSIONTO CATCH YOUR BREATH

Nim: a game with a complete theory

There are three heaps of matches on the table, for example with 3, 4 and 5 matches. Two players take turns removing as many matches as they like from a single heap. Whoever takes the last match wins. The game looks complicated, but it has a complete solution, which Charles L. Bouton published in 1901.

You write the heap sizes as binary numbers one below the other, $3 = 011$, $4 = 100$, $5 = 101$, and count the ones in each column. If all the counts are even, the player whose turn it is loses. Here the middle column is odd: whoever starts takes 2 matches from the first heap. With $1 = 001$, $4$, $5$ all the columns are even, and this can be restored after every reply.

Source: C. L. Bouton, “Nim, a game with a complete mathematical theory”, Annals of Mathematics, 2nd series, vol. 3 (1901/02), pp. 35–39.
