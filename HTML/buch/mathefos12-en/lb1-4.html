UNIT 13 LESSONS

1Perpendicular line, foot of the perpendicular and distance

The lamp hook is at $P(3, 3, 3)$, the wall lies in $E\colon x + 2y + 2z = 6$. The shortest way is perpendicular to the wall, that is in the direction of the normal vector: $l\colon \vec{x} = (3, 3, 3) + t \cdot (1, 2, 2)$. Substitute: $15 + 9t = 6$, so $t = -1$ and the foot $F(2, 1, 1)$. The distance is $|\vec{PF}| = |(-1, -2, -2)| = 3$.

The line through $P$ that is perpendicular to the plane is called the perpendicular line. Its point of intersection with the plane is the foot of the perpendicular $F$. The distance from $P$ to the plane is the length of the line segment $PF$. If you go on from $F$ the same distance again, you reach the mirror point $P'$.

KEY POINT · PERPENDICULAR AND DISTANCE POINT–PLANE

Perpendicular line: $l\colon \vec{x} = \vec{OP} + t \cdot \vec{n}$ · foot of the perpendicular $F$: substitute $l$ into $E$, calculate $t$

Distance: $d(P, E) = |\vec{PF}| = |t| \cdot |\vec{n}|$, directly without the foot of the perpendicular: $d(P, E) = \dfrac{|n_1 p_1 + n_2 p_2 + n_3 p_3 - d|}{|\vec{n}|}$

Mirror point: $\vec{OP'} = \vec{OP} + 2t \cdot \vec{n}$, which is the same as $\vec{OP'} = 2\,\vec{OF} - \vec{OP}$

Why perpendicular? Every other way from $P$ to the plane is the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle with $PF$ as one of its legs, and is therefore longer.

Distance between parallel planes: take a point of one plane and calculate its distance to the other.

INTERACTIVE · THE PERPENDICULAR TO A PLANE

The plane is given by its normal vector $\vec{n}$ and the number $d$. Move $P$ and watch the foot of the perpendicular $F$, the distance (red) and the mirror point $P'$.

EXAMPLE 1 · FOOT OF THE PERPENDICULAR AND DISTANCE

Find the foot of the perpendicular from $P(5, 2, 5)$ to $E\colon 2x + y + 2z = 4$ and the distance from $P$ to $E$.

Substitute $l\colon \vec{x} = (5, 2, 5) + t \cdot (2, 1, 2)$: $22 + 9t = 4$, $t = -2$. $F(1, 0, 1)$

$d = |t| \cdot |\vec{n}| = 2 \cdot 3 = 6$. Check with the formula: $\dfrac{|22 - 4|}{3} = 6$ ✔

EXAMPLE 2 · REFLECTING

Reflect $P(5, 2, 5)$ in the plane $E$ from example 1.

$\vec{OP'} = 2\,\vec{OF} - \vec{OP} = (2, 0, 2) - (5, 2, 5)$.

$P'(-3, -2, -3)$, also at a distance of 6, on the other side.

EXERCISES

How far is the origin from $E\colon 2x + y + 2z = 9$? Where is the foot of its perpendicular?

$d = \tfrac{|0 - 9|}{3} = 3$. Substitute $t \cdot (2, 1, 2)$: $9t = 9$, $F(2, 1, 2)$.

Find the foot of the perpendicular and the distance from $P(4, 1, 3)$ to the $x$-$y$-plane.

$F(4, 1, 0)$, distance 3. For coordinate planes you can read them off directly.

Calculate the foot of the perpendicular and the distance from $P(2, -2, 3)$ to $E\colon x - 2y + 2z = 3$. Reflect $P$ in $E$.

$12 + 9t = 3$, $t = -1$: $F(1, 0, 1)$, distance $3$, mirror point $P'(0, 2, -1)$.

Show that $E_1\colon x + 2y + 2z = 3$ and $E_2\colon x + 2y + 2z = 12$ are parallel, and calculate the distance between them.

The same normal vector. Point $(3, 0, 0)$ of $E_1$: $d = \tfrac{|3 - 12|}{3} = 3$.

Which points on the $z$-axis are at a distance of 6 from $E\colon 2x + y + 2z = 4$?

Put $P(0, 0, c)$ into the distance formula. An absolute value has two cases.

$\dfrac{|2c - 4|}{3} = 6$, so $2c - 4 = 18$ or $2c - 4 = -18$: $(0, 0, 11)$ and $(0, 0, -7)$

2Areas and volume with the cross product

A rectangular roof surface has the corner $A(0, 0, 3)$ and the edges $\vec{AB} = (8, 0, 0)$ and $\vec{AD} = (0, 4, 3)$, in metres. $\vec{AB} \times \vec{AD} = (0, -24, 32)$ has the length $\sqrt{576 + 1024} = 40$. As a check: $|\vec{AB}| = 8$ and $|\vec{AD}| = 5$, the roof has 40 m².

The length of the cross product is the area of the parallelogram spanned by the two vectors. This is also true if it lies at a slant in space and there is no right angle. A triangle is half a parallelogram. For the volume of a pyramid you also need its height, and that is the distance of the apex from the plane of the base.

KEY POINT · AREAS AND VOLUME

Parallelogram: $A = |\vec{u} \times \vec{v}|$ · triangle $ABC$: $A = \tfrac12\,|\vec{AB} \times \vec{AC}|$

Behind this is $|\vec{u} \times \vec{v}| = |\vec{u}| \cdot |\vec{v}| \cdot \sin \varphi$, base times height.

Pyramid: $V = \tfrac13 \cdot G \cdot h$, the height $h$ is the distance of the apex from the plane of the base.

Without the cross product it works too: calculate sides and angle with magnitude and scalar product, then $A = \tfrac12\,ab \sin \gamma$. That usually takes longer.

Check: a right-angled triangle with the legs $a$ and $b$ has $\tfrac12 ab$; that is what must come out.

INTERACTIVE · PYRAMID AND PRISM

The base is a square in the $x$-$y$-plane. Move the apex: the volume stays the same, the lateral surface made of the triangles $\tfrac12\,|\vec{a} \times \vec{b}|$ does not.

EXAMPLE 3 · A TRIANGLE IN SPACE

Calculate the area of the triangle $A(1, 0, 0)$, $B(0, 2, 0)$, $C(0, 0, 3)$.

$\vec{AB} \times \vec{AC} = (-1, 2, 0) \times (-1, 0, 3) = (6, 3, 2)$ with the length $\sqrt{49} = 7$.

$A = 3.5$ square units

EXAMPLE 4 · VOLUME OF A PYRAMID

The triangle from example 3 is the base of a pyramid with its apex at the origin. Calculate its volume.

Base in $E\colon 6x + 3y + 2z = 6$, height $h = \tfrac{|0 - 6|}{7} = \tfrac67$.

$V = \tfrac13 \cdot 3.5 \cdot \tfrac67$: $V = 1$. Check: base $\tfrac12 \cdot 1 \cdot 2$ in the $x$-$y$-plane, height 3: $\tfrac13 \cdot 1 \cdot 3 = 1$ ✔

EXERCISES

Calculate the area of the parallelogram formed by $\vec{u} = (3, 0, 0)$ and $\vec{v} = (1, 2, 0)$.

$\vec{u} \times \vec{v} = (0, 0, 6)$, $A = 6$

Calculate the area of the triangle $A(0, 0, 0)$, $B(4, 0, 0)$, $C(0, 3, 0)$ with the cross product and check it.

$\tfrac12\,|(0, 0, 12)| = 6$, check: right-angled, $\tfrac12 \cdot 4 \cdot 3 = 6$

Calculate the area of the triangle $A(1, 1, 1)$, $B(3, 1, 2)$, $C(1, 4, 1)$. What do you notice about the angle at $A$?

$\vec{AB} \times \vec{AC} = (2, 0, 1) \times (0, 3, 0) = (-3, 0, 6)$, $A = \tfrac12\sqrt{45} \approx 3.35$. $\vec{AB} \circ \vec{AC} = 0$: a right angle at $A$.

A pyramid has the square base $A(0, 0, 0)$, $B(4, 0, 0)$, $C(4, 4, 0)$, $D(0, 4, 0)$ and the apex $S(2, 2, 6)$. Calculate its volume and lateral surface area.

$V = \tfrac13 \cdot 16 \cdot 6 = 32$. Side face $ABS$: $\tfrac12\,|(4, 0, 0) \times (2, 2, 6)| = \tfrac12\,|(0, -24, 8)| \approx 12.65$, lateral surface $\approx 50.6$.

A triangular sun sail is fixed at $A(4, 0, 3)$, $B(0, 5, 3)$ and $C(0, 0, 5)$ (in m). Calculate its area, a coordinate equation of its plane and the height of the sail above the point $(1, 1, 0)$ on the ground.

$\vec{AB} \times \vec{AC} = (-4, 5, 0) \times (-4, 0, 2) = (10, 8, 20)$, $A = \tfrac12\sqrt{564} \approx 11.87$ m². Cancelled $(5, 4, 10)$, with $A$: $5x + 4y + 10z = 50$. Above $(1, 1)$: $10z = 41$, 4.10 m high.

$E\colon x + y + z = 4$. Origin: $\tfrac{4}{\sqrt3} \approx 2.31$, $G$: $\tfrac{8}{\sqrt3} \approx 4.62$. Together $4\sqrt3$, the length of the space diagonal: the plane is perpendicular to it and divides it in the ratio 1 : 2.

EXCURSIONTECHNOLOGY

Where am I? Four spheres and a clock

Every GPS satellite constantly transmits where it is and what time its atomic clock shows. A receiver measures how long the signal has travelled and multiplies by the speed of light. That way it knows its distance to the satellite and knows: I am on a sphere around it. Two spheres intersect in a circle, three usually in two points, one of which lies far out in space.

So why do you still need four satellites? The clock in a phone is inaccurate. An error of just one millionth of a second makes an error of 300 m at 300 000 km/s. With the fourth distance the receiver also works out its clock error as a fourth unknown: four equations for $x$, $y$, $z$ and the time.

Source: MathWorks, “GPS and How It Works” (Satellite Communications Toolbox, documentation); official overview: GPS.gov, “GPS Overview” of the US government.
