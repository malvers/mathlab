Combining functions, composition and the tangent function

New functions can be built from simple ones: by adding, multiplying, dividing and by composition, when the result of one function is substituted into the next. A particularly important result arises when you divide sine by cosine: the tangent function, with gaps, poles and the period $\pi$.

UNIT 14 LESSONS

1Combining and composing

The cost per shirt is a quotient of two functions: $k(x) = \tfrac{200 + 5x}{x} = \tfrac{200}{x} + 5$. In the discount question two functions are carried out one after the other: $g(p) = p - 10$ and $h(p) = 1.19p$. Discount first: $h(g(100)) = 107.10$ €, tax first: $g(h(100)) = 109$ €. The order matters.

KEY POINT · COMBINING AND COMPOSING

$(f + g)(x) = f(x) + g(x)$, and likewise $f - g$ and $f \cdot g$. $\left(\tfrac{f}{g}\right)(x) = \tfrac{f(x)}{g(x)}$ only for $g(x) \neq 0$. The result is defined where both functions are defined.

Composition: $(f \circ g)(x) = f(g(x))$. First the inner function $g$ is calculated, and its result is substituted into the outer function $f$. This only works for $x$ for which $g(x)$ lies in the domain of $f$.

In general $f(g(x)) \neq g(f(x))$.

You read from the inside out: in $\sqrt{4 - x^2}$ you first calculate $4 - x^2$, then the square root.

Familiar compositions: shifting and stretching are compositions with linear functions: $(x + 2)^2$, $\sin(2x)$, $2^{x - 1}$.

INTERACTIVE · COMBINING FUNCTIONS

Choose $f$, $g$ and the combination. With composition, try both orders.

EXAMPLE 1 · TWO ORDERS

$f(x) = x^2$ and $g(x) = x + 2$. Find $f(g(x))$ and $g(f(x))$ and describe the graphs.

$f(g(x)) = (x + 2)^2$: the standard parabola shifted 2 to the left.

$g(f(x)) = x^2 + 2$: shifted 2 upwards. Two different functions.

EXAMPLE 2 · SPLITTING UP

Split $h(x) = \sqrt{4 - x^2}$ into an inner and an outer function and find the domain.

Inner function $g(x) = 4 - x^2$, outer $f(u) = \sqrt{u}$. Allowed is $4 - x^2 \geq 0$, so $-2 \leq x \leq 2$.

The graph is a semicircle with radius 2, because $y^2 = 4 - x^2$ means $x^2 + y^2 = 4$.

Student company: total costs $K(x) = 200 + 5x$ for $x$ shirts. Calculate the cost per shirt for 50 and for 200 shirts. Which value does it approach?

$k(50) = 9$ €, $k(200) = 6$ €. For very large $x$, $\tfrac{200}{x}$ becomes tiny: $k(x)$ approaches €5, and the line $y = 5$ is a horizontal asymptote.

$f(u) = \sqrt{u}$ and $g(x) = x - 4$. Find $f(g(x))$ and $g(f(x))$ with their domains.

$f(g(x)) = \sqrt{x - 4}$ for $x \geq 4$; $g(f(x)) = \sqrt{x} - 4$ for $x \geq 0$. The domains are different too.

The discount question in general: an item costs $p$ euros ($p \geq 10$). Compare “first a €10 discount, then 19 % tax” with the reverse order. What changes with a 10 % discount instead of €10?

$h(g(p)) = 1.19p - 11.90$ and $g(h(p)) = 1.19p - 10$: discount first always saves €1.90. With a 10 % discount both functions are multiplications, $1.19 \cdot 0.9 \cdot p = 0.9 \cdot 1.19 \cdot p$: then the order makes no difference.

UNIT 24 LESSONS

2The tangent function

A 12 % gradient means $\tan\alpha = \tfrac{12}{100}$, so $\alpha \approx 6.8^\circ$. 100 % is $45^\circ$. The closer the angle gets to $90^\circ$, the larger the percentage becomes, and at $90^\circ$ there is none any more: the path leads straight up, and you make no horizontal progress at all.

KEY POINT · TANGENT FUNCTION

$\tan x = \dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}$ for all $x$ with $\cos x \neq 0$. Domain: all real numbers except $\tfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$ ($k$ an integer).

There the graph has poles with vertical asymptotes. The range is all real numbers, the period is $\pi$, and the zeros are at $k\pi$.

Between two poles the graph is strictly increasing, and it is point-symmetric about the origin. On the unit circle, $\tan\alpha$ is the segment cut off on the tangent at $x = 1$.

Gradient and angle: a line with gradient $m$ is inclined to the $x$-axis at the angle $\alpha$ with $\tan\alpha = m$.

Period $\pi$: after half a turn sine and cosine have both changed sign, so their quotient is the same again.

INTERACTIVE · TANGENT ON THE UNIT CIRCLE

Turn the angle. On the left the orange segment on the tangent, on the right the same value on the tangent curve. At $90^\circ$ and $270^\circ$ the radius runs parallel to the tangent.

EXAMPLE 3 · SPECIAL VALUES

Calculate $\tan\tfrac{\pi}{4}$, $\tan\tfrac{\pi}{3}$ and $\tan\tfrac{3\pi}{4}$. What about $\tan\tfrac{\pi}{2}$?

$\tan\tfrac{\pi}{4} = \tfrac{\sqrt2/2}{\sqrt2/2} = 1$; $\tan\tfrac{\pi}{3} = \tfrac{\sqrt3/2}{1/2} = \sqrt3$; $\tan\tfrac{3\pi}{4} = \tfrac{\sqrt2/2}{-\sqrt2/2} = -1$

$\cos\tfrac{\pi}{2} = 0$: $\tan\tfrac{\pi}{2}$ is not defined; there is a pole there.

EXAMPLE 4 · ANGLE OF INCLINATION

At which angle does the line $y = 2x + 1$ rise? Which angle belongs to a 12 % gradient?

$\tan\alpha = 2 \Rightarrow \alpha \approx 63.4^\circ$ (key $\tan^{-1}$).

$\tan\alpha = 0.12 \Rightarrow$ $\alpha \approx 6.8^\circ$

Solve $\tan x = 2$ in the interval $[0, 2\pi]$.

The calculator gives one solution. The next one lies one period further on.

$x_1 \approx 1.107$, $x_2 = x_1 + \pi \approx 4.249$

Use the unit circle to justify that $\tan(x + \pi) = \tan x$. Why does the tangent have the period $\pi$, but sine and cosine $2\pi$?

The point for $x + \pi$ lies opposite the point for $x$: $\sin(x + \pi) = -\sin x$ and $\cos(x + \pi) = -\cos x$. In the quotient the minus signs cancel. Sine and cosine on their own need a full turn.

REVIEW · DEEPEN · CONNECT

$f(x) = \sin x$ and $g(x) = \cos x$. Form $\tfrac{f}{g}$ and $f \cdot g$ and give the domain of each.

$\tfrac{f}{g} = \tan x$ without $\tfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$; $f \cdot g = \sin x \cdot \cos x$, for all real numbers

Connecting with chapter 4.1: $f(x) = 2^x$ and $g(x) = \log_2 x$. Calculate $f(g(x))$ and $g(f(x))$. What do you notice?

$f(g(x)) = 2^{\log_2 x} = x$ for $x > 0$, $g(f(x)) = \log_2 2^x = x$ for all $x$. A function and its inverse cancel each other out when composed.

Connecting with chapter 1.2: use composition to explain why $\sin(2x)$ has the period $\pi$ and $\sin(0.5x)$ the period $4\pi$.

The inner function $2x$ already runs through a full period $2\pi$ of the outer function while $x$ only goes up to $\pi$. With $0.5x$, $x$ needs $4\pi$ for this.

A ramp is to have a gradient of at most 6 %. How long must it be at least, measured horizontally, to overcome a height of 0.90 m? At which angle does it then rise?

$\tfrac{0.90}{0.06} = 15$ m. $\alpha = \tan^{-1} 0.06 \approx 3.4^\circ$.

EXCURSIONHISTORY

Thales measures a pyramid

Thales of Miletus is said to have determined the height of the pyramids in Egypt around 600 BC without climbing them. He waited, so the story goes, for the moment when his own shadow was exactly as long as he was tall. At that moment the shadow of the pyramid is also as long as the pyramid is high, measured from the centre of its base.

In the language of this chapter: the sun is then $45^\circ$ high, and $\tan 45^\circ = 1$. If you know the tangent, you do not have to wait for that moment: with the sun at an altitude $\alpha$ and a shadow length $s$, the height is $h = s \cdot \tan\alpha$. With the sun at $30^\circ$, every shadow is about $1.73$ times as long as the object.

Source: Diogenes Laertius: “Lives and Opinions of Eminent Philosophers”, book I, section 27 (he refers to Hieronymus of Rhodes).
