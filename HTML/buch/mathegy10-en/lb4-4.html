UNIT 14 LESSONS

1Classifying real functions

The function from “Who am I?” is an exponential function $b^x$ with $b > 1$, for example $2^x$. Three properties were almost enough: “defined everywhere” rules out root, logarithm and $\tfrac1x$, “always positive” the lines and parabolas through zero, and $(0, 1)$ and “steeper and steeper” the rest.

KEY POINT · CLASSES OF FUNCTIONS AND PROFILE

Polynomial: $a_n x^n + \ldots + a_1 x + a_0$ (linear, quadratic, cubic functions …) · rational: the quotient of two polynomial functions, for example $\tfrac1x$ · root functions · exponential functions $a \cdot b^x$ · logarithmic functions $\log_a x$ · trigonometric functions $\sin$, $\cos$, $\tan$

A profile contains: term, domain and range, zeros and the point of intersection with the $y$-axis, symmetry, monotonicity, behaviour for large $|x|$ and asymptotes, and the period if there is one.

Symmetry: $f(-x) = f(x)$ for all $x$: symmetric about the $y$-axis · $f(-x) = -f(x)$: point-symmetric about the origin.

Make your own overview: one page per class, with a typical graph, the profile and an example from everyday life. It will help you up to the Abitur.

Power functions $x^n$ belong to different classes depending on the exponent: $x^3$ is polynomial, $x^{-2} = \tfrac{1}{x^2}$ rational, $x^{1/2} = \sqrt{x}$ a root function.

INTERACTIVE · THE CLASSES OF FUNCTIONS

Choose a class: typical graphs and the profile.

EXAMPLE 1 · A PROFILE

Write a profile for $f(x) = x^3 - 4x$.

Polynomial of degree 3, defined for all real numbers, and the range is the same. $x(x - 2)(x + 2)$: zeros $-2$, $0$, $2$, so also $(0, 0)$ on the $y$-axis.

$f(-x) = -x^3 + 4x = -f(x)$: point-symmetric about the origin. Upwards for large $x$, downwards for very negative $x$, no asymptote.

EXAMPLE 2 · PROPERTY AND TERM

Which function fits? “Only defined for $x > 0$, zero at 1, strictly decreasing.”

Only positive $x$ and the zero 1: a logarithmic function. Decreasing: the base lies between 0 and 1.

for example $f(x) = \log_{0.5} x$

UNIT 2WITHOUT AIDS · 4 LESSONS

2Graphs without aids

In the exploration $f(x) = -(x - 1)^2 + 4$ was drawn quickly: vertex $(1, 4)$, opening downwards, zeros at $-1$ and $3$. For $g$ the two asymptotes helped most. Nobody needed a table of values. If you recognise the basic function and its shifts, you almost have the graph already.

KEY POINT · SKETCHING WITHOUT AIDS

1. Recognise the basic function: $x^2$, $x^3$, $\tfrac1x$, $\sqrt{x}$, $2^x$, $\log_2 x$, $\sin x$ …

2. Read off the changes: shifted, stretched, reflected? For $a \cdot f(x - d) + e$: stretch $a$, $d$ to the right, $e$ upwards.

3. Special points and lines: points of intersection with the axes, vertex, maxima and minima, asymptotes and poles, period.

4. Two or three more points, join them smoothly, mind the behaviour for large $|x|$. 5. Check with the CAS.

Multiple zeros: for $(x - 3)^2$ the graph only touches the $x$-axis, for $(x - 3)^1$ it crosses it.

Good points are the ones you can work out in your head: for $2^x$ whole numbers, for $\log_2 x$ powers of two, for the sine multiples of $\tfrac{\pi}{2}$.

INTERACTIVE · SKETCHING STEP BY STEP

For each function, think it through yourself first, then reveal the steps. At the end the graph appears.

EXAMPLE 3 · A PARABOLA

Sketch $f(x) = -2(x + 1)^2 + 3$.

Vertex $(-1, 3)$, opening downwards, narrower than the standard parabola. $y$-axis: $f(0) = -2 + 3 = 1$, reflected $(-2, 1)$.

Zeros: $(x + 1)^2 = 1.5 \Rightarrow$ $x \approx 0.22$ and $x \approx -2.22$ (for the sketch “just to the right of 0” is enough).

EXAMPLE 4 · AN EXPONENTIAL FUNCTION

Sketch $g(x) = 3 \cdot 0.5^x + 1$.

Basic function $0.5^x$, decreasing, stretched by 3 and shifted up by 1: asymptote $y = 1$, no zero.

Points: $(0, 4)$, $(1, 2.5)$, $(-1, 7)$; to the right the graph nestles up to $y = 1$.

EXERCISES

Sketch without aids and give the special points: a) $(x - 2)^2 - 1$   b) $2^x + 1$   c) $-\sin x$ for $0 \leq x \leq 2\pi$   d) $\tfrac1x - 2$

a) vertex $(2, -1)$, zeros 1 and 3   b) asymptote $y = 1$, $(0, 2)$   c) minimum $\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2}, -1\right)$, maximum $\left(\tfrac{3\pi}{2}, 1\right)$, zeros $0$, $\pi$, $2\pi$   d) asymptotes $x = 0$ and $y = -2$, zero $\tfrac12$

Which of the functions $x^2$, $x^3$, $\sqrt{x}$, $\tfrac1x$, $2^x$ pass through $(1, 1)$, which through $(0, 0)$?

Through $(1, 1)$: $x^2$, $x^3$, $\sqrt{x}$, $\tfrac1x$ (not $2^x$, since $2^1 = 2$). Through $(0, 0)$: $x^2$, $x^3$, $\sqrt{x}$.

Sketch $f(x) = \log_2(x - 1) + 2$.

$D$: $x > 1$, asymptote $x = 1$. Zero: $\log_2(x - 1) = -2 \Rightarrow x = 1.25$. Points $(2, 2)$, $(3, 3)$, $(5, 4)$.

Sketch $f(x) = 3\sin(2x)$ in the interval $[0, \pi]$.

Period $\pi$, amplitude 3: maximum $\left(\tfrac{\pi}{4}, 3\right)$, minimum $\left(\tfrac{3\pi}{4}, -3\right)$, zeros $0$, $\tfrac{\pi}{2}$, $\pi$.

Sketch $f(x) = x(x - 3)^2$. Pay attention to the kind of zeros and the behaviour for large $|x|$.

Zero 0 (single, change of sign), zero 3 (double, the graph touches the axis). Multiplied out, the term begins with $x^3$: upwards on the right, downwards on the left. Points $(1, 4)$, $(4, 4)$, $(-1, -16)$.

REVIEW · DEEPEN · CONNECT

Make a page of your overview for each of the six classes of functions: a typical graph, the profile, an example from nature, technology or everyday life.

Individual solution. The widget “The classes of functions” shows a suggestion for each class.

Connecting with chapter 4.3: $f(x) = 2^{-x^2}$ is a composition. Give the inner and the outer function and write a profile.

Inner $-x^2$, outer $2^u$. Defined for all $x$, always positive, largest value $f(0) = 1$, symmetric about the $y$-axis, tending to 0 for large $|x|$: a bell curve with the asymptote $y = 0$.

How many solutions does the equation $\sin x = \lg x$ have? Justify with a sketch and find them with the graphing calculator.

$\lg x$ is only greater than 1 for $x > 10$.

For $x > 10$, $\lg x > 1 \geq \sin x$, so there are only solutions between 0 and 10: three of them, $x \approx 2.70$, $7.33$ and $8.26$.

A profile in reverse: we are looking for a function of the form $f(x) = \tfrac{a}{x - d} + e$ with the asymptotes $x = 1$ and $y = 2$ whose graph passes through the origin.

$d = 1$, $e = 2$. $f(0) = -a + 2 = 0 \Rightarrow a = 2$: $f(x) = \tfrac{2}{x - 1} + 2$

EXCURSIONHISTORY

Euler sorts the functions

For a long time a “function” was for mathematicians a curve rather than a rule of calculation. In 1748, in his textbook “Introduction to the Analysis of the Infinite”, Leonhard Euler put the concept at the beginning: a function of a variable quantity is an expression of calculation that is composed in some way of this quantity and fixed numbers.

Then he sorted, as in this chapter: algebraic functions, which manage with the basic operations of arithmetic and roots alone (polynomial, rational, root functions), and transcendental ones, to which the exponential and logarithmic functions and the trigonometric functions belong. Euler treated sine and cosine as functions on the circle with radius 1, just as you did in chapter 1.2. Many of the notations we use today go back to him.

Source: Leonhard Euler: “Introductio in analysin infinitorum”, volume 1, Lausanne 1748, chapter 1.
