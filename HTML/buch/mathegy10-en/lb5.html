Interest

Money in an account grows through interest, and anyone who borrows money pays interest. In this short chapter you connect percentages with exponential growth: you calculate interest and compound interest, draw up a repayment plan for a loan and find out what a loan really costs.

  • compare offers and judge what a loan really costs

UNIT 12 LESSONS

1Interest and compound interest

If the interest is paid out, €30 is added every year, so after 18 years $1000 + 18 \cdot 30 = 1540$ €. If it stays in the account, it is multiplied by $1.03$ every year: $1000 \cdot 1.03^{18} \approx 1702.43$ €. The difference of over €160 is interest on interest.

KEY POINT · INTEREST AND COMPOUND INTEREST

Annual interest: $Z = K \cdot \tfrac{p}{100}$ · for $m$ months: $Z = K \cdot \tfrac{p}{100} \cdot \tfrac{m}{12}$

Compound interest: if the interest is left in, it earns interest too: $$K_n = K_0 \cdot q^n \quad \text{with the interest factor } q = 1 + \tfrac{p}{100}$$

Rearranging: initial capital $K_0 = \tfrac{K_n}{q^n}$ · interest factor $q = \sqrt[n]{\tfrac{K_n}{K_0}}$ · term $n = \tfrac{\lg(K_n / K_0)}{\lg q}$

Rule of 72: at $p\,\%$ interest, capital doubles after about $\tfrac{72}{p}$ years. At 6 %, after about 12 years.

Whole years: if interest is only credited at the end of the year, a calculated term such as 7.4 years is rounded up to 8 years.

EXAMPLE 1 · FINAL CAPITAL

€1000 is invested for 18 years at 3 %. Compare simple interest and compound interest.

Simple: $1000 + 18 \cdot 30 = 1540$ €. Compound interest: $1000 \cdot 1.03^{18} \approx 1702.43$ €.

Compound interest brings €162.43 more.

EXAMPLE 2 · TERM

How long does it take for €5000 to grow to €6000 at 2.5 % interest?

$5000 \cdot 1.025^n = 6000 \Rightarrow 1.025^n = 1.2 \Rightarrow n = \tfrac{\lg 1.2}{\lg 1.025} \approx 7.4$.

With interest credited annually, the €6000 is reached after 8 years.

2Loan and repayment

When buying in instalments you pay $24 \cdot 41 = 984$ €, so €84 more, a good 9 % of the price. But because the full sum is only borrowed at the start and the debt falls with every instalment, the interest rate per year is higher than the 9 % over two years suggests. A repayment plan shows exactly how a loan runs.

KEY POINT · REPAYMENT

With a loan you pay interest every year on the remaining debt and repay part of it. Instalment = interest + repayment.

Equal repayments of capital: the same repayment $T = \tfrac{S_0}{n}$ every year; the interest and with it the instalments get smaller.

Repayment by annuity: the same instalment $A$ every year, the annuity. The interest part falls, the repayment part rises.

A repayment plan shows the remaining debt, interest, repayment and instalment year by year. What the loan costs is the sum of all the interest.

Spreadsheet: remaining debt in B2, interest =B2*6%, repayment =Instalment-C2, new remaining debt =B2-D2, then fill down.

Annuity: $A = S_0 \cdot \dfrac{q^n (q - 1)}{q^n - 1}$. You do not have to know the formula by heart; the widget and any spreadsheet (function PMT) work it out.

EXAMPLE 3 · EQUAL REPAYMENTS OF CAPITAL

€10,000 is paid back at 6 % in 5 years with equal repayments. Set up the instalments. What does the loan cost?

Repayment €2000 every year. Interest on the remaining debt: €600, 480, 360, 240 and 120. Instalments: €2600, 2480, 2360, 2240 and 2120.

Cost: €1800 interest.

EXAMPLE 4 · REPAYMENT BY ANNUITY

The same loan with equal instalments: the annuity is €2373.96. Set up the first year of the repayment plan and calculate the cost.

Year 1: interest $10{,}000 \cdot 0.06 = 600$ €, repayment $2373.96 - 600 = 1773.96$ €, remaining debt €8226.04.

In total $5 \cdot 2373.96 = 11{,}869.80$ €, so about €1869.80 interest. More than with equal repayments of capital, because the debt is paid off more slowly at first.

A current account is €1500 overdrawn for 3 months, and the bank charges 12 % per year for it (overdraft). What does that cost? Compare with an instalment loan at 4 %. What do you advise?

Overdraft: $1500 \cdot 0.12 \cdot \tfrac{3}{12} = 45$ €. At 4 % it would only be €15. An overdraft is meant for short gaps; in the long run it is very expensive.

REVIEW · DEEPEN · CONNECT

Check the rule of 72 for 6 % and for 4 %: after how many years does capital really double?

6 %: $\tfrac{\lg 2}{\lg 1.06} \approx 11.9$ years (rule of thumb 12). 4 %: $\approx 17.7$ years (rule of thumb 18).

Inflation: prices rise by 2 % per year. What is €100 still worth in 10 years, measured by what you can buy with it today?

$\tfrac{100}{1.02^{10}} \approx 82.03$ €. So without interest, money loses value.

Connecting with chapter 4.5: at the end of every year €1200 is paid into an account at 3 % interest. Give the balances as a recursive sequence and use the widget (savings plan) or a table to calculate the balance after 10 years.

$K_0 = 0$, $K_{n+1} = 1.03 \cdot K_n + 1200$. After 10 years about €13,756.66, of which €12,000 was paid in.

Which is better: €10,000 now or €11,500 in 4 years, if you can invest the money at 3 %? Justify with the present value $K_0 = \tfrac{K_n}{q^n}$.

Present value of €11,500 in 4 years: $\tfrac{11{,}500}{1.03^4} \approx 10{,}217.60$ €. That is more than €10,000: the later payment is better.

EXCURSIONECONOMIC HISTORY

Pacioli’s rule of 72

In 1494 a thick arithmetic book for merchants appeared in Venice, the “Summa de arithmetica” of the Franciscan friar Luca Pacioli. It summed up the mathematical knowledge of the Italian trading cities and contains, among other things, the first printed account of double-entry bookkeeping.

In the middle of it stands a rule without any justification: if you want to know in how many years capital will double, divide 72 by the interest rate. At 6 % it is 12 years. Today we can explain it: the exact doubling time is $\tfrac{\lg 2}{\lg(1 + p/100)}$, and for small interest rates this value is close to $\tfrac{70}{p}$. 72 is taken because it can be divided exactly by 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9 and 12. Since Pacioli does not derive the rule, it was probably already known among merchants before him.

Source: Luca Pacioli: “Summa de arithmetica, geometria, proportioni et proportionalità”, Venice 1494, folio 181, no. 44.

INTEREST

$Z = K \cdot \tfrac{p}{100}$, for $m$ months times $\tfrac{m}{12}$.

COMPOUND INTEREST

$K_n = K_0 \cdot q^n$ with $q = 1 + \tfrac{p}{100}$

REARRANGING

$K_0 = \tfrac{K_n}{q^n}$, $q = \sqrt[n]{\tfrac{K_n}{K_0}}$, $n = \tfrac{\lg(K_n/K_0)}{\lg q}$

€5000 → €6000 at 2.5 %: after 8 years

COST OF A LOAN

The sum of all the interest. A short term and a low interest rate save money.
