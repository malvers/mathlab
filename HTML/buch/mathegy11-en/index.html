CONTENTSIN THE ORDER OF THE SCHOOL YEAR

The chapters follow the teaching plan of Year 11 in the basic course: first differential calculus (learning area 1), from limits via the rules of differentiation to investigating functions. Then come matrices (learning area 2), because with them you solve the systems of equations for functions made to measure. The first half of Year 11 ends with numerical methods (elective 2). In the second half of the year you go into space: vectors, lines and planes (learning area 3), and finally multi-stage random experiments and the binomial distribution (learning area 4). The chapter numbers follow the curriculum, which is why chapter 2 comes between 1.4 and 1.5. On the right you can see when each chapter is due and how many self-checks you have already solved.

HOW TO USE THIS BOOK
EVERY CHAPTER HAS THE SAME STRUCTURE
  1. Opener with learning goals and a check-in: what you should already be able to do.
  2. Explorations: open questions to try out, ideally in groups.
  3. Units: starter, explanation, key-point box, examples, interactive elements, exercises and “Test yourself!”.
  4. Review · Deepen · Connect: mixed exercises.
  5. Excursion: history, an application or a nice puzzle.
  6. Looking back on one page and a test with two rounds.
THE SYMBOLS

Every exercise has a hint and the solution to fold out. Try it yourself first.

In the self-checks you type in your result. Fractions ($3/10$), decimals ($0.3$) and percentages ($30\%$) are all understood. Your browser remembers what you have solved.

In the basic course you work with a CAS. What has to work without aids is set out in the curriculum and marked as such in the book. Labs open in a new tab. There you can experiment for longer and more freely than in the book. The labs and the weekly exercise sheets are in German for now.

KEY POINT

Whatever is in an orange box, you need to know. Everything else helps you to understand it.
