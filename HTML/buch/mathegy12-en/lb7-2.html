Angles of intersection

How steep is a ramp, how flat a roof, at what angle does a ray of light hit the ground? With the scalar product you calculate angles of intersection between two lines, between a line and a plane and between two planes. The trick is the same every time: find the right angle between direction or normal vectors and turn it into the one you are looking for.

UNIT 14 LESSONS

1Angles between lines and between a line and a plane

Two intersecting lines form two angles that add up to $180^\circ$. The angle of intersection is the smaller one, so at most $90^\circ$. The scalar product gives the angle between the direction vectors. If it is obtuse because one vector points “the wrong way”, the absolute value in the numerator turns it into the acute one.

For a line and a plane you measure the angle between the line and its perpendicular projection onto the plane. The detour via the normal vector is easier: the angle $\alpha$ between the line and the normal adds up with the angle $\varphi$ you want to $90^\circ$. Because $\cos\alpha = \sin(90^\circ - \alpha) = \sin\varphi$, the formula contains a sine.

KEY POINT · ANGLES OF INTERSECTION WITH LINES

Line and line with the direction vectors $\vec u$, $\vec v$: $\cos\varphi = \dfrac{|\vec u \circ \vec v|}{|\vec u| \cdot |\vec v|}$

Line and plane with direction vector $\vec u$ and normal vector $\vec n$: $\sin\varphi = \dfrac{|\vec u \circ \vec n|}{|\vec u| \cdot |\vec n|}$

Always $0^\circ \le \varphi \le 90^\circ$. If $\vec u \circ \vec n = 0$, the line is parallel to the plane or lies in it.

Point of intersection first? You do not need it for the angle, only the directions. For skew lines people still often speak of the angle between their directions.

Angle of inclination: The angle of a line with the $x$-$y$ plane is its gradient in space; the normal vector is then $(0, 0, 1)$.

EXAMPLE 1 · TWO LINES

$g\colon \vec x = (1, 2, 0) + t(1, 2, 2)$ and $h\colon \vec x = (1, 2, 0) + s(0, 0, 1)$ intersect at $(1, 2, 0)$. At what angle?

$\cos\varphi = \dfrac{|(1, 2, 2) \circ (0, 0, 1)|}{3 \cdot 1} = \tfrac23$

$\varphi \approx 48.2^\circ$

EXAMPLE 2 · LINE THROUGH A PLANE

Where and at what angle does $g\colon \vec x = t(1, 2, 2)$ pierce the plane $E\colon 2x - y + 2z = 3$?

Substituting: $2t - 2t + 4t = 3$, $t = \tfrac34$: $S(0.75, 1.5, 1.5)$.

$\sin\varphi = \dfrac{|2 - 2 + 4|}{3 \cdot 3} = \tfrac49$: $\varphi \approx 26.4^\circ$. To the normal it is $63.6^\circ$.

EXERCISES

At what angle do lines with the direction vectors a) $(1, 0, 0)$ and $(1, 1, 0)$   b) $(1, 2, 2)$ and $(2, 1, -2)$ intersect?

a) $45^\circ$   b) scalar product $0$: $90^\circ$

What angle does the $z$-axis make with the plane $x + y + z = 1$?

$\sin\varphi = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}$: $\varphi \approx 35.3^\circ$

A pyramid has the base with the corners $(\pm 1, \pm 1, 0)$ and the apex $S(0, 0, 2)$. What angle does a lateral edge make with the base?

Edge $(-1, -1, 2)$, length $\sqrt6$: $\sin\varphi = \tfrac{2}{\sqrt6}$, $\varphi \approx 54.7^\circ$.

The direction vectors of two intersecting lines enclose $135^\circ$. What is the angle of intersection? Why is there an absolute value in the formula?

$45^\circ$. Without the absolute value the cosine would be negative and you would get the obtuse supplementary angle.

a) What is the angle of inclination of a line with the direction vector $(3, 4, 5)$? b) After take-off an aeroplane climbs in the direction $(3, 4, 1.25)$. At what angle?

a) $\sin\varphi = \tfrac{5}{\sqrt{50}}$, $45^\circ$: horizontally $5$, vertically $5$. b) $\sin\varphi = \tfrac{1.25}{\sqrt{26.5625}}$, $\varphi \approx 14.0^\circ$.

2Angles between planes and inclinations on solids

A roof surface passes through $(0, 0, 3)$, $(10, 0, 3)$ and $(0, 4, 6)$. Over $4$ m horizontally it rises by $3$ m: $\tan\varphi = \tfrac34$, so $\varphi \approx 36.9^\circ$. With vectors: normal vector $(0, -3, 4)$, floor $(0, 0, 1)$, $\cos\varphi = \tfrac45$, the same angle. The angle between the planes is the angle between their normals.

It is measured across the line of intersection, in a plane perpendicular to it. Because a normal vector may point either way, you again take the absolute value and get an angle between $0^\circ$ and $90^\circ$. On solids you are often interested in the interior angle at an edge. It can be obtuse; then it is the supplementary angle of the angle of intersection.

KEY POINT · ANGLE OF INTERSECTION OF TWO PLANES

$$\cos\varphi = \frac{|\vec n_1 \circ \vec n_2|}{|\vec n_1| \cdot |\vec n_2|}, \quad 0^\circ \le \varphi \le 90^\circ$$

Inclination to the floor $z = 0$: $\cos\varphi = \dfrac{|n_3|}{|\vec n|}$

Parallel normal vectors: the planes are parallel or identical, $\varphi = 0^\circ$. Scalar product $0$: they are perpendicular to each other.

The line of intersection has the direction vector $\vec n_1 \times \vec n_2$; the widget draws it in white.

Roofers give the pitch in degrees, road builders the gradient in per cent: $10\,\%$ means $\tan\varphi = 0.1$.

EXAMPLE 3 · TWO SLANTED PLANES

Calculate the angle of intersection of $E_1\colon 2x + y + 2z = 3$ and $E_2\colon 2x - y + 2z = 1$.

$\vec n_1 \circ \vec n_2 = 4 - 1 + 4 = 7$, both magnitudes $3$: $\cos\varphi = \tfrac79$.

$\varphi \approx 38.9^\circ$

EXAMPLE 4 · ANGLES ON THE PYRAMID

Pyramid with the base corners $(\pm 1, \pm 1, 0)$ and the apex $(0, 0, 2)$: what angle does a lateral face make with the base, and what angle do two neighbouring lateral faces make?

Normal vectors $(2, 0, 1)$ and $(0, 2, 1)$. With the floor: $\cos\varphi = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt5}$, $\varphi \approx 63.4^\circ$.

With each other: $\cos\varphi = \tfrac15$, angle of intersection $78.5^\circ$; the interior angle at the edge is $180^\circ - 78.5^\circ = 101.5^\circ$.

EXERCISES

Calculate the angle of intersection of the planes $z = 0$ and $x + z = 1$.

$\cos\varphi = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}$: $45^\circ$

A slope lies in the plane $x + y + z = 10$; the $z$-axis points upwards. How steep is it?

$\cos\varphi = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}$: $\varphi \approx 54.7^\circ$, a very steep slope.

Calculate the angle of intersection of $x = 0$ and $x + y + \sqrt2\,z = 0$.

$|(1, 1, \sqrt2)| = 2$: $\cos\varphi = \tfrac12$, $60^\circ$.

For which $a$ are $x + y + az = 1$ and $x - y + z = 0$ perpendicular to each other?

$1 - 1 + a = 0$: $a = 0$

A gable roof has two roof surfaces, each with a pitch of $40^\circ$. What angle do the roof surfaces enclose on the inside at the ridge, and what is their angle of intersection?

The cross-section is an isosceles triangle with base angles $40^\circ$: inside at the ridge $100^\circ$, angle of intersection of the planes $80^\circ$.

For which $a$ does a line with the direction vector $(1, 0, a)$ make an angle of $45^\circ$ with the $x$-$y$ plane? Interpret both solutions.

EXCURSIONAPPLICATION

The slope of the Great Pyramid

The Great Pyramid of Giza was built around 2600 BC. According to what we know today, it was planned with a square base of side $440$ royal cubits and a height of $280$ cubits. The master builders gave the slope as the seked, the horizontal run per cubit of height, here $5\tfrac12$ palms per cubit of $7$ palms.

With vectors: base corners $(\pm 220, \pm 220, 0)$, apex $(0, 0, 280)$. A lateral face has the normal vector $(14, 0, 11)$, and its inclination to the ground is $\cos\varphi = \tfrac{11}{\sqrt{14^2 + 11^2}}$, so $\varphi \approx 51.8^\circ$. The measured value is $51^\circ 50' 40''$. The edges are flatter: $\tan\varphi = \tfrac{280}{220\sqrt2}$, $\varphi \approx 42.0^\circ$.

Source: Wikipedia (English), article “Great Pyramid of Giza”, sections on dimensions and seked.
