Calculating with fractions and decimals

A recipe is for four people, but there are six of you. A board is 2.4 m long and is to be sawn into pieces of 0.3 m. For questions like these you need the four operations with fractions and decimals. You calculate in your head, on paper and with the calculator, and you see why multiplying sometimes makes things smaller and dividing sometimes makes them bigger.

UNIT 14 LESSONS

1Adding and subtracting

You cannot simply add halves and thirds. The pieces are of different sizes. If you cut both pizzas into sixths, it works: $\tfrac12 = \tfrac36$ and $\tfrac13 = \tfrac26$. Together that is $\tfrac56$ of a pizza.

Fractions with the same denominator have a common denominator. With them you only add or subtract the numerators. The denominator stays, because the pieces stay the same size.

If the denominators are different, you first give the fractions a common denominator. The easiest is the lowest common denominator: the lowest common multiple of the denominators.

With decimals you line up the decimal points. Then tenths are under tenths and hundredths under hundredths. You fill missing places with 0.

KEY POINT · ADDING AND SUBTRACTING

Same denominator: $\tfrac{a}{n} + \tfrac{b}{n} = \tfrac{a + b}{n}$   ·   $\tfrac{a}{n} - \tfrac{b}{n} = \tfrac{a - b}{n}$

Different denominators: first expand to the lowest common denominator. $\tfrac34 - \tfrac16 = \tfrac{9}{12} - \tfrac{2}{12} = \tfrac{7}{12}$

Decimals: line up the decimal points, then calculate as with natural numbers.

Mixed numbers: calculate the whole numbers and the fractions separately. $2\tfrac12 + 1\tfrac34 = 3 + \tfrac24 + \tfrac34 = 3 + \tfrac54 = 4\tfrac14$

Typical mistake: $\tfrac12 + \tfrac13 = \tfrac25$? No! $\tfrac25$ is even less than $\tfrac12$.

INTERACTIVE · FINDING A COMMON DENOMINATOR

The strips are divided into as many parts as the lowest common denominator says. Change the fractions and switch to “minus”.

2Multiplying

$\tfrac23$ of a garden is planted, and $\tfrac34$ of that is lawn. What part of the whole garden is lawn? Draw a square, divide it vertically into thirds and horizontally into quarters. The lawn is 6 of 12 boxes: $\tfrac34$ of $\tfrac23$ is $\tfrac{6}{12} = \tfrac12$.

With fractions the word “of” means times: $\tfrac34$ of $\tfrac23$ is $\tfrac34 \cdot \tfrac23$. You can see the rule in the picture: the numerators give the coloured boxes, the denominators all the boxes.

Before working it out, it pays to simplify: a numerator and a denominator may also be divided crosswise by the same number.

With decimals you first calculate without the decimal point. Then the result gets as many decimal places as the two factors have together.

KEY POINT · MULTIPLYING

Fraction times fraction: numerator times numerator, denominator times denominator. $\tfrac{a}{b} \cdot \tfrac{c}{d} = \tfrac{a \cdot c}{b \cdot d}$

Fraction times natural number: only multiply the numerator. $\tfrac35 \cdot 2 = \tfrac65$

Decimals: calculate without the decimal point, add up the decimal places. $0.2 \cdot 0.3 = 0.06$ (1 + 1 = 2 places)

Smaller by multiplying: if you multiply by a number less than 1, you get a smaller result. Half of 8 is 4.

Simplifying crosswise: $\tfrac49 \cdot \tfrac38 = \tfrac{\cancel{4}^{\,1}}{\cancel{9}_{\,3}} \cdot \tfrac{\cancel{3}^{\,1}}{\cancel{8}_{\,2}} = \tfrac16$

INTERACTIVE · A PART OF A PART

Orange is the first fraction, blue the second. Green is both: the product.

EXAMPLE 3 · SIMPLIFY FIRST

Calculate $\tfrac{4}{9} \cdot \tfrac{3}{8}$.

Divide 4 and 8 by 4, divide 3 and 9 by 3: $\tfrac{1}{3} \cdot \tfrac{1}{2}$.

$\tfrac{4}{9} \cdot \tfrac{3}{8} = \tfrac16$. Without simplifying: $\tfrac{12}{72}$, which is more work.

EXAMPLE 4 · WITH A DECIMAL POINT

Calculate $2.4 \cdot 0.15$. Estimate first.

Estimate: $2 \cdot 0.15 = 0.3$. Without the decimal point: $24 \cdot 15 = 360$. Places: 1 + 2 = 3.

$2.4 \cdot 0.15 = 0.360 = 0.36$

3Dividing

$1\tfrac12$ litres are 6 quarter litres. So you can fill 6 glasses: $\tfrac32 : \tfrac14 = 6$. Division asks: how many times does $\tfrac14$ fit into $\tfrac32$? You get the same result with $\tfrac32 \cdot 4 = 6$.

Dividing by $\tfrac14$ is the same as multiplying by 4. In general: you divide by a fraction by multiplying by its reciprocal. You get the reciprocal by swapping numerator and denominator: the reciprocal of $\tfrac23$ is $\tfrac32$.

With decimals you move the decimal point the same number of places to the right in both numbers until you are dividing by a natural number. The result does not change: $3.6 : 0.4 = 36 : 4 = 9$.

KEY POINT · DIVIDING

By a fraction: multiply by the reciprocal. $\tfrac{a}{b} : \tfrac{c}{d} = \tfrac{a}{b} \cdot \tfrac{d}{c}$

Fraction by a natural number: multiply the denominator. $\tfrac34 : 3 = \tfrac{3}{12} = \tfrac14$

Decimals: move the decimal point the same number of places in both numbers. $7.2 : 0.06 = 720 : 6 = 120$

You cannot divide by $0$.

Bigger by dividing: if you divide by a number less than 1, you get more out. 6 halves fit into 3: $3 : \tfrac12 = 6$.

Check: the result times the divisor must give the first number again. $6 \cdot \tfrac14 = \tfrac32$ ✓

INTERACTIVE · HOW MANY TIMES DOES IT FIT?

EXAMPLE 5 · WITH THE RECIPROCAL

Calculate $\tfrac56 : \tfrac23$.

$\tfrac56 : \tfrac23 = \tfrac56 \cdot \tfrac32 = \tfrac{15}{12} = \tfrac54$.

$\tfrac56 : \tfrac23 = 1\tfrac14$. Check: $\tfrac54 \cdot \tfrac23 = \tfrac{10}{12} = \tfrac56$ ✓

EXAMPLE 6 · MOVING THE DECIMAL POINT

A board is $2.4$ m long. How many pieces of $0.3$ m do you get?

$2.4 : 0.3$. Both decimal points one place to the right: $24 : 3 = 8$.

8 pieces. Check: $8 \cdot 0.3 = 2.4$ ✓

How many cups of $0.2$ ℓ can you fill from $1.5$ ℓ of cocoa? What is left over?

$1.5 : 0.2 = 15 : 2 = 7.5$. So 7 full cups; there is only enough to fill an eighth cup halfway: $0.1$ ℓ is left over.

$\tfrac34$ of a bar of chocolate is shared fairly between three friends. How much does each get? How much would each get if a fourth friend joins?

$\tfrac34 : 3 = \tfrac14$ of the bar. With four people: $\tfrac34 : 4 = \tfrac{3}{16}$ of the bar.

Ben says: “Dividing always makes things smaller.” Find two counterexamples. When is $a : b$ larger than $a$?

For example $6 : \tfrac12 = 12$ and $1 : 0.1 = 10$. The quotient is larger than $a$ if $b$ is less than 1 (and greater than 0).

EXCURSIONHISTORY

The little book of tenths

450 years ago merchants, land surveyors and wine traders calculated with many different fractions. That was laborious: every time you had to find a common denominator. In 1585 the Flemish engineer Simon Stevin published a little book with the title “De Thiende”, in English “The Tenth”.

His idea: you calculate only with tenths, hundredths, thousandths. Then you can calculate on paper with fractions just as with natural numbers. Stevin did not yet know the decimal point. After each digit he wrote a small number in a circle that gave its place. $3.27$ became “3 ⓪ 2 ① 7 ②”. Stevin even suggested dividing measures, weights and money into tenths. Today we do that: €1 has 100 cents.

Stevin did not invent decimals. The Arab mathematician al-Uqlidisi was already using them in the 10th century. But only Stevin’s little book made them known in Europe.

Source: Simon Stevin, De Thiende, Leiden 1585, in the same year in French as “La Disme”. English translation by Robert Norton, London 1608.
