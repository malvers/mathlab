Proportional assignments

Twice as many apples cost twice as much. Twice as many guests get only half as much cake. Assignments like these are called proportional. If you recognise them, you can work out the fourth number from three known ones: with the rule of three. But careful: not everything that starts with “the more” is proportional.

UNIT 14 LESSONS

1Directly proportional

Apples cost €2.50 per kilogram. 2 kg cost €5, 4 kg cost €10, half of 4 kg costs half of €10. In every row of the table $\text{price} : \text{mass} = 2.5$. That is the price for 1 kg.

An assignment is called directly proportional if double goes with double, triple with triple and half with half. Then the quotient $y : x$ is the same for all pairs of values. It is called the constant of proportionality.

In the coordinate system all the points lie on a straight line through the origin, because for $x = 0$, $y = 0$ as well.

“The more, the more” alone is not enough. For a taxi with a basic fare, 2 km do not cost twice as much as 1 km.

KEY POINT · DIRECTLY PROPORTIONAL

$k$ times $x$ goes with $k$ times $y$.

Equal quotients: $\dfrac{y_1}{x_1} = \dfrac{y_2}{x_2} = \ldots = m$, so $y = m \cdot x$

Graph: the points lie on a straight line through the origin $(0, 0)$.

Checking: in each row of the table work out $y : x$. If the same number always comes out, the assignment is directly proportional.

Examples: quantity → price, time → distance at the same speed, number of laps → distance run.

INTERACTIVE · QUOTIENT AND STRAIGHT LINE

Drag the yellow point. Compare “Apples” with “Taxi”: where does the quotient stay the same?

EXAMPLE 1 · CHECKING WITH THE QUOTIENT

Is the assignment directly proportional?   litres of petrol: 10, 20, 35 → price in €: 17, 34, 59.50

$17 : 10 = 1.7$ · $34 : 20 = 1.7$ · $59.50 : 35 = 1.7$. The quotient is always the same.

directly proportional, $y = 1.7 \cdot x$ (1 litre costs €1.70)

EXAMPLE 2 · NOT PROPORTIONAL

Taxi: 1 km costs €5, 2 km cost €7, 4 km cost €11. Is that directly proportional?

$5 : 1 = 5$, but $7 : 2 = 3.5$. The quotient changes. 2 km do not cost double 1 km.

not proportional. The basic fare of €3 is to blame.

The point $(4, 6)$ lies in the chart of a directly proportional assignment. Give three more points. Which $y$-value goes with $x = 1$?

Quotient $6 : 4 = 1.5$. For example $(2, 3)$, $(8, 12)$, $(10, 15)$. With $x = 1$ goes $y = 1.5$.

2Inversely proportional

24 pieces of cake: with 2 people each gets 12, with 4 people 6, with 8 people 3. Twice as many people, half as much cake. In every row $\text{people} \cdot \text{pieces} = 24$. Of course, it is always the same cake.

An assignment is called inversely proportional if double goes with half and triple with a third. Then the product $x \cdot y$ is the same for all pairs of values.

The graph is not a straight line but a curve. It falls steeply at first, then more and more gently, and never touches the axes.

Here too: not every “the more, the less” is inversely proportional. For the candle from chapter 2.1 “the longer, the shorter” holds, but the product is not the same.

KEY POINT · INVERSELY PROPORTIONAL

$k$ times $x$ goes with the $k$-th part of $y$.

Equal products: $x_1 \cdot y_1 = x_2 \cdot y_2 = \ldots = c$, so $y = c : x$

Graph: a curve that comes closer and closer to the axes but never reaches them.

Checking: in each row work out $x \cdot y$. Always the same number? Then it is inversely proportional.

Examples: number of people → share of an amount, speed → travel time for a fixed distance, number of pumps → time to empty.

INTERACTIVE · PRODUCT AND CURVE

Drag the yellow point to the right: more people, less cake. The last column always shows the same number.

EXAMPLE 3 · CHECKING WITH THE PRODUCT

For 240 km you need 4 hours at 60 km/h, 3 hours at 80 km/h, 2 hours at 120 km/h. Inversely proportional?

$60 \cdot 4 = 240$ · $80 \cdot 3 = 240$ · $120 \cdot 2 = 240$. The product is always the distance.

inversely proportional, $t = 240 : v$

EXAMPLE 4 · COMPLETING A TABLE

6 pumps empty a pool in 8 hours. How long do 2, 3, 4 and 12 pumps take?

Product: $6 \cdot 8 = 48$ pump-hours. So $48 : 2 = 24$, $48 : 3 = 16$, $48 : 4 = 12$, $48 : 12 = 4$.

24 h, 16 h, 12 h, 4 h

EXERCISES

A prize of €120 is shared fairly. Make a table for 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 and 8 people. Check that the products are equal.

€120, 60, 40, 30, 24, 20, 15. The product is always 120.

Directly proportional, inversely proportional or neither? a) 2 → 30, 3 → 20, 6 → 10   b) 2 → 30, 3 → 45, 6 → 90   c) 2 → 30, 3 → 25, 6 → 10

a) product always 60: inverse   b) quotient always 15: direct   c) products 60, 75, 60 and different quotients: neither

A rectangle is to have an area of 36 cm². Make a table for the length $a$ and the width $b$ (at least five pairs) and draw the graph.

For example 1 → 36, 2 → 18, 3 → 12, 4 → 9, 6 → 6, 9 → 4, 12 → 3. Inversely proportional, $a \cdot b = 36$. The graph is a curve.

For a class trip the coach costs €600. How much does each person pay with 20, 24 and 25 travellers? How many have to come so that each person pays only €20?

€30, €25, €24. For €20 per person: $600 : 20 = 30$ travellers.

In an inversely proportional assignment the value $y = 1.2$ goes with $x = 5$. Which $y$-value goes with $x = 0.5$? And which $x$-value with $y = 10$?

Product $5 \cdot 1.2 = 6$. With $x = 0.5$: $y = 6 : 0.5 = 12$. With $y = 10$: $x = 6 : 10 = 0.6$.

UNIT 34 LESSONS

3The rule of three

3 km in 45 minutes. For 1 km the class needs a third of that, so 15 minutes. For 9 km nine times as long: 135 minutes. Three lines, and you have arrived. That is why the method is called the rule of three (in Britain also the unitary method).

With the rule of three you first work back to one and then up to the quantity you want. Whether you do the same calculation on the right-hand side or the opposite one depends on the kind of assignment.

Before calculating, you have to check whether the assignment is proportional at all. Otherwise the rule of three is wrong.

KEY POINT · RULE OF THREE

directly proportional: the same calculation on the left and the right.   3 kg → €7.50   | : 3   1 kg → €2.50   | · 5   5 kg → €12.50

inversely proportional: the opposite calculation on the right.   4 pumps → 6 h   | : 4 and · 4   1 pump → 24 h   | · 3 and : 3   3 pumps → 8 h

Instead of the rule of three you can also use equal quotients $\tfrac{7.50}{3} = \tfrac{y}{5}$ or equal products $4 \cdot 6 = 3 \cdot y$.

Counterexample: a cockerel crows for one minute. How long do three cockerels crow? Also one minute. Not every question is a rule-of-three problem.

Shortcut: sometimes you do not have to go back to 1. 4 kg → €10, so 2 kg → €5.

INTERACTIVE · THREE LINES

Choose a problem and go step by step. Watch the arrows on the left and the right.

EXAMPLE 5 · DIRECT

5 exercise books cost €4. How much do 8 exercise books cost?

5 books → €4   | : 5   1 book → €0.80   | · 8   8 books → €6.40

8 exercise books cost €6.40.

EXAMPLE 6 · INVERSE

A supply of food lasts 12 rabbits for 10 days. How long does it last 8 rabbits?

12 rabbits → 10 days   | : 12, right · 12   1 rabbit → 120 days   | · 8, right : 8   8 rabbits → 15 days

The supply lasts 15 days.

EXERCISES

a) 4 rolls cost €1.60. What do 7 rolls cost?   b) 3 painters paint a hall in 8 days. How long do 4 painters take?

a) 1 roll €0.40, 7 rolls €2.80   b) 1 painter 24 days, 4 painters 6 days

A car uses 6 ℓ of petrol per 100 km. How much does it use for 250 km? How far does it get with 45 ℓ?

250 km: $2.5 \cdot 6 = 15$ ℓ. With 45 ℓ: $45 : 6 = 7.5$, so $750$ km.

A recipe for 4 people needs 300 g of pasta and $\tfrac34$ ℓ of sauce. Convert the recipe for 6 people.

6 people is $1.5$ times as many: 450 g of pasta and $\tfrac34 \cdot \tfrac32 = \tfrac98$ ℓ, so about $1.1$ ℓ of sauce.

Is this a rule-of-three problem? Justify. a) An egg boils in 5 minutes. How long do 4 eggs take in the same pan?   b) A runner does 100 m in 15 seconds. How long does he take for 10 km?

a) No, 4 eggs boiling at the same time also take 5 minutes.   b) No, a runner cannot keep up his sprinting pace for 10 km. The assignment is not proportional.

A supply of hay lasts 6 horses 20 days. After 5 days 4 more horses arrive. How long does the rest last?

After 5 days the rest would last 6 horses another 15 days.

Rest: 15 days for 6 horses, that is $90$ horse-days. For 10 horses: $90 : 10 = 9$ days.

REVIEW · DEEPEN · CONNECT

A printer prints 18 pages in 3 minutes. How many pages in 10 minutes? How long does it take for 45 pages?

6 pages per minute: 60 pages in 10 minutes, 45 pages in $7.5$ minutes.

Connecting with chapter 1.2: $\tfrac34$ kg of cheese cost €9. What does $\tfrac25$ kg cost?

1 kg: $9 : \tfrac34 = 12$ €. $\tfrac25$ kg: $\tfrac25 \cdot 12 = 4.80$ €.

Connecting with chapter 2.1: in 50 throws the six came up 12 times. Based on this result, how often would you expect a six in 200 throws? And how often with a fair dice?

Based on the result: $12 \cdot 4 = 48$. With a fair dice about $\tfrac16 \cdot 200 \approx 33$. 12 out of 50 is quite a lot for a fair dice, but still possible.

Gearwheels: a wheel with 40 teeth drives a wheel with 10 teeth. How often does the small wheel turn when the large one goes round once? How are the number of teeth and the number of turns related?

Four times. Number of teeth times number of turns is the same for both ($40 \cdot 1 = 10 \cdot 4$): number of teeth → number of turns is inversely proportional. That is how the gears on a bicycle work.

EXCURSIONHISTORY

How big is the Earth?

More than 2200 years ago the scholar Eratosthenes lived in Alexandria in Egypt. He had heard that in the city of Syene in the south the sun stands exactly overhead at noon on the longest day of the year. In Alexandria a stick cast a shadow at the same time: there the sun’s rays came in at a slant of $\tfrac{1}{50}$ of a full circle, that is $7.2^\circ$.

Eratosthenes thought proportionally: the angle is $\tfrac{1}{50}$ of the circle, so the way from Syene to Alexandria is also $\tfrac{1}{50}$ of the Earth’s circumference. The way was about 5000 stadia long (a stadion is an old measure of length). So the Earth’s circumference is $50 \cdot 5000 = 250\,000$ stadia.

Exactly how long a stadion was is not known for certain today. But with the usual assumptions his result is astonishingly close to the 40,000 km we know today.

Check it: how many degrees are $\tfrac{1}{50}$ of $360^\circ$? How long would a stadion be if 250,000 stadia were exactly 40,000 km?

$360^\circ : 50 = 7.2^\circ$. $40\,000$ km $: 250\,000 = 0.16$ km $= 160$ m.

Source: Eratosthenes’ own work “On the Measurement of the Earth” is lost. His method is handed down by Cleomedes in “Caelestia” (Book I, chapter 7). Other ancient authors give 252,000 stadia.
