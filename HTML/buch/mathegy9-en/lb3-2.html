Trigonometry in right-angled triangles

How tall is a tree if you only know your distance from it and one angle? How steep is a road with a 12 % gradient? How was the height of Mount Everest measured without climbing it? With sine, cosine and tangent you work out lengths from angles and the other way round, calculate areas and regular polygons and approximate $\pi$ the way Archimedes did.

UNIT 14 LESSONS

1Sine, cosine and tangent

All right-angled triangles with an angle of 35° are similar: they have the same angles, only their size differs. In similar triangles corresponding sides are in the same ratio. That is why $\tfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$ depends only on the angle; for 35° it is about $0.574$. This ratio gets a name.

Seen from an acute angle $\alpha$, the leg next to $\alpha$ is called the adjacent side, the one facing it the opposite side. For the other acute angle the two swap roles.

KEY POINT · SINE, COSINE, TANGENT

In a right-angled triangle, for an acute angle $\alpha$:

$$\sin\alpha = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}} \qquad \cos\alpha = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}} \qquad \tan\alpha = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$$

Relationships: $\tan\alpha = \dfrac{\sin\alpha}{\cos\alpha}$ · $\sin^2\alpha + \cos^2\alpha = 1$ · $\sin\alpha = \cos(90^\circ - \alpha)$

The angle from a ratio: with the inverse keys $\sin^{-1}$, $\cos^{-1}$, $\tan^{-1}$ of the calculator.

Calculator on DEG! In this chapter we work in degrees. If the calculator is set to RAD, $\sin 30$ gives the value $-0.988$ instead of $0.5$.

Memory aid: SOH CAH TOA: sine is opposite over hypotenuse, cosine adjacent over hypotenuse, tangent opposite over adjacent.

Special values: $\sin 30^\circ = \tfrac12$, $\sin 45^\circ = \tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$, $\sin 60^\circ = \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$, $\tan 45^\circ = 1$.

INTERACTIVE · THREE RATIOS
EXAMPLE 1 · IN THE 3-4-5 TRIANGLE

A right-angled triangle has the legs $a = 3$, $b = 4$ and the hypotenuse $c = 5$. Find $\sin\alpha$, $\cos\alpha$, $\tan\alpha$ and $\alpha$.

$\sin\alpha = \tfrac35 = 0.6$, $\cos\alpha = \tfrac45 = 0.8$, $\tan\alpha = \tfrac34 = 0.75$.

$\alpha = \sin^{-1}(0.6) \approx$ $36.87^\circ$. Check: $0.6^2 + 0.8^2 = 1$.

EXAMPLE 2 · SPECIAL VALUES

Justify $\sin 30^\circ = \tfrac12$ and $\cos 30^\circ = \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$ using an equilateral triangle.

If you halve an equilateral triangle with side 2, you get a right-angled one with the angles 30°, 60°, 90°, the hypotenuse 2 and the short leg 1. The long leg is $\sqrt{4 - 1} = \sqrt3$.

$\sin 30^\circ = \tfrac12$, $\cos 30^\circ = \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$, and likewise $\cos 60^\circ = \tfrac12$.

Explain why $0 < \sin\alpha < 1$ for every acute angle. For which angles is $\tan\alpha > 1$?

The opposite side is shorter than the hypotenuse and longer than zero. $\tan\alpha > 1$ if the opposite side is longer than the adjacent side, that is for $\alpha > 45^\circ$.

Justify $\sin 45^\circ = \cos 45^\circ = \tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$ and $\tan 45^\circ = 1$ using half a square.

The diagonal divides a square with side 1 into two isosceles right-angled triangles with hypotenuse $\sqrt2$. So $\sin 45^\circ = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} = \tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$, the cosine likewise, and $\tan 45^\circ = \tfrac11 = 1$.

Prove $\sin^2\alpha + \cos^2\alpha = 1$ and $\tan\alpha = \dfrac{\sin\alpha}{\cos\alpha}$. Use them to calculate $\cos\alpha$ and $\tan\alpha$ if $\sin\alpha = 0.28$.

$\left(\tfrac{a}{c}\right)^2 + \left(\tfrac{b}{c}\right)^2 = \tfrac{a^2 + b^2}{c^2} = 1$ by Pythagoras. $\tfrac{a/c}{b/c} = \tfrac{a}{b}$. $\cos\alpha = \sqrt{1 - 0.0784} = 0.96$, $\tan\alpha = \tfrac{0.28}{0.96} \approx 0.2917$.

UNIT 24 LESSONS

2Calculating lengths and angles

You stand 20 m from the tree and sight its top at an angle of 35°; your eyes are 1.60 m above the ground. The distance and the height of the tree above eye level are the adjacent and the opposite side: $\tan 35^\circ = \tfrac{x}{20}$, so $x = 20 \cdot \tan 35^\circ \approx 14.0$ m. The tree is about 15.6 m tall.

Which ratio you use depends on what you know and what you are looking for. If you know the angle and one side, you can find every other side. If you know two sides, you can find the angle.

KEY POINT · HOW TO PROCEED
  1. Make a sketch, mark the right angle, enter the given quantities and the ones you are looking for.
  2. From the known or wanted angle, name the sides: opposite, adjacent, hypotenuse.
  3. Choose the ratio in which exactly one unknown occurs, set it up and rearrange it.
  4. Check the result with common sense: the hypotenuse is the longest side.

A gradient in per cent is the tangent of the angle of slope: 12 % means $\tan\alpha = 0.12$, so $\alpha \approx 6.8^\circ$. 100 % is 45°, not 90°.

Angles of elevation are measured upwards from the horizontal, angles of depression downwards.

EXAMPLE 3 · TWO LEGS

Given $c = 8$ cm and $\alpha = 40^\circ$ (right angle at $C$). Calculate $a$, $b$ and $\beta$.

$a = c \cdot \sin\alpha \approx 5.14$ cm, $b = c \cdot \cos\alpha \approx 6.13$ cm, $\beta = 50^\circ$.

Check: $5.14^2 + 6.13^2 \approx 64$

EXAMPLE 4 · THE LADDER

A ladder 6 m long may stand at most 75° steep. How high does it reach then, and how far is its foot from the wall?

Height $6 \cdot \sin 75^\circ \approx 5.80$ m, distance $6 \cdot \cos 75^\circ \approx 1.55$ m.

5.80 m high, foot 1.55 m from the wall

A road has a gradient of 12 %. What is its angle of slope? How much height do you gain over 2 km of road (measured along the road)?

$\alpha = \tan^{-1} 0.12 \approx 6.84^\circ$. Height $2000 \cdot \sin 6.84^\circ \approx 238$ m.

A wheelchair ramp may have a gradient of at most 6 %. How long must it be horizontally, at least, to overcome a height of 50 cm? What angle does it then have?

$\tfrac{0.5}{0.06} \approx 8.33$ m horizontally, angle $\tan^{-1} 0.06 \approx 3.43^\circ$.

An isosceles triangle has a base of 10 cm and legs of 13 cm. Calculate the height, the base angles and the angle at the apex.

The height bisects the base: $h = \sqrt{169 - 25} = 12$ cm. $\cos\beta = \tfrac{5}{13} \Rightarrow \beta \approx 67.38^\circ$, apex $180^\circ - 2\beta \approx 45.24^\circ$.

From a point $A$ you see the top of a tower at an angle of 30°. If you walk 50 m towards the tower, it is 45°. How tall is the tower?

Let $d$ be the distance from the second point to the tower. Then $h = d \cdot \tan 45^\circ$ and $h = (d + 50) \cdot \tan 30^\circ$.

$d = \tfrac{d + 50}{\sqrt3} \Rightarrow d(\sqrt3 - 1) = 50 \Rightarrow d \approx 68.3$ m. The tower is about 68.3 m tall.

UNIT 34 LESSONS

3Areas and regular polygons

In a triangle with the sides $b$, $c$ and the angle $\alpha$ between them, the height on $c$ is the opposite side of $\alpha$: $h_c = b \cdot \sin\alpha$. So the area is $A = \tfrac12 \cdot c \cdot h_c = \tfrac12\,b\,c\,\sin\alpha$, even without measuring the height.

A regular $n$-gon has $n$ sides of equal length and equal angles. Its vertices lie on a circle, the circumcircle. If you join them to the centre, you get $n$ isosceles triangles with the angle $\tfrac{360^\circ}{n}$ at the apex. Every time the number of vertices is doubled, the polygon hugs the circle more closely. Its perimeter and area come arbitrarily close to those of the circle. We say: they tend to a limit.

KEY POINT · AREAS WITH TRIGONOMETRY

Triangle: $A = \tfrac12\,b\,c\,\sin\alpha$ (two sides and the included angle) · parallelogram: $A = a\,b\,\sin\alpha$

Regular $n$-gon with circumradius $r$: side $s = 2r \cdot \sin\dfrac{180^\circ}{n}$ · area $A = \dfrac{n}{2}\,r^2 \sin\dfrac{360^\circ}{n}$

As $n$ grows, $n \cdot \sin\dfrac{180^\circ}{n}$ tends to $\pi$.

Dodecagon: $A = 6 r^2 \sin 30^\circ = 3r^2$. The circle has $\pi r^2 \approx 3.14\,r^2$. Just twelve vertices get within about 5 % of the area of the circle.

Obtuse angles only come in Year 10. Here $\alpha$ is always an acute angle or a right angle.

INTERACTIVE · π BETWEEN TWO POLYGONS

Archimedes doubled the number of vertices from 6 to 96 and obtained $3\tfrac{10}{71} < \pi < 3\tfrac17$. Try it yourself.

EXAMPLE 5 · AREA OF A TRIANGLE

A triangle has the sides $b = 7$ cm, $c = 5$ cm and the included angle $\alpha = 40^\circ$. What is its area?

$A = \tfrac12 \cdot 7 \cdot 5 \cdot \sin 40^\circ \approx 17.5 \cdot 0.643$

$A \approx 11.25$ cm²

EXAMPLE 6 · THE HEXAGON

Calculate the side and the area of a regular hexagon with circumradius 4 cm.

$s = 2 \cdot 4 \cdot \sin 30^\circ = 4$ cm. $A = 3 \cdot 16 \cdot \sin 60^\circ = 48 \cdot \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$.

$s = 4$ cm, $A = 24\sqrt3 \approx 41.57$ cm²

A regular octagon has a side length of 5 cm. What is its circumradius?

$5 = 2r \sin 22.5^\circ \Rightarrow r = \tfrac{5}{2 \sin 22.5^\circ} \approx 6.53$ cm

CAS Calculate $n \cdot \sin\dfrac{180^\circ}{n}$ for $n = 6, 12, 24, 48, 96, 1000$. From which $n$ on do two decimal places agree with $\pi$?

$3$; $3.1058$; $3.1326$; $3.1394$; $3.1410$; $3.14158$. Rounded to two places it agrees from the 48-gon on ($3.14$), with the correct digits $3.14\ldots$ from the 96-gon on. For $n = 1000$ the difference is less than $0.00001$.

Show that the regular $n$-gon inscribed in a circle with radius $r$ has the area $A = \tfrac{n}{2} r^2 \sin\tfrac{360^\circ}{n}$. Which value does $\tfrac{A}{r^2}$ tend to as $n$ grows?

Each of the $n$ triangles has two sides $r$ and the angle $\tfrac{360^\circ}{n}$ between them, so the area $\tfrac12 r^2 \sin\tfrac{360^\circ}{n}$. The polygons fill the circle better and better, and $\tfrac{A}{r^2}$ tends to $\pi$.

REVIEW · DEEPEN · CONNECT

An aircraft lands on a glide path of 3°. At what distance from the runway (measured horizontally) must it join the glide path at a height of 1000 m?

$\tfrac{1000}{\tan 3^\circ} \approx 19\,081$ m, that is a good 19 km.

Connecting with chapter 3.1: the diagonal of a rectangle is 10 cm long and makes an angle of 25° with the long side. Calculate the sides and the area.

$10\cos 25^\circ \approx 9.06$ cm and $10\sin 25^\circ \approx 4.23$ cm, $A \approx 38.30$ cm².

Connecting with chapter 2: an arc with $r = 6$ cm has an angle at the centre of 50°. How long is the chord between its end points, and how long is the arc?

Chord $2 \cdot 6 \cdot \sin 25^\circ \approx 5.07$ cm, arc $\tfrac{50}{360} \cdot 2\pi \cdot 6 \approx 5.24$ cm. The arc is a little longer.

From the bank you can see a lighthouse on the other side of a river. You measure a baseline of 100 m along the bank. From one end the tower lies exactly perpendicular to the baseline, from the other end at 62° to the baseline. How wide is the river?

A right-angled triangle with the adjacent side 100 m: width $100 \cdot \tan 62^\circ \approx 188$ m.

EXCURSIONHISTORY AND SURVEYING

How high is Mount Everest?

From 1802 the British surveyed the whole of India with a network of triangles, the “Great Trigonometrical Survey”. A baseline was measured very precisely, a third point was sighted from its ends, and distances were calculated with angles, triangle by triangle, over thousands of kilometres.

From more than 150 km away, surveyors sighted the peaks of the Himalayas. In 1852 the Bengali mathematician Radhanath Sikdar calculated from these measurements that “Peak XV” is the highest mountain on Earth. In 1856 the head of the survey, Andrew Waugh, announced its height as 29,002 feet, about 8840 m. Today the accepted height is 8848.86 m, measured jointly by Nepal and China in 2020. The 19th-century calculation was only a little more than 9 m out.

Sources: J. Keay: The Great Arc. The Dramatic Tale of How India Was Mapped and Everest Was Named. HarperCollins, London 2000. Joint announcement of the height of 8848.86 m by Nepal and China on 8 December 2020.
