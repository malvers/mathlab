Geometry in the advanced course

TOOLKITWHAT YOU NEED FOR THIS CHAPTER
KEY POINT · ADVANCED COURSE

Vector product: $|\vec{a} \times \vec{b}|$ is the area of the parallelogram, half of it that of the triangle.

Line and plane: $\sin \alpha = \dfrac{|\vec{u} \circ \vec{n}|}{|\vec{u}| \cdot |\vec{n}|}$; here it is the sine.

Family of planes: if every plane contains the same line, sort by $t$: the bracket and the rest are both zero.

Trapezium: one pair of parallel sides is enough. For a parallelogram it takes two.

TASK 1LEVEL I · REPRODUCING

1The house with a hipped roof

A house with a hipped roof is modelled by the solid with the vertices $A(0, 0, 0)$, $B(6, 0, 0)$, $C(6, 10, 0)$, $D(0, 10, 0)$, $E(0, 0, 4)$, $F(6, 0, 4)$, $G(6, 10, 4)$ and $H$. The rectangle $ABCD$ is the base, the rectangle $EFGH$ the eaves level. The ridge joins $P(3, 3, 6)$ to $Q(3, 7, 6)$. The $x$-$y$ plane is the horizontal ground, one unit of length corresponds to one metre.

Add the coordinates of the missing vertex $H$ and state the length of the ridge $\overline{PQ}$.

$H$ lies vertically above $D$ at eaves height. From $\overrightarrow{DH} = \overrightarrow{AE} = \begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\4\end{pmatrix}$ it follows that $H(0, 10, 4)$. For the ridge $\overrightarrow{PQ} = \begin{pmatrix}0\\4\\0\end{pmatrix}$, so $\overline{PQ} = 4$ m.

Prove by calculation that the wall $BCGF$ is a rectangle, and calculate its area.

$\overrightarrow{BC} = \begin{pmatrix}0\\10\\0\end{pmatrix}$ and $\overrightarrow{FG} = \begin{pmatrix}0\\10\\0\end{pmatrix}$ are equal, so $BCGF$ is a parallelogram. With $\overrightarrow{BF} = \begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\4\end{pmatrix}$, $\overrightarrow{BC} \circ \overrightarrow{BF} = 0 + 0 + 0 = 0$, so adjacent sides are perpendicular. A parallelogram with a right angle is a rectangle; its area is $10 \cdot 4 = 40$ m².

Prove that the roof face $FGQP$ is an isosceles trapezium but not a parallelogram.

$\overrightarrow{FG} = \begin{pmatrix}0\\10\\0\end{pmatrix}$ and $\overrightarrow{PQ} = \begin{pmatrix}0\\4\\0\end{pmatrix} = 0.4 \cdot \overrightarrow{FG}$: the two sides are parallel but of different lengths — a trapezium and not a parallelogram. For the legs, $\overrightarrow{FP} = \begin{pmatrix}-3\\3\\2\end{pmatrix}$ and $\overrightarrow{GQ} = \begin{pmatrix}-3\\-3\\2\end{pmatrix}$ with $\left|\overrightarrow{FP}\right| = \left|\overrightarrow{GQ}\right| = \sqrt{9+9+4} = \sqrt{22}$. So the trapezium is isosceles.

Use the vector product to calculate the area of the roof face $FGQP$ and that of the hip triangle $EFP$. Use them to state the total roof area.

Split into the triangles $FGQ$ and $FQP$ with $\overrightarrow{FQ} = \begin{pmatrix}-3\\7\\2\end{pmatrix}$: $\overrightarrow{FG} \times \overrightarrow{FQ} = \begin{pmatrix}20\\0\\30\end{pmatrix}$ and $\overrightarrow{FQ} \times \overrightarrow{FP} = \begin{pmatrix}8\\0\\12\end{pmatrix}$, so $A_{FGQP} = \tfrac12\sqrt{1300} + \tfrac12\sqrt{208} = 5\sqrt{13} + 2\sqrt{13} = 7\sqrt{13} \approx 25.2$ m². For the hip triangle $\overrightarrow{EF} \times \overrightarrow{EP} = \begin{pmatrix}0\\-12\\18\end{pmatrix}$ and hence $A_{EFP} = \tfrac12\sqrt{468} = 3\sqrt{13} \approx 10.8$ m². Both shapes occur twice: $A_{\text{roof}} = 2 \cdot 7\sqrt{13} + 2 \cdot 3\sqrt{13} = 20\sqrt{13} \approx 72.1$ m².

TASK 2LEVEL II · MAKING CONNECTIONS

2Plane, space diagonal and compactness

We again look at the house from task 1. The points $B$, $D$ and $E$ determine a plane $\sigma$, which cuts off a piece of the building at the corner $A$. The line segment from $A$ to $G$ is the space diagonal of the cuboid living part $ABCDEFGH$.

Prove that $\sigma: 10x + 6y + 15z = 60$ is an equation of the plane through $B$, $D$ and $E$.

With $\overrightarrow{BD} = \begin{pmatrix}-6\\10\\0\end{pmatrix}$ and $\overrightarrow{BE} = \begin{pmatrix}-6\\0\\4\end{pmatrix}$, $\vec{n} = \overrightarrow{BD} \times \overrightarrow{BE} = \begin{pmatrix}40\\24\\60\end{pmatrix}$, reduced $\begin{pmatrix}10\\6\\15\end{pmatrix}$ — these are the coefficients of the equation. Substituting the three points: $B$: $10 \cdot 6 = 60$, $D$: $6 \cdot 10 = 60$, $E$: $15 \cdot 4 = 60$. All three satisfy the equation, and since they do not lie on one line, $\sigma$ is exactly this plane.

Calculate the size of the angle at which the space diagonal $\overline{AG}$ meets the plane $\sigma$.

The line through $A$ and $G$ has the direction vector $\vec{u} = \overrightarrow{AG} = \begin{pmatrix}6\\10\\4\end{pmatrix}$ with $\left|\vec{u}\right| = \sqrt{36+100+16} = \sqrt{152} = 2\sqrt{38}$; further $\left|\vec{n}\right| = \sqrt{100+36+225} = \sqrt{361} = 19$ and $\vec{n} \circ \vec{u} = 60 + 60 + 60 = 180$. For the angle between line and plane $\sin \alpha = \dfrac{\left|\vec{n} \circ \vec{u}\right|}{\left|\vec{n}\right| \cdot \left|\vec{u}\right|} = \dfrac{180}{19 \cdot 2\sqrt{38}} = \dfrac{90}{19\sqrt{38}} \approx 0.7684$, so $\alpha \approx 50.2^\circ$.

The plane $\sigma$ meets the space diagonal $\overline{AG}$ at the point $S$. Determine the coordinates of $S$ and the ratio in which $S$ divides the line segment $\overline{AG}$.

The line $AG$ is $\vec{x} = t \cdot \begin{pmatrix}6\\10\\4\end{pmatrix}$. Substituting into $\sigma$: $60t + 60t + 60t = 180t = 60$, so $t = \tfrac13$ and $S\left(2, \tfrac{10}{3}, \tfrac43\right)$. Since $t = \tfrac13$, $\overline{AS} : \overline{SG} = \tfrac13 : \tfrac23$, so the ratio of division is $1 : 2$. An elegant way to see it: since $ABCDEFGH$ is a cuboid with $A$ at the origin, $\overrightarrow{OG} = \overrightarrow{OB} + \overrightarrow{OD} + \overrightarrow{OE}$, and $S$ with $\overrightarrow{OS} = \tfrac13\left(\overrightarrow{OB} + \overrightarrow{OD} + \overrightarrow{OE}\right)$ is precisely the centroid of the triangle $BDE$.

Calculate the volume of the house and compare its ratio of surface area to volume with that of a flat-roofed building with the same base and the same volume. The surface area includes the floor slab, the walls and the roof faces.

The living part is a cuboid: $V_1 = 6 \cdot 10 \cdot 4 = 240$ m³. The hipped roof splits into a prism over the ridge length $4$ — its cross-section is a triangle with base $6$ and height $2$, i.e. $6$ m² — and two pyramids with base $6 \cdot 3 = 18$ and height $2$: $V_2 = 6 \cdot 4 + 2 \cdot \tfrac13 \cdot 18 \cdot 2 = 24 + 24 = 48$ m³. Together $V = 288$ m³. Contributing to the surface area are: floor slab $60$ m², walls $2 \cdot (6+10) \cdot 4 = 128$ m² and roof $20\sqrt{13} \approx 72.1$ m² — together $A \approx 260.1$ m², so $\dfrac{A}{V} \approx 0.90\ \text{m}^{-1}$. The flat-roofed building has the height $h = \dfrac{288}{60} = 4.8$ m and hence $A = 2 \cdot 60 + 32 \cdot 4.8 = 273.6$ m², so $\dfrac{A}{V} = 0.95\ \text{m}^{-1}$. Although the pitched roof costs extra area, the house with the hipped roof is the more compact form: it needs about $5\%$ less envelope area per cubic metre.

TASK 3LEVEL III · EVALUATING

3The family of planes at the ridge

The ridge lies on the line $g$ through $P(3, 3, 6)$ and $Q(3, 7, 6)$. For every real number $t$, $E_t: t \cdot x + z = 3t + 6$ gives a plane.

Prove that the line $g$ lies in every plane $E_t$.

An equation of $g$ is $\vec{x} = \begin{pmatrix}3\\3\\6\end{pmatrix} + r \cdot \begin{pmatrix}0\\1\\0\end{pmatrix}$, $r \in \mathbb{R}$; so any point of $g$ has the coordinates $(3, 3+r, 6)$. Substituting into $E_t$ gives $t \cdot 3 + 6 = 3t + 6$ — a true statement, and independent of $r$ and of $t$. The reason: $y$ does not occur in the equation of $E_t$, and on $g$, $x = 3$ and $z = 6$ are constant. So $g$ lies in every plane of the family.

State which vertices of the house lie in the plane $E_{2/3}$, and interpret the result geometrically.

$E_{2/3}: \tfrac23 x + z = 8$, multiplied by $3$: $2x + 3z = 24$. Substituting: $F(6, 0, 4)$: $12 + 12 = 24$, $G(6, 10, 4)$: $24$, $P$: $6 + 18 = 24$, $Q$: $24$ — these four lie in the plane. For the other points we get: $A$ and $D$: $0$; $B$, $C$, $E$ and $H$: $12$. The four points $F$, $G$, $Q$, $P$ are exactly the corners of the large roof face from task 1: $E_{2/3}$ is the plane of this roof face. Correspondingly, $t = -\tfrac23$ belongs to the opposite roof face $EHQP$.

Calculate the angle between the two roof planes $E_{-2/3}$ and $E_{2/3}$ and state the size of the opening angle of the roof at the ridge.

From $E_{-2/3}: -2x + 3z = 12$ and $E_{2/3}: 2x + 3z = 24$ you read off the normal vectors $\vec{n_1} = \begin{pmatrix}-2\\0\\3\end{pmatrix}$ and $\vec{n_2} = \begin{pmatrix}2\\0\\3\end{pmatrix}$. It follows that $\cos \varphi = \dfrac{\left|\vec{n_1} \circ \vec{n_2}\right|}{\left|\vec{n_1}\right| \cdot \left|\vec{n_2}\right|} = \dfrac{\left|-4+9\right|}{\sqrt{13} \cdot \sqrt{13}} = \dfrac{5}{13} \approx 0.3846$, so $\varphi \approx 67.4^\circ$. The opening angle of the roof at the ridge is its supplementary angle: $180^\circ - 67.4^\circ \approx 112.6^\circ$.

Assess the statement: “Every plane that contains the ridge line $g$ can be written in the form $E_t$.”

The statement is false. A counterexample is the plane $x = 3$: since $x_P = x_Q = 3$, it contains the whole line $g$. In every equation $t \cdot x + z = 3t + 6$, however, $z$ has the coefficient $1$, and even after multiplying by some $\lambda \neq 0$ this coefficient stays non-zero — in $x = 3$, $z$ does not occur at all. So the family covers all planes through $g$ except this one; as $t \to \pm\infty$ the planes $E_t$ only approach it. The correct statement would be: every plane through $g$ that is not perpendicular to the $x$-$y$ plane belongs to the family.

LOOKING BACKTHE IDEA AND THE TRAP

TASK 1 · THE HOUSE WITH A HIPPED ROOF

You split up trapezium and triangles with the vector product.

Trap: the height of the trapezium is not the leg length $\sqrt{22} \approx 4.69$. Measured perpendicular to the ridge, the roof is only $\sqrt{13} \approx 3.61$ wide — if you calculate with $\sqrt{22}$, you get $32.8$ m² instead of $25.2$ m² and make the roof almost a third too large.

TASK 2 · PLANE, SPACE DIAGONAL AND COMPACTNESS

Normal vector from the plane, direction vector from the diagonal: the angle with the sine, the point of intersection by substituting.

Trap: for the angle between a line and a plane it is the sine, not the cosine. If you set $\cos\alpha = 0.7684$, you get $39.8^\circ$ — that is the angle between the line and the normal. The two add up to $90^\circ$.

TASK 3 · THE FAMILY OF PLANES AT THE RIDGE

Sorting by $t$ reveals the common line. Check whether every plane of the family really occurs.

Trap: the angle between two planes is always the acute angle, here $67.4^\circ$. If you give it as the roof opening, you confuse it with its supplementary angle $112.6^\circ$ — a ridge with $67^\circ$ would belong to a much steeper roof than the one drawn.

TESTTHE SAME IDEAS, NEW NUMBERS
ROUND 1

A triangle is spanned by $\vec{a} = (2, 0, 0)$ and $\vec{b} = (0, 3, 0)$. What is its area?

$|\vec{a} \times \vec{b}| = 6$, half of it is $3$.

At what angle in degrees does a line with direction $(0, 0, 1)$ meet the plane $z = 0$?

$\sin \alpha = 1$

ROUND 2

The planes $E_t: t \cdot x + z = 2t + 1$ share a line. What value does $x$ have on it?

$t \cdot (x - 2) + z - 1 = 0$ holds for every $t$ exactly when $x = 2$, $z = 1$.

In which plane $E_t$ does $A(0, 0, 5)$ lie?

$5 = 2t + 1$
