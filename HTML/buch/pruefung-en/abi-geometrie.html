Analytic geometry

Almost every geometry task begins with a solid made of points: a house, a tent, a roof. From it come plane, inclination and distance. And in three of four years the sun fell on it at a slant at the end: casting shadows.

CHECK-IN: WHAT YOU NEED

How long is the vector $\begin{pmatrix}2\\-1\\2\end{pmatrix}$?

$\sqrt{4 + 1 + 4} = 3$

Calculate $\begin{pmatrix}1\\2\\3\end{pmatrix} \circ \begin{pmatrix}2\\-1\\0\end{pmatrix}$.

$2 - 2 + 0 = 0$, the vectors are orthogonal.

Substitute $P(1, 2, 3)$ into $x + y + z$. What value do you get?

$1 + 2 + 3 = 6$, the point lies in $x + y + z = 6$.

TOOLKITWHAT YOU NEED FOR THIS CHAPTER
KEY POINT · CALCULATING IN SPACE

Rectangle: opposite sides are equal vectors, and adjacent sides have scalar product $0$.

Inclination of two planes: $\cos \alpha = \dfrac{|\vec{n}_1 \circ \vec{n}_2|}{|\vec{n}_1| \cdot |\vec{n}_2|}$. For the ground $\vec{n}_2 = (0, 0, 1)$.

Distance from $E: ax + by + cz = d$: $\dfrac{|a p_1 + b p_2 + c p_3 - d|}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}}$.

Shadow: line $\vec{x} = \vec{p} + t \cdot \vec{s}$ through the point, then set $z = 0$.

TASK 1LEVEL I · REPRODUCING

1The garden shed

A garden shed with a lean-to roof is modelled by the solid $ABCDEFGH$ with $A(0, 0, 0)$, $B(6, 0, 0)$, $C(6, 4, 0)$, $D(0, 4, 0)$, $E(0, 0, 3)$, $F(6, 0, 3)$, $G(6, 4, 2)$ and $H(0, 4, 2)$. The quadrilateral $EFGH$ is the roof surface, the $x$-$y$ plane the horizontal ground. One unit of length corresponds to one metre.

State the lengths of the edges $\overline{AB}$ and $\overline{AE}$.

$\overline{AB}$ runs parallel to the $x$-axis: $\overline{AB} = 6$ m. $\overline{AE}$ runs parallel to the $z$-axis: $\overline{AE} = 3$ m.

Prove by calculation that the roof surface $EFGH$ is a rectangle.

$\overrightarrow{EF} = \begin{pmatrix}6\\0\\0\end{pmatrix}$, $\overrightarrow{HG} = \begin{pmatrix}6\\0\\0\end{pmatrix}$ — the vectors are equal, so $EFGH$ is a parallelogram. With $\overrightarrow{EH} = \begin{pmatrix}0\\4\\-1\end{pmatrix}$, $\overrightarrow{EF} \circ \overrightarrow{EH} = 0 + 0 + 0 = 0$, so the adjacent sides are perpendicular. A parallelogram with a right angle is a rectangle.

Calculate the area of the roof surface.

The side lengths are $\left|\overrightarrow{EF}\right| = 6$ and $\left|\overrightarrow{EH}\right| = \sqrt{0^2 + 4^2 + (-1)^2} = \sqrt{17}$. So $A_{\text{roof}} = 6 \cdot \sqrt{17} \approx 24.7$ m².

Determine the volume of the garden shed.

Every section perpendicular to the $x$-axis is the same trapezium with the parallel sides $3$ (front) and $2$ (back) and the height $4$: $A_{\text{trapezium}} = \dfrac{3+2}{2} \cdot 4 = 10$. The solid is a prism of length $6$, so $V = 10 \cdot 6 = 60$ m³.

TASK 2LEVEL II · MAKING CONNECTIONS

2Plane and inclination

We again look at the garden shed from task 1. The roof surface $EFGH$ lies in a plane $\varepsilon$.

State an equation of the line on which the edge $\overline{EH}$ lies.

With the position vector $\overrightarrow{OE}$ and the direction vector $\overrightarrow{EH}$: $g: \vec{x} = \begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\3\end{pmatrix} + r \cdot \begin{pmatrix}0\\4\\-1\end{pmatrix}$, $r \in \mathbb{R}$.

Prove that $\varepsilon: y + 4 \cdot z = 12$ is an equation of this plane.

A normal vector is $\vec{n} = \overrightarrow{EF} \times \overrightarrow{EH} = \begin{pmatrix}0\\6\\24\end{pmatrix}$, reduced $\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\\4\end{pmatrix}$ — these are the coefficients of the equation. Substituting all four vertices: $E: 0 + 12 = 12$, $F: 0 + 12 = 12$, $G: 4 + 8 = 12$, $H: 4 + 8 = 12$. All four satisfy the equation.

Calculate the size of the angle at which the roof is inclined to the horizontal ground.

The ground has the normal vector $\begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\1\end{pmatrix}$. For the angle between two planes $\cos \alpha = \dfrac{\left|\vec{n_1} \circ \vec{n_2}\right|}{\left|\vec{n_1}\right| \cdot \left|\vec{n_2}\right|} = \dfrac{4}{\sqrt{17}} \approx 0.970$, so $\alpha \approx 14.0^\circ$.

Calculate the distance of the point $C$ from the plane $\varepsilon$.

Hesse normal form: $d = \dfrac{\left|4 + 4 \cdot 0 - 12\right|}{\sqrt{17}} = \dfrac{8}{\sqrt{17}} \approx 1.94$ m.

TASK 3LEVEL III · EVALUATING

3Shadow in the afternoon

In the afternoon the sunlight falls on the garden shed in the direction of the vector $\vec{s} = \begin{pmatrix}2\\1\\-3\end{pmatrix}$. The roof $EFGH$ casts a shadow $E'F'G'H'$ on the horizontal ground.

Calculate the coordinates of the shadow point $F'$ of $F(6, 0, 3)$.

The ray of light through $F$ is $\vec{x} = \begin{pmatrix}6\\0\\3\end{pmatrix} + t \cdot \begin{pmatrix}2\\1\\-3\end{pmatrix}$. On the ground $z = 0$: $3 - 3t = 0$, so $t = 1$. Substituting gives $F'(8, 1, 0)$.

The other shadow points are $E'(2, 1, 0)$, $G'\left(\tfrac{22}{3}, \tfrac{14}{3}, 0\right)$ and $H'\left(\tfrac{4}{3}, \tfrac{14}{3}, 0\right)$. Prove that $E'F'G'H'$ is a parallelogram.

$\overrightarrow{E'F'} = \begin{pmatrix}6\\0\\0\end{pmatrix}$ and $\overrightarrow{H'G'} = \begin{pmatrix}\tfrac{22}{3} - \tfrac{4}{3}\\0\\0\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}6\\0\\0\end{pmatrix}$. Two opposite sides are parallel and of equal length, so $E'F'G'H'$ is a parallelogram.

Calculate the area of the shadow.

With $\overrightarrow{E'H'} = \begin{pmatrix}-\tfrac{2}{3}\\ \tfrac{11}{3}\\0\end{pmatrix}$, $A = \left|\overrightarrow{E'F'} \times \overrightarrow{E'H'}\right| = \left|\begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\22\end{pmatrix}\right| = 22$. The shadow is $22$ m².

Assess the statement: “The shadow of a roof is always larger than the roof itself.”

The statement is false. Here the shadow, at $22$ m², is smaller than the roof surface at $6\sqrt{17} \approx 24.7$ m². The reason: the sun is positioned so that it hits the roof at a slant — only the part perpendicular to the direction of the rays is projected. Only when the rays come in almost parallel to the ground does the shadow become very large.

LOOKING BACKTHE IDEA AND THE TRAP

TASK 1 · THE GARDEN SHED

First set up vectors, then rectangle and area. The garden shed is a prism with a trapezium as its cross-section.

Trap: the roof surface is not $6 \cdot 4 = 24$. The roof is inclined; its width is the distance from $E$ to $H$ — so $\sqrt{17} \approx 4.12$ and not $4$.

TASK 2 · PLANE AND INCLINATION

The normal vector carries everything: checking points, inclination and distance.

Trap: in part d) the perpendicular distance is asked for, not the height of the wall. Vertically above $C$ lies $G$ at a height of $2$ m — the distance to the inclined plane is smaller, at $1.94$ m. If you write $2$, you have forgotten the inclination.

TASK 3 · SHADOW IN THE AFTERNOON

Every vertex gets its line in the direction of the sun. Where it meets the ground, $z = 0$.

Trap: $\vec{s}$ points away from the sun, i.e. downwards ($z$-component negative). If you read $\vec{s}$ as the direction towards the sun, you calculate with $t < 0$ and end up behind the house.

TESTTHE SAME IDEAS, NEW NUMBERS
ROUND 1

The point $P(2, 0, 4)$ casts a shadow on $z = 0$ with $\vec{s} = (1, 1, -2)$. What is its $x$-coordinate?

$4 - 2t = 0$ gives $t = 2$, so $x = 2 + 2 = 4$.

What is the distance from $P(1, 2, 5)$ to the plane $2x + y + 2z = 4$?

$\frac{|14 - 4|}{3} = \frac{10}{3}$

ROUND 2

At what angle in degrees is the plane $y + z = 3$ inclined to the ground?

$\cos \alpha = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$

How long is the line segment from $A(1, 1, 1)$ to $B(4, 5, 1)$?

$\sqrt{9 + 16} = 5$
