KEY POINT · ADVANCED COURSE

At least once: $P = 1 - P(\text{never})$.

Bayes: $P_B(A) = \dfrac{P(A) \cdot P_A(B)}{P(B)}$.

Type I error: $H_0$ is true and is rejected; its probability is at most $\alpha$.

Type II error: $H_0$ is false and is not rejected. For $\beta$ you need the true $p$.

INTERACTIVE · THE SIGNIFICANCE TEST

Set $n$, $p_0$, the direction and $\alpha$ and read off the rejection region. Then you can see how large the errors are.

TASK 1LEVEL I · REPRODUCING

1Final inspection in lamp production

A company makes rechargeable hand lamps. Each lamp goes through three inspections one after another: the size check (event $M$: size OK), the leak test (event $D$) and the function test (event $F$). Experience shows that $4\%$ of the lamps fail the size check, $5\%$ the leak test and $10\%$ the function test. The three inspections are independent of each other; every lamp goes through all three.

Calculate the probability that a lamp chosen at random passes all three inspections.

A path in the tree diagram is multiplied through (multiplication rule for paths): $P(M \cap D \cap F) = 0.96 \cdot 0.95 \cdot 0.90 = 0.8208$, i.e. about $82.1\%$.

Determine the probability that a lamp fails exactly one of the three inspections.

Exactly one fault means: exactly one of the three factors is the failure rate. The three paths are added (addition rule for paths): $0.04 \cdot 0.95 \cdot 0.90 + 0.96 \cdot 0.05 \cdot 0.90 + 0.96 \cdot 0.95 \cdot 0.10 = 0.0342 + 0.0432 + 0.0912 = 0.1686$, i.e. about $16.9\%$.

Calculate the probability that a lamp receives at least one fault.

Via the complementary event “no fault”: $P(\text{at least one}) = 1 - 0.8208 = 0.1792$, i.e. about $17.9\%$. The difference from b) is the lamps with two or three faults: $0.1792 - 0.1686 = 0.0106$.

$2500$ lamps are made every day. State how many of them are faultless on average and how many receive at least one fault.

Expected values for $2500$ lamps: $2500 \cdot 0.8208 = 2052$ faultless lamps and $2500 \cdot 0.1792 = 448$ lamps with at least one fault. Together that is $2500$ again.

TASK 2LEVEL II · MAKING CONNECTIONS

2Where does the cell come from?

The battery cells for the lamps come from two suppliers: $70\%$ of the cells come from supplier 1 (event $Z_1$), $30\%$ from supplier 2 (event $Z_2$). Of the cells from the first supplier, $2\%$ are defective (event $S$). Overall, $3.2\%$ of all cells delivered are defective.

Determine the proportion of defective cells from supplier 2.

The overall proportion is made up of both suppliers: $P(S) = P(Z_1) \cdot P_{Z_1}(S) + P(Z_2) \cdot P_{Z_2}(S)$, so $0.032 = 0.70 \cdot 0.02 + 0.30 \cdot x = 0.014 + 0.30x$. From this $0.30x = 0.018$ and $x = 0.06$: for supplier 2, $6\%$ of the cells are defective, three times as many as for supplier 1.

A cell taken at random is not defective. Calculate the probability that it comes from supplier 1.

We need the conditional probability $P_{\overline{S}}(Z_1)$. $P(Z_1 \cap \overline{S}) = 0.70 \cdot 0.98 = 0.686$ and $P(\overline{S}) = 1 - 0.032 = 0.968$, so $P_{\overline{S}}(Z_1) = \dfrac{0.686}{0.968} \approx 0.7087$, about $70.9\%$. So a faultless cell comes from supplier 1 somewhat more often than its share of the deliveries, $70\%$, would suggest.

$40$ cells are taken at random from a large delivery; the random variable $X$ counts the cells from supplier 2. Determine the probability that $X$ deviates from the expected value by at most half a standard deviation.

Because the delivery is large, $X$ may be regarded as binomially distributed with $n = 40$ and $p = 0.30$: $E(X) = 12$ and $\sigma = \sqrt{40 \cdot 0.30 \cdot 0.70} = \sqrt{8.4} \approx 2.898$. Half a standard deviation is $1.449$, so we need $P(10.55 \leq X \leq 13.45) = P(11 \leq X \leq 13) = P(X{=}11) + P(X{=}12) + P(X{=}13) \approx 0.3945$, about $39.5\%$.

Find the minimum number of cells that must be checked so that, with a probability of at least $99\%$, there is at least one defective cell among them.

Each cell is defective with $p = 0.032$, independently of the others. We need the smallest $n$ with $1 - 0.968^{\,n} \geq 0.99$, i.e. $0.968^{\,n} \leq 0.01$. Taking logarithms gives $n \geq \dfrac{\ln 0.01}{\ln 0.968} \approx 141.6$, so 142 cells must be checked. Check: $1 - 0.968^{141} \approx 0.9898$ is not quite enough, $1 - 0.968^{142} \approx 0.9901$ is enough.

TASK 3LEVEL III · EVALUATING

3Does the battery keep the advertising promise?

The company advertises that at least $85\%$ of the batteries still have at least $80\%$ of their initial capacity after $500$ charging cycles. A testing laboratory tests $200$ batteries chosen at random; $163$ of them meet the condition. The advertising claim is to be checked with a significance test at the significance level $\alpha = 5\%$. The random variable $X$ counts the batteries in the sample that meet the condition.

State the null and alternative hypotheses, justify the direction of the test and calculate the expected value and standard deviation of $X$ under $H_0$.

$H_0$: $p \geq 0.85$ (the advertising claim is true), $H_1$: $p < 0.85$. Doubts only arise with too few durable batteries, so the test is left-tailed. We calculate with the boundary value $p = 0.85$: $X$ is binomially distributed with $n = 200$ and $p = 0.85$, so $E(X) = 170$ and $\sigma = \sqrt{200 \cdot 0.85 \cdot 0.15} = \sqrt{25.5} \approx 5.05$.

Determine the rejection region of the null hypothesis and state the actual probability of a type I error.

We need the largest $k$ with $P(X \leq k) \leq 0.05$. $P(X \leq 161) \approx 0.0498$ and $P(X \leq 162) \approx 0.0720$. So $\overline{A} = \{0, 1, \dots, 161\}$ is the rejection region and $A = \{162, \dots, 200\}$ the acceptance region. The actual probability of a type I error is $0.0498$, i.e. about $4.98\%$ — just below the level of $5\%$.

Make a decision based on the sample and formulate a recommendation for the testing laboratory.

The observed value $163$ lies in the acceptance region. $H_0$ is not rejected: the sample does not contradict the advertising claim at the $5\%$ level, although the sample proportion, $\frac{163}{200} = 0.815$, is below $0.85$. Recommendation: the claim may stand, but the result is no proof of it — for a reliable statement the laboratory would have to test more batteries.

Suppose that in reality only $80\%$ of the batteries meet the condition. Calculate the probability of a type II error and interpret the result.

A type II error means: $H_0$ is retained although it is false. For $p = 0.80$, $E(X) = 160$ and $\sigma = \sqrt{32} \approx 5.66$; we need the probability that the sample still lands in the acceptance region: $\beta = P(X \geq 162, p = 0.80) = 1 - P(X \leq 161, p = 0.80) \approx 0.4019$, i.e. about $40.2\%$. In just over two out of five cases a deterioration to $80\%$ would therefore go undetected — with $n = 200$ the test has little power.

LOOKING BACKTHE IDEA AND THE TRAP

TASK 1 · FINAL INSPECTION IN LAMP PRODUCTION

Passing all inspections is a single path; the other cases go via the complementary event.

Trap: “exactly one fault” and “at least one fault” are two different events. For “at least” the complementary event almost always pays off; for “exactly” you really have to write out the paths one by one and add them.

TASK 2 · WHERE DOES THE CELL COME FROM?

The backward question puts the condition in the denominator: first $P(\overline{S})$, then the share of supplier 1.

Trap: in b) the question is turned round: the effect is given (cell OK), the cause is wanted (supplier). $P_{\overline{S}}(Z_1) \approx 0.709$ and $P_{Z_1}(\overline{S}) = 0.98$ are completely different numbers. For comparison: a defective cell comes from supplier 2 with probability $\dfrac{0.018}{0.032} = 0.5625$, although that supplier delivers only $30\%$.

TASK 3 · DOES THE BATTERY KEEP THE ADVERTISING PROMISE?

The direction of the test follows from the suspicion. The rejection region comes from $H_0$, the type II error from the true $p$.

Trap: “$H_0$ not rejected” does not mean “$H_0$ proved”. The type II error is calculated with the assumed true probability, here $p = 0.80$, and over the acceptance region — not over the rejection region and not with $p = 0.85$.

$0.006 + 0.008 = 0.014$
