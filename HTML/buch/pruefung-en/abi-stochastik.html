TOOLKITWHAT YOU NEED FOR THIS CHAPTER
KEY POINT · PROBABILITY AND STATISTICS

Conditional probability: $P_A(B) = \dfrac{P(A \cap B)}{P(A)}$.

Independent: $P(A \cap B) = P(A) \cdot P(B)$.

Binomial distribution: $P(X = k) = \binom{n}{k} \cdot p^k \cdot (1 - p)^{n - k}$ with $\mu = n \cdot p$ and $\sigma = \sqrt{n \cdot p \cdot (1 - p)}$.

Left-tailed test: the rejection region is $\{0, \ldots, k\}$ with the largest $k$ for which $P(X \leq k) \leq \alpha$.

TASK 1LEVEL I · REPRODUCING

1Barbecue stand at the school fête

At the school fête of a Berufliches Gymnasium, $60\%$ of the visitors are students (event $S$). $45\%$ of all visitors eat at the barbecue stand (event $G$). $30\%$ of all visitors are students and eat at the barbecue stand. A visitor is chosen at random.

Copy the two-way table and complete it.

Row and column totals fill the table: $P(S \cap \overline{G}) = 0.60 - 0.30 = 0.30$, $P(\overline{S} \cap G) = 0.45 - 0.30 = 0.15$, $P(\overline{S} \cap \overline{G}) = 1 - 0.60 - 0.45 + 0.30 = 0.25$, $P(\overline{S}) = 0.40$ and $P(\overline{G}) = 0.55$.

Determine the probability that a student chosen at random eats at the barbecue stand.

What is asked for is the conditional probability $P_S(G) = \dfrac{P(S \cap G)}{P(S)} = \dfrac{0.30}{0.60} = 0.5$, i.e. $50\%$.

Investigate whether the events $S$ and $G$ are stochastically independent.

For independence, $P(S) \cdot P(G) = P(S \cap G)$ would have to hold. But $0.60 \cdot 0.45 = 0.27 \neq 0.30$. So the events are dependent — students go to the barbecue stand somewhat more often than average.

Describe in context an event whose probability is $0.15$.

$P(\overline{S} \cap G) = 0.15$: “The visitor chosen is not a student and eats at the barbecue stand.”

TASK 2LEVEL II · MAKING CONNECTIONS

2The lottery stall

At a lottery stall each ticket wins, independently of the others, with probability $p = 0.25$. A class draws $12$ tickets. The random variable $X$ describes the number of wins.

State the expected value and the standard deviation of $X$.

$X$ is binomially distributed with $n = 12$ and $p = 0.25$. $E(X) = n \cdot p = 3$ and $\sigma = \sqrt{n \cdot p \cdot (1-p)} = \sqrt{12 \cdot 0.25 \cdot 0.75} = \sqrt{2.25} = 1.5$.

Calculate the probability of exactly three wins and the probability of at least one win.

$P(X = 3) = \binom{12}{3} \cdot 0.25^3 \cdot 0.75^9 = 220 \cdot 0.25^3 \cdot 0.75^9 \approx 0.258$. For at least one win, via the complementary event: $P(X \geq 1) = 1 - P(X = 0) = 1 - 0.75^{12} \approx 0.968$.

Determine the probability that the number of wins deviates from the expected value by at most one standard deviation.

One standard deviation around the expected value means $3 - 1.5 \leq X \leq 3 + 1.5$, i.e. $1.5 \leq X \leq 4.5$. Since $X$ only takes whole numbers, this is $P(2 \leq X \leq 4) = P(X = 2) + P(X = 3) + P(X = 4) \approx 0.684$.

Find the minimum number of tickets that must be drawn so that there is at least one win with a probability of at least $99\%$.

We need the smallest $n$ with $1 - 0.75^{\,n} \geq 0.99$, i.e. $0.75^{\,n} \leq 0.01$. Taking logarithms: $n \geq \dfrac{\ln 0.01}{\ln 0.75} \approx 16.01$. Since $n$ is a whole number, 17 tickets are needed.

TASK 3LEVEL III · EVALUATING

3Is the stall still worth it?

The organiser advertises that at least every fourth ticket wins. At the end of the day $200$ tickets sold, chosen at random, are checked; among them are $38$ wins. The claim is to be checked with a left-tailed significance test at the significance level $\alpha = 5\%$.

State the null hypothesis and justify why the test is left-tailed.

$H_0$: $p \geq 0.25$ (“at least every fourth ticket wins”), $H_1$: $p < 0.25$. Doubts only arise with too few wins, so the rejection region lies on the left. Under $H_0$ we calculate with the boundary value $p = 0.25$: $X$ is binomially distributed with $n = 200$ and $p = 0.25$, $E(X) = 50$ and $\sigma = \sqrt{200 \cdot 0.25 \cdot 0.75} \approx 6.12$.

Determine the rejection region of the null hypothesis.

We need the largest $k$ with $P(X \leq k) \leq 0.05$. From the table or with the calculator: $P(X \leq 39) \approx 0.0405$ and $P(X \leq 40) \approx 0.0579$. So $\overline{A} = \{0, 1, \dots, 39\}$ is the rejection region, $A = \{40, \dots, 200\}$ the acceptance region.

Make a decision and formulate a recommendation for the organiser.

The observed value $38$ lies in the rejection region. So $H_0$ is rejected: at the $5\%$ level the sample speaks against at least every fourth ticket winning. Recommendation: the organiser should withdraw the advertising claim or increase the proportion of winning tickets.

State the actual probability of a type I error and describe the type II error in context.

The type I error is the probability of rejecting $H_0$ although it is true: $P(X \leq 39, p = 0.25) \approx 0.0405$, i.e. about $4.1\%$ — less than the permitted $5\%$, because $X$ only takes whole values. The type II error would be: the proportion of winning tickets is in fact below $25\%$, but the sample gives $40$ or more wins — the advertising claim would go unchallenged although it is false.

LOOKING BACKTHE IDEA AND THE TRAP

TASK 1 · BARBECUE STAND AT THE SCHOOL FÊTE

Fill in the table, then compare: $P(S \cap G)$ against $P(S) \cdot P(G)$.

Trap: $P_S(G) = 0.5$ and $P(S \cap G) = 0.30$ are two different things. In the first, the population is only the students; in the second, it is all visitors.

TASK 2 · THE LOTTERY STALL

“At least one” goes via the complementary event; for the number wanted, the logarithm helps.

Trap: when taking logarithms the inequality sign turns round, because $\ln 0.75$ is negative. And $16.01$ is rounded up — $16$ tickets are not quite enough.

TASK 3 · IS THE STALL STILL WORTH IT?

Hypothesis, rejection region, decision, a sentence for the organiser. In exactly this order.

Trap: the significance level is not used up completely. $5\%$ is a bound; the actual probability of error is the value at the boundary of the rejection region — here $4.1\%$. If you take $k = 40$, you exceed the level with $5.8\%$.
