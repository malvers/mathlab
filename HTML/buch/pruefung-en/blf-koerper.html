Solids and surfaces

A greenhouse, a glass roof, an ice-cream cone: in the BLF, solids are almost always put together from everyday things. Split the solid up, look for the right-angled triangle and decide whether the result makes sense.

TOOLKITWHAT YOU NEED FOR THIS CHAPTER
KEY POINT · SOLIDS

Prism: $V = G \cdot h$. Pyramid and cone: $V = \frac{1}{3} \cdot G \cdot h$.

Sphere: $V = \frac{4}{3} \pi r^3$. Lateral surface of a cone: $M = \pi \cdot r \cdot s$.

Look for Pythagoras in the solid: height, half the diagonal and lateral edge form a right-angled triangle.

Rounding up: for rolls, tins or buses the next whole number counts, even where you would otherwise round down.

TASK 1LEVEL I · REPRODUCING

1The greenhouse

A greenhouse $12$ m long has as its cross-section a rectangle with a semicircle on top. It is $4.00$ m wide and $3.20$ m high in total (see figure). The side walls, the roof and the two end walls are covered with plastic film.

Justify that each side wall is $1.20$ m high.

The semicircle sits on the whole width, so its radius is $r = \frac{4.00}{2} = 2.00$ m. Of the total height, $3.20 - 2.00 = 1.20$ m is left for the wall.

Calculate the area of the cross-section.

Rectangle plus semicircle: $A = 4.00 \cdot 1.20 + \frac{1}{2} \pi \cdot 2^2 = 4.8 + 2\pi \approx 11.08\ \mathrm{m}^2$.

Calculate the volume of the air in the greenhouse.

The greenhouse is a prism with this cross-section: $V = A \cdot 12 \approx 133\ \mathrm{m}^3$.

The film is sold in rolls of $50\ \mathrm{m}^2$ each. Find the minimum number of rolls that must be bought.

Side walls and roof together: $(2 \cdot 1.20 + \pi \cdot 2) \cdot 12 \approx 104.2\ \mathrm{m}^2$ (half the circumference is $\pi r$). Plus two end walls: $2 \cdot 11.08 \approx 22.2\ \mathrm{m}^2$. In total about $126.4\ \mathrm{m}^2$, that is $2.5$ rolls. So three rolls must be bought.

TASK 2LEVEL II · MAKING CONNECTIONS

2The glass roof

A courtyard is covered by a glass roof in the shape of a right square pyramid. The base edge is $6$ m long, each lateral edge $7$ m (see figure, not to scale). Only the four side faces are glass.

Calculate the height $h$ of the pyramid.

The foot $M$ of the height lies at the centre of the base; half a diagonal is $\frac{6\sqrt{2}}{2} = 3\sqrt{2} \approx 4.24$ m. In the triangle $AMS$: $h = \sqrt{7^2 - (3\sqrt{2})^2} = \sqrt{49 - 18} = \sqrt{31} \approx 5.57$ m.

Calculate the volume of the covered space above the base.

$V = \frac{1}{3} \cdot 6^2 \cdot \sqrt{31} = 12\sqrt{31} \approx 66.8\ \mathrm{m}^3$.

Calculate the size of the angle at the apex of a side face.

Cosine rule in the isosceles triangle $ABS$: $\cos\varphi = \frac{7^2 + 7^2 - 6^2}{2 \cdot 7 \cdot 7} = \frac{62}{98}$, so $\varphi \approx 50.8°$.

Calculate the area of the glass.

The height of a side face (green) is $h_s = \sqrt{7^2 - 3^2} = \sqrt{40} \approx 6.32$ m. Four triangles: $A = 4 \cdot \frac{1}{2} \cdot 6 \cdot \sqrt{40} = 12\sqrt{40} \approx 75.9\ \mathrm{m}^2$ of glass.

TASK 3LEVEL III · EVALUATING

3Does the ice cream overflow?

An ice-cream cone has the shape of a cone: inside $6$ cm across at the rim and $12$ cm deep. On top sits a scoop of ice cream, also $6$ cm across. The thickness of the wafer is ignored.

Calculate the volume of the scoop and the inside volume of the cone.

Sphere: $V = \frac{4}{3}\pi \cdot 3^3 = 36\pi \approx 113.1\ \mathrm{cm}^3$. Cone: $V = \frac{1}{3}\pi \cdot 3^2 \cdot 12 = 36\pi \approx 113.1\ \mathrm{cm}^3$.

The scoop melts completely into the cone. Investigate whether the cone overflows.

The two volumes are exactly equal, $36\pi\ \mathrm{cm}^3$. So the cone becomes full to the brim but (in the model) does not overflow. In reality ice cream contains air; melted, it even needs a little less space.

Find how high (measured from the tip) the melted ice cream stands in the cone when only half of the scoop has melted.

The ice cream fills a smaller cone of the same shape: radius and height are always in the ratio $3 : 12$, so $r = \frac{x}{4}$ at the filling height $x$. $\frac{1}{3}\pi \left(\frac{x}{4}\right)^2 x = 18\pi$ gives $x^3 = 864$ and $x \approx 9.52$ cm. So half the volume is not at half the height but already at about four fifths of the depth.

Calculate the minimum amount of wafer needed for the lateral surface of the cone.

Slant height $s = \sqrt{3^2 + 12^2} = \sqrt{153} \approx 12.37$ cm. Lateral surface $M = \pi r s = \pi \cdot 3 \cdot \sqrt{153} \approx 116.6\ \mathrm{cm}^2$.

LOOKING BACKTHE IDEA AND THE TRAP

TASK 1 · THE GREENHOUSE

Split the cross-section: rectangle plus semicircle. The length turns it into the volume.

Trap: for the roof it is half the circumference, $\pi r$, that counts, not $2\pi r$. And the result of $2.5$ rolls must be rounded up: you cannot buy half rolls.

TASK 2 · THE GLASS ROOF

First the diagonal of the base, then Pythagoras for the height.

Trap: in a) half the diagonal is needed, not half the base edge. With $3$ m instead of $4.24$ m you get $\sqrt{40}$, and that is the height of a side face from d), not the height of the pyramid.

TASK 3 · DOES THE ICE CREAM OVERFLOW?

Comparing equal volumes works without a calculator. With half the ice cream, the volume goes with the cube of the height.

Trap: in c) the guess “half the volume, half the height” is tempting, but the cone is narrow at the bottom. If the filling height is halved, the volume shrinks to an eighth, because all three dimensions are halved.

$10 \cdot 12 = 120$

$\frac{1}{3} \cdot 36 \cdot 5 = 60$

ROUND 2

A sphere has $r = 3$. The volume is $k \cdot \pi$. What is $k$?

$\frac{4}{3} \cdot 27 = 36$

You need $113$ m² of film; one roll has $50$ m². How many rolls do you buy?

$2.26$ is rounded up.
