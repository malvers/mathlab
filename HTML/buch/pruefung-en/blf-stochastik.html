KEY POINT · DATA AND CHANCE

Angle in the pie chart: relative frequency times $360°$.

Weighted mean: every value counts as often as it occurs: $\overline{x} = \frac{\sum h_i \cdot x_i}{\sum h_i}$.

Without replacement: the bag gets smaller; the denominator falls by one at the second draw.

Fair: $E(G) = 0$ for the profit $G$, i.e. payout minus stake.

TASK 1LEVEL I · REPRODUCING

1How do you get to school?

In a class of $28$ students they were asked how they usually get to school: $14$ by bike, $7$ by bus, $5$ on foot and $2$ by car.

State the relative frequencies of the four answers.

Bike $\frac{14}{28} = \frac{1}{2} = 50\%$, bus $\frac{7}{28} = \frac{1}{4} = 25\%$, on foot $\frac{5}{28} \approx 17.9\%$, car $\frac{2}{28} = \frac{1}{14} \approx 7.1\%$.

The results are to be shown in a pie chart. Calculate the sizes of the four central angles.

Each share times $360°$: bike $180°$, bus $90°$, on foot $\frac{5}{28} \cdot 360° \approx 64.3°$, car $\frac{2}{28} \cdot 360° \approx 25.7°$. Check: together $360°$.

Lena gave the relative frequencies, rounded, as $0.50$, $0.35$, $0.18$ and $0.07$. Justify that at least one of her values is wrong.

The relative frequencies of all possible answers must add up to $1$. Lena's values give $0.50 + 0.35 + 0.18 + 0.07 = 1.10$. Rounding does not explain that: four values rounded to two decimal places differ from $1$ by at most $4 \cdot 0.005 = 0.02$ in total. The mistake is in the bus: $\frac{7}{28} = 0.25$ and not $0.35$.

By bike the students take $12$ minutes on average, by bus $25$, on foot $10$ and by car $15$ minutes. State the meaning of the term $\frac{1}{28} \cdot (14 \cdot 12 + 7 \cdot 25 + 5 \cdot 10 + 2 \cdot 15)$ in context and calculate its value.

The term is the arithmetic mean of the journey times of the whole class: each group's time is weighted with the number of people and divided by all $28$. $\frac{168 + 175 + 50 + 30}{28} = \frac{423}{28} \approx 15.1$ minutes.

TASK 2LEVEL II · MAKING CONNECTIONS

2Drawing without replacement

A bag contains five red and three blue balls. Two balls are drawn one after the other without putting the first one back.

Calculate the probability that both balls are red.

First draw red with $\frac{5}{8}$; after that there are four red among seven balls: $P = \frac{5}{8} \cdot \frac{4}{7} = \frac{20}{56} = \frac{5}{14} \approx 0.357$.

Calculate the probability that the two balls have different colours.

Two paths, red–blue and blue–red: $P = \frac{5}{8} \cdot \frac{3}{7} + \frac{3}{8} \cdot \frac{5}{7} = \frac{30}{56} = \frac{15}{28} \approx 0.536$.

The random variable $X$ is the number of red balls drawn. State the probability distribution of $X$ and calculate the expected value.

$P(X = 0) = \frac{3}{8} \cdot \frac{2}{7} = \frac{3}{28}$, $P(X = 1) = \frac{15}{28}$, $P(X = 2) = \frac{10}{28}$. Check: $\frac{3 + 15 + 10}{28} = 1$. Expected value: $E(X) = 0 \cdot \frac{3}{28} + 1 \cdot \frac{15}{28} + 2 \cdot \frac{10}{28} = \frac{35}{28} = 1.25$.

Justify without calculation that “red twice” is more likely with replacement than without, and confirm it by calculation.

Without replacement one red ball is missing at the second draw; the proportion of red falls from $\frac{5}{8}$ to $\frac{4}{7}$. With replacement it stays at $\frac{5}{8}$. Calculation: $\left(\frac{5}{8}\right)^2 = \frac{25}{64} \approx 0.391 > 0.357$.

TASK 3LEVEL III · EVALUATING

3Is the dice game fair?

At a school fête a game is played with two dice. The stake is €$1$. For a double (two equal scores) €$4$ is paid out, for a total of $7$ €$2$ is paid out, otherwise nothing. A game is called fair if the expected value of the profit is zero.

Calculate the probabilities of a double and of a total of $7$.

Of the $36$ equally likely outcomes, $6$ are doubles and $6$ have the total $7$ (see table): $\frac{6}{36} = \frac{1}{6}$ each. The two events exclude each other, because a double always has an even total.

The random variable $G$ is a player's profit (payout minus stake). State the distribution of $G$ and investigate whether the game is fair.

$G$ takes the values €$3$ (double), €$1$ (total $7$) and −€$1$ (otherwise), with the probabilities $\frac{1}{6}$, $\frac{1}{6}$ and $\frac{2}{3}$. $E(G) = 3 \cdot \frac{1}{6} + 1 \cdot \frac{1}{6} - 1 \cdot \frac{2}{3} = \frac{3 + 1 - 4}{6} = 0$. The game is fair.

Calculate the standard deviation of $G$.

$V(G) = (3 - 0)^2 \cdot \frac{1}{6} + (1 - 0)^2 \cdot \frac{1}{6} + (-1 - 0)^2 \cdot \frac{2}{3} = \frac{9 + 1 + 4}{6} = \frac{7}{3}$, so $\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{7}{3}} \approx 1.53$ euros. So individual games deviate clearly from the mean of zero, although the game is fair.

The class wants to take €$0.20$ per game on average. Only the payout for a double is to be changed. Calculate the new payout.

The mean payout must be €$1 - 0.20 = 0.80$: $x \cdot \frac{1}{6} + 2 \cdot \frac{1}{6} = 0.80$ gives $x + 2 = 4.80$, so $x = 2.80$ euros. Check: $E(G) = \frac{1.80 + 1 - 4}{6} = -0.20$ euros for the player.

LOOKING BACKTHE IDEA AND THE TRAP

TASK 1 · HOW DO YOU GET TO SCHOOL?

Relative frequencies always add up to $1$. In the mean, every group counts with its size.

Trap: in d), $\frac{12 + 25 + 10 + 15}{4} = 15.5$ is not the same: this unweighted mean pretends that as many people travel by car as by bike.

TASK 2 · DRAWING WITHOUT REPLACEMENT

A tree with a shrinking denominator. With replacement the proportion stays the same and is therefore larger.

Trap: the expected value is the same in both cases, namely $2 \cdot \frac{5}{8} = 1.25$. Replacement changes the distribution, not the mean.

TASK 3 · IS THE DICE GAME FAIR?

First count all the cases, then calculate $E(G)$. Fair means zero, not “feels fair”.

Trap: profit and payout are not the same. If in b) you calculate with the payouts $4$ and $2$ and forget the stake, you get $E = 1$ and think the game is a present to the players.
