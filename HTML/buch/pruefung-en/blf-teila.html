TASK 1LEVEL I · REPRODUCING

1Four multiple-choice questions

In each part exactly one of the five answers is correct. Write down only the letter; no working is required. Calculators and formula booklets are not allowed.

The term $\dfrac{a^{5} \cdot a^{-2}}{a^{4}}$ with $a \neq 0$ is equivalent to:

  • (A) $a^{3}$
  • (B) $\frac{1}{a}$
  • (C) $a$
  • (D) $a^{-14}$
  • (E) $\frac{1}{a^{3}}$

The answer is (B). In the numerator the exponents are added: $a^{5} \cdot a^{-2} = a^{3}$. When dividing, they are subtracted: $a^{3} : a^{4} = a^{-1} = \frac{1}{a}$. (A) is only the numerator, (D) comes from calculating $5 \cdot (-2)$ instead of $5 + (-2)$, and (C) and (E) have lost the sign or the size of the exponent. Check with $a = 2$: $\frac{32 \cdot \frac{1}{4}}{16} = \frac{8}{16} = \frac{1}{2}$.

A volume of $0.35\ \mathrm{m}^3$ is:

  • (A) $35\ \mathrm{dm}^3$
  • (B) $3500\ \mathrm{cm}^3$
  • (C) $350\ \ell$
  • (D) $35\ \mathrm{hl}$
  • (E) $0.35\ \ell$

The answer is (C). A cubic metre has $1000$ cubic decimetres, and a cubic decimetre is a litre. So $0.35\ \mathrm{m}^3 = 350\ \mathrm{dm}^3 = 350\ \ell$. (A) calculates with the factor $100$ as for areas, (B) is only $3.5\ \ell$, (D) is $3500\ \ell$ and (E) is a thousand times too small. In hectolitres, $3.5\ \mathrm{hl}$ would have been right.

A price is first increased by $10\%$ and then reduced by $10\%$. Compared with the original price:

  • (A) It has stayed the same.
  • (B) It has fallen by $1\%$.
  • (C) It has risen by $1\%$.
  • (D) It has fallen by $0.1\%$.
  • (E) It has fallen by $10\%$.

The answer is (B). Both changes are factors: $1.1 \cdot 0.9 = 0.99$. The final price is $99\%$ of the original price, so $1\%$ lower. The $10\%$ discount refers to the increased price and is therefore more than the increase. Example: €100 → €110 → €99.

Which function $f$ defined on $\mathbb{R}$ has a graph that crosses the $y$-axis at the point $(0, 3)$ and is monotonically decreasing on its whole domain?

  • (A) $f(x) = 3x$
  • (B) $f(x) = -x + 3$
  • (C) $f(x) = x^2 + 3$
  • (D) $f(x) = 3 \cdot 2^{x}$
  • (E) $f(x) = -3x$

The answer is (B). The graph of $f(x) = -x + 3$ is a line with gradient $-1$ (decreasing) and $y$-intercept $3$. (D) also crosses the $y$-axis at $3$, since $3 \cdot 2^{0} = 3$, but it is monotonically increasing. (C) has its vertex at $(0, 3)$ and only decreases for $x < 0$. (A) and (E) pass through the origin.

TASK 2LEVEL II · MAKING CONNECTIONS

2A line and its cone

The figure shows the graph of the linear function $g$, which passes through the points $P(-2, 5)$ and $Q(4, 2)$. The task is to be solved without a calculator; roots and $\pi$ may be left in the result.

State an equation of the function $g$.

Gradient from the two points: $m = \frac{2 - 5}{4 - (-2)} = \frac{-3}{6} = -\frac{1}{2}$. Substituting $Q$ into $y = -\frac{1}{2}x + n$: $2 = -2 + n$, so $n = 4$. So $g(x) = -\frac{1}{2}x + 4$. Check with $P$: $-\frac{1}{2} \cdot (-2) + 4 = 1 + 4 = 5$.

Calculate the value of $x$ at which $g$ takes the function value $-46$.

$-\frac{1}{2}x + 4 = -46$ gives $-\frac{1}{2}x = -50$ and hence $x = 100$. At $x = 100$, $g$ has the function value $-46$.

The graph of $g$ and the two coordinate axes bound a triangle. Calculate the length of the side of this triangle that lies on the graph of $g$.

The line crosses the $y$-axis at $(0, 4)$ and the $x$-axis where $-\frac{1}{2}x + 4 = 0$, i.e. at $(8, 0)$. The side we want is the hypotenuse of the right-angled triangle with the legs $8$ and $4$: $\sqrt{8^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{80} = \sqrt{16 \cdot 5} = 4\sqrt{5}$. Since $\sqrt{5} \approx 2.24$, that is about $9$ units of length.

The triangle rotates about the $y$-axis. Justify without a calculator that the volume of the resulting solid is greater than $250$ units of volume.

Rotating about the $y$-axis produces a cone with radius $r = 8$ (the section on the $x$-axis) and height $h = 4$. $V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h = \frac{1}{3} \cdot \pi \cdot 64 \cdot 4 = \frac{256}{3}\pi$. Since $\pi > 3$, $V > \frac{256}{3} \cdot 3 = 256 > 250$. So the volume is greater than $250$ units of volume.

TASK 3LEVEL III · EVALUATING

3The spinner with three colours

A spinner consists of three sectors: the red one has a central angle of $180°$, the blue one $120°$ and the yellow one $60°$. A random experiment consists of spinning the wheel and noting the colour the pointer points to.

Justify that spinning once is not a Laplace experiment.

In a Laplace experiment all outcomes are equally likely. Here the sectors are of different sizes: $P(\text{red}) = \frac{180}{360} = \frac{1}{2}$, $P(\text{blue}) = \frac{120}{360} = \frac{1}{3}$ and $P(\text{yellow}) = \frac{60}{360} = \frac{1}{6}$. So the three colours are not equally likely.

The wheel is spun twice. Calculate the probability that the same colour comes up both times.

Three paths with two equal colours: $P = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{1}{6}\right)^2 = \frac{9}{36} + \frac{4}{36} + \frac{1}{36} = \frac{14}{36} = \frac{7}{18}$. That is just under $39\%$.

The wheel is spun three times. Calculate the probability that yellow comes up at least once.

Via the complementary event “no yellow”: $P = 1 - \left(\frac{5}{6}\right)^3 = 1 - \frac{125}{216} = \frac{91}{216}$. That is about $42\%$. Without a calculator the fraction is enough.

The wheel is spun three times. State an event whose probability can be calculated with the term $3 \cdot \frac{1}{3} \cdot \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{2}$.

$\frac{1}{3}$ is the probability of blue, $\frac{2}{3}$ that of “not blue”. A path with blue once and something else twice has the probability $\frac{1}{3} \cdot \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^2$, and there are three such paths, depending on the spin at which blue comes. So the event is: “In three spins blue comes up exactly once.”

LOOKING BACKTHE IDEA AND THE TRAP

TASK 1 · FOUR MULTIPLE-CHOICE QUESTIONS

Transform step by step and rule out what is wrong. A percentage stays a factor.

Trap: in c) it is very tempting to add $+10\%$ and $-10\%$ to zero. But percentages always refer to the current base value, so they are multiplied as factors, not added.

TASK 2 · A LINE AND ITS CONE

First the line, from it the intercepts, then Pythagoras and the cone.

Trap: in d) the axis of rotation matters. About the $y$-axis the $8$ becomes the radius; about the $x$-axis it would be the other way round, radius $4$ and height $8$, with only $\frac{128}{3}\pi$. And for the justification the rough estimate $\pi > 3$ is enough; you do not have to calculate with $\pi$ at all.

TASK 3 · THE SPINNER WITH THREE COLOURS

“At least once” goes via the complementary event; the fractions stay fractions.

Trap: in c) the complementary event helps. If you calculate “at least once” directly, you have to add the cases one, two and three yellows separately and can easily forget a path. And in d) the factor $3$ belongs to “exactly once”, not to “at least once”.
