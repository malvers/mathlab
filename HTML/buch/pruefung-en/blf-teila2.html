TASK 1LEVEL I · REPRODUCING

1Four more multiple-choice questions

In each part exactly one of the five answers is correct. Write down only the letter. The figure belongs to part a).

Which statement about the triangle $ABC$ shown is false?

  • (A) $\sin\alpha = \frac{a}{c}$
  • (B) $\cos\alpha = \frac{b}{c}$
  • (C) $\tan\alpha = \frac{a}{b}$
  • (D) $\sin\beta = \frac{a}{c}$
  • (E) $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$

The false one is (D). For $\beta$, $b$ is the opposite side, so $\sin\beta = \frac{b}{c}$. The fraction $\frac{a}{c}$ belongs to $\cos\beta$. (A) to (C) are correct: $a$ lies opposite $\alpha$, $b$ is adjacent to $\alpha$, $c$ is the hypotenuse. (E) is Pythagoras' theorem with the right angle at $C$.

For the function $f$ defined on $\mathbb{R}$ with $f(x) = 2 \cdot \sin x + 1$:

  • (A) The range is $-1 \leq y \leq 3$.
  • (B) The smallest period is $\pi$.
  • (C) The graph is symmetric about the $y$-axis.
  • (D) $f(0) = 2$
  • (E) $x = 0$ is a zero of $f$.

The answer is (A). $\sin x$ runs between $-1$ and $1$; doubled that is $-2$ to $2$, shifted up by $1$ it is $-1$ to $3$. (B) The period stays $2\pi$, because there is no factor in front of the $x$. (C) The graph is not symmetric about the axis, because $f\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right) = 3$ but $f\left(-\frac{\pi}{2}\right) = -1$. (D) and (E) fail because $f(0) = 2 \cdot 0 + 1 = 1$.

The system of equations $2x + y = 7$ and $x - y = -1$ has the solution:

  • (A) $x = 3, y = 1$
  • (B) $x = 2, y = 3$
  • (C) $x = 1, y = 5$
  • (D) $x = 3, y = 4$
  • (E) $x = -2, y = -1$

The answer is (B). Adding the two equations eliminates $y$: $3x = 6$, so $x = 2$ and $y = 7 - 4 = 3$. Check: $2 - 3 = -1$. The other four pairs each satisfy only one of the two equations, (A) and (C) the first, (D) and (E) the second. So a check must always test both equations.

A bicycle lock has three dials, each with the digits $0$ to $9$. How many different settings are there?

  • (A) $30$
  • (B) $100$
  • (C) $720$
  • (D) $1000$
  • (E) $59\,049$

The answer is (D). Each dial has $10$ possibilities independently of the others, and digits may repeat: $10 \cdot 10 \cdot 10 = 1000$. These are exactly the numbers from $000$ to $999$. (C) $= 10 \cdot 9 \cdot 8$ forbids repetitions, (A) adds instead of multiplying, and (E) $= 3^{10}$ swaps base and exponent.

TASK 2LEVEL II · MAKING CONNECTIONS

2Circle, sector and Thales

In a coordinate system there is a circle with its centre $M$ at the origin. It passes through the point $P(3, 4)$. Calculate without a calculator; $\pi$ and roots may be left in the result.

State the radius of the circle.

The radius is the distance from $M(0, 0)$ to $P$: $r = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5$.

Show that $Q(-4, 3)$ also lies on the circle and that the line segments $\overline{MP}$ and $\overline{MQ}$ are perpendicular.

$(-4)^2 + 3^2 = 16 + 9 = 25 = r^2$, so $Q$ is also at distance $5$ from $M$ and lies on the circle. The line $MP$ has gradient $\frac{4}{3}$, the line $MQ$ gradient $\frac{3}{-4} = -\frac{3}{4}$. The product is $\frac{4}{3} \cdot \left(-\frac{3}{4}\right) = -1$, so the two line segments are perpendicular.

Calculate the area of the sector between $\overline{MP}$ and $\overline{MQ}$ and the length of the corresponding arc.

The central angle is $90°$; the sector is a quarter circle. $A = \frac{1}{4} \pi r^2 = \frac{25}{4}\pi$ and $b = \frac{1}{4} \cdot 2\pi r = \frac{5}{2}\pi$. That is about $19.6$ units of area and $7.9$ units of length.

A third point $R$ lies on the circle so that $\angle PQR = 90°$. State the length of the line segment $\overline{PR}$ and justify your answer.

$\overline{PR} = 10$. By the converse of Thales' theorem, $\angle PQR$ is a right angle exactly when $\overline{PR}$ is a diameter of the circle. So $\overline{PR} = 2r = 10$ and $R$ is the point $(-3, -4)$ opposite $P$.

TASK 3LEVEL III · EVALUATING

3Equations without a calculator

The four parts are independent of each other and are to be solved without aids.

The volume of a cone is $V = \frac{1}{3} \pi r^2 h$. Rearrange the formula for $h$.

Multiply by $3$ and divide by $\pi r^2$: $h = \dfrac{3V}{\pi r^2}$.

Determine $p$ so that $x = -2$ is a solution of the equation $x^2 + p \cdot x - 10 = 0$, and state the second solution.

Substitute: $(-2)^2 + p \cdot (-2) - 10 = 0$, so $4 - 2p - 10 = 0$ and $p = -3$. The equation is then $x^2 - 3x - 10 = 0$, factorised $(x + 2)(x - 5) = 0$. The second solution is $x = 5$. By Vieta this fits: $-2 \cdot 5 = -10$ and $-2 + 5 = 3 = -p$.

A rectangle has perimeter $34$ cm, and its diagonal is $13$ cm long. Calculate the area of the rectangle.

From the perimeter $a + b = 17$, from Pythagoras $a^2 + b^2 = 169$. Squaring the first equation gives $a^2 + 2ab + b^2 = 289$. Subtracting the second leaves $2ab = 120$, so $A = ab = 60\ \mathrm{cm}^2$. You do not need the sides themselves for this; they are $12$ cm and $5$ cm.

State all values of $c$ for which the equation $x^2 - 6x + c = 0$ has no real solution. Justify your answer.

Complete the square: $x^2 - 6x + c = (x - 3)^2 - 9 + c$. The equation $(x - 3)^2 = 9 - c$ has no solution if the right-hand side is negative, because a square is never negative. So for all $c > 9$. For $c = 9$ there is exactly one solution ($x = 3$), for $c < 9$ two.

LOOKING BACKTHE IDEA AND THE TRAP

TASK 1 · FOUR MORE MULTIPLE-CHOICE QUESTIONS

In the system of equations every wrong answer satisfies one of the equations. Always check both.

Trap: in a) it helps to ask each time from the angle: which side is opposite, which is adjacent? If you memorise “$a$ is always on top”, you get $\beta$ wrong.

TASK 2 · CIRCLE, SECTOR AND THALES

Radius from Pythagoras, perpendicularity from the scalar product or the gradients, then the sector as a quarter circle.

Trap: in d) you do not have to calculate anything if you recognise Thales: the right angle is at $Q$, so the side opposite $Q$ is the diameter.

TASK 3 · EQUATIONS WITHOUT A CALCULATOR

For “no solution” the term under the root decides.

Trap: in c) the detour via the separate sides is possible but longer: you would have to solve a quadratic equation. The trick with $(a + b)^2$ gives the product $ab$ directly, and that is what is asked for.
