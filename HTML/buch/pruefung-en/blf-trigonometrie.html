Trigonometry

How far is the buoy from the shore, and does the tower fit in the photo? Trigonometry measures what you cannot walk to. In the BLF, what counts above all is choosing the right rule and a clean sketch.

TOOLKITWHAT YOU NEED FOR THIS CHAPTER
KEY POINT · TRIANGLES

Sine rule: $\frac{a}{\sin \alpha} = \frac{b}{\sin \beta} = \frac{c}{\sin \gamma}$. You need a pair of a side and its opposite angle.

Cosine rule: $c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab \cdot \cos \gamma$. You need two sides and the angle between them, or all three sides.

Area: $A = \frac{1}{2} \cdot a \cdot b \cdot \sin \gamma$.

Do not calculate with rounded intermediate values, otherwise the last digit is no longer right.

TASK 1LEVEL I · REPRODUCING

1One triangle, three rules

In the triangle $ABC$, $a = 8$ cm, $b = 5$ cm and $\gamma = 60°$ are given (see figure, not to scale).

Calculate the length of the side $c$.

Cosine rule: $c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab \cdot \cos\gamma = 64 + 25 - 2 \cdot 8 \cdot 5 \cdot 0.5 = 49$, so $c = 7$ cm.

Calculate the sizes of the angles $\alpha$ and $\beta$.

The angle opposite the shorter side first; then the sine rule is unambiguous: $\sin\beta = \frac{b \cdot \sin\gamma}{c} = \frac{5 \cdot \sin 60°}{7} \approx 0.619$, so $\beta \approx 38.2°$ (acute, because $b < c$ and hence $\beta < \gamma$). Then $\alpha = 180° - 60° - 38.2° \approx 81.8°$. Check with the cosine rule: $\cos\alpha = \frac{25 + 49 - 64}{2 \cdot 5 \cdot 7} = \frac{1}{7}$, $\alpha \approx 81.8°$.

Calculate the area of the triangle.

$A = \frac{1}{2} \cdot a \cdot b \cdot \sin\gamma = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 8 \cdot 5 \cdot \sin 60° = 10\sqrt{3} \approx 17.3\ \mathrm{cm}^2$.

Calculate the length of the height $h_c$ onto the side $c$.

From $A = \frac{1}{2} \cdot c \cdot h_c$ it follows that $h_c = \frac{2A}{c} = \frac{20\sqrt{3}}{7} \approx 4.95$ cm.

TASK 2LEVEL II · MAKING CONNECTIONS

2The buoy off the shore

A buoy $L$ is sighted from two points $A$ and $B$ on a straight lake shore that are $800$ m apart. $\angle BAL = 52°$ and $\angle ABL = 71°$ (see figure). The point $F$ is the point on the shore closest to the buoy.

Calculate the distance of the buoy from the point $A$.

The angle at $L$ is $180° - 52° - 71° = 57°$. Sine rule: $\overline{AL} = \frac{800 \cdot \sin 71°}{\sin 57°} \approx 902$ m.

Calculate the shortest distance $d$ of the buoy from the shore.

In the right-angled triangle $AFL$, $d$ is the side opposite $52°$: $d = \overline{AL} \cdot \sin 52° \approx 902 \cdot 0.788 \approx 711$ m.

Calculate the distance of the point $F$ from the point $A$.

$\overline{AF} = \overline{AL} \cdot \cos 52° \approx 555$ m. So the point $F$ lies between $A$ and $B$, nearer to $B$.

The point $C$ lies on the shore $300$ m beyond $B$. A boat travels in a straight line from the buoy to $C$. Calculate the length of this route.

Cosine rule in the triangle $ALC$ with $\overline{AC} = 1100$ m: $\overline{LC}^2 = 902^2 + 1100^2 - 2 \cdot 902 \cdot 1100 \cdot \cos 52°$, so $\overline{LC} \approx 895$ m. A second way via Pythagoras: $\overline{FC} \approx 1100 - 555 = 545$ m and $\overline{LC} = \sqrt{711^2 + 545^2} \approx 896$ m. The difference of one metre comes only from the rounded intermediate values.

TASK 3LEVEL III · EVALUATING

3Does the tower fit in the photo?

A camera $K$ stands on a tripod at a height of $1.5$ m, $30$ m from the foot $F$ of a tower $45$ m high. The lens has a vertical angle of view of $60°$: everything that lies within this angle appears in the photo. The camera may be tilted unless stated otherwise.

Calculate the angle at which the top of the tower $S$ appears above the horizontal as seen from $K$.

The top is $45 - 1.5 = 43.5$ m above the lens: $\tan\varepsilon = \frac{43.5}{30}$, so $\varepsilon \approx 55.4°$.

Investigate whether the tower fits into a photo at full height as seen from $K$.

The foot is $1.5$ m below the lens: $\tan\delta = \frac{1.5}{30}$, $\delta \approx 2.9°$. The tower appears at an angle $\angle FKS \approx 55.4° + 2.9° = 58.3°$. That is less than $60°$: the tower just fits into the photo if the camera is tilted suitably.

Find the smallest distance from the tower from which the whole tower just fits into a photo.

We need $x$ with $\arctan\frac{43.5}{x} + \arctan\frac{1.5}{x} = 60°$. The MMS gives $x \approx 28.3$ m. The camera must not come any closer, because the angle gets larger as the distance gets smaller.

The camera is now held horizontally, so the centre of the picture points horizontally forwards. Justify that the tower does not then fit completely into the photo from a distance of $30$ m, and calculate from what distance it works.

Held horizontally, the picture reaches only $30°$ above the horizontal. But the top appears at $55.4°$ and is therefore outside. It works as soon as $\frac{43.5}{x} \leq \tan 30°$, i.e. from $x = \frac{43.5}{\tan 30°} = 43.5 \cdot \sqrt{3} \approx 75.3$ m. The foot is then only $1.1°$ below the horizontal and is in the picture as well.

LOOKING BACKTHE IDEA AND THE TRAP

TASK 1 · ONE TRIANGLE, THREE RULES

Two sides and the angle between them: first the cosine rule, then the sine rule for the angles.

Trap: in b) the sine rule for $\alpha$ first gives $\sin\alpha \approx 0.990$, and two angles fit that, $81.8°$ and $98.2°$. The calculator only shows the acute one. If you begin with the angle opposite the shorter side or use the cosine rule, you avoid this trap.

TASK 2 · THE BUOY OFF THE SHORE

The baseline with two angles: third angle, sine rule, then the height in the right-angled part of the triangle.

Trap: with rounded intermediate results the answer in d) is one metre out. Keep calculating with stored values in the MMS and only round the answer.

TASK 3 · DOES THE TOWER FIT IN THE PHOTO?

Sketch with a horizontal line through the camera. The angle is measured from there upwards and downwards.

Trap: the angle of view is the angle between the two edge rays, not the angle to the top alone. In b) the small angle downwards to the foot therefore belongs to it, and exactly that decides that it still fits with $58.3°$.
