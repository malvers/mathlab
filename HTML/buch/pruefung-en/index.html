Practise as in the exam

We went through four years of Abitur papers, three years of BLF papers and four years of Vergleichsarbeit papers task by task. From them came 96 tasks of our own in exam format: three tasks per building block, from reproducing to evaluating, each with a worked solution, with the typical trap and with three dress rehearsals against the real clock.

This book trains for three exams in Saxony whose German names it keeps: the Abitur is the school-leaving exam, here at the Berufliches Gymnasium (BGY, a vocational grammar school); the BLF (Besondere Leistungsfeststellung) is the special performance assessment at the end of Year 10 at the Gymnasium (grammar school); the Vergleichsarbeit is the state-wide comparison test in Year 11 at the Berufliches Gymnasium.

CONTENTSABITUR, BLF, VERGLEICHSARBEIT

The book has three parts. The first takes you through the Abitur at the Berufliches Gymnasium, the second through the Besondere Leistungsfeststellung (BLF) in Year 10 at the Gymnasium, the third through the Vergleichsarbeit in Year 11 at the Berufliches Gymnasium. All three begin with a chapter that shows how the exam is built and end with a dress rehearsal against the real clock. On the right you can see who each chapter is meant for and how many self-checks you have already solved.

HOW TO USE THIS BOOK
EVERY CHAPTER HAS THE SAME STRUCTURE
  1. Opener: what is tested, plus a check-in.
  2. Toolkit: key-point box, a widget to try out and links into the textbooks.
  3. Three tasks in exam format: requirement levels I, II and III, with parts a to d.
  4. Looking back: the idea of each task and its typical trap.
  5. Test with two rounds: the same ideas, new numbers.
THE SYMBOLS

Level I   Level II   Level III

The levels are the three requirement levels of the German exams (Anforderungsbereiche, AFB): I reproducing, II making connections, III evaluating. Points in the exam are counted in marks (German: Bewertungseinheiten, BE).

Every part of a task has its solution to fold out. Work it out yourself first, on paper.

The tasks are worded as in the exam, as instructions such as “Calculate”. Everything else in this book talks to you directly.

In the self-checks you type in your result. Fractions ($3/10$), decimals ($0.3$) and percentages ($30\%$) are all understood. Your browser remembers what you have solved.

The textbooks linked from the chapters open in their English editions.

KEY POINT

Whatever is in an orange box, you need to be able to recall in the exam. Everything else helps you to understand it.
