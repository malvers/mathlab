CHECK-IN: WHAT YOU NEED

A ramp rises $1$ m over a horizontal length of $10$ m. What is its gradient in percent?

$\frac{1}{10} = 10\%$

What is $\tan 45°$?

The opposite and the adjacent side are equally long.

A square has side length $8$. How long is half its diagonal? Round to two decimal places.

$\frac{8\sqrt{2}}{2} = 4\sqrt{2} \approx 5.66$

TOOLKITWHAT YOU NEED FOR THIS CHAPTER
KEY POINT · TRIANGLES AND SOLIDS

Gradient: $\frac{\text{height}}{\text{horizontal length}} = \tan \alpha$. A gradient of $12.9\%$ is not an angle of $12.9°$.

Sine rule: $\frac{a}{\sin \alpha} = \frac{b}{\sin \beta}$. Cosine rule: $c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab \cdot \cos \gamma$.

Pyramid: height, half the diagonal and lateral edge form a right-angled triangle. Lateral surface of a cone: $M = \pi \cdot r \cdot s$.

Rounding up: trips, buckets and rolls are rounded up, even at $7.1$.

TASK 1LEVEL I · REPRODUCING

1The slipway at the Kriebstein reservoir

At the Kriebstein reservoir a slipway is being built for letting boats into the water. The ramp is a right triangular prism of concrete with a right-angled triangle as its base: it rises $1.8$ m over a horizontal length of $14.0$ m and is $4.5$ m wide (see figure, not to scale).

Calculate the length $s$ of the sloping driving surface and the angle of inclination $\alpha$ of the ramp.

Pythagoras in the right-angled triangle: $s = \sqrt{14.0^2 + 1.8^2} = \sqrt{199.24} \approx 14.1$ m. Angle of inclination: $\tan\alpha = \frac{1.8}{14.0}$, so $\alpha \approx 7.3°$.

A guide recommends a gradient of at most $15\%$ for slipways. Check whether the ramp meets this recommendation.

The gradient is the ratio of the height to the horizontal length: $\frac{1.8}{14.0} \approx 0.129$, i.e. $12.9\%$. That is less than $15\%$; the ramp meets the recommendation.

Calculate the volume of concrete in the ramp. A truck mixer brings at most $8\ \mathrm{m}^3$ of concrete per trip. Find the number of trips needed.

Base: $G = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 14.0 \cdot 1.8 = 12.6\ \mathrm{m}^2$. Volume: $V = G \cdot 4.5 = 56.7\ \mathrm{m}^3$. Trips: $\frac{56.7}{8} \approx 7.1$. After seven trips $0.7\ \mathrm{m}^3$ are still missing, so 8 trips are needed.

The driving surface gets a non-slip coating; one bucket is enough for $10\ \mathrm{m}^2$. Find how many buckets must be bought.

The driving surface is a rectangle with the sides $s$ and $4.5$ m: $A = s \cdot 4.5 \approx 63.5\ \mathrm{m}^2$; with the rounded value $s = 14.1$ m it is $63.45\ \mathrm{m}^2$. Six buckets are only enough for $60\ \mathrm{m}^2$ in both cases, so 7 buckets must be bought.

TASK 2LEVEL II · MAKING CONNECTIONS

2A bearing to the Bastei

From two points $P$ and $Q$ on the bank of the Elbe opposite the Bastei, the viewing platform $B$ is sighted. $P$ and $Q$ are $140$ m apart on a horizontal line through the point $F$, which lies vertically below $B$ at the level of the river bank. From $P$, $B$ appears at an angle of elevation of $31°$, from $Q$ at $47°$ (figure 1). On a hiking map (figure 2) you measure $3.2$ km from the Bastei to the Lilienstein $L$ and $4.9$ km to Königstein Fortress $K$; the two directions enclose an angle of $28°$.

Justify that the triangle $PQB$ has an angle of $16°$ at $B$, and calculate the length of the line segment $\overline{QB}$.

In the triangle $PQB$ the angle at $Q$ is the supplementary angle of $47°$, i.e. $180° - 47° = 133°$. Angle sum: $180° - 31° - 133° = 16°$. Sine rule: $\overline{QB} = \frac{140 \cdot \sin 31°}{\sin 16°} \approx 261.6$ m.

Calculate how high the platform is above the bank of the Elbe.

In the right-angled triangle $QFB$ the height $h = \overline{FB}$ is the side opposite $47°$: $h = 261.6 \cdot \sin 47° \approx 191.3$ m. The platform is about $191$ m above the river bank. If you rounded $\overline{QB}$ to $262$ m, you get $191.6$ m, i.e. almost $192$ m; both are correctly calculated. A second way without the sine rule: with $x = \overline{FQ}$, $h = x \cdot \tan 47°$ and $h = (x + 140) \cdot \tan 31°$. Setting equal gives $x \approx 178.4$ m and again $h \approx 191.3$ m.

Calculate the distance between the Lilienstein and Königstein Fortress.

Cosine rule in the triangle $BLK$: $\overline{LK}^2 = 3.2^2 + 4.9^2 - 2 \cdot 3.2 \cdot 4.9 \cdot \cos 28° \approx 6.561$, so $\overline{LK} \approx 2.56$ km.

Calculate the size of the angle $\lambda$ at which you see the Bastei and Königstein Fortress from the Lilienstein.

Sine rule: $\sin\lambda = \frac{4.9 \cdot \sin 28°}{2.561} \approx 0.898$. The calculator gives $63.9°$. But $\lambda$ lies opposite the longest side $\overline{BK}$ and is therefore the largest angle in the triangle; with $63.9°$ the larger angle $88.1°$ would be left for $K$. So $\lambda = 180° - 63.9° \approx 116.1°$. Check with the cosine rule: $\cos\lambda = \frac{3.2^2 + 2.561^2 - 4.9^2}{2 \cdot 3.2 \cdot 2.561} \approx -0.440$, so $\lambda \approx 116.1°$. With the rounded value $2.56$ km you get $116.0°$.

TASK 3LEVEL III · EVALUATING

3A new tower roof in Görlitz

A tower in the old town of Görlitz has a square plan with a side length of $8$ m. It is getting a roof in the shape of a right square pyramid whose lateral edges are $9$ m long (see figure, not to scale). The roof faces are covered with copper sheet.

Calculate the height $h$ of the roof.

The foot $M$ of the height is the centre of the square. Half the diagonal is $\frac{8\sqrt{2}}{2} = 4\sqrt{2} \approx 5.66$ m. In the right-angled triangle $AMS$: $h = \sqrt{9^2 - (4\sqrt{2})^2} = \sqrt{81 - 32} = \sqrt{49} = 7$ m.

Calculate the size of the angle of inclination of the roof faces to the horizontal and the area covered with copper.

In the section through $M$ and the midpoint $E$ of a base edge, the roof face rises $7$ m over a horizontal distance of $4$ m: $\tan\varphi = \frac{7}{4}$, so $\varphi \approx 60.3°$. Height of a roof face (green): $h_s = \sqrt{7^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{65} \approx 8.06$ m, likewise from $\sqrt{9^2 - 4^2}$. Copper area: $4 \cdot \frac{1}{2} \cdot 8 \cdot \sqrt{65} = 16\sqrt{65} \approx 129.0\ \mathrm{m}^2$.

As an alternative, a conical roof of the same height is proposed whose base circle passes through the four corners of the tower. Prove that its slant height is also $9$ m long, and calculate the size of the apex angle of the cone.

The base circle is the circumcircle of the square; its radius is half the diagonal, $r = 4\sqrt{2}$ m. Slant height: $m = \sqrt{r^2 + h^2} = \sqrt{32 + 49} = 9$ m, exactly as long as the lateral edge of the pyramid. The apex angle $\omega$ is the angle at the tip of the axial section: $\tan\frac{\omega}{2} = \frac{4\sqrt{2}}{7} \approx 0.808$, so $\frac{\omega}{2} \approx 38.94°$ and $\omega \approx 77.9°$.

A town councillor says: “A cone is the more economical shape; it needs less copper than the pyramid.” Assess this statement.

Lateral surface of the cone: $M = \pi \cdot r \cdot m = \pi \cdot 4\sqrt{2} \cdot 9 \approx 159.9\ \mathrm{m}^2$. That is about $24\%$ more than the $129.0\ \mathrm{m}^2$ of the pyramid; for this roof the statement is false. The reason: the base circle has to enclose the corners of the tower and, at $\pi \cdot 32 \approx 100.5\ \mathrm{m}^2$, is much larger than the square with $64\ \mathrm{m}^2$; the roof overhangs all round. A cone over the incircle with $r = 4$ m and $m = \sqrt{65} \approx 8.06$ m would need only $\pi \cdot 4 \cdot \sqrt{65} \approx 101.3\ \mathrm{m}^2$, but would leave the four corners of the tower open.

LOOKING BACKTHE IDEA AND THE TRAP

TASK 1 · THE SLIPWAY AT THE KRIEBSTEIN RESERVOIR

First Pythagoras for the sloping surface, then $\tan \alpha$. The gradient in percent is $\tan \alpha$, not the angle.

Trap: in b), $12.9\%$ is not an angle of $12.9°$. The gradient in percent is $\tan\alpha$; the angle itself is only $7.3°$. In c) and d) you round up, even when the decimal part is small: $7.1$ trips means $8$ trips.

TASK 2 · A BEARING TO THE BASTEI

The angle at $B$ from the angles of elevation, then the sine rule for the distance and the right-angled part of the triangle for the height.

Trap: for $\sin\lambda \approx 0.898$ the sine rule gives two angles, $63.9°$ and $116.1°$; the calculator only shows the acute one. If the angle you want lies opposite the longest side, the cosine rule is the safe way: a negative cosine reveals the obtuse angle at once.

TASK 3 · A NEW TOWER ROOF IN GÖRLITZ

Half the diagonal leads from the lateral edge to the height. For the angle of inclination of the roof face, the midpoint of the base edge counts.

Trap: in b) the angle of inclination of the roof face is asked for, measured above the midpoint of a base edge. If you take the lateral edge instead, you calculate $\tan\beta = \frac{7}{4\sqrt{2}}$ and get $\beta \approx 51.1°$: the angle of inclination of the edge, about nine degrees too little.

TESTTHE SAME IDEAS, NEW NUMBERS
ROUND 1

A ramp rises $3$ m over a horizontal length of $20$ m. What is its angle of inclination in degrees? Round to one decimal place.

$\tan \alpha = 0.15$, so $\alpha \approx 8.5°$

$a = 5$, $b = 8$ and $\gamma = 60°$. What is $c$?

$c^2 = 25 + 64 - 80 \cdot 0.5 = 49$

ROUND 2

A square pyramid has base edge $6$ and lateral edge $\sqrt{34}$. What is its height?

Half the diagonal is $3\sqrt{2}$, so $h^2 = 34 - 18 = 16$.

You need $63.2$ m² of coating; one bucket is enough for $10$ m². How many buckets do you buy?

$6.32$ is rounded up.
