Multi-stage random experiments

Three tickets, three cups, a survey: every one of the four Vergleichsarbeit papers analysed had a probability task, mostly with “all the same” and “exactly one”. A tree or a two-way table brings order before you calculate.

WHAT IS TESTED
  • Path rules for independent stages
  • Drawing without replacement
  • “At least one” via the complementary event
  • Two-way table and the right reference group
CHECK-IN: WHAT YOU NEED

A coin is tossed three times. What is the probability of three heads?

$\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^3 = \frac{1}{8}$

$5$ red and $5$ blue balls. After a red one, a second ball is drawn without replacement. What is the probability that it is red?

$9$ balls are left, $4$ of them red.

$P(\text{win}) = 0.2$. What is the probability of a blank?

Complementary event: $1 - 0.2 = 0.8$

TOOLKITWHAT YOU NEED FOR THIS CHAPTER
KEY POINT · MULTI-STAGE

Path rules: multiply along a path, add several paths.

At least one: $P = 1 - P(\text{none})$. $3 \cdot 0.2$ counts paths more than once.

Exactly one: the probability of one path times the number of orders, so with three stages times $3$.

Without replacement: numerator and denominator change from draw to draw.

Reference group: the denominator is the group within which you count. $\frac{180}{240}$ and $\frac{180}{244}$ ask different things.

TASK 1LEVEL I · REPRODUCING

1Three tickets at the lottery stall

A lottery stall at Dresden's Striezelmarkt Christmas market advertises with the slogan “Every fifth ticket wins!”. There are so many tickets in the drum that for each ticket drawn you can calculate with the probability of winning $0.2$, independently of the other tickets. Tom buys three tickets.

Calculate the probability that all three tickets are blanks.

For each ticket a blank has probability $0.8$. By the path rule $P = 0.8^3 = 0.512$, i.e. just over $51\%$.

Calculate the probability that Tom has at least one win.

“At least one win” is the complement of “three blanks”: $P = 1 - 0.8^3 = 1 - 0.512 = 0.488$.

Calculate the probability that Tom has exactly one win.

There are three paths with exactly one win (G N N, N G N and N N G), each with the probability $0.2 \cdot 0.8^2 = 0.128$. So $P = 3 \cdot 0.2 \cdot 0.8^2 = 0.384$.

Tom's sister says: “If I buy five tickets, I am sure to win, because $5 \cdot 0.2 = 1$.” Assess this statement.

The statement is false. Even with five tickets all five can be blanks, with the probability $0.8^5 \approx 0.328$. So at least one win has only the probability $1 - 0.8^5 \approx 0.672$, about two thirds. The product $5 \cdot 0.2 = 1$ states how many wins you can expect on average with five tickets and is no guarantee.

TASK 2LEVEL II · MAKING CONNECTIONS

2Mulled wine cups without replacement

At a mulled wine stall at the Striezelmarkt there are still $12$ cups in a box: $6$ with the Frauenkirche motif, $4$ with a Schwibbogen (candle arch) and $2$ with a Christmas pyramid. An employee takes out three cups at random one after another, without putting any of them back.

Calculate the probability that all three cups show the Frauenkirche motif.

$P = \frac{6}{12} \cdot \frac{5}{11} \cdot \frac{4}{10} = \frac{120}{1320} = \frac{1}{11} \approx 0.091$. After each Frauenkirche cup there is one such cup and one cup in total fewer.

Calculate the probability that all three cups show the same motif.

Three Frauenkirchen as in a), three candle arches with $\frac{4}{12} \cdot \frac{3}{11} \cdot \frac{2}{10} = \frac{24}{1320}$. Three pyramids are impossible, because there are only two in the box. Together: $P = \frac{120 + 24}{1320} = \frac{144}{1320} = \frac{6}{55} \approx 0.109$.

Calculate the probability that exactly one of the three cups shows the Frauenkirche motif.

With $F$ for Frauenkirche and $A$ for another motif there are three paths with exactly one $F$ (see tree diagram). Each has the probability $\frac{180}{1320}$, for example $FAA$ with $\frac{6}{12} \cdot \frac{6}{11} \cdot \frac{5}{10}$. So $P = 3 \cdot \frac{180}{1320} = \frac{540}{1320} = \frac{9}{22} \approx 0.409$.

Calculate the probability that all three motifs are represented.

A path with three different motifs, say Frauenkirche, candle arch, pyramid, has the probability $\frac{6}{12} \cdot \frac{4}{11} \cdot \frac{2}{10} = \frac{48}{1320}$. The three motifs can come in $3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1 = 6$ orders, and every order has the same factors in the numerator and the denominator: $P = 6 \cdot \frac{48}{1320} = \frac{288}{1320} = \frac{12}{55} \approx 0.218$.

TASK 3LEVEL III · EVALUATING

3The canteen survey

At a school $400$ students were asked about the canteen. The feature $M$ means “eats regularly in the canteen”, the feature $Z$ “is satisfied with the food”. $240$ of those asked eat regularly in the canteen; of these, $180$ are satisfied. In total $244$ of those asked are satisfied.

Complete the two-way table.

Row $M$: $240 - 180 = 60$ dissatisfied. Column $Z$: $244 - 180 = 64$ satisfied students who do not eat regularly in the canteen. Row $\overline{M}$: $400 - 240 = 160$, of whom $160 - 64 = 96$ are dissatisfied. Column $\overline{Z}$: $400 - 244 = 156$; check: $60 + 96 = 156$.

One person is chosen at random from those asked. Calculate the probability that they eat regularly in the canteen or are satisfied.

“$M$ or $Z$” includes everyone except the $96$ people who neither eat regularly in the canteen nor are satisfied: $P = \frac{400 - 96}{400} = \frac{304}{400} = 0.76$. A second way: $\frac{240 + 244 - 180}{400} = 0.76$; otherwise the $180$ people with both features would be counted twice.

State the meaning of the terms $\frac{180}{240}$ and $\frac{180}{244}$ in context. The student council writes: “$75\%$ of those who are satisfied eat regularly in the canteen.” Assess this statement.

$\frac{180}{240} = 0.75$ is the proportion of satisfied students among those who eat regularly in the canteen. $\frac{180}{244} \approx 0.738$ is the proportion of regular canteen eaters among those who are satisfied. The student council's statement confuses the reference groups. Correct would be: “$75\%$ of the regular canteen eaters are satisfied” or “about $74\%$ of those who are satisfied eat regularly in the canteen”.

For an interview, three of the $400$ students asked are chosen at random. Calculate the probability that all three are dissatisfied, and justify that you may also calculate approximately with $0.39^3$ here.

$156$ people are dissatisfied. Without replacement: $P = \frac{156}{400} \cdot \frac{155}{399} \cdot \frac{154}{398} \approx 0.0586$. With the fixed proportion $\frac{156}{400} = 0.39$ at each choice, you would get $0.39^3 \approx 0.0593$. Three people are so few compared with $400$ that the proportion hardly changes from draw to draw ($\frac{155}{399} \approx 0.388$ and $\frac{154}{398} \approx 0.387$); the approximation is only about $0.0007$ out.

LOOKING BACKTHE IDEA AND THE TRAP

TASK 1 · THREE TICKETS AT THE LOTTERY STALL

With independent tickets the probability stays the same. You calculate “at least one” via “none”.

Trap: in b), $3 \cdot 0.2 = 0.6$ is wrong: when you add, the paths with two or three wins are counted more than once. The complementary event “no win” is the shortest way.

TASK 2 · MULLED WINE CUPS WITHOUT REPLACEMENT

Without replacement the denominator gets smaller. For “exactly one” and “all different” the orders count too.

Trap: without replacement numerator and denominator change from draw to draw. If in a) you calculate with replacement, you get $\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^3 = 0.125$ instead of $0.091$. In c) and d) the most common omission is the factor for the orders, $3$ and $6$.

TASK 3 · THE CANTEEN SURVEY

First fill in the table completely, then name the reference group for each proportion.

Trap: in c) what decides is what the proportion refers to: the denominator is the group within which you count. $\frac{180}{240}$ and $\frac{180}{244}$ have the same numerator but different reference groups.

TESTTHE SAME IDEAS, NEW NUMBERS
ROUND 1

Every fifth ticket wins. What is the probability of winning at least once with two tickets?

$1 - 0.8^2 = 1 - 0.64 = 0.36$

With the same chance $0.2$: what is the probability of exactly one win with two tickets?

Two orders: $2 \cdot 0.2 \cdot 0.8 = 0.32$

ROUND 2

$6$ of $12$ cups show the Frauenkirche. Two are drawn without replacement. What is the probability that both show it?

$\frac{6}{12} \cdot \frac{5}{11} = \frac{30}{132} = \frac{5}{22}$

$180$ of $240$ canteen eaters are satisfied. What percentage of the canteen eaters is that?

The reference group is the $240$ canteen eaters: $\frac{180}{240} = 75\%$
