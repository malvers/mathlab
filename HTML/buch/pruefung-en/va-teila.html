CHECK-IN: WHAT YOU NEED

By what percentage does a value fall from $50$ to $40$?

The change of $10$ refers to the old value: $\frac{10}{50} = 20\%$

What is $\sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{8}$?

$\sqrt{2 \cdot 8} = \sqrt{16} = 4$

What is the gradient of the line through $(-2, 1)$ and $(2, 3)$?

$\frac{3 - 1}{2 - (-2)} = \frac{2}{4} = 0.5$

TOOLKITWHAT YOU NEED FOR THIS CHAPTER
KEY POINT · KNOWN WITHOUT A CALCULATOR

Percentages: a change always refers to the old value: $\frac{\text{change}}{\text{old value}}$.

Roots: $\sqrt{a} \cdot \sqrt{b} = \sqrt{a \cdot b}$, but $\sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b}$ is not $\sqrt{a + b}$.

Units: $1\ \ell = 1\ \mathrm{dm}^3$. A cube with an edge length of $5$ dm holds $125\ \ell$.

Parallel lines have the same gradient. Rhombus: $A = \frac{e \cdot f}{2}$ from the two diagonals.

Without replacement: at the second stage numerator and denominator change. The branches from one node add up to $1$.

STRATEGY · MULTIPLE CHOICE

TASK 1LEVEL I · REPRODUCING

1Six multiple-choice questions

This task corresponds to the multiple-choice questions A1 to A6 of the Vergleichsarbeit; each part contains two of them. In each, exactly one of the five answers is correct. Write down only the letter. Calculators and formula booklets are not allowed.

A1 At the school bike check, $40$ bikes had faults last year, this year only $30$. By what percentage has the number of bikes with faults fallen?

  • (A) $10\%$
  • (B) $25\%$
  • (C) $30\%$
  • (D) $33\frac{1}{3}\%$
  • (E) $75\%$ ‖ A2 The term $\sqrt{8} \cdot \sqrt{2} + 4^{-1}$ has the value:
  • (A) $3.75$
  • (B) $\sqrt{10} - 4$
  • (C) $0$
  • (D) $16.25$
  • (E) $4.25$

A1: The answer is (B). The decrease is $10$ bikes, relative to the old value: $\frac{10}{40} = \frac{1}{4} = 25\%$. (D) relates the decrease to the new value, $\frac{10}{30} = 33\frac{1}{3}\%$, and (E) is the new value as a percentage of the old one, $\frac{30}{40} = 75\%$. A2: The answer is (E). $\sqrt{8} \cdot \sqrt{2} = \sqrt{16} = 4$ and $4^{-1} = \frac{1}{4}$, together $4.25$. (A) comes from $4^{-1} = -\frac{1}{4}$, (C) from $4^{-1} = -4$, (D) from leaving out the square root of $16$, and (B) adds the radicands and also calculates $4^{-1} = -4$.

A3 A cube-shaped container holds exactly $125$ litres. Its edge length is:

  • (A) $5\ \mathrm{cm}$
  • (B) $12.5\ \mathrm{cm}$
  • (C) $50\ \mathrm{cm}$
  • (D) $1.25\ \mathrm{m}$
  • (E) $5\ \mathrm{m}$ ‖ A4 Each interior angle of a regular octagon is:
  • (A) $135°$
  • (B) $45°$
  • (C) $120°$
  • (D) $144°$
  • (E) $1080°$

A3: The answer is (C). $125\ \ell = 125\ \mathrm{dm}^3$, and since $5^3 = 125$, the edge is $5\ \mathrm{dm} = 50\ \mathrm{cm}$ long. A cube with a $5\ \mathrm{cm}$ edge (A) holds only $125\ \mathrm{cm}^3 = 0.125\ \ell$, one with a $5\ \mathrm{m}$ edge (E) a whole $125\ \mathrm{m}^3$. (B) and (D) only divide by $10$ or $100$ instead of taking the cube root. A4: The answer is (A). The sum of the interior angles of an $n$-gon is $(n - 2) \cdot 180°$, for the octagon $6 \cdot 180° = 1080°$ (E). Shared out over eight equal angles that is $1080° : 8 = 135°$. (B) is the central angle $360° : 8$, (C) belongs to the hexagon and (D) to the decagon.

A5 For the function $f$ defined on $\mathbb{R}$ with $f(x) = -(x - 2)^2 + 3$:

  • (A) The graph opens upwards.
  • (B) $S(-2, 3)$ is the vertex.
  • (C) The point $(0, 3)$ lies on the graph.
  • (D) $f$ has no zero.
  • (E) $S(2, 3)$ is the vertex. ‖ A6 A dice is thrown three times. The probability that no six comes up is:
  • (A) $\frac{1}{216}$
  • (B) $\frac{1}{2}$
  • (C) $\frac{91}{216}$
  • (D) $\frac{125}{216}$
  • (E) $\frac{5}{6}$

A5: The answer is (E). From the vertex form $f(x) = a(x - d)^2 + e$ you read off $S(d, e) = S(2, 3)$; (B) has the wrong sign of $d$. Since $a = -1 < 0$, the parabola opens downwards (A). $f(0) = -4 + 3 = -1$, so the graph crosses the $y$-axis at $(0, -1)$ and not at $(0, 3)$ (C). The vertex lies above the $x$-axis and the parabola opens downwards, so $f$ has two zeros (D). A6: The answer is (D). At each throw “no six” has the probability $\frac{5}{6}$, so by the path rule $\left(\frac{5}{6}\right)^3 = \frac{125}{216}$. (C) is the probability of the complementary event “at least one six”, (A) that of three sixes, (B) comes from the wrong approach $1 - 3 \cdot \frac{1}{6}$, and (E) only holds for one throw.

TASK 2LEVEL II · MAKING CONNECTIONS

2Two parallel lines and a rhombus

The figure shows the graph of the linear function $g$, which passes through the points $P(-2, 1)$ and $Q(2, 3)$. The task is to be solved without a calculator; roots may be left in the result.

State an equation of the function $g$ and calculate its zero.

Gradient: $m = \frac{3 - 1}{2 - (-2)} = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$. Substituting $Q$ into $y = \frac{1}{2}x + n$: $3 = 1 + n$, so $n = 2$ and $g(x) = \frac{1}{2}x + 2$. Check with $P$: $\frac{1}{2} \cdot (-2) + 2 = 1$. Zero: $\frac{1}{2}x + 2 = 0$ gives $x = -4$.

The line $h$ runs parallel to $g$ through the point $N(4, 0)$. State an equation of $h$.

Parallel lines have the same gradient, so $h(x) = \frac{1}{2}x + n$. With $N$: $0 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 4 + n$, so $n = -2$ and $h(x) = \frac{1}{2}x - 2$.

The four points where the two lines cross the coordinate axes are the vertices of a quadrilateral. Calculate its area.

The vertices are $A(-4, 0)$ and $B(0, 2)$ on $g$ and $C(4, 0)$ and $D(0, -2)$ on $h$. The diagonals $\overline{AC}$ and $\overline{BD}$ lie on the axes, so they are perpendicular, and they are $8$ and $4$ long. Area: $\frac{1}{2} \cdot 8 \cdot 4 = 16$ units of area. Check: the axes split the quadrilateral into four right-angled triangles with the legs $4$ and $2$, each with the area $\frac{1}{2} \cdot 4 \cdot 2 = 4$.

Justify that this quadrilateral is a rhombus but not a square.

All four sides are hypotenuses of such triangles with the legs $4$ and $2$, so they are equally long: $\sqrt{4^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{20} = 2\sqrt{5}$. A quadrilateral with four sides of equal length is a rhombus. It is not a square, because its diagonals have different lengths. You can also see it from the gradients: $\overline{AB}$ rises with $\frac{1}{2}$, $\overline{BC}$ falls with $-\frac{1}{2}$, and the product $-\frac{1}{4}$ is not $-1$. So the sides are not perpendicular.

TASK 3LEVEL III · EVALUATING

3The raffle drum at the school fête

At the raffle of the school fête, a drum now contains only winning tickets ($G$) and blanks ($N$). Lena draws two tickets one after the other; a ticket drawn is not put back. The incomplete tree diagram describes this random experiment.

Fill in the three missing probabilities in the tree diagram.

At every branching point the probabilities add up to $1$. First stage: blank with $1 - \frac{2}{5} = \frac{3}{5}$. After a winning ticket: blank with $1 - \frac{1}{3} = \frac{2}{3}$. After a blank: blank with $1 - \frac{4}{9} = \frac{5}{9}$.

Justify that before Lena drew, there were exactly $10$ tickets in the drum, $4$ of them winning tickets.

Let there be $n$ tickets with $k$ winning tickets in the drum. At the second draw $n - 1$ tickets are left; after a blank all $k$ winning tickets are still there, after a winning ticket only $k - 1$. So $\frac{k}{n - 1} = \frac{4}{9}$ and $\frac{k - 1}{n - 1} = \frac{1}{3} = \frac{3}{9}$. The difference of the two fractions is $\frac{1}{n - 1} = \frac{1}{9}$, so $n - 1 = 9$ and $n = 10$. Then $k = \frac{4}{9} \cdot 9 = 4$. Check with the first stage: $\frac{4}{10} = \frac{2}{5}$.

Calculate the probability that Lena draws at least one winning ticket.

Via the complementary event “two blanks”: $P = 1 - \frac{3}{5} \cdot \frac{5}{9} = 1 - \frac{1}{3} = \frac{2}{3}$. A second way via the three paths with a win: $\frac{2}{5} \cdot \frac{1}{3} + \frac{2}{5} \cdot \frac{2}{3} + \frac{3}{5} \cdot \frac{4}{9} = \frac{2}{15} + \frac{4}{15} + \frac{4}{15} = \frac{10}{15} = \frac{2}{3}$.

State an event whose probability can be calculated with the term $1 - \frac{2}{5} \cdot \frac{1}{3}$, and state the value of the term.

$\frac{2}{5} \cdot \frac{1}{3}$ is the probability of the path “winning ticket, winning ticket”. So the term belongs to the complementary event: “Lena draws at most one winning ticket”, equivalent to “Lena draws at least one blank”. Value: $1 - \frac{2}{15} = \frac{13}{15}$.

LOOKING BACKTHE IDEA AND THE TRAP

TASK 1 · SIX MULTIPLE-CHOICE QUESTIONS

A percentage refers to the old value; roots are only combined when multiplying. Rule out what is wrong first.

Trap: in A1 the base value decides: a change in percent always refers to the old value. If you divide by $30$, you end up at (D). And in A2, $\sqrt{a} \cdot \sqrt{b} = \sqrt{a \cdot b}$ holds, but $\sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b}$ is not $\sqrt{a + b}$.

TASK 2 · TWO PARALLEL LINES AND A RHOMBUS

The same gradient means parallel. The rhombus fills exactly half of the rectangle formed by its diagonals.

Trap: in c) the area is not $8 \cdot 4 = 32$: that would be the rectangle surrounding the rhombus. The rhombus fills exactly half of it.

TASK 3 · THE RAFFLE DRUM AT THE SCHOOL FÊTE

Read the tree backwards: only both stages together fix the number of tickets. “At least one” goes via the complementary event.

Trap: in d), “exactly one winning ticket” is wrong: it only has the probability $\frac{4}{15} + \frac{4}{15} = \frac{8}{15}$, because the path “blank, blank” also belongs to the event. And in b) the first stage alone is not enough: $\frac{2}{5}$ also fits $6$ of $15$ or $8$ of $20$ tickets. Only the second stage fixes the number.

TESTTHE SAME IDEAS, NEW NUMBERS
ROUND 1

A price falls from €$80$ to €$60$. By what percentage has it fallen?

$\frac{20}{80} = 25\%$, relative to the old price.

The line $h$ is parallel to $y = 2x + 1$ and passes through $(3, 2)$. What is its $y$-intercept $n$?

$2 = 2 \cdot 3 + n$, so $n = -4$.

ROUND 2

A rhombus has the diagonals $6$ and $10$. What is its area?

$\frac{6 \cdot 10}{2} = 30$, half of the surrounding rectangle.

$3$ red and $2$ blue balls, two draws without replacement. What is the probability of at least one blue?

Complementary event “red twice”: $1 - \frac{3}{5} \cdot \frac{2}{4} = 1 - \frac{3}{10} = \frac{7}{10}$
